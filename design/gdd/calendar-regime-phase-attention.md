## Summary

Calendar, Regime Phase & Attention is the clock and the barometer of Paper Walls: it reads the beat the dossier counts, computes the regime's phase and each character's effective attention from the dossier's aggregates, and turns them into the only things the player sees of either, the date stamp's ink reach, the surveillance stamp's fill and the tear-off calendar, as tiers that change only between scenes. It holds the authored timetable (day, slot and Plan Week per scene) and the small `calendar.*` store of clocks and event flags that rules read and write, and it writes nothing to the dossier itself. It exists because Pillar 5 needs the state's attention to be visible without a meter, Pillar 2 needs the state to have moods that are read off paper and never fall, and the simulator and the runtime need one shared notion of when things happen.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Foundation` · Priority: `MVP` (phase and per-character attention) / `Vertical Slice` (calendar) · Key deps: `Dossier State Model` (aggregates, thresholds, the beat, the effect record), `Reactive Event Rules & Story Simulator` (the only writer of `calendar.*`; the ratchet rows; `phase()` through the reader), `Narrative Content Format & Strings` (scene lists, placeholders, `phase_at_least`); read by Scene Flow, Overlay & Surveillance Rendering, Paper UI, Audio, Investigation & Fact, Save/Load

## Overview

*Framing: infrastructure first (Category `Core`, Layer Foundation), with its player-facing effect named because this is the one Core system the player reads off the frame every scene. No ADR cites it yet; ADR-1 (the dossier) is to confirm Core Rule 1a's `calendar.*` channel.*

Calendar, Regime Phase & Attention is the clock and the barometer of Paper Walls. It owns three things that nothing else may own: **the beat**, the act-wide count of scene boundaries that every window, drift period and law is stated over; **the regime phase**, a pure function of the dossier's regime aggregates (stability, unrest, the `paranoid` flag and the `phase_reached` ratchet) that yields one of four values, confident, paranoid, panicking, collapsing, and never falls; and **effective attention**, the per-character reading of the state's eye (the stored `attention` scalar, whether the person is listed, and the phase) rendered as tiers, never as numbers: how far stamp red reaches on this person's paper. At the Vertical Slice it also becomes the calendar proper: the authored timetable that gives each scene a day, a weekday and a Plan Week for the date stamp and the tear-off transition, and the small `calendar.*` store of world clocks and window suspensions (Marta's breath, the paused route) that rules read and write through the same effect records as the dossier. The player never operates it. They read it: the typed date in the top-left corner, the ink reach of that date stamp, the surveillance stamp filling on an open sheet, the day that turns over while they were not there. Without it the state would have no tempo and no face: rules could not be timed, red could not know how far to reach, the simulator could not say which week an ending belongs to, and Pillar 5 (pressure comes from attention, not reflexes) would have nothing to show the pressure with. It writes nothing to the dossier; it is read by Rules, Scene Flow, Overlay & Surveillance Rendering, Paper UI, Audio and the simulator, and it is pure logic that runs headless.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees a counter, a meter or a quantity of attention or phase; the only numerals on screen are dates typed on in-world paper. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "the date is stamped, and so are you" chosen; "time passes without you" kept as the calendar transition's own line from art bible §2.1; "attention has a colour" left as the Overlay's identity, not this system's).*

**The date is stamped, and so are you.** This system serves two feelings that share one red. The first is time going by without you: the city keeps its dates whether or not anyone has done anything. The second is being looked at. It is not being chased. The state's interest is a colour that spreads across the page a little further each time a scene ends, and it only gets further if you give it a reason to.

The anchor moment: it is Friday of week one and the date stamp in the corner is black, with red only in the margin. A few scenes later a memo is circulated, typed and passive: *heightened vigilance is requested*. The scene ends and the wall calendar tears a page off. In the next scene the stamp's ink has bled past its border. There is a red telephone on Vera's desk. In the corner of Jan's sheet the surveillance stamp is half filled, but in the corner of Tomas's it is still only hatched. Nobody says the state is now paranoid, and nobody says it is looking at Jan. The memo said one and the paper shows the other. The player works out that the second came from the first, and that it came from something they filed.

Pillar alignment. Pillar 5, "pressure comes from attention, not reflexes": the tension is how far the red has spread, never a running clock, and the player may sit with a sheet as long as they like. Pillar 2, "the state is a character": its moods are four kinds of paper, tidy, counting, noise and ash, and the reader learns them the way they would learn a person's moods. Its last mood is the worst one, because then the red withdraws: stamps go unanswered, and the date keeps stamping with no one behind it. Pillar 4, "consequences cross lives": attention is tracked per character, so a filing in one chapter turns up as red on someone else's page.

**The one rule this system must obey: time moves only between scenes, and red moves only with it.** No day turns, no phase rises and no stamp fills while the player is reading. The calendar has no wall clock, and the state's attention never animates; it changes at a scene boundary, and only because of something on paper. A phase is announced on paper before its red appears. The red's reach is one of four fixed steps, never a gradient the player could watch creep. So when the red has moved, there is always a page behind it the player could have read.

*Cost accepted:* the calendar cannot be a ticking clock. A player who stalls feels no pressure until they act, so urgency has to be written as windows counted in scenes, not in seconds. Attention is felt in four coarse steps: a small gain stays invisible until it crosses into the next step, so the warning comes late and comes all at once. Marta's breath is the act's real clock, and it is felt only through paper; some players will not notice it until the ambulance comes.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary), the gameplay programmer (feasibility) and the engine programmer (engine integration); rulings where they differed are stated in place. Engine facts are quoted only from the pinned reference, which has no `core.md` module and nothing on `RefCounted`, `Time` or `Timer` beyond one deprecation line: this design needs none of them, which is the point of Rule 10. Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a GDD not yet written (Scene Flow 8, Overlay 9, Paper UI 13, Chapter & Story Structure 14, Audio 16, Save/Load). Six amendments to GDD 1 and GDD 6 that this section needed are applied there and named where they bite.*

**0. Vocabulary.** The *beat* is GDD 1's boundary counter (Rule 0 there): one per scene boundary, act-wide, monotone in **play order**. The *day* is a calendar day offset from the act's opening date, authored per scene; it is **story time** and is not a function of the beat. A *slot* is the part of a day a scene occupies (`morning | day | night`). The *timetable* is the per-act data file that gives every authored scene a day and a slot. A *calendar beat* is an authored scene of kind `calendar`: it closes a boundary with an empty decision batch and spends a beat without asking the player anything. A *transition* is the data the Calendar hands Scene Flow when two consecutive scenes of one chapter differ in day (art bible §2.1 "time passes without you"). A *clock* is a `calendar.*` scalar that a Rules drift counts down or up (act one: `calendar.breath_marta`). An *event flag* is a `calendar.*` flag a world move sets (act one: `courier_at_gate`, `family_at_gate`, `ledger_due`, `review_called`, `needs_paper`) or a rule suspends (`route_paused`). A *tier* is a small integer from a closed set that a consumer renders; the Calendar emits tiers and booleans, never a float and never a count. The *snapshot* is the one `BoundaryView` the Calendar computes per boundary at Scene Flow's "phase and attention" step; everything Overlay, Paper UI and Audio read is in it.

**1. One counter, two clocks: the beat is the dossier's and the Calendar never increments it.** The beat is GDD 1's boundary counter, incremented by `end_boundary()` and by nothing else (GDD 1 Rule 4a; GDD 6 Rule 7 reads it the same way). The Calendar receives the beat in the boundary context Scene Flow assembles — `ctx {beat, scene_id, chapter_id, day, slot}` — where `beat` is the index of the boundary in progress (the value the counter will hold when `end_boundary` returns, i.e. `counter + 1` during the bracket; GDD 1 Rule 4a now says so), `day`/`slot` come from this system's `date_for(scene_id)`, and `day_high` is the Calendar's **high-water day**, the latest `day` any played scene of the act has reached (held as store state, Rule 11): records written by `rules` carry `day_high` as their receipt date, records written by the player's own writers carry `day` (GDD 1 Rule 5, amended for this GDD). The Calendar exposes no `advance()`, holds no counter of its own, and a `calendar.*` change record stamps the `beat` it was handed. *Rationale:* one clock, saved once, stamped on every change record by the same call that emits them; a second counter in the Calendar would be a second truth the save could disagree with. *Rejected:* the Calendar's `advance()` called by Scene Flow before `end_boundary` (two increments to keep in step, and the dossier's change records would stamp a beat the Calendar had not yet reached, or the reverse).

**2. A boundary is a boundary; the calendar beat is an authored scene with no decision.** Every authored scene closes with GDD 1's full bracket, and the beat counts authored scenes only. A calendar beat is a scene row of `kind: calendar` in the chapter's scene list (Narrative Content's data, Chapter & Story Structure's placement **(PROV)**), with a timetable row like any scene. Scene Flow runs it as `begin_boundary(id)` → `apply([])` (an empty decision batch) → the rules step (drift ticks, world moves and NPC moves may fire: time passes and the state moves while you are not there) → `end_boundary` → snapshot → reveal. This is the **only** way a beat is spent without a decision, which settles GDD 6's open question: the two slack beats exist as act-close calendar beats (`act_close` in the timetable, Rule 3), so the review can sit at the closing bell with no decision offered. An insert scene (a fired rule's `beat: insert`) closes **no boundary of its own**: it plays between two authored boundaries, inherits the day and slot of the boundary that fired it, and any dialogue effects it produces ride the decision batch of the next authored boundary (writer `dialogue`). *Rationale:* GDD 6 Rule 7 says the beat does not advance on an NPC reaction and GDD 1 says it advances on every boundary; the two agree only if an NPC reaction has no boundary, so this rule makes that explicit (an amendment applied to GDD 6 Rule 7). *Rejected:* an implicit calendar beat generated by the Calendar whenever consecutive scenes differ in day (the Calendar would be spending beats from timetable data the simulator cannot see in the rules file, and the act's twelve beats would no longer be countable off the chapter's scene list). *Also rejected:* counting insert scenes as beats (a telling with three reactive events would run three beats shorter than the same telling in the solver, and every window would mean something different at runtime).

**3. The timetable: days are authored per scene; chapters overlap in calendar time; play order is recorded, never chosen here.** One data file per act, authored as `assets/content-src/calendar/<act>.calendar.yaml` and compiled to `assets/data/calendar/calendar_<act>.json` by the content compiler (GDD 2's two-layer pipeline; `.claude/rules/data-files.md` keeps YAML out of `assets/data/`), loaded by a loader outside the Calendar that rejects any non-integral number naming the key (no file I/O inside the system):

```yaml
act: a1
opening: { date: 1989-10-06, plan_week: 40, weekday: fri }   # day 0 is Friday, week one (narrative brief §3); Plan Weeks are ISO weeks, Monday start
week_start: mon                              # which weekday begins a Plan Week; a knob, not a rule
play_order: [a1_vera, a1_jan, a1_tomas]      # Chapter & Story Structure's decision (the narrative director's ruling), recorded here so windows are checked against it (PROV)
chapters:                                    # beat spans under this order: Vera 1–4, Jan 5–8, Tomas 9–12, act close 13–14
  a1_vera:
    scenes:                                  # every authored scene id of the chapter, incl. kind: calendar rows
      v_office: { day: 0, slot: day }        # Fri 6 Oct, PW 40: the office (V1)
      v_night:  { day: 0, slot: night }      # the night (V6 warn / V7 leak)
      v_turn:   { day: 3, slot: day }        # Mon 9 Oct, PW 41: the turn (V2/V3/V5); R1's insert inherits this date
      v_person: { day: 4, slot: morning }    # Tue 10 Oct: the person (V4/V8); the Friday-to-Monday jump is a transition, not a beat
  a1_jan:
    scenes:
      j_route:  { day: 0, slot: night }      # rewinds to Friday: chapters overlap in calendar time
      j_press:  { day: 2, slot: night }
      j_turn:   { day: 4, slot: day }
      j_person: { day: 6, slot: morning }    # Thu 12 Oct
  a1_tomas:
    scenes:
      t_turn:   { day: 0, slot: night }      # the courier at the gate
      t_cal:    { day: 3, slot: night }      # the act's one in-chapter calendar beat (beat 10): Ondra decides about the log offstage; the inquiry can arrive in the morning
      t_person: { day: 4, slot: morning }
      t_family: { day: 6, slot: night }      # Thu 12 Oct
act_close:                                   # the HORIZON_SLACK calendar beats; no chapter; dated at the act's last day, Fri 13 Oct (DAYS_PER_ACT 8)
  - { id: a1_close_1, day: 7, slot: day }    # the review sits (beat 13)
  - { id: a1_close_2, day: 7, slot: night }  # the card (beat 14)
```

Validation at load (build fails; runtime logs and serves the last good date): every authored scene in a chapter's scene list has a row and every row names an authored scene; `day` is monotone non-decreasing within a chapter and `slot` is monotone within a day (`morning < day < night`); a `kind: calendar` scene's `day` is strictly greater than the preceding scene's (a calendar beat on which no time passes is an error); `0 ≤ day ≤ DAYS_PER_ACT − 1`; the act-close rows carry the act's last day; `play_order` lists every chapter once. The day **rewinds at chapter open** by construction: Vera's chapter ends on Tuesday 10 October and Jan's opens on Friday 6 October. The beat does not rewind. This is the whole of the overlap problem and the Calendar's answer is that the two axes are independent: **the beat is the clock the state moves on; the day is the stamp the player reads.** A consequence the narrative director has signed off: a Calendar clock runs on beats, so Marta's breath runs down across all three chapters in play order, and under the act-one spans an unsupplied Marta collapses at beat 6 or 7, in Jan's chapter, on Vrba Street where someone sees the stretcher, on a date earlier than the one Vera's chapter closed on with Marta at home. That is Pillar 4 in its literal form (consequences flow forward in play order; GDD 1 Edge Case 12 says time order is the truth of a file), and the art bible's "time passes without you" is the cover for it. Two riders hold it: the collapse lands in Jan's chapter, never Tomas's (a span constraint the simulator's `first_beat` report checks), and **the file dates rules-written records by receipt**: a change record written by `rules` carries the Calendar's high-water day `day_high` (Rule 1), so a hospital admission on the Sunday reaches the Section's file the day the file is next opened and no entry is dated before a page the player has already read (GDD 1 Rule 5, amended). *Rationale:* deriving a date from the beat (e.g. 1.2 beats per day) makes every chapter the same length in days and puts the date under the control of how many reactive events fired, which no writer can plan a scene around; authored days let the writer say "Monday, the ledger is due" and have it be Monday. *Rejected:* per-chapter clocks (breath restarting at 2 for each protagonist: the collapse would happen three times or never, and `breath_is_a_clock` would have three answers). *Also rejected:* authoring the day on the chapter sheet or in the dossier (the date is not on any file and is read by every system; GDD 1 Rule 1 sorts it to the Calendar).

**4. Transitions are data; the Calendar plays nothing and spends nothing.** `transition_between(a, b) → {kind: none | slot | day, from: date_a, to: date_b, days_elapsed}` for two consecutive authored scenes of **one chapter**; Scene Flow calls it when it advances and plays `kind: day` as the Calendar-transition state (the tear-off leaves are `days_elapsed`; the change notices on that screen are Paper UI's, drawn from the boundary's change records, never from here) and `kind: slot` as a plate change inside the scene (Overlay's). A transition is not a beat and has no boundary; when the writer wants time to pass *and* the state to move, the chapter's scene list carries a `kind: calendar` scene (Rule 2) and the transition plays before it. Chapter open has no transition: the chapter's first date is stamped cold (the chapter's opening card is Chapter & Story Structure's **(PROV)**), and `transition_between` is never called across a chapter edge, so `days_elapsed` is never negative. No transition plays before an insert scene (Rule 2). *Rationale:* "time passes without you" is a presentation of authored data; letting it spend a beat would make the act's length depend on how the writer chose to stage a gap. *Rejected:* the Calendar emitting a `transition` signal (a signal is a call into whatever is connected; a returned value is replayable headlessly, GDD 6 Rule 9's argument).

**5. The Calendar writes nothing: not a dossier field, not an effect record, not its own store unprompted.** The Calendar produces no effect records of any kind and holds no writer tag (the writer enum is GDD 1's; adding `calendar` is a schema change this GDD does not request). Its store (Rule 6) changes only through `apply_calendar(batch, ctx)` with effects whose `source` parses to writer `rules` (or `migrate`, `sim`), produced by Rules and routed by Scene Flow — and routed **inside the cascade**: the `apply` Scene Flow injects into `run_boundary` is a composite that dispatches `dossier.*`/`sheet.*` targets to the dossier and `calendar.*` targets here, so a cascade step sees a drift tick on `calendar.breath_marta` at once and `marta_collapses` fires in the boundary where breath reaches `BREATH_ZERO`, not the next one (an amendment applied to GDD 6 Rules 5 and 9); `RulesResult.calendar_effects[]` stays as the trace of what was routed. GDD 1 Formulas §2's "a boundary rule writes `max regime.phase_reached = phase`" is honoured as reserved `world_move` rows in the act's rules file, one per phase the act can reach — act one declares `phase_ratchet_1` only:

```yaml
- world_move: phase_ratchet_1
  class: bookkeeping          # kind event; a class with no paper and no scene
  window: [1, HORIZON_ACT]
  pick: fixed
  needs: { at_most: [regime.phase_reached, PHASE_CONFIDENT] }   # not already ratcheted; PHASE_* are named constants 0..3
  licences: { reached: { phase_at_least: 1 } }
  effects: [ { target: regime, field: phase_reached, op: max, value: 1 } ]
  beat: none                  # GDD 6 Rule 2 admits `none` on a bookkeeping world move (amendment applied)
```

Because `phase_at_least` reads `phase()` live during the rules step (pre-boundary state plus batches applied so far, GDD 1 Rule 4a), a hit in the decision batch that moves the band ratchets in the same boundary; a hit from a cascade step ratchets at the next step, or at the next boundary if the cascade cap intervenes, and `phase()` is exact meanwhile because it is `max(phase_reached, band)` either way. The ratchet is a world move so it is exempt from the per-boundary agent cap and from the `state: true` obligations; its `needs` guard keeps it from the "all effects no-op" authoring smell (GDD 6 Rule 10c). The announcements (`regime_paranoid` and the act-two moves) remain the paper the player sees; the ratchet row is never paper. *Rationale:* every write that crosses chapters starts from something on paper and carries a `rules` source the simulator can replay; a ratchet the Calendar wrote silently would be the one write in the game with no rule behind it. *Rejected:* a `calendar` writer (schema change; breaks GDD 1's Interactions row "Nothing written here by the Calendar"); the `chapter` writer ratcheting at chapter close (`regime.*` accepts it, but a band reached mid-chapter and lost to relief before the close would never ratchet — harmless in act one, wrong in acts two and three); dropping `phase_reached` and keeping the phase in the Calendar's store (the phase is an ending aggregate and GDD 1 owns the regime record).

**6. The `calendar.*` store: a small closed-world schema with GDD 1's ops and no tags.** Declared in `assets/data/calendar/schema.json` (every field: `name, type (flag | scalar), range or —, default, writers (allow-list; `rules`, `migrate`, `sim`), cross_life: true` — always true, GDD 1 Rule 1a); nothing undeclared is readable or writable (authoring: build fails; runtime: read returns the default and logs, write rejected and logged). Act-one fields: scalar `breath_marta` (0–`BREATH_MAX` 4, default `BREATH_OPENING` 2), flags `route_paused`, `courier_at_gate`, `family_at_gate`, `ledger_due`, `review_called`, `needs_paper` (default `false`). Ops are GDD 1 §4's with three differences: flags are **two-state** (`set true|false`; `clear` → `false`, not `unknown`; `is_unknown` on a `calendar.*` target is a content error, because a schedule has no blanks), `tag` is rejected (the calendar carries no provenance marginalia), and `set` on a scalar is legal from any allowed writer (there is no authored decision writer here to protect against). Scalars clamp to the declared range; `add`s in one batch sum, `max`/`min` apply after (GDD 1 Edge Case 2); same-field conflicts within one batch resolve by list order, across cascade steps by application order (all writers are `rules`, so GDD 1's writer precedence never arises). A change record is emitted once per effect that changed something, in GDD 1's shape with `target: calendar`, `store: calendar` (its `id` space is this store's own `next_change_id`, so consumers key on `(store, id)`), `source_protagonist: null`, `cross_life: true`, `status_change: null`, `contradicts: null`, `projections: []`; a no-op emits nothing. The records go back to the caller (the composite apply appends them to the boundary-local change list, so `{changed: calendar.breath_marta}` is a legal leaf) and are kept in the store's own change log for the act. Event flags are cleared by rules, not by expiry (`courier_at_gate` clears when a rule sees the gate decided: `changed: border.courier_through` or `border.courier_detained`); the Calendar never expires anything (Rule 5). *Rationale:* one effect grammar everywhere (GDD 1 Rule 1a's argument); two-state flags because the calendar is a schedule, not a file; no tags because nobody initials a wall calendar. *Rejected:* tri-state flags (GDD 1 needs the blank for "not on file"; an event is or is not scheduled); windows as store fields with a `ttl` (the Calendar would write on its own, and windows are rule data by GDD 6 Rule 2); reusing the dossier's `next_change_id` (two stores incrementing one counter across a composite apply is a race in all but name).

**7. Reads: `calendar.*` is read through the one reader and budgeted; the Calendar's own aggregate reads are not.** `read(field)` serves `calendar.*` to the composed `ConditionReader` (GDD 5's shape; the composition routes target `calendar` here — a requirement on Scene Flow's reader assembly **(PROV)**) and to Scene Flow; reads are side-effect free, see pre-boundary state plus batches applied so far, and are logged per chapter as distinct field ids against the cross-life read budget (GDD 1 Rule 1a; Formulas §7). The phase and attention functions read GDD 1's aggregates — `regime.{stability, unrest, paranoid, phase_reached}`, `x.{attention, under_observation}` — through an injected `aggregates()` accessor that is **not** logged against any chapter's budget (an amendment applied to GDD 1 Rule 6); content that wants the phase pays GDD 2's one virtual read `regime.phase` through `phase_at_least`, and nothing else. *Rationale:* the Calendar reads the four regime aggregates at every boundary for every protagonist; budgeting that would charge every chapter four cross-life reads it never authored. *Rejected:* the Calendar caching the aggregates at `end_boundary` and serving stale values during the next boundary (the rules step must see the cascade's own writes to the regime record, GDD 1 Rule 4a).

**8. The phase is GDD 1's function, computed on demand, never stored, never a function of the beat or the day.** `phase() = max(phase_reached, band(stability, unrest))` with GDD 1 Formulas §2's `band` and constants, verbatim and by registry name; it is evaluated from the live aggregates whenever called (the rules step calls it through the reader mid-cascade), and the snapshot (Rule 9) freezes one value per boundary for the presentation systems. The Calendar imposes no earliest beat on any phase: **`PARANOID_EARLIEST` is a window bound on one world move and stays in GDD 6 and the act rules file**; this GDD does not reference it, and the registry's planned-consumer entry should be dropped rather than moved. How announcement and band compose: facts license `regime_paranoid` (GDD 6 Rule 13); the announcement writes the aggregates (`paranoid`, `phase_reached max 1`, `stability −1`); the function reads them. The band can also reach 1 from stability alone (two hits: `9 − 2 = 7 ≤ T_PARANOID`) before the announcement fires, so the phase can be paranoid a beat or two before any paper says so — the state counting before it speaks. The act rules data closes most of that gap: `regime_paranoid` carries a second licence `by_number: {at_most: [regime.stability, T_PARANOID]}` (applied to GDD 6 Rule 13; the narrative director confirms the hit placement, Open Questions), so the announcement is also licensed by the number and an unannounced paranoid run lasts at most until the window's `lo`; the simulator reports the longest such run in the game space, as a report, never a law; the Calendar reports the phase and does not hide it. *Rationale:* GDD 1 §2 says the function reads only the aggregates; a beat or day term in it would make the regime's mood a function of the schedule, which is the anti-pillar's "the game decided" in a different coat. *Rejected:* gating `phase()` at `PARANOID_EARLIEST` (the phase would then disagree with the band the ending selector reads); storing the phase in the Calendar's store (GDD 1 Core Rule: "phase itself is not stored").

**9. Outputs are tiers and booleans in one snapshot per boundary; Overlay owns pixels.** At Scene Flow's "phase and attention" step (after the rules step, before the reveal) the Calendar computes and returns one plain-data `BoundaryView`, memoised by beat and invalidated at the next `begin_boundary`:

```yaml
BoundaryView:
  beat: 7
  date: { day: 9, weekday: sun, plan_week: 41, slot: night }     # date_for(scene_id); the date-stamp text is Narrative Content's string with placeholders {date}/{plan_week}
  phase: 1                                                       # 0 confident · 1 paranoid · 2 panicking · 3 collapsing
  ink_reach: 2                                                   # PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]: 0 dry · 1 crisp · 2 bleeds · 3 floods
  per_character:                                                 # every record of kind person that carries `attention`
    jan:  { tier: 3, looked: true,  watched: true,  listed: true,  red_reach: 2, stamp_tier: 2 }
    vera: { tier: 1, looked: false, watched: false, listed: false, red_reach: 1, stamp_tier: 1 }
```

where `tier = red_band(x)` (GDD 1 §3, uncapped: what the state's eye weighs on this person), `looked = A_eff(x) ≥ ATTN_LOOK`, `watched = A_eff(x) ≥ ATTN_WATCHED` (the two attention states Audio backs), `listed = is_true(x.under_observation)`, `red_reach(x) = min(red_band(x), PHASE_RED_CAP[phase])` with `PHASE_RED_CAP = [1, 2, 3, 0]` (confident: margin only; paranoid: props; panicking: key light; collapsing: withdraws), `stamp_tier(x) = red_reach(x)` for the surveillance stamp on a sheet whose protagonist is `x` (art bible §7.5: hatched outline at 0 and 1, partial fill at 2, full at 3, back to hatch at collapsing because the cap is 0 — the §4.3 phase tier is the stamp's ceiling and the person's reach is its fill), and `ink_reach = PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]` for the HUD date stamp, which reads the phase alone (art bible §7.1). Nothing in the view is a float, a count, a threshold or a constant; `A_eff` itself never leaves the Calendar. Consumers (Overlay 9, Paper UI 13, Audio 16 **(PROV)**) read the snapshot and never call `phase()` or `attention_view()` mid-scene, so every tier changes only at a boundary and the stamp is static (art bible §7.5 "no motion"). *Rationale:* Pillar 5 and art bible Principle A — attention has a colour and a step, not a meter; a float would invite a lerp and a lerp is a meter. *Rejected:* per-character caps or a per-protagonist phase (the regime has one mood); exposing `A_eff` "for Audio to shape a mix" (Audio gets `looked`/`watched` and the tier, and designs its two states on those).

**10. No wall clock, no settings, no scene tree.** The Calendar reads no engine time source of any kind and owns no timer; the interrogation timer (`timer_mode`) is Dialogue's and Overlay's, and the Settings reader is never imported here (GDD 4's CI path check names Dossier, Rules and Calendar). It is a `RefCounted`-style plain object, constructor-injected with the schema, the timetable and the `aggregates()` accessor, with no autoload, no scene-tree access and no file I/O; the loader that reads the YAML/JSON lives outside it. The only engine-time fact the pinned reference holds is that `Time.get_ticks_msec()` replaced `OS.get_ticks_msec()` (deprecated-apis.md); the Calendar calls neither. *Rationale:* the story simulator runs the same code under `--headless` thousands of boundaries at a time; a system that asked the clock would not replay. *Rejected:* hosting the interrogation timer here "because it is time" (it is a decision's pressure, not the state's calendar; Pillar 5 lives in the frame, not in this file).

**11. Persistence: the store is state, the timetable and schema are data.** `serialize() → {schema_version, fields, change_log, next_change_id, day_high}` (`day_high` is the high-water day, Rule 1); `deserialize(data)` accepts only the current `CALENDAR_SCHEMA_VERSION` and only at a scene boundary, rejects whole on mismatch (`SCHEMA_MISMATCH`), on an undeclared field, or on a declared field missing (migrations fill with `defaults()`); the snapshot is never saved and is rebuilt at the first boundary after load; the current day is not saved because Scene Flow's saved `scene_id` recovers it through `date_for`. Save/Load owns the registry of stateful systems and migration (writer `migrate`) **(PROV)**. *Rationale:* GDD 1 Rule 10's contract, applied to a store one tenth the size. *Rejected:* saving the current date (two sources of truth for one scene's day).

**12. The simulator sees the calendar as fluents and the trace carries the date.** `topic_gen` takes the calendar schema beside the dossier schema, so `calendar.breath_marta`, `route_paused` and the event flags are fluents with ranges and defaults in the generated domain (an amendment applied to GDD 6 Rule 1); the drift on `calendar.breath_marta` and the world moves that set event flags are already Rules data. The replay trace's per-boundary `day` (GDD 6 Rule 11) comes from `date_for(scene_id)`; a Python twin of `date_for` and of `PHASE_RED_CAP` is checked against the GDScript on the golden act-one fixture, like GDD 6's RNG twin. The simulator's phase-reachability check (GDD 1 Tuning cluster b) is run over the game space with the timetable loaded, so a writer can read "paranoid is first reachable at beat 7, Monday of Plan Week 41, in Jan's chapter". *Rationale:* the solver cannot prove `breath_is_a_clock` over a fluent it does not have. *Rejected:* a hand-maintained list of calendar fluents in the `.lp` (one source of truth, GDD 6 Rule 1).

**13. What the system cannot do.** Never writes a dossier field, a sheet field or a phase; never produces an effect record (not even `calendar.*`); never increments, resets or reads back the beat except from the context it is handed; never reads a wall clock, a frame time or a timer; never makes the phase or any tier a function of the beat or the day; never emits a float, a count, a threshold or a raw `A_eff` to any consumer; never fills a placeholder from a dossier field (`{date}`/`{plan_week}` are filled from the timetable, GDD 2 V44 holds); never schedules, expires or suspends anything on its own (windows are Rules' data, `route_paused` is a rules write); never chooses chapter order or scene order and never plays a scene or a transition (it returns values; Scene Flow calls it, never the reverse); never imports the settings reader; never holds UI state (seen, animation, stamp pixels); never persists anything but its store; never reads a held fact; never runs mid-scene. Its errors go through an injected `DebugSink` like GDD 6's, so every assert is observable headless.

### States and Transitions

**The act, as the Calendar sees it:** `unopened` (no timetable loaded) → `open` (timetable validated; store at `defaults()`) → per chapter in `play_order`: `in_chapter` (dates monotone; transitions legal) → `chapter_edge` (the day rewinds at the next chapter's first scene; no transition) → ... → `act_close` (the `HORIZON_SLACK` calendar beats; no chapter; dates fixed at the act's last day) → `closed` (store compacted with the change log at archive; a new act loads a new timetable and `defaults()` for its own schema).

**One boundary:** `idle` → `in_boundary` (from `begin_boundary`: `read`, `phase()`, `attention_view()` serve live values including batches applied so far; `apply_calendar` accepts `rules` batches from the composite apply) → `snapshotted` (the "phase and attention" step produced the `BoundaryView`; memoised by beat) → `idle` at the next `begin_boundary` (snapshot invalidated). Within `in_boundary`, a calendar beat differs only in that the decision batch is empty.

**A store field:** `default` → `written` on the first changing effect (a change record with `before` the default) → `written` thereafter; a flag `set false` or `clear`ed goes to `false`, never to a blank; a scalar at a bound clamps with no change record. No lifecycle statuses, no closed or archived states — the calendar is a schedule, not a file.

**The transition between consecutive authored scenes of one chapter:** `none` (same day, same slot) · `slot` (same day, later slot) · `day` (later day; the Calendar-transition state plays, `days_elapsed` leaves torn). Never `rewind` (chapter edges do not call it) and never before an insert.

**The regime phase (GDD 1's sequence, restated for the consumers):** confident → paranoid → panicking → collapsing, never backward, held by `phase_reached` (Rule 5's ratchet row). Act one by construction stops at paranoid. The red caps follow: 1 → 2 → 3 → 0.

## Formulas

*Proposed by the systems designer; three items it raised are ruled here: the `calendar.*` store emits one change record per field per batch (a stated divergence from GDD 1 Rule 4a's per-boundary compaction, kept because the `changed:` leaf needs each cascade step's record and no calendar scalar projects); the `breath_supply` priority above `marta_collapses` is rules data the narrative director confirms (Open Questions); the act-close pseudo-chapter `<act>_close` is a requirement on Chapter & Story Structure and GDD 1's source parser (amendment applied to GDD 1 Rule 5).* *Every constant appears by its registry name; the act-one value is quoted in the table. GDD 1's `regime_phase` (Formulas §2) and `attention_effective` with `red_band` (Formulas §3) are cited, never restated. Indices: `b` is the beat (the boundary in progress), `s` the cascade step inside it, `x` a person record, `d` a day offset. Square brackets `[P]` are the Iverson bracket (1 if `P`, else 0). Nothing below reads a wall clock, a beat-to-day ratio or a float.*

### 1. Phase: GDD 1's function, and how the ratchet row and the announcement compose with it

The phase is defined as GDD 1 Formulas §2 (registry `regime_phase`): `phase_{b,s} = max( phase_reached_{b,s}, band(stability_{b,s}, unrest_{b,s}) )`, evaluated live against the boundary view (pre-boundary state plus batches applied through step `s`). This GDD adds nothing to the function; it adds two rows of rules data and one derived predicate:

`ratchet_fires(n, b, s) = [ phase_{b,s} ≥ n ] · [ phase_reached_{b,s} ≤ n − 1 ] · [ ¬fired_act(phase_ratchet_n) ] · [ 1 ≤ beat_b ≤ HORIZON_ACT ]` — when 1, the row's effect `regime.phase_reached max n` is applied at step `s` (or the next step the policy admits it; it carries `priority: 100` so it wins any step it is a candidate in).

`announce_licensed(b, s) = [ PARANOID_EARLIEST ≤ beat_b ≤ HORIZON_ACT ] · ( facts_{b,s} ∨ [ stability_{b,s} ≤ T_PARANOID ] )` where `facts = is_true(jan.leaflets_distributed) ∨ is_true(vera.discrepancy) ∨ is_true(office.border_report) ∨ is_true(kastan.exposed)` — the four named licences plus `by_number`; `regime_paranoid` fires at the first boundary where this holds (GDD 6 Formulas §3), writing `paranoid true`, `phase_reached max 1`, `stability add −1`.

`unannounced_b = [ band_b ≥ 1 ] · [ ¬is_true(paranoid_b) ]` evaluated at the snapshot (the phase is paranoid on the aggregates and no paper has said so).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `phase_reached` | int | 0–3 | calculated (GDD 1 regime record, `max` only) | The ratchet scalar |
| `band` | int | 0–3 | calculated (GDD 1 §2) | Phase from the aggregates alone |
| `stability`, `unrest` | int | 0–10, 0–12 | calculated (GDD 1 aggregates via `aggregates()`) | The regime scalars |
| `paranoid` | bool (tri-state read as `is_true`) | — | calculated (written by `regime_paranoid`) | The state has named the phase |
| `facts` | bool | — | calculated | Any of the four fact licences |
| `n` | int | 1–3 | data file (one `phase_ratchet_n` row per reachable phase per act) | The ratchet row's phase |
| `beat_b` | int | 1–`HORIZON_ACT` (14) | ctx (GDD 1's counter) | The boundary in progress |
| `PARANOID_EARLIEST` | int | 1–`HORIZON_ACT`; act one 6 | constant (GDD 6, registry) | Window `lo` of `regime_paranoid`; never read by this system |
| `HORIZON_ACT` | int | `BEATS_PER_ACT + HORIZON_SLACK` = 14 | constant (registry) | Window `hi` |
| `T_PARANOID` | int | 6–8; 7 | constant (GDD 1, registry) | Stability ceiling of band 1 and the `by_number` licence |
| `fired_act(r)` | bool | — | calculated (Rules' `world_move_state`) | A world move fires once per act |
| `phase` | int | 0–3 | calculated | The output; `phase_at_least` and the snapshot read it |
| `unannounced_b` | int | 0–1 | calculated (snapshot) | Input to the §9 report |

**Output Range:** `phase` is bounded 0–3 by construction; monotone non-decreasing across boundaries once the ratchet row has landed (Edge Case 11 names the one gap, which the simulator fails as `truncated`); act one reaches at most 1 (GDD 1 §2's argument: three hits and unrest near 5). Minimum 0 at the act-one opening (9, 1, unknown, 0); maximum 3 only in a later act. `unannounced_b` is 0 or 1; it can be 1 only while `band_b = 1` and `beat_b < PARANOID_EARLIEST`, or after the window closed unlicensed (impossible once `by_number` is a licence, since band 1 implies `stability ≤ T_PARANOID` or `paranoid`).

**Example:** the chapter holding beats 5–8 is Jan's. J3 at beat 5: `stability 9 → 8`, `band 0`, `phase 0`. J4 at beat 6: the decision batch takes `stability` to 7; at step 0 (after drift) `band = 1` (`7 ≤ T_PARANOID`), so `phase = 1` at once; `phase_ratchet_1` is a candidate (`phase_at_least 1`, `phase_reached 0 ≤ 0`) and so is `regime_paranoid` (`6 ≥ PARANOID_EARLIEST`; `leaflets_distributed` and `by_number` both hold). Both are world moves under the pseudo-agent `world`; by `(priority desc, id asc)` the ratchet (priority 100) fires at step 1 (`phase_reached 1`), the announcement at step 1 or 2 (`paranoid true`, `phase_reached max 1` now a no-op, `stability 6`, `attention +1` to everyone at ≥ 1). At the snapshot `phase 1`, `unannounced_6 = 0`. Contrast a `play_order` with Jan first: J3 at beat 2, J4 at beat 3 give `band 1` at beat 3 while the announcement's window opens at 6 — `unannounced` is 1 at beats 3, 4, 5, which §9 reports as a run of 3 and the narrative director decides on. *Rejected:* a `phase_at_least` term in the announcement's licence instead of `by_number` (it would license the announcement from `phase_reached`, so a later act's relief could not be answered by a second announcement without a new row; the number is what the state reads).

### 2. Effective attention and the per-character view

Effective attention and the red band are defined as GDD 1 Formulas §3 (registry `attention_effective`): `A_eff(x) = clamp( attention(x) + OBS_BONUS·[under_observation(x)] + PHASE_BONUS·[phase ≥ 2], 0, ATTENTION_CAP )` and `red_band(x) = 0 if A_eff = 0; 1 if A_eff < ATTN_LOOK; 2 if A_eff ≤ ATTN_WATCHED; 3 otherwise`. The per-character view this system emits is defined as:

`view(x) = { tier: red_band(x), looked: [A_eff(x) ≥ ATTN_LOOK], watched: [A_eff(x) ≥ ATTN_WATCHED], listed: [is_true(under_observation(x))], red_reach: §3, stamp_tier: §4 }`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `attention(x)` | int | 0–`ATTENTION_CAP` | calculated (GDD 1 scalar via `aggregates()`) | The state's eye on `x` |
| `under_observation(x)` | tri-state | — | calculated (GDD 1 flag) | Listed; `unknown` counts as not listed in both the bonus and `listed` |
| `phase` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§1) | Regime phase at the moment of evaluation |
| `OBS_BONUS`, `PHASE_BONUS` | int | 0–2; 1, 1 | constants (GDD 1, registry) | Weight of listing; weight of panic |
| `ATTENTION_CAP` | int | 4–8; 6 | constant (GDD 1, registry) | Scalar cap |
| `ATTN_LOOK`, `ATTN_WATCHED` | int | `ATTN_LOOK < ATTN_WATCHED ≤ ATTENTION_CAP`; 3, 4 | constants (GDD 1, registry) | The two attention states |
| `A_eff(x)` | int | 0–`ATTENTION_CAP` | calculated | Never leaves this system |
| `tier` | int | 0–3 | calculated | none, margin, prop, light (uncapped) |
| `looked`, `watched`, `listed` | bool | — | calculated | The booleans Audio reads; `watched ⇒ looked` by the cluster constraint |

**Output Range:** `tier` 0–3; the booleans are monotone in `A_eff` (`watched ⇒ looked`) because `ATTN_LOOK < ATTN_WATCHED` is a GDD 1 tuning constraint, and `tier = 3 ⇒ watched`, `tier ≥ 2 ⇒ looked`; `tier = 2` includes `A_eff = ATTN_WATCHED`, so `watched ∧ tier = 2` is a legal pair (the two cuts of one integer differ by design: Audio's states are thresholds, Overlay's tiers are bands). Minimum: `{0, false, false, false}` at `attention 0`, unlisted; maximum: `{3, true, true, true}` at the cap. **Example:** Vera after V3 (`+1`) and R1 (`+2`, listed): `attention 3`, `A_eff = min(6, 3 + 1 + 0) = 4` → `tier 2`, `looked true`, `watched true`, `listed true`. Tomas, untouched: `A_eff 0` → `tier 0`, all false. *Rejected:* emitting `A_eff` and letting consumers threshold it (every consumer would own a copy of `ATTN_LOOK`, and one of them would draw a bar).

### 3. Phase-capped red reach

The red reach is defined as:

`red_reach(x) = min( red_band(x), PHASE_RED_CAP[phase] )`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `red_band(x)` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§2) | The person's uncapped tier |
| `phase` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§1) | Regime phase at the snapshot |
| `PHASE_RED_CAP` | int[4] | each 0–3; `[1, 2, 3, 0]` | constant (this GDD, registry) | The art bible §2.2/§4.3 ceiling per phase: margin, props, key light, withdrawn |
| `red_reach(x)` | int | 0–3 | calculated | The tier Overlay draws on `x`'s props and thresholds |

**Output Range:** bounded 0–3. Minimum 0 at `A_eff(x) = 0`, or for everyone at `phase 3` (the cap is 0: red withdraws). Maximum 3 only at `phase 2` with `A_eff(x) > ATTN_WATCHED`; tier 3 is unreachable in act one (phase ≤ 1 caps at 2) and at collapsing, which is the intended hole: red in the key light belongs to panic alone. With the defaults, at `phase 0` every watched person reads as 1 (a margin line) — the per-character signal at confident is `none` versus `margin`, and the rest is carried by signs and files (GDD 1 §7). **Example:** Jan after K1, J2, J3: `attention 5`, listed → `A_eff 6`, `red_band 3`; at `phase 1` → `red_reach = min(3, 2) = 2` (the red telephone, the red folder); the same Jan at `phase 0` → 1; at `phase 3` → 0. *Rejected:* a per-character cap (`min(red_band, cap[x])`) or a reach that adds phase and person (`red_band + phase`, which exceeds 3 and makes a calm person red in a panic for no reason of his own).

### 4. Surveillance stamp tier and date-stamp ink reach

The stamp tier and the ink reach are defined as:

`stamp_tier(x) = red_reach(x)` · `ink_reach = PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]`

with the invariant `stamp_tier(x) ≤ ink_reach` for every `x` at every snapshot (immediate from `min(a, c) ≤ c`).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `red_reach(x)` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§3) | The person's capped reach |
| `phase` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§1) | Regime phase at the snapshot |
| `PHASE_RED_CAP` | int[4] | `[1, 2, 3, 0]` | constant (registry) | As §3 |
| `stamp_tier(x)` | int | 0–3 | calculated | Fill tier of the art bible §7.5 stamp on a sheet whose protagonist is `x`: 0 and 1 hatched outline, 2 partial fill, 3 full fill |
| `ink_reach` | int | 0–3 | calculated | Ink reach of the §7.1 HUD date stamp: 0 dry, 1 crisp, 2 bleeds past the outline, 3 floods the corner |

**Output Range:** both 0–3; `ink_reach` takes exactly the values 1, 2, 3, 0 in phase order, so it is never 0 before collapsing and never 3 outside panicking. `stamp_tier` reaches 3 only when `ink_reach = 3`. The invariant is the reviewer test of art bible §7.5 ("fill percentage matches the §4.3 budget tier") restated as a bound: the phase tier is the stamp's ceiling, the person's reach its fill; Overlay asserts it in debug on every snapshot. **Example:** the act-one close at `phase 1`: `ink_reach 2`; Vera at `A_eff 4` (`red_band 2`) → `stamp_tier 2`; Tomas at `A_eff 0` → `stamp_tier 0`, a hatched outline under the same bleeding date. At `phase 3` with Jan at the cap: `ink_reach 0`, `stamp_tier 0` — the stamp goes unanswered while `view(jan).watched` stays true (Edge Case 13). *Rejected:* `stamp_tier = ink_reach` (the stamp would say the same thing as the date in the corner above it, and an unwatched conscript would wear a filled stamp in a paranoid week); a continuous fill percentage (a meter).

### 5. Date for a scene

The date for a scene is defined as, for the timetable row `(chapter_id, scene_id) → {day: d, slot}`:

`weekday(d) = (W_open + d) mod 7` · `plan_week(d) = PW_open + ⌊ ((W_open − W_start) mod 7 + d) / 7 ⌋` · `doy(d) = DOY_open + d` · `(month(d), day_of_month(d))` = the unique pair with `cum[month − 1] < doy(d) ≤ cum[month]` over the cumulative month-length table of `opening.year`, and `day_of_month = doy − cum[month − 1]`

and `date_for(chapter_id, scene_id) = { day: d, slot, weekday, plan_week, month, day_of_month, year: opening.year }`. Plan Weeks are ISO weeks: `W_start = mon` and `PW_open` is the ISO week of `opening.date`, which the content compiler checks against the authored `plan_week` and `weekday` (its own ISO calendar routine; a tooling fact, not an engine one).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d` | int | 0–`DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` (act one 0–7) | data file (timetable row) | The scene's day offset |
| `W_open` | int | 0–6 (mon 0 … sun 6); act one 4 (fri) | data file (`opening.weekday`) | Opening weekday |
| `W_start` | int | 0–6; 0 | data file (`week_start`; ISO fixes it at mon) | First day of a Plan Week |
| `PW_open` | int | 1–53; 40 | data file (`opening.plan_week`) | Opening Plan Week |
| `DOY_open` | int | 1–365; 279 for 1989-10-06 | compiled from `opening.date` | Day of year of the opening |
| `cum[0..12]` | int[13] | `[0, 31, 59, …, 365]` for 1989 (not leap) | compiled (month table of `opening.year`) | Cumulative month lengths |
| `DAYS_PER_ACT` | int | 7–21; act one 8 (the narrative director's ruling: Plan Week 41 closes Friday 13 October, day 7) | constant (this GDD, registry) | Act length in days; bounds `d` |
| `weekday(d)` | int | 0–6 | calculated | The stamped weekday id (the locale table renders it) |
| `plan_week(d)` | int | `PW_open`–`PW_open + 3` | calculated | The stamped Plan Week |
| `doy(d)` | int | `DOY_open`–`DOY_open + DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` | calculated | Day of year; the year bound is checked here |
| `month`, `day_of_month` | int | 1–12, 1–31 | calculated | The typed date on the stamp |

**Output Range:** `weekday` wraps 0–6; `plan_week` never wraps — `plan_week(DAYS_PER_ACT − 1) ≤ weeks_in_year(opening.year)` (52 for 1989; 53 in a long year) and `doy(DAYS_PER_ACT − 1) ≤ days_in_year(opening.year)` are build-time validation predicates (§8 V10), so the act stays inside its year and no date is ever computed past 31 December; with the act-one opening the maximum reachable is `d = 7` → Friday 13 October 1989, Plan Week 41, the closing bell (the narrative director's ruling: a Plan Week ends at close of business Friday; the weekend 14–15 October is the act-two transition's two torn leaves and belongs to no act). **Example:** `d = 3` → `weekday (4 + 3) mod 7 = 0` Monday; `plan_week 40 + ⌊(4 + 3)/7⌋ = 41`; `doy 282` → `cum[9] = 273 < 282 ≤ cum[10] = 304` → October, `day_of_month 9`: Monday 9 October 1989, Plan Week 41. `d = 7` → Friday 13 October, Plan Week 41, the act's last day; `d = 9` would be Sunday 15 October and is outside the act (`V6`). *Rejected:* an engine date routine from a Unix timestamp (NOT SOURCEABLE in the pinned reference, carries a time zone, and puts the wall clock one call away from a system that must never touch it); deriving `d` from the beat (Rule 3).

### 6. Transition between consecutive scenes

The transition is defined, for two authored scenes `a`, `b` with `chapter(a) = chapter(b)` and `index(b) = index(a) + 1` in that chapter's scene list, as:

`days_elapsed(a, b) = d_b − d_a` · `kind(a, b) = day if days_elapsed > 0; slot if days_elapsed = 0 ∧ slot_b > slot_a; none otherwise`

`transition_between(a, b) = { kind, from: date_for(a), to: date_for(b), days_elapsed }`; when the precondition fails (different chapters, non-consecutive, or either id absent from the timetable — which is what an insert scene id is) the call returns `TRANSITION_ILLEGAL(a, b)` and no fields (Edge Cases 2, 20).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d_a`, `d_b` | int | 0–`DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` | data file (timetable) | The two scenes' days |
| `slot_a`, `slot_b` | enum | `morning < day < night` | data file | The two scenes' slots |
| `index(·)` | int | 0–`|scenes(chapter)| − 1` | data file (scene list order) | Position in the chapter |
| `days_elapsed` | int | 0–`DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` | calculated | Leaves torn off the wall calendar (art bible §2.1) |
| `kind` | enum | `none | slot | day` | calculated | What Scene Flow plays: nothing, a plate change, the Calendar-transition state |

**Output Range:** `days_elapsed ≥ 0` always, because §8 V3 forbids a day decrease within a chapter and the precondition forbids crossing a chapter edge; the maximum is `DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` (a chapter whose second scene is the act's last day). `kind = none` with `days_elapsed = 0` and equal slots is legal (two scenes the same evening). **Example:** Vera `s3 {day 3, morning}` → `cal_1 {day 6, day}`: `days_elapsed 3`, `kind day` (three leaves torn, then the calendar beat's boundary runs); `s1 {day 0, day}` → `s2 {day 0, night}`: `days_elapsed 0`, `kind slot` (the lamp comes on; no screen). *Rejected:* a `rewind` kind for chapter edges (a tear-off calendar re-hung is a joke the art bible does not make; the chapter card is the edge).

### 7. The `calendar.*` store: applying one batch

The store apply is defined, for one batch and one declared field `f` with current value `v_f`, as:

scalar: `v'_f = clamp( min( max( (set_f ?? v_f) + Σ_i add_{f,i}, max_j max_{f,j} ), min_k min_{f,k} ), lo_f, hi_f )` — the registry's `scalar_apply` (GDD 1 Formulas §1) with the calendar's own `[lo_f, hi_f]`;
flag: `v'_f = last( {set_f, clear_f} in list order ) ?? v_f` with `clear ≡ set false`;
changes: `changes(batch) = | { f ∈ fields(batch) : v'_f ≠ v_f } |`, one change record per field whose value moved, `before = v_f` (the pre-batch value), `after = v'_f`; rejected effects (`tag`, an undeclared field, a writer outside the allow-list, `add` on a flag) are dropped from the batch before the formula runs and logged with their `source`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `v_f` | int or bool | scalar `[lo_f, hi_f]`; flag `{true, false}` | calculated (store) | Value before the batch |
| `set_f` | int or bool or absent | — | data file (effect) | The last `set` on `f` in list order, if any |
| `add_{f,i}` | int | −`hi_f`–`hi_f` | data file (effects) | Every `add` on `f` in the batch; they sum |
| `max_{f,j}`, `min_{f,k}` | int | `[lo_f, hi_f]` | data file (effects) | Ratchets and caps, applied after the adds |
| `lo_f`, `hi_f` | int | declared; `breath_marta` 0–`BREATH_MAX` 4 | data file (`schema.json`) | Clamp bounds |
| `changes(batch)` | int | 0–`|fields(batch)|` | calculated | Change records emitted for the batch |

**Output Range:** `v'_f` is clamped into `[lo_f, hi_f]`; a batch that leaves a field where it was emits nothing for that field, so `changes` is 0 for a no-op batch and never exceeds the number of distinct fields the batch names. Across the cascade steps of one boundary each batch is applied and recorded separately (`before` is the pre-batch value), so one field can carry two records in one boundary — a deliberate difference from GDD 1 Rule 4a's one-record-per-field-per-boundary compaction, because the Rules reader needs each step's record for `{changed: F}` and nothing projects a calendar scalar to the player. **Example:** `breath_marta = 2`; step-0 drift batch `[add −1]` → `clamp(2 − 1, 0, 4) = 1`, one record `{before 2, after 1}`; step-1 batch `[add 3]` (the courier crossed) → `4`, one record `{before 1, after 4}`; a later batch `[add 3]` at 4 → `clamp(7) = 4`, zero records. A batch `[add −1, add −1]` from two drift rows on the same clock → `0` from 2, one record. *Rejected:* applying `max`/`min` before the adds (an `add −1` after `max 1` would undo the ratchet); emitting a record for a clamped no-op (Paper UI would be handed a notice that nothing changed).

### 8. Timetable validation predicates

The timetable is valid iff `valid = ∧_{i} V_i` over the following booleans, each checked by the content compiler (build fails naming the first false predicate and its row) and re-checked by the runtime loader over the compiled JSON (a false predicate rejects the file; the Calendar then has no timetable and Edge Case 4 applies to every scene):

- `V1 rows_cover_scenes = ∀ c ∈ chapters, ∀ s ∈ scenes(c) : (c, s) ∈ rows`
- `V2 rows_are_scenes = ∀ (c, s) ∈ rows : s ∈ scenes(c)` (no row for a scene the chapter does not have; insert scene ids never appear)
- `V3 day_monotone = ∀ c, ∀ consecutive (a, b) in c : d_b ≥ d_a`
- `V4 slot_monotone = ∀ c, ∀ consecutive (a, b) in c : d_b = d_a ⇒ slot_b ≥ slot_a`
- `V5 calendar_advances = ∀ c, ∀ b with kind(b) = calendar : index(b) > 0 ∧ d_b > d_{prev(b)}`
- `V6 day_in_act = ∀ (c, s) ∈ rows : 0 ≤ d ≤ DAYS_PER_ACT − 1`
- `V7 act_close_shape = |act_close| = HORIZON_SLACK ∧ ∀ r ∈ act_close : d_r = DAYS_PER_ACT − 1 ∧ slots non-decreasing in list order`
- `V8 play_order_total = play_order is a permutation of chapters`
- `V9 integral = every numeric value in the file is an integer` (the loader's own rule; `3.0` is rejected, not rounded)
- `V10 year_bound = doy(DAYS_PER_ACT − 1) ≤ days_in_year(opening.year) ∧ plan_week(DAYS_PER_ACT − 1) ≤ weeks_in_year(opening.year)`
- `V11 opening_consistent = iso_weekday(opening.date) = opening.weekday ∧ iso_week(opening.date) = opening.plan_week` (compiler only; the runtime trusts the compiled file)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rows` | set of `(chapter_id, scene_id) → {d, slot}` | — | data file | The timetable's rows |
| `scenes(c)` | ordered list | — | data file (GDD 2 chapter scene list) | Authored scenes, with `kind` |
| `prev(b)`, `index(b)` | scene, int | — | calculated | List neighbours |
| `HORIZON_SLACK` | int | 2 | constant (registry) | Act-close beats |
| `DAYS_PER_ACT` | int | 7–21; 8 | constant (registry) | Act length in days |
| `days_in_year`, `weeks_in_year` | int | 365/366, 52/53 | compiled (year table) | Year bounds |
| `valid` | bool | — | calculated | The build gate |

**Output Range:** a single boolean; the compiler reports every failing predicate (not only the first) with `(chapter, scene, predicate)` triples so a writer fixes a file in one pass. **Example:** the act-one draft in Core Rule 3 passes `V1–V11`; moving `cal_1` to `{day: 3, slot: night}` after `s3 {day: 3, morning}` passes `V3`, `V4` and fails `V5` ("cal_1: a calendar beat on which no day passes"); an `act_close` with one row fails `V7`; a row `{day: 7.5}` fails `V9`. *Rejected:* warnings instead of failures for `V5` and `V7` (a calendar beat that passes no time, or a closing bell with one beat, are pacing bugs the simulator would otherwise find a sprint later); runtime repair (shifting a bad day forward) — the loader serves the last good date and logs, it never invents one.

### 9. Simulator reports over the game space: phase reachability and the longest unannounced run

The two reports are defined over the game space `G` (GDD 6 Rule 6: policy on, player choices free, `play_order` fixed) with the timetable loaded, as:

`first_beat(n) = min { b : ∃ t ∈ G, phase_t(b) ≥ n }` (∅ if no telling reaches phase `n`), reported with `chapter_at(b)` (the chapter whose scene list holds beat `b` under `play_order`, or `act_close`) and `date_for(chapter_at(b), scene_at(b))`;

`run_t = max { b_2 − b_1 + 1 : ∀ b ∈ [b_1, b_2], unannounced_{t,b} = 1 }` (0 if never unannounced) · `U = max_{t ∈ G} run_t`, reported with one witness telling id and the run's beats and dates.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `G` | set of tellings | — | calculated (simulator, `space` command) | The game space |
| `phase_t(b)` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§1 on telling `t` at beat `b`) | Phase in a telling |
| `unannounced_{t,b}` | int | 0–1 | calculated (§1) | Band paranoid, paper silent |
| `n` | int | 1–3 | report parameter | Which phase |
| `chapter_at(b)`, `scene_at(b)` | id | — | calculated (`play_order` × scene lists) | Where beat `b` falls in play order |
| `first_beat(n)` | int or ∅ | 1–`HORIZON_ACT` | calculated | Earliest beat phase `n` is reachable |
| `U` | int | 0–`HORIZON_ACT` | calculated | Longest unannounced-paranoid run in beats |

**Output Range:** `first_beat(n)` is in `[1, HORIZON_ACT]` or empty; for act one `first_beat(2) = first_beat(3) = ∅` is an acceptance criterion (GDD 1: act one cannot exceed 1) and `first_beat(1) ≠ ∅` is another (the announcement must be reachable). `U` is 0–14; it is a **report, never a law**: the writers' output states it as "the state counts for `U` beats before it speaks, from beat `b_1` (date) to `b_2` (date), in telling `t`", with no frequency and no fraction (GDD 6 Rule 11's output rules). **Example:** `play_order [a1_vera, a1_jan, a1_tomas]`: `first_beat(1) = 6` (Jan's chapter; a Tuesday of Plan Week 41 under Jan's timetable), `U = 0`. `play_order [a1_jan, a1_vera, a1_tomas]`: `first_beat(1) = 3`, `U = 3` (beats 3–5: J4 at 3, the window opens at 6), witness the telling where Jan distributes at 2 and is raided at 3. *Rejected:* a law `phase_is_announced` with `U ≤ k` (the writers may want the state counting silently for a week in act two; a law would forbid it; a report lets them see it); reporting `U` as a share of tellings (a frequency word in the writers' output, which GDD 6 forbids).

## Edge Cases

*Each case states what happens, who logs it and what the player sees. "Logged" means through the injected `DebugSink` (a recording twin in tests, an assert in debug builds, a logger in release); "build fails" means the content compiler or the rules generator refuses the file. The Calendar never repairs data, never invents a date and never writes anything to put a case right.*

1. **If a chapter closes and the next chapter in `play_order` opens (the chapter edge):** Scene Flow does not call `transition_between`; the next chapter's first scene is stamped cold with its own `date_for` (act one: back to Friday, day 0) under the chapter card that Chapter & Story Structure plays; the beat continues from the dossier's counter without a reset; the snapshot's `date` simply differs from the previous one; no leaves are torn; nothing is logged. *Rationale:* the rewind is the overlap of three lives in one fortnight, not time passing; the tear-off calendar has no gesture for going back, and the chapter card is the honest one.

2. **If `transition_between(a, b)` is called across a chapter edge by mistake (or for any pair that is not consecutive in one chapter):** the call returns `TRANSITION_ILLEGAL(a, b)` with no `kind`, no dates and no `days_elapsed` — a negative elapsed count is never computed, so no screen can tear off a negative number of leaves; Scene Flow plays nothing and stamps the new scene's date cold as in case 1; logged with both ids; debug builds assert. *Rationale:* a precondition violated by the caller is a caller bug; serving a plausible value (`abs`, or 0) would hide it in a release and let a wrong screen play.

3. **If a `kind: calendar` scene's timetable row does not advance the day (same day as the preceding scene):** at authoring the build fails `V5` naming the scene. If such data reaches the runtime anyway (hot-loaded content), the scene still runs as a full boundary — the beat is spent, the empty decision batch is applied, the rules step runs, drift ticks and world moves may fire, the snapshot is taken — and the transition before it is `slot` or `none`, so the player sees paper arrive without the calendar screen; logged `CALENDAR_BEAT_NO_DAY`. *Rationale:* a beat is a beat whatever the date says (Rule 2); the Calendar does not refuse to count a boundary because the writer mis-dated it, since the simulator counted it.

4. **If a scene has no timetable row at runtime (or the timetable failed to load and there are no rows at all):** `date_for` returns the last good date served in this chapter (the previous scene's `day` and `slot`), or the act's opening date (`day 0`, `slot` the first scene's declared slot or `day`) when the chapter has served none; the boundary runs normally, GDD 1's change records carry the served day, the snapshot's `date` carries the served day and a `served: stale` marker that the HUD ignores and the trace records; `transition_between` involving the scene returns `TRANSITION_ILLEGAL`; logged `TIMETABLE_MISSING(chapter, scene)` once per scene; debug asserts. At authoring this is `V1` and the build fails. *Rationale:* the honest options at runtime are a stale date or a crash, and a stale date on a story screen is the lesser harm; the marker keeps the trace from lying about it.

5. **If the act-close calendar beats run (the `HORIZON_SLACK` boundaries that belong to no chapter):** Scene Flow opens them under the pseudo-chapter id `<act>_close` (Chapter & Story Structure declares it with `reads: []`, `writes: []`, `assumes: []` **(PROV)**); `ctx.chapter_id = a1_close`, `date_for` serves the `act_close` rows (day `DAYS_PER_ACT − 1`); fired rules cite `rules:a1_close/a1_close_1:<id>`, GDD 1 parses `source_protagonist = null` from it; rules' writes count toward `a1_close`'s own four counts, checked against `WRITE_MAX`/`CROSS_WRITE_MAX` like any chapter, and the `CROSS_READ_MIN` warning is waived for it as for an act's first chapter; no decision is offered; the review sits here (`review_sits` is checked at these beats); the snapshot and reveal run as usual. *Rationale:* GDD 1 charges rules' writes to the chapter whose boundary fired them and parses the chapter from the source; a pseudo-chapter gives the closing bell a name without pretending a protagonist was there.

6. **If a `calendar.*` effect arrives from a writer other than `rules`, `migrate` or `sim` (a `filing`, `dialogue`, `chapter` or `assume` source):** the effect is dropped before the batch formula runs, no change record, the rest of the batch applies, logged `CALENDAR_WRITER_REJECTED(source)`; at authoring the content validator rejects a `calendar` target in any content effect (build fails) and the rules loader rejects a `calendar.*` effect on a row it would stamp with a non-`rules` source. A `sim` source is accepted only when the Calendar was constructed with `allow_sim: true` (the simulator and the unit tests); in a shipped build it is rejected the same way. *Rationale:* GDD 1 Rule 1a — every consequence that crosses chapters starts from something on paper read by a rule; a decision that moved the calendar directly would be a hidden second channel.

7. **If a condition uses `{is_unknown: calendar.F}` or an effect uses `op: tag` on a calendar target:** `is_unknown` on a `calendar.*` target is a content and rules error (build fails: "a schedule has no blanks"); if it reaches the runtime it evaluates `false` and is logged once per leaf. `tag` is rejected at load on a rules row and at runtime in a batch (dropped, no change record, logged `CALENDAR_OP_REJECTED(tag, source)`); `add`/`max`/`min` on a flag and `set unknown` on anything are rejected the same way. *Rationale:* two-state flags and no tags are Rule 6's whole schema; the evaluator must give a defined answer and a build must give a loud one.

8. **If a calendar scalar is at a bound and an effect pushes past it (breath at 0 with a drift `add −1`; breath at `BREATH_MAX` with a supply `add 3`):** clamped, no change record, no `changed:` leaf this step; the drift keeps ticking against the floor harmlessly (its anchor is Rules' and does not care); `marta_collapses`'s licence `at_most [calendar.breath_marta, BREATH_ZERO]` holds on the value, not on a change, so it fires once per act whenever it is first licensed in its window and never again. *Rationale:* GDD 1 Edge Cases 5 and 6 applied to the calendar; a notice that nothing changed is a lie to Paper UI and a phantom `changed` to Rules.

9. **If two drift rows tick the same clock in one step (two `drift` rows on `calendar.breath_marta` whose `while` both hold and whose periods both land on this beat):** both `add`s are in the step-0 drift batch; they sum before clamping (`2 + (−1) + (−1) = 0`); one change record with `before 2, after 0`; `marta_collapses` sees 0 at step 1. The rules loader warns (not fails) on two drift rows naming one field, because a clock that runs down twice as fast is usually one row with the wrong period. *Rationale:* GDD 1 Edge Case 2 — adds commute, so two rows and one row of double value are the same telling, which the simulator needs.

10. **If the courier's supply (`calendar.breath_marta add 3` on `changed: border.courier_through`) and `marta_collapses` are both candidates in one boundary (breath reaches 0 at step 0 by drift, and this boundary's decision batch set `courier_through`):** both are world moves under the pseudo-agent `world`; the policy orders them by `(priority desc, id asc)`. If `breath_supply` carries the higher priority it fires first (step 1): breath 3, the collapse's licence no longer holds, it is dropped — not queued — and fires only if breath reaches 0 again in its window. If `marta_collapses` carries the higher priority it fires first: `marta.hospitalised`, `marta.under_observation`, `vera.attention +1` land, and the supply applies at the next step (breath 3, Marta in hospital with medicine in the flat). The Calendar is indifferent; the authoring recommendation (narrative director to confirm) is `breath_supply` above `marta_collapses`, so a courier who crosses tonight counts tonight — the state is not faster than a courier. If the two share a priority the loader does not object (neither has a `state: true` author), and `id asc` puts `breath_supply` first. *Rationale:* GDD 6 Rule 5 makes the policy a total order the writers can read off the file; this case is exactly what the priority column is for, and the Calendar must not add a tie-break of its own.

11. **If a rule licensed by `phase_at_least: 1` is evaluated at the cascade step where the band moved, before `phase_ratchet_1` has landed:** it is licensed at once — `phase()` is `max(phase_reached, band)` and the band already says 1; the ratchet row changes nothing the reader can see this boundary. The ratchet matters only if the band later falls: with `priority: 100` it is selected at the first step after the band moved, so it lands in the same boundary unless the band moved at step `CASCADE_MAX − 1` and the cascade stops; in that one case the simulator fails the telling as `truncated` (GDD 6 Rule 5.5) before it ships, and at runtime the ratchet fires at the next boundary's first step **if the band still holds**. If relief in that same next boundary's decision batch drops the band first (not possible in act one, which has no relief), `phase()` reads 0 for one boundary, the ratchet's licence lapses, and the snapshot logs `PHASE_FELL(prev, now)` and asserts in debug. *Rationale:* the ratchet is a rule so that it is replayable (Rule 5); the cost is one provable gap, which the cascade cap's build failure closes in CI and the snapshot's comparison names at runtime.

12. **If `phase_reached` is higher than the band (relief has raised `stability` above the band's ceiling, acts two and three):** `phase() = phase_reached`; the snapshot's `phase`, `ink_reach` and every `red_reach` are unchanged from the previous boundary; `PHASE_BONUS` still applies to everyone's `A_eff` when `phase_reached ≥ 2`, because the bonus reads the phase, not the band; `unannounced_b = 0` by construction (`band ≥ 1` is false). *Rationale:* GDD 1 Edge Case 17 — relief changes the number for the next threshold, never the mood already reached.

13. **If the phase is collapsing (`PHASE_RED_CAP[3] = 0`) and a person is listed at the cap (`A_eff 6`):** `red_reach 0`, `stamp_tier 0` (a hatched outline), `ink_reach 0` (the date stamp dry); `tier 3`, `looked true`, `watched true`, `listed true` stand in the same view, so Audio keeps the watched state and Overlay draws no red; Paper UI's signs and files still project whatever GDD 1's thresholds say. *Rationale:* art bible §2.2 — red withdraws, the state's eye does not; a colour is a phase's property and the weight is a person's, and the view carries both so no consumer has to guess which it lost.

14. **If a `kind: person` record has no `attention` scalar in the dossier schema (a future record, or a schema edit):** the content compiler warns (`PERSON_WITHOUT_ATTENTION`); at runtime the record is omitted from `per_character`, and `attention_view(x)` for it returns `{tier 0, looked false, watched false, listed: is_true(x.under_observation), red_reach 0, stamp_tier 0}` and logs once per record per act; a sheet whose protagonist is such a record shows a hatched stamp. *Rationale:* GDD 1 says a missing scalar reads as its default and the Calendar emits only closed sets; `null` in the view would make every consumer branch on it.

15. **If `deserialize(data)` receives a payload with a different `schema_version`, an undeclared field, or a declared field missing:** rejected whole — `SCHEMA_MISMATCH(expected, got)`, `UNDECLARED_FIELD(name)` or `MISSING_FIELD(name)` — nothing partial is loaded, the store keeps its pre-call contents (`defaults()` on a fresh Calendar), Save/Load runs its migration (writer `migrate`, which fills a missing field from `defaults()`) and calls again; a payload newer than the code is rejected (no downgrade); a `deserialize` between `begin_boundary` and `end_boundary` is rejected `NOT_AT_BOUNDARY` without inspecting the payload. *Rationale:* GDD 1 Edge Case 13's strictness, both ways; a blank clock silently loaded would move Marta's collapse and nobody would know why.

16. **If the snapshot is requested before Scene Flow's phase-and-attention step (during the decision or rules step), or twice in one boundary:** before the step, `snapshot(ctx)` returns the previous boundary's memoised `BoundaryView` (stale by one beat; its `beat` field says so) or `null` for the act's first boundary, logs `SNAPSHOT_EARLY(beat)` and does not compute — the cascade is not finished and a mid-cascade tier would be a tier that moves inside a scene; at the step it computes once and memoises by beat; a second request in the same boundary returns the same object with no recompute; a request after `end_boundary` and before the next `begin_boundary` returns the current one. *Rationale:* Rule 9 — every tier changes only at a boundary, and the memo is what makes the stamp static (art bible §7.5).

17. **If `date_for` would cross the year end (`opening.date + DAYS_PER_ACT − 1` past 31 December):** the build fails `V10` (`YEAR_BOUND`) naming the overflow day; the runtime loader rejects the compiled file the same way, so no running game ever computes a date past the year; the game is autumn 1989 and no act crosses it. *Rationale:* a month table for one year is all the arithmetic the stamp needs; a wrap would need leap years and a second year's ISO weeks, which is a calendar library, and the pinned reference has none the Calendar may touch.

18. **If `plan_week` would pass the year's ISO week count (52 for 1989; 53 in a long year), or `opening.plan_week`/`opening.weekday` disagree with `opening.date`:** build fails `V10` (`PLAN_WEEK_OVERFLOW`) or `V11` (`OPENING_INCONSISTENT(date, authored, computed)`); never wraps to Plan Week 1; the runtime never sees either case. *Rationale:* Plan Week 1 of the next year on a date stamp in October is the kind of defect a reviewer would catch once and a player would catch first.

19. **If the rules file names a `calendar.*` field absent from `schema.json` (a drift on `calendar.breath_ilona`, a world move setting `calendar.ledger_due` before the schema declares it):** `topic_gen` fails `UNDECLARED_CALENDAR_FIELD(rule_id, field)`; the runtime rules loader rejects the rules file with the same error (GDD 1 Edge Case 10's rule applied to this store); no fluent is invented with a guessed range, and no default is served for it. If a declared field is read by no rule and written by no rule, the generator warns `CALENDAR_FIELD_UNUSED`. *Rationale:* the solver cannot prove a law over a fluent it guessed, and the runtime cannot clamp a value it has no range for.

20. **If `transition_between` is called with an insert scene as either argument (an id that is not in the timetable because inserts are never authored there):** returns `TRANSITION_ILLEGAL` as in case 2; nothing plays; the insert inherits the day and slot of the boundary that fired it (Rule 2), and the transition Scene Flow should ask for is between the two authored scenes on either side of the insert, computed as if the insert were not there. *Rationale:* Rybar's call comes the same evening the ledger was burned; a torn-off leaf between the burning and the telephone would put a night where the state's reaction needs none.

21. **If two calendar beats are consecutive (the two act-close rows, or a chapter authored with back-to-back calendar scenes):** both run as full boundaries with empty decision batches; the transition between them is `slot` (`act_close` rows `day` → `night`) or `none`, never `day` for the act-close pair (`V7` fixes both at the last day), and `day` for a mid-chapter pair (`V5` demands each advance); world moves may fire at both, NPC moves may fire at both, the reveal shows the paper that arrived; the player decides nothing at either. *Rationale:* the closing bell is two boundaries so the review can convene at one and sit at the other (`review_sits`); two decision-free beats in a row are what "time passes without you" means at an act's end.

22. **If Chapter & Story Structure opens a chapter out of the timetable's `play_order` (a debug jump, a future branching act, or a data mismatch):** the Calendar serves dates regardless — rows are per chapter, not per position — and the snapshot and transitions are correct for that chapter; it logs `PLAY_ORDER_MISMATCH(expected, opened)` once; the §9 reports and the window lint computed for the declared order are void for that run, which the trace header records (`play_order_actual`). *Rationale:* the date of a scene is a fact of the scene; the order is a fact of the act; the Calendar must not refuse a scene because the act is being played in a debugger.

23. **If a scene id is reused across chapters (`s1` in `a1_vera` and `s1` in `a1_jan`) and a caller asks `date_for(scene_id)` without a chapter:** the lookup key is `(chapter_id, scene_id)` and `ctx` always carries both; a bare `scene_id` is accepted only when it is unique across the act's timetable, otherwise the call returns `AMBIGUOUS_SCENE(scene_id, chapters)` and serves nothing (case 4 then applies to that boundary if Scene Flow passes nothing better); the content compiler does not forbid the reuse, because chapter-local ids are GDD 2's convention. *Rationale:* a guessed chapter would stamp Jan's Friday on Vera's sheet; refusing is cheaper than being wrong on the one HUD element that is always on screen.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Optional for a Core system, written because this system's two outputs are the most-seen pixels in the game; drafted with the art director from art bible §1.2, §2.1–2.2, §4.2–4.3, §4.5, §7.1–7.5 and §8.2. This system draws nothing and plays nothing: it returns one `BoundaryView` per boundary (Core Rule 9) and one `transition_between` record per scene change (Core Rule 4). Everything below is a constraint on how Overlay (9), Paper UI (13) and Audio (16) **(PROV)** render those two values, not a new visual. The §4.3 red budgets are measured on the composited frame including the elements named here. The art director's ruling on the open point G4 is accepted: the phase tier is the surveillance stamp's ceiling and the protagonist's red reach is its fill, because §4.2 says red on a document means the state is reading it at this moment, and a phase-only stamp would put red on Tomas's sheet in a week the state is not reading Tomas. Eight art-bible amendments this implies (A1–A8) are proposals for the user, listed in Open Questions and not applied.*

**1. The date stamp carries the phase and nothing else; four ink reaches, four shapes.** The HUD date stamp (§7.1: top-left, one of the three screen-space elements) is a rubber date stamp set in the typewriter face at stamp size (§7.2: 32 px at 720, 48 at 1080), CAPS, inside a bordered box; its text is Narrative Content's string with `{date}`/`{plan_week}` filled from the timetable (GDD 2 V44), and it is the **only numeral this system ever puts on screen**: the day of the month, the year, the Plan Week. The slot is never typed: morning, day and night are read off the plate (§4.3: window bone by day, soot by night), not off the stamp. Its ink reach is `ink_reach = PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]`, rendered as four hard-edged states of one single-colour stamp (§7.3: no outline-plus-fill, no gradient), each distinguishable by shape alone (§4.5): **0 `dry`**, the pad has run out: box and glyphs impressed in soot at a flat 40% value, broken strokes, no stamp-red pixel anywhere in the element (Collapsing, §2.2 "stamps unanswered"); **1 `crisp`**, a clean red impression, 1 px strokes, counters open, red confined to its own box (Confident: "UI margin and official stamps only"); **2 `bleeds`**, over-inked, not soft: strokes thicken to 2 px, counters close, and the impression is doubled by a second hard-edged impression offset 1 px; a halo is forbidden, a bleed here is a rubber stamp pressed too hard (Paranoid); **3 `floods`**, the box is a solid red block with the date knocked out in paper bone (Panicking: red reaches the key light, and the HUD's one red element reaches its own edge). The four names are the data tokens of Core Rule 9; the technical artist confirms the 1 px offset survives `canvas_items` scaling at 1080 (§7.2). *Reviewer test:* cover the hue; the four states must still sort. The element is static (§7.4 "never moves"): its text and ink reach change by **cut** at the reveal step of the boundary whose snapshot changed them, never by tween, fade or re-strike.

**2. The surveillance stamp carries the person, ceilinged by the phase.** On every open sheet, the §7.5 square stamp (top-right corner, 16 px in, never overlapping the chapter tab or the "changed" marker, §7.6 C6) renders `stamp_tier(x)` for the sheet's protagonist `x`, where `stamp_tier = red_reach = min(red_band, PHASE_RED_CAP[phase])`: **0–1 `hatched`**, stamp-red hatching inside a red outline, no fill (the pad was not pressed; at tier 1 the state's red is in the margin, which is the date stamp, not the sheet, §2.2); **2 `half-struck`**, the lower half of the square filled solid red along a hard horizontal edge, hatching kept in the upper half so the fill is read by geometry and not by hue (§4.5); **3 `struck`**, the full square solid, bordered edge only; **Collapsing**, `hatched` again by the cap (`PHASE_RED_CAP[3] = 0`), the stamp still on the sheet and unanswered (§2.2). The phase is the ceiling, so a watched person in a confident week is `hatched` and a half-watched person in a panicking week is `half-struck`: the stamp shows what the state lets you see; the Files view's threshold line at `ATTN_LOOK` (GDD 6 V4) shows what it weighs, which is Pillar 1 in practice, you learn you are watched by reading paper. Because consumers read the snapshot and never call `phase()` or `attention_view()` mid-scene (Core Rule 9), **a stamp is never seen to change; it is different on the next sheet you open.** Act one reaches `half-struck` at most: at confident the cap is 1 and every stamp is `hatched`; at paranoid the cap is 2, so a looked-at person's sheet is `half-struck`, and the first `struck` is an act-two sight. A sheet whose subject is a person record carrying `attention` renders that person's tier (a file about Jan opened in the archive carries Jan's reach: Pillar 4, §4.2); every other sheet renders the chapter protagonist's (a requirement on Paper UI **(PROV)**, Open Questions).

**3. The Calendar-transition state is flat paper, and its number is counted in leaves.** When Scene Flow receives `transition_between(a, b) = {kind: day, days_elapsed: n}` it plays the §2.1 Calendar-transition state: no lamp, no plate, paper at UI value (§4.4), a tear-off wall calendar in state paper (typed, gridded, bone, soot type) showing the current leaf. **Exactly `n` leaves tear, one per day, in sequence**, each a rigid downward translate off-frame at 300 ms ease-out quadratic with at most 2° rotation and a hard torn stub left at the top (§7.4 "calendar tear"; no curl, no bend, no flutter); each uncovered leaf shows the next date's weekday and day, so the player counts the days as they pass. Leaves are never stacked into one tear, never accelerated for long gaps, and `days_elapsed` is never printed ("3 days later" is a defect): the number of leaves *is* the number; the timetable loader warns above `TRANSITION_MAX_LEAVES` (Tuning Knobs). On the destination leaf the **date stamp is struck**, the §7.4 stamp impression, 90 ms down + 60 ms settle with its one 2 px overshoot, in the ink reach of the snapshot already in force; on the settle frame the HUD date stamp cuts to the same text and reach. **The strike restates, never announces:** a transition has no boundary (Core Rule 4), so no snapshot can change between the reveal that preceded it and the strike, and no ink reach is ever first seen as motion. The dossier change notices on this screen are Paper UI's **(PROV)**: typed slips pinned beside the calendar in the §7.6 C6 "changed" grammar, overprinted in the originating chapter's ink when cross-life (Principle B); they are drawn from change records, never from this system, and they appear only when the next scene is a `kind: calendar` beat: the screen holds after the strike, the boundary runs with no visible duration, and the reveal pins the notices onto it before the cut to the next plate (the order is Scene Flow's **(PROV)**, Open Questions). A plain day transition before an authored scene with a decision tears, strikes and cuts; nothing is pinned, because nothing moved. Chapter open plays no transition (Core Rule 4): the chapter's first date is stamped cold on the opening card (Chapter & Story Structure **(PROV)**).

**4. No tier change animates; the one motion is paper.** Every tier this system emits (`ink_reach`, `stamp_tier`, `tier`, `looked`, `watched`, `listed`) changes only at a boundary and is presented as a cut at the reveal (§7.5 "static, no motion"; §7.4 "never moves: the date stamp"). Nothing tweens between `crisp` and `bleeds`; no stamp fills while you watch; no portrait, plate or margin reacts to a tier change on the frame it happens. The only motion in the whole of this system's presentation is the paper of the transition state in §3, the tear and the strike, both at §7.4 durations, both showing a value already in force. There is no exception to the tier rule; the strike is not one, it is the §7.4 stamp impression applied to a date (proposed art-bible amendment A4 admits it outside filing for this screen alone).

**5. The slot change is a plate light, owned by Overlay.** `kind: slot` (same day, later slot) is not a screen of its own and shows no calendar: the next scene's plate loads in its slot's lighting state as a cut, never a crossfade through a light the art bible does not define. Direction for Overlay (9) **(PROV)**, within §2.1 Investigation and §4.3: `morning`, window bone, the desk lamp still lit from the night (the only warmth in a cold early room); `day`, window bone, lamp off, the plate at its flattest and most even; `night`, window soot, the lamp pool as the mood carrier. Three slots on two switches (window value, lamp on or off), so no plate needs three paintings; the technical artist confirms the lamp and window as lighting layers rather than plate variants (§6.2). No sun in any slot (§2.1). The slot never changes the red reach, the stamp, or any tier.

**6. What must never happen.** No meter, bar, dial or ring for attention, stability, unrest, phase, breath or the beat; no gradient or soft edge on any red (a bleed is over-inking, §1); no lerp, fade or tween between tiers; no numeral on screen other than the date stamp's date and Plan Week: not the beat, not `days_elapsed`, not `A_eff`, not Marta's breath (a `calendar.*` clock is read only as the paper a Rules drift produces, GDD 6 V4); no phase banner, toast, card or "the regime is now paranoid" line, the announcement is paper density (GDD 6 V2) and the date stamp's reach; no red on a sheet whose person the state is not reading (§4.2); no stamp that moves, pulses, breathes or flickers (§7.4); no plate built per calendar week, §2.2's week labels are expected pacing and the phase is the function (Core Rule 8; proposed amendment A6); no colour as the only carrier of a tier (§4.5: the four date-stamp shapes and three stamp fills are the backup, and the audio in §7 backs the stamp). *Reviewer test:* the two stamps and the leaves are the only places this system's output is visible; a screenshot with any other pixel attributable to it is a defect.

**7. Audio naming** (for the audio director, per §8.2's token order as GDD 6 V6 applies it; each cue named by its object, never by its rule or its number; the audio director owns how they play). The §4.5 backup for the two attention states, named for the stamp they back and **keyed on `stamp_tier`, not on the uncapped booleans** (the art director's ruling, accepted: a §4.5 backup means what its colour means; the uncapped `looked`/`watched` stay in the snapshot for the dossier's threshold line and for Audio's own mix decisions, never for these two cues): `sfx_stamp_surveillance_half_01` at `stamp_tier` 2 and `sfx_stamp_surveillance_struck_01` at 3, dry and clerical, the family of a stamp, a drawer, a carriage; never a sting, a swell, a drone or a tone (the state does not score itself). The transition: `sfx_calendar_leaf_tear_01` (one per leaf, so the audio also counts the days) and `sfx_stamp_date_strike_01` for the strike on the destination leaf, with the §8.2 phase token available if the pad is to sound as it looks (`sfx_stamp_date_strike_ph1_01` crisp through `_ph4_01` dry; `ph1`–`ph4` = phases 0–3, proposed amendment A7). The slot change names no cue here; a time-of-day ambience is Audio 16's own. The visual mood these sit in: a still, flat office at UI bone; nothing in it should sound larger than a desk.

## Game Feel

Optional for a Core system; two feel properties follow from it and are stated so Overlay and Paper UI inherit them as targets.

- **Nothing here moves while the player reads.** Every value the Calendar emits changes at a scene boundary and nowhere else; the date turns, the stamp fills and the red reaches further only across the paper wipe. A player who sits with a sheet for ten minutes sees exactly what they saw when it opened. The one continuous motion on screen, the interrogation timer, is not this system's.
- **Time is felt as a gap, not a rate.** A day transition is a tear-off of `days_elapsed` leaves at the §7.4 paper tempo (180 ms a sheet, rigid, no bend), brisk and clerical, never a clock face or a fade; the player reads how much time passed by how many leaves fell. A chapter opens on its date cold, with no tear-off, so a rewind is never shown as time running backwards.

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen and must not have one. It owns no HUD element: the date stamp (top-left, art bible §7.1) and the surveillance stamp on every open sheet (§7.5) are Paper UI's and Overlay's, drawn from the snapshot's `date`, `ink_reach` and `stamp_tier`; the Calendar-transition screen is Scene Flow's state and Paper UI's sheet, fed by `transition_between` and GDD 1's change records. The only numerals this system puts within reach of the screen are the date's (`{date}`, `{plan_week}` through GDD 2's strings); no tier, phase name, attention value or count is ever rendered as text or a meter, and the four phases have no on-screen label. Debug builds only: an inspector that prints the current snapshot, the timetable row for the current scene and the store's fields, reachable through the debug menu and absent from release (the same rule as GDD 2's and GDD 6's debug surfaces). The writers' tools are the simulator's phase-reachability and longest-unannounced-run reports under `tools/sim/`.

> **📌 UX Flag — Calendar, Regime Phase & Attention**: no player-facing UI of its own. The date stamp, the surveillance stamp and the tear-off transition are Paper UI's and Overlay's; the `/ux-design` work belongs to those systems, and `design/accessibility-requirements.md` must state that no attention or phase signal is carried by red alone (every tier has a shape backup: hatching, fill extent, ink spread; art bible §4.5).
