## Summary

Overlay & Surveillance Rendering draws the state's attention on the painted plate and nothing else: from the Calendar's per-boundary tiers and each scene's authored overlay data it builds a plan of what sits above the plate, grain, the carbon ghost on everything the player has filed, the stamp instances and red props and key light that the phase's reach allows, the lamp, window and threshold lighting switches, and applies it by cut at scene entry through a host seam with a null twin. It exists so Principle A, "attention has a colour", has pixels, so Pillar 5's pressure is visible without a meter, and so the red the player reads is always authored, measured against the art bible's phase budgets, and never on a person. It computes no tier, reads no file, animates nothing, and changes nothing in an idle room but the ghost the player's own filing earns.

## Overview

*Framing: infrastructure first (Category `Core`, Layer Foundation), with its player-facing effect named because this is the one Core system whose whole output is pixels. No ADR cites it yet; ADR-5 (the renderer) will be built from it.*

Overlay & Surveillance Rendering is the system that puts the state's attention on the screen. It never computes a tier and never reads a file: it takes the Calendar's per-boundary snapshot (the regime phase, the red reach per character, the ink reach, the stamp tier) and the scene's authored overlay data, and from them builds a plan of what to draw above the painted plate, the four layers of the art bible's overlay stack: grain on everything; the carbon ghost that makes a filed object look read; the stamp instances placed where the phase's reach allows; the red that climbs from the paper margin to the props to the state's key light and withdraws when the state collapses. It also owns the lighting switches a plate has (the lamp, the window, the threshold light when the state arrives), the Panicking flicker under the accessibility cap, and the rendering of the interrogation timer's margin fill, the one continuous motion in the game. The player never operates it; they read it: a room that is a little redder than the last one, a telephone that has turned red since this morning, a drawer that has gone carbon blue because they read what was in it. Without it Principle A ("attention has a colour") would have no pixels, Pillar 5 would have no pressure the player could see, and every other system's tiers would be numbers nobody rendered. Its logic is headless (a plan, plain data, byte-equal across hosts); its drawing lives behind a host seam with a null twin, like the Scene & Interaction Framework's, so the simulator and the unit tests run without a frame. It reads three settings (`flicker`, `motion`, `red_as_hatching`) and changes how the room is shown, never what is in it.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees a meter, a counter or a gradient of attention; they read it off the frame the way they read a page. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "attention has a colour" chosen, art bible §1.2 Principle A, as GDD 7 left it to this system; "a read room looks read" kept as the anchor moment's last beat, from §6.4; "the room knows what the file knows" rejected, because this system never reads the file and the room never reacts within a scene).*

**Attention has a colour.** This system serves two feelings that share one room. The first is being looked at without being told. Stamp red belongs to the state alone. It sits on the margin when the state is merely keeping records, reaches the telephone when it starts counting, and stains the light itself when it panics. Sodium is the state's light and the desk lamp is yours, and the player learns to tell them apart without being taught. The second feeling is leaving a mark. When you file something, the room keeps a carbon of it: the thing you filed carries a blue double strike, so a room you have read looks read. Red is the state reading you. Blue is what you have handed it.

The anchor moment: Vera's office, Friday 6 October, the day slot. Bone window, the lamp lit, one red stamp on the cover sheet of report 4471 in the margin of the frame, and nothing else red in the room. She files the ledger line, and the ledger's ghost appears beside it by a cut, blue and two pixels off true. In week three it is the same office at night. The window is soot, the lamp pool is lower, and the telephone and one folder on the desk are red. Nobody has said the state is paranoid. Then Rybar comes. The lamp does not dim, it is replaced: a torch through the door, a hard shadow from the threshold, a stamp on the door frame. By week five the overhead light in a portrait is red and flickers. In week six the red goes, the sodium poles go dark one by one, and the player finds this is the worst room of all. Nothing in any of these rooms counted down. The player learned to read the room the way Vera reads a file: from the edge in.

Pillar alignment. Pillar 5, "pressure comes from attention, not reflexes": the tension is how far the red has reached, and the room never makes the player hurry. The only red that moves is the interrogation timer filling the page margin, in five ticks and with no numerals, and it exists only where the pillar allows a clock. Pillar 2, "the state is a character": its moods are four kinds of light, tidy, counting, noise and ash, and it always arrives the same way: through a threshold, replacing your light with its own, never as a flash, a sting or a vignette. Pillar 4, "consequences cross lives": attention is tracked per character, so red in Jan's room can be the reach of something Vera filed. Pillar 3, "no one is clean": red is never drawn on a person, their clothing or their paper. Colour here says who is watching, never who is guilty.

**The one rule this system must obey: the plate shows the state's attention as it stood when the player walked in, in one of four steps and never on a person, and nothing on it changes state while the player reads except by the player's own act.** Red reach comes only from the tiers handed over at the boundary. The system never computes attention, never infers it from the file, and never draws a gradient the player could watch creep. A tier change lands with the next room, by a cut; a slot change is a cut; the state's arrival is a cut. Within a scene, the carbon ghost is the only thing that appears, and it appears because the player filed something. Two things move and neither is a change of state: the Panicking flicker was in the room when the player entered, at 3 Hz or less, or not at all if they asked for none; and the timer is Dialogue's clock in the margin, under interrogation only. And red never carries meaning by colour alone: every stamp and fill is a bordered shape with hatching, so a player who turns red into hatching loses the colour and keeps the lesson.

*Cost accepted:* the room cannot answer the state's interest while the player is in it. A filing that will draw the state's eye shows only its own ghost until the door, and the red arrives one room late. Attention is felt in four coarse steps, so a room looks exactly as calm one filing before it turns as it did a week earlier. Every red the player sees is authored: each room needs its margin, prop and key-light layers and its threshold light drawn by hand, and a room built without a red prop can show the Paranoid phase only in its margin. The Collapsing phase withdraws the red, and some players will read the empty room as safety; soot, loose paper and dark sodium have to carry the dread the colour no longer does. Players who turn flicker off lose the Panicking noise in the light, so the state's own misregistered paper has to carry it alone.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary), the technical artist (render constraints) and the engine programmer (engine integration); rulings where they differed are stated in place. Engine facts are quoted only from the pinned reference (`modules/rendering.md`: 4.7 `DrawableTexture2D` exists, `CanvasItem` lines draw without a feather, `LinearToSRGB` no longer clamps; nothing on `CanvasLayer` order, `CanvasModulate`, blend modes, screen texture, uniforms or read-back is sourced, and the stack is designed so that only sibling draw order must be proven). Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a GDD not yet written (Paper UI 13, Dialogue 11, Character Presentation 10, Audio 16). Four rulings by the main session on the specialists' options: Paper UI draws the interrogation timer fill from Overlay's style; tier-3 red is carried by the authored key light with a red-free figure band, never a frame tint; an anchor may carry an authored `subject`; the only flicker in the game is the interrogation portrait's. The amendments to GDDs 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 this needed are applied there and named where they bite; the art-bible amendments are proposals in Open Questions.*

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary); the technical artist's T4 spike list and the art director's §4.2/§4.5 rules are treated as locked and cited by section. Where this draft departs from the brief's working proposals the departure is stated in place with the alternative it displaced.*

**0. Vocabulary.** A *tier* is a small integer from a closed set (GDD 7 Rule 0); this system receives tiers and produces pixels, and no float crosses its boundary in either direction. The *snapshot* is GDD 7's `BoundaryView`, received only through Scene Flow's `PresentationSink.reveal` (GDD 8 Rule 13). The *reach* of a scene is one tier, `red_reach(protagonist)` from the snapshot, that selects which authored red is lit (Rule 4). An *anchor* is an authored red-carrying layer in the scene sidecar's `overlay` block: a *stamp* (tier 1, an atlas instance on a document edge, a door frame, a notice), a *prop* (tier 2, the tinted variant of an object: the red telephone, folder, pen) or the *key light* (tier 3, the state's hard light tinted red). A *lighting layer* is an authored, cropped, red-free layer switched on or off by the plan: `lamp`, `window_day`, `window_night`, `overhead`, `threshold_<hotspot>`, `sodium_<n>`, `amber_windows`. The *plan* (`OverlayPlan`) is the plain-data result of one computation at scene entry: every layer's state, every stamp's placement and variant, the tint tier, the grain, the flicker allowance and the red estimate. A *ghost* is the carbon double-strike on a filed hotspot (art bible §6.4). The *host* is the thin seam through which the plan becomes pixels; its *null twin* records. A *cut* is a change that completes on one frame with no intermediate value.

**1. Overlay is a renderer of tiers: a headless core computes a plan, an engine host applies it, and the two never share a type the simulator cannot load.** `OverlayCore extends RefCounted`, constructed with nothing but a `DebugSink`, exposes pure functions: `plan(input) → OverlayPlan`, `ghost(plan, hotspot_id) → GhostOp`, `flicker_allowance(flicker, enforceable) → {allowed, max_hz}`, `red_style(red_as_hatching) → {variant: fill | hatch}`, `red_estimate_ppm(...)`, and the validator `OverlayDataValidator.check(sidecar) → findings[]` (Rule 3). Its `input` is plain data assembled by the engine `PresentationSink` fan-out (GDD 8 Rule 13): `{sidecar.overlay, snapshot | null, protagonist_id, scene_kind ∈ {authored, insert}, slot, settings: {flicker, red_as_hatching}, host_caps: {can_count_flashes}}`. `OverlayHost extends RefCounted` (abstract; `@abstract` sourced for 4.5, breaking-changes.md line 69, syntax verified in the spike) has two implementations: `EngineOverlayHost` holds the layer nodes under the scene root GDD 3 provides and draws the T4 stack; `NullOverlayHost` records every call in order into a dictionary tests and the story simulator read, and returns the capability flags a test sets. Calls: `load(plan) → {ok, errors[]}` (layers resident, nothing visible; called at `scene_preloaded`), `apply(plan)` (at `scene_ready`, a cut), `apply_after_threshold(plan)` (inserts only, Rule 9), `apply_ghost(op)` (Rule 8), `clear(scene_id)`, `can_count_flashes() → bool`, `capture_red_share_ppm() → int | null` (debug and CI only, Gate B, Rule 6; **NOT SOURCEABLE**, the frame-capture spike), `report_resident_bytes` (into GDD 3's W13 sheet). The CI path check extends to `src/overlay/`: `src/overlay/core/*` imports nothing from the scene tree or `src/overlay/engine/*`; `tools/sim/` and `tests/unit/overlay/` bind `NullOverlayHost`. The `OverlayHost` contract suite runs against both twins. *Rationale:* the one system that draws is still mostly decisions (which tier → which layer; is the budget met; may this flicker), and every decision must be replayable by the simulator with no renderer, exactly as GDD 3's `SceneHost` and GDD 8's `PresentationSink` are. *Rejected:* a scene-tree `Node` as the system's root with the logic in `_ready` (one import and the simulator cannot load it; GDD 3 Rule 8's argument); the core calling the Calendar for the snapshot (GDD 7 Rule 9 forbids every consumer that pull; the snapshot is pushed through the sink).

**2. The plan is plain data, computed once per scene entry, immutable for the life of the scene, and byte-equal across hosts.** Shape (keys sorted at serialisation; no float anywhere, fractions are integer parts-per-million, opacities integer percent):

```yaml
OverlayPlan:
  schema_version: 1
  scene_id: a1_vera/v_office
  scene_kind: authored                  # authored | insert
  phase: 0                              # snapshot.phase, 0–3
  reach: 1                              # red_reach(protagonist) — the plan's tier (Rule 4)
  ink_reach: 1                          # PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]; read for the assert only
  slot: day                             # morning | day | night
  lighting:                             # every declared lighting layer, by id (Rule 7)
    lamp: off
    window_day: on
    window_night: off
    threshold_telephone: off
  lighting_after_threshold: null        # inserts only (Rule 9): the second cut's table
  stamps:                               # tier-1 anchors lit at this reach; ≤ STAMP_MAX 12
    - { anchor: stamp_door_01, atlas: stamp_square_official, variant: fill, rect: [1612, 118, 96, 96] }
  props: []                             # tier-2 anchors lit; each {anchor, hotspot | null, variant, rect}
  key_light: null                       # tier-3 anchor lit, or null; {anchor, variant, rect}
  tint: { tier: 0, hatch: false }       # the full-frame phase layer's uniform (Rule 5)
  grain: { tile: grain_bone_01, opacity_pct: 10 }
  flicker: { allowed: false, max_hz: 0 }   # the allowance Paper UI's portrait obeys (Rule 10)
  red_est_ppm: 4147                     # Formulas F3; the headless proxy
  red_budget_ppm: 20000                 # PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM[phase]
  ghosts_at_entry: [ledger]             # hotspots already filed when the room is entered
  findings: []                          # DebugSink codes raised while planning (empty on a clean plan)
```

The plan is computed at `scene_preloaded` (so `load` can bring the selected layers in before `scene_ready`) from the latest snapshot received at `reveal` and the `enter_scene` context (Rule 11), and is **never recomputed while the scene is `active` or `suspended`**: not on a settings change (a `red_as_hatching` or `flicker` change applies at the next scene entry and the sheet says so, mirroring GDD 4 Edge Case 19's "at the next beat"), not on a `changed` signal, not on a clock. The one in-scene change is the ghost (Rule 8), which is a separate `GhostOp`, not a plan mutation. *Rationale:* GDD 7 Rule 9 makes every tier a per-boundary value; a plan that could change mid-scene would be a place for a tier to move inside a room, and GDD 8 VA §6 forbids any room change while the player is idle. Byte-equality is what lets the simulator diff a `NullOverlayHost` log against the engine's, the same guarantee Scene Flow gives with `BoundaryRecord`. *Rejected:* live-applying `red_as_hatching` mid-scene (the plan would have two versions in one room, and the budget assert would need re-running against a frame the player is looking at); a `Resource` subclass for the plan (engine serialisation drift; GDD 1's ADR-4 principle); floats for the estimate (float formatting is not byte-stable across platforms, and tiers are never floats).

**3. Red reach on a plate is authored, never computed; the sidecar's `overlay` block declares every red pixel the room can show, and the validator makes the art bible's rules asserts.** GDD 3's `scene.json` gains one block (GDD 3 Rule 1 amendment; written by the art export, normalised by the compiler, never hand-edited):

```yaml
overlay:
  schema_version: 1
  space: private                        # state | private | street | print_shop (art bible §6.3 areas)
  grain: { tile: grain_bone_01, opacity_pct: 10 }        # 8–12 on plates (T4); UI grain is Paper UI's
  red_anchors:
    - { id: stamp_door_01, kind: stamp, tier: 1, atlas: stamp_square_official, rect: [1612, 118, 96, 96], red_px: 4600, red_px_hatch: 0 }
    - { id: prop_telephone, kind: prop, tier: 2, hotspot: telephone, layer: { png: v_office_prop_telephone.png, x: 1210, y: 599 }, layer_hatch: { png: v_office_prop_telephone_h.png, x: 1210, y: 599 }, red_px: 11000, red_px_hatch: 0 }
    - { id: key_light, kind: key_light, tier: 3, layer: {...}, layer_hatch: {...}, red_px: 180000, red_px_hatch: 0 }   # state space or insert only
  lighting:
    - { id: lamp, layer: { png: v_office_lamp.png, x: 880, y: 300 } }
    - { id: window_day, layer: {...} }
    - { id: window_night, layer: {...} }
    - { id: threshold_telephone, hotspot: telephone, layer: {...} }     # inserts: exactly one
    - { id: sodium_01, layer: {...}, lit_phases: [0, 1, 2] }           # street only; dark from the phase named
```

**4. Whose reach a room uses: the chapter protagonist's, per anchor overridable by an authored `subject`, resolved from the snapshot alone.** `reach_for(anchor) = snapshot.per_character[anchor.subject ?? protagonist_id].red_reach` (Formulas F1), and an anchor is lit when `anchor.tier ≤ reach_for(anchor)` (F2: reach is cumulative; at tier 2 the margin stamps stay and the props join them, per §2.2's margin → props → key light). The default is the chapter protagonist: the room is theirs and §4.2 says red on a thing means the state is reading *it* now, which in a protagonist's room means reading them. An anchor may carry `subject: <person_id>` (a dossier person record id; cross-checked in `tools/` only, X02, against the content vocabulary, since the sidecar validator cannot see the dossier schema) so that Marta's door in Jan's chapter can wear the stamp Vera's filing earned her (Pillar 4), the same rule GDD 7 VA §2 gives sheets: a person's own file carries that person's tier. A `kind: person` hotspot never carries an anchor (§4.2: red never on people; O05–O07). A `threshold` hotspot's anchors use the room's default: Rybar's telephone reddens by Vera's reach because the call is the state's attention on *her*. If `per_character[subject]` is absent from the snapshot, the anchor's reach is 0 (GDD 7 Edge Case 14's shape) and `SUBJECT_UNKNOWN(anchor, subject)` is logged once per scene. The `protagonist_id` arrives in the `enter_scene` context (Rule 11), never from a string on the sidecar. *Rationale:* the snapshot already carries every watched person; keying on it keeps Overlay dossier-blind while letting a consequence cross chapters on the plate as it already does on paper. *Rejected:* a per-room blend or max over everyone present (`max(red_reach)` makes a calm protagonist's room red because a watched friend is in it, GDD 7 §3's rejected "red for no reason of his own"); the brief's "person hotspot's threshold uses the subject's" as a rule by kind (a telephone is not a person; the subject binding is per anchor and explicit, or there is none).

**5. Four tiers, four looks, and the tint carries no red where a person stands.** At `reach 0` nothing authored is lit and `tint.tier = 0`: the plate shows no red-tolerance pixel (Collapsing's "withdraws", and every unlooked-at protagonist at any phase). At `reach 1` the tier-1 stamps are lit from the one stamp atlas. At `reach 2` the tier-2 prop layers join them. At `reach 3` the `key_light` layer joins them and `tint.tier = 3`: the full-frame phase layer (T4's fourth layer, T3's one draw) takes the uniform `3`. the stamp-red of tier 3 is carried by the authored `key_light` layer, which O07 keeps off the `figure_band` and off people; the full-frame tint is the "hard light" of §4.3 ("tinting state key light, hard light") — a value and temperature shift of the plate toward the state's hard overhead — and must leave **no red-tolerance pixel inside any `figure_band` or `kind: person` rect** after compositing (a Gate B sub-check, Rule 6). Whether the tint shader (`CanvasModulate` versus a full-frame tint shader, blend mode: **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike) carries any red hue outside the band is the art director's call under that check; the plan carries `tint.tier` either way. Under `red_as_hatching` (GDD 4), `red_style` returns `hatch` and every lit anchor renders its hatch variant: a stamp's hatch atlas cell, a prop's `layer_hatch`, the key light's `layer_hatch` (a hatched edge on a value-only light), each guaranteed red-free by O08; the tint's `hatch: true` tells the shader to drop any hue term and keep the value shift. Tier 1 and 2 are the act-one sights (the MVP shows `reach ≤ 1`, the Vertical Slice `reach 2` in Jan's chapter at paranoid, GDD 7 Open Questions); tier 3 is first seen in act two. *Rationale:* §4.2 ("never on skin or clothing") and GDD 8 VA §3 ("no red is on the figure") are absolute, and a full-frame red over a y-sorted figure cannot honour them; authored red on authored rects can. *Rejected:* lifting figures above the tint (breaks GDD 3 Rule 10's y-sort under walking avatars); a hatched full-frame vignette under `red_as_hatching` (a vignette is a grade, GDD 6 VA §2 forbids one; and §4.5 wants shape, not a frame).

**6. The red budget is an assert, not a tuning: the plan's estimate is the headless proxy, Gate B's composited capture is the truth.** `PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM = [20000, 50000, 150000, 10000]` (art bible §4.3's 2/5/15/1%, registered as a pointer, not retunable in any GDD). The core computes `red_est_ppm` (Formulas F3) from the lit anchors' `red_px` plus `HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[ink_reach]` (Paper UI's date stamp, the one red HUD element always on screen; art bible A8 puts the HUD inside the measurement) over `FRAME_PX` 2,073,600, and raises `RED_BUDGET(scene_id, phase, est, budget)` through the `DebugSink` when `red_est_ppm > red_budget_ppm`; O10 runs the same function for every phase at build so no shipped plate can trip it at runtime. Gate B (art bible T7): a CI job loads each plate at each phase through `NullSettings` + `EngineOverlayHost`, calls `capture_red_share_ppm()` (frame capture **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike), and asserts the measured share ≤ budget, Collapsing soot ≥ `SOOT_MIN_PPM` 400,000, and (Rule 5) red inside every `figure_band` and person rect = 0. The proxy over-counts (overlaps are summed, occlusion ignored) and never under-counts, so a plan that passes the proxy may fail Gate B only through the tint or Paper UI's sheets, which the capture sees and the proxy cannot. *Rationale:* the budgets are the art bible's way of making Principle A measurable; a tuning knob on them would let a plate negotiate with the state's colour. *Rejected:* measuring per plate on export (GDD 1's open question, answered by T7: the plate carries no red, so only the composite can be measured); clamping the estimate by hiding anchors at runtime (a room would differ between players for a build error).

**7. Slot lighting is two switches, red-free, cut at entry, and never a function of the phase.** From `slot` and `space` the plan sets every lighting layer (Formulas table below); the layers are authored crops (GDD 7 VA §5: "three slots on two switches, lighting layers rather than plate variants"; the technical artist confirms the blend in the spike):

| `space` | `morning` | `day` | `night` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state`, `private`, `print_shop` | `window_day` on, `lamp`/`overhead` on | `window_day` on, pool off | `window_night` on, `lamp`/`overhead` on |
| `street` | nothing lit (overcast, §4.3) | nothing lit | `sodium_<n>` on where `phase ∈ lit_phases`, `amber_windows` on |

Switch one is the window's value (bone by day, soot by night; never blue, never sun); switch two is the pool (lamp in private, overhead in state). A slot change between consecutive scenes is seen only as the next plate entering already lit (GDD 7 VA §5: a cut, never a crossfade); Overlay plays nothing at `transition`. The only phase term in this table is `sodium_<n>.lit_phases`: Collapsing's "sodium poles dark one by one" (§2.2) is authored per pole per plate, so the sequence across the act's street plates is the artist's, and no count, beat or clock chooses a pole (GDD 7 Rule 13: no tier is a function of the beat). *Rationale:* §2.1's light recipes have one source per state and the Calendar already said the slot never changes a tier; two booleans per plate are all the lighting state the game has. *Rejected:* three plate paintings per room (T1: three plates is 33 MB per scene); lowering lamps by phase at runtime (§2.2's "lamps lowered" is plate authoring for the phase a scene is expected in, a conflict recorded in Open Questions for the game designer and art director; a runtime dimming would be a meter in light).

**8. The carbon ghost is the one in-scene change, made by the player's own act, drawn by cut, never moved.** On GDD 3's `set_hotspot_state(id, filed)` the host emits `ghost_requested(id)`; the core returns `GhostOp {hotspot: id, source: layer_filed, offset: [GHOST_OFFSET_PX, GHOST_OFFSET_PX], colour: CARBON_WORLD}` and the host draws, on the next frame and with no fade, a flat carbon-blue silhouette (`#434C7C`, the crop's alpha filled with one colour; mechanism — a mask export or a one-line shader — **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike) of the hotspot's `layer_filed` crop, offset `GHOST_OFFSET_PX` = 3 px right and down at 1080 (2 px at 720 under the ⅔ stretch, whole pixels; art bible §6.4's 2–3 px), **directly beneath that hotspot's layer in GDD 3's draw order** so the ghost peeks out on two edges and the object's hard edge stays intact. Ghosts are drawn only for `filed`, never for `void` (the state removed the paper; nothing was copied) or `hidden`; a hotspot already `filed` at entry is in `ghosts_at_entry` and drawn with `apply(plan)`; a ghost stays put when its hotspot lifts on focus (GDD 3 VA 7c: the gap is the focus read) and costs no extra draw for the lift (answers GDD 3's open question). Count ≤ `GHOST_MAX` 16 (T3) and, by GDD 3 E02, ≤ 8 in practice; the plan asserts `|ghosts| ≤ n_hotspots`. The ghost never animates, never fades, never reacts to a tier, and is not removed in-scene (availability moves `filed → unfiled` only through Scene Flow at a boundary). *Rationale:* "a read room looks read" (§6.4); the carbon copy is the state's duplicate and a duplicate does not move; drawing it as a flat silhouette makes T7's carbon count predictable and keeps the keyline out of the copy (a blue keyline would re-say "a fact is here", GDD 3 VA 7c). *Rejected:* a modulated duplicate of the `layer` crop (restores the keyline in blue, and modulating a midtone object by carbon gives a muddy third colour, not a copy); a 2 px offset at 1080 (1.33 px at 720, a blurred edge under T6's integer rule).

**9. The reactive-event threshold light is two switches on two cuts, and the figure comes first.** For `scene_kind: insert` the plan carries two lighting tables. `lighting` is the room as the player left it: the inherited slot's recipe, `threshold_<id>` off. `lighting_after_threshold` is the §2.1 reactive-event recipe: every slot layer off ("the room's light source is replaced"), `threshold_<id>` on (torch through the door, sodium through the window, the bare bulb over the telephone), and the red anchors lit at the inherited reach exactly as in any scene, so red crosses the threshold line at the phase's reach (a stamp on the door at tier 1, the red telephone at tier 2, the key light at tier 3 — O07 admits the key light on inserts). The host applies `lighting` with `apply(plan)` at `scene_ready` (the figure is already on the line, Character Presentation **(PROV)**; GDD 8 VA §3 frame one) and `lighting_after_threshold` with `apply_after_threshold(plan)` exactly `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later (500 ms; a knob), **never on the same frame and never as a crossfade**. The delay is a story beat, not a tween, so `motion: reduced` does not shorten it; the two cuts within one second are at most one opposing luminance pair, under `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` by construction. *Rationale:* GDD 6 VA §7 and GDD 8 VA §3: the floor goes after the state is in the room, and no light announces a person not yet on the line. *Rejected:* letting Scene Flow time the second cut (it owns no clock inside a scene, GDD 8 Rule 13; the host's tick is the one clock here, injected for the null twin); a single cut with the threshold light on frame one (GDD 8's reviewer test fails it by name).

**10. Flicker is an allowance this system issues, enforced by construction, and nothing in a room ever flickers.** `flicker_allowance(flicker, can_count_flashes)` (Formulas F4): `off` → `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}`; `limited` → `{allowed: true, max_hz: min(FLICKER_HZ, FLASH_MAX_PER_S)}` when the host's toggle gate is live, else `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}` (GDD 4 Edge Case 21: `limited` behaves as `off` until the cap exists). The gate is the core's pure function `toggle_ok(now_ms, last_toggle_ms) = now_ms − last_toggle_ms ≥ 1000 / (2 × max_hz)` with injected time, so a driven square wave can never exceed `max_hz` flashes per sliding second (WCAG 2.3.1; `flash_rate_ok` in the registry is satisfied by construction rather than by measurement), and `can_count_flashes()` is the host's statement that it drives toggles through the gate on real frame time (the engine twin returns `false` until the spike proves frame timing; `NullOverlayHost` returns what the test sets). The **only** flicker in the game is §2.2's Panicking overhead flicker **on the portrait pinned to the interrogation transcript sheet** (interrogation is "the only state with a pulse", §2.1); it is Paper UI's **(PROV)** to draw from this allowance and this gate, and it never carries red (the portrait has none, §4.2), so the red-flash threshold GDD 4 left to Overlay is met with `red_flashes = 0`. No lighting layer, stamp, prop, tint or ghost ever toggles, periodically or once, while a scene is `active` (GDD 3 Game Feel "nothing moves unprompted"; GDD 8 VA §6). Overlay reads `motion` for nothing: every change it makes is a cut at 0 ms under `full` and `reduced` alike, so it holds no subscription (GDD 4's reader row amended for this GDD). Act one never reaches Panicking (`first_beat(2) = ∅`), so the allowance is exercised by tests and the Vertical Slice's portrait only. *Rationale:* a cap that is measured can be missed for a frame; a cap that is a minimum interval between toggles cannot, and the one periodic element in the game is then the whole sum WCAG asks about. *Rejected:* an overhead lighting layer flickering in Panicking state rooms (it would be the one thing moving in an idle room, Open Questions); counting luminance transitions from a frame capture (the spike that may never land, and a count after the fact is not a cap).

**11. Inputs arrive by push through the presentation seam; Overlay calls nothing that computes.** The engine `PresentationSink` fan-out (GDD 8 Rule 13) delivers to Overlay: `reveal(records, snapshot, paper[], counts)` → `on_snapshot(snapshot)` (stored; the plate is hidden behind the full-frame sheet at that moment, GDD 8 VA §1, so nothing is applied); and a new call, **`enter_scene(scene_id, scene_kind, slot, protagonist_id)`** (GDD 8 amendment, amendment applied), emitted by Scene Flow immediately before `host.preload_scene` for an authored scene and before `activate_scene(wipe = false)` for an insert, carrying the facts Scene Flow already holds (`date_for(id).slot`, the chapter's protagonist, the row's `kind`). GDD 3's host delivers `scene_preloaded(id)` (plan, `load`), `scene_ready(id)` (`apply`), `ghost_requested(id)` (Rule 8) and `scene_exited(id)` (`clear`). Settings values are read through a `SettingsReader` at plan time only (`flicker`, `red_as_hatching`) and never subscribed to. If no snapshot has arrived (the act's first scene; the first scene after a load, GDD 7 Rule 11), the plan treats the snapshot as `{phase 0, ink_reach 1, per_character: {}}`: every reach 0, slot lighting applied, `SNAPSHOT_MISSING(scene_id)` logged, and Open Questions asks the Calendar for the view the room should have had. *Rationale:* GDD 7 Rule 9 forbids consumers calling `phase()` or `attention_view()`; GDD 8 Rule 13 forbids signals from Scene Flow; what remains is a recorded call list, which is what the simulator diffs. *Rejected:* reading the slot off `transition(data)` (not called for `kind: none`, for inserts or at chapter open; Open Questions); reading the protagonist off the sidecar (a content string in a presentation system, and wrong the moment a room is reused across chapters).

**12. Sheet red is Paper UI's to draw and this system's to style; the timer's one float never enters here.** `red_style(red_as_hatching) → {variant: fill | hatch}` and the stamp atlas (with its hatch cells, Rule 5) are this system's; every red on a sheet — the §7.5 surveillance stamp from `stamp_tier`, the §7.1 date stamp from `ink_reach` (both already Paper UI's by GDD 7 Rule 9), and the §7.5 **interrogation timer margin fill** — is drawn by Paper UI **(PROV)** from that style and that atlas. The timer fill is Dialogue's state **(PROV)**: Dialogue owns `fraction ∈ [0, 1]` under `timer_mode` and hands it to its own transcript sheet (a Paper UI Control), which fills the right margin top-to-bottom in `fill` or `hatch`, bordered, with hatching ticks at every `1 / TIMER_TICKS` (5; §7.5's 20%), no numerals, the screen-reader countdown string beside it. It is the one continuous motion in the game and it stays under `motion: reduced` (GDD 4: information, not decoration). Overlay never receives the float, never draws in sheet space, and never knows a timer is running. *Rationale:* the fill lives on a sheet, and sheets are Control trees above the T4 stack (§4.4: UI never takes tint; §7.2: UI grain is its own ≤ 6%); GDD 7 Rule 9 already put the surveillance stamp on Paper UI from a tier, and the timer is the same kind of object with one more input. Giving Overlay a second drawing path into UI space would put one system on both sides of the tint for one bar. *Rejected:* the brief's proposal 7 (Overlay renders the fill from Dialogue's `fraction`): it makes Overlay the one system that handles a float, and it needs the sheet's layout under `ui_scale`, which is Paper UI's; GDD 7 Rule 10's "Dialogue's and Overlay's" is amended to "Dialogue's and Paper UI's; Overlay supplies the red style" (amendment applied).

**13. Budgets are pointers, reported as observations.** Draws (art bible T3, `DRAW_TARGET` 150): grain 1, tint 1, ghosts ≤ `GHOST_MAX` 16, stamps ≤ `STAMP_MAX` 12, lighting layers ≤ `LIGHT_MAX` 6, props and key light ≤ 1 + the prop count (each lit anchor one draw); the core's `draws_est_overlay` feeds GDD 3 Formulas §8's `STAMP_MAX + 2 + n_vis` terms and the lighting term is new (amendment applied: GDD 3 §8 gains `+ LIGHT_MAX`). Memory (T1): every overlay layer is cropped (T9; O14) and summed into GDD 3's resident-byte sheet under owner `overlay` (O15), with the budget lines `SCENE_BUDGET_WARN` 224 / `SCENE_BUDGET_MAX` 256 deciding; the art bible's worksheet line "overlays and layers ≈ 10 MB" is revised to carry three 1024² lighting layers (Open Questions). The debug overlay shows `measured − draws_est` (GDD 3 W14) and Overlay's bytes; neither is a verdict. *Rejected:* an Overlay-local ceiling (the ceiling is per scene, not per owner; GDD 3 Formulas §7's rejection).

**14. What this system never does.** Never reads a dossier field, `A_eff`, `red_band`, a threshold constant (`T_*`, `ATTN_*`) or the beat; never calls `phase()`, `attention_view()` or `snapshot()`; never recomputes a plan while a scene is `active` or `suspended`; never animates, tweens, fades or crossfades anything (every change is a cut; the one periodic element is Paper UI's portrait under this system's allowance); never draws red on a figure, a person hotspot or inside a `figure_band`; never draws anything in a room the player is idle in that was not in the plan at entry, except the ghost the player's own filing made; never draws a meter, bar, dial, numeral or banner (the timer fill is Paper UI's, from Dialogue); never touches a plate's pixels or a crop's (Gate A); never draws in sheet or HUD space; never changes a stamp, tint or light on a phase change (the announcement is paper, GDD 6 VA §2); never plays a sound; never holds UI state or persists anything (a plan is rebuilt at every entry from pushed inputs); never reads a wall clock (the host's tick is injected; the core has none); never varies a plate's density or lamp height by phase; never substitutes a layer at runtime for one the validator rejected (release degrades by *hiding* the anchor and logging, so a missing stamp is less red, never more). Its errors go through the injected `DebugSink`, so every assert is observable headless.

### States and Transitions

**The system, per scene (one state machine, keyed by `scene_id`):** `idle` (no plan for this id) → `planned` (at `scene_preloaded`: `plan()` computed from the stored snapshot and the `enter_scene` context; `host.load(plan)`; layers resident, nothing visible; the plan is immutable from here) → `applied` (at `scene_ready`: `host.apply(plan)` on one frame — slot lighting, lit anchors at the reach, tint tier, grain, ghosts for `ghosts_at_entry`; for an insert this is the first-cut table) → [inserts only] `relit` (at `scene_ready + THRESHOLD_CUT_MS`: `apply_after_threshold(plan)`, one frame, the slot layers off and `threshold_<id>` on) → `applied`/`relit` holds for the life of the scene, accepting exactly one kind of change, `ghost_requested(id)` → `apply_ghost(op)` (next frame, by cut, additive, never reversed in-scene) → `cleared` (at `scene_exited`: `host.clear(scene_id)`; layers released; the id returns to `idle`). A `reveal` arriving while a scene is `applied` updates the **stored snapshot only**; nothing on the plate changes (the plate is behind the full-frame sheet and will be left by the wipe, GDD 8 VA §1). A `plan()` request for an `applied` scene is refused with `PLAN_LOCKED(scene_id)` (debug assert; release ignores and logs).

**The snapshot, as this system holds it:** `none` (act start; after a load) → `held(beat n)` at each `reveal` → `held(beat n+1)` …; never mutated, never saved (GDD 7 Rule 11), never requested. `none` plans as all-zero reach with `SNAPSHOT_MISSING` (Rule 11; Open Questions).

**The ghost, per hotspot:** `absent` → `drawn` on `ghost_requested` or on entry with `filed` availability → stays `drawn` through focus (lift above it), reveal, sheets, the insert's two cuts → `absent` only at `clear` or when the next entry's availability says `unfiled`/`void`/`hidden` (re-derived by Scene Flow at a boundary, GDD 3 Rule 5).

**The tint, as one uniform:** `0` for every scene at `reach ≤ 2` → `3` only for a scene entered at `reach 3`; never any value between, never changes within a scene, never carries red into a figure band (Rule 5). With `red_as_hatching`: the same tiers with `hatch: true`.

**The flicker allowance, per plan:** `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}` under `flicker: off`, or under `limited` while `can_count_flashes()` is false → `{allowed: true, max_hz: FLICKER_HZ}` under `limited` once the gate is live. Consumed by Paper UI's portrait; the allowance changes only with the plan, so a settings change mid-interrogation applies at the next scene entry (parallel to GDD 4 Edge Case 19).

**The timer fill (for the record; not this system's state):** Dialogue's `fraction` 0 → 1 under `timer_mode`, drawn continuously by Paper UI in `red_style`; Overlay has no state for it.

**The red budget, per plan:** `within` (`red_est_ppm ≤ red_budget_ppm`; the plan's normal state) or `over` (`RED_BUDGET` raised through the `DebugSink`; the plan is still applied in release, since hiding anchors at runtime would make rooms differ between players, and O10 means a shipped build never reaches this state).

## Formulas

*Proposed by the systems designer; three items it raised are ruled here: T3's "stamps ≤ 12" is read as the draw budget for **all lit red anchors** (stamps, props and the key light together), so `STAMP_MAX` bounds `|red_anchors|` and O14 counts every kind (a tightening of Section C's O14 wording); the overlay block's bytes are added to GDD 3's build-time estimate (`bytes_est = bytes_fw + bytes_overlay + OTHERS_RESERVE_MB`, an amendment to GDD 3 Formulas §7, because `OTHERS_RESERVE_MB` 181 is exactly sheet + portraits + UI atlas + documents and holds no overlay line); and the proxy-versus-capture tolerance `RED_PROXY_TOLERANCE_PP` is a CI constant, not an art knob. Every constant appears by its registry name; the shipped value is quoted in the table. GDD 7's `red_reach`, `stamp_tier` and `PHASE_RED_CAP` (Formulas §3–§4) are cited, never restated. Indices: `a` is an anchor, `l` a lighting layer, `h` a hotspot, `p` a phase 0–3, `k` an ink reach 0–3. Square brackets `[P]` are the Iverson bracket (1 if `P`, else 0). Every quantity below is an integer, an id, a boolean or a rect of integers; fractions are parts per million (`ppm`) and opacities integer percent; nothing reads a wall clock except §5's gate, whose time is injected.*

### 1. Reach for an anchor

The reach an anchor is lit against is defined as:

`subject(a) = a.subject if a.subject ≠ null else protagonist_id` · `reach_for(a) = view[subject(a)].red_reach if subject(a) ∈ view else 0`

where `view` is the snapshot's `per_character` (GDD 7 Rule 9) and `view[x].red_reach = min(red_band(x), PHASE_RED_CAP[phase])` (GDD 7 §3, registry `red_reach`). An anchor on a `threshold: true` hotspot carries no `subject` by O06/O11's shape and so uses the room's default; a `kind: person` hotspot carries no anchor at all (O05–O07). With no snapshot held (Core Rule 11), `view = {}` and every `reach_for = 0`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a.subject` | id or null | a dossier person record id, or null | data file (sidecar `overlay.red_anchors[].subject`; X02 cross-check in `tools/`) | The person whose attention this anchor shows; null on almost every anchor |
| `protagonist_id` | id | `{vera, jan, tomas}` in act one | `enter_scene` (GDD 8 Rule 13) | The chapter's protagonist: the room is theirs |
| `view` | map id → record | every person record carrying `attention` | snapshot (`reveal`, GDD 8 Rule 13; GDD 7 Rule 9) | The per-character view; absent persons read as 0 (GDD 7 Edge Case 14) |
| `view[x].red_reach` | int | 0–3 | calculated (GDD 7 §3) | Phase-capped reach of `x` |
| `reach_for(a)` | int | 0–3 | calculated | The tier this anchor is lit against |

**Output Range:** bounded 0–3. Minimum 0 when the subject is absent from the view, unwatched (`A_eff 0`), or the phase is collapsing (`PHASE_RED_CAP[3] = 0`), and for every anchor when no snapshot is held. Maximum 3 only at panicking with the subject above `ATTN_WATCHED` (act two and later; act one's `first_beat(2) = ∅`). Because the default subject is one person, every anchor without a `subject` in one room shares one reach, so a room reads as one tier plus named exceptions; a `subject` anchor can be above or below the room's tier (Marta's door hatched in a room whose telephone is red, or the reverse). **Example:** Jan's `j_route` at the snapshot `{phase 1, per_character: {jan: {red_reach 2}, marta: {red_reach 1}}}`, `protagonist_id jan`: `prop_telephone` (no subject) → `reach_for 2`; `stamp_marta_door` (`subject: marta`) → `reach_for 1`; `stamp_notice_01` with `subject: ilona` (not in the view) → 0, `SUBJECT_UNKNOWN` logged once. *Rejected:* the room's reach as `max` over everyone in the view (a calm protagonist's room reddens because a watched friend exists; GDD 7 §3's "red for no reason of his own"); the subject as a per-hotspot-kind rule ("a `person` hotspot's threshold uses the subject's", the brief's wording: a telephone is not a person, and the binding must be visible in the data or there is none).

### 2. Lit: anchors, the slot's two switches, the threshold's two switches

What is drawn is defined as three predicates over the sidecar's layers:

`lit_anchor(a) = [ a.tier ≤ reach_for(a) ]` (cumulative: the margin stays lit when the props join it)

`lit_light(l, slot, space, phase) = [ l.id ∈ RECIPE[space][slot] ] · [ l.id ∉ SODIUM ∨ phase ∈ l.lit_phases ]` with
`RECIPE[interior][morning] = {window_day, POOL}`, `RECIPE[interior][day] = {window_day}`, `RECIPE[interior][night] = {window_night, POOL}`, `RECIPE[street][night] = SODIUM ∪ {amber_windows}`, `RECIPE[street][morning] = RECIPE[street][day] = ∅`, where `interior = {state, private, print_shop}`, `POOL = {lamp} ∪ {overhead}` (whichever the sidecar declares), `SODIUM = {sodium_<n>}`

`lit_light_after(l) = [ l.id = threshold_<t> ]` for `scene_kind = insert` and the insert's one threshold `t` (every slot layer off: "the room's light source is replaced", art bible §2.1)

Switch one of the slot is the window's value (`window_day` xor `window_night`), switch two is the pool on or off; switch one of the threshold is `threshold_<t>` off then on, switch two is the slot set kept then replaced. The tint is `tint.tier = 3 · [reach_for(default) = 3]`, with `reach_for(default)` the protagonist's reach.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a.tier` | int | 1–3 | data file (fixed by `kind`: stamp 1, prop 2, key_light 3; O02) | The reach at which the anchor lights |
| `reach_for(a)` | int | 0–3 | calculated (§1) | |
| `slot` | enum | `morning`, `day`, `night` (GDD 7 `SLOTS`) | `enter_scene` | The scene's slot from the timetable |
| `space` | enum | `state`, `private`, `street`, `print_shop` | data file (`overlay.space`) | Art bible §6.3 area |
| `l.lit_phases` | int[] | subset of 0–3; default all | data file (`sodium_<n>` only) | Phases in which this pole is lit; "dark one by one" is authored per pole |
| `phase` | int | 0–3 | snapshot | |
| `scene_kind` | enum | `authored`, `insert` | `enter_scene` | |
| `lit_anchor`, `lit_light`, `lit_light_after` | bool | — | calculated | Drawn or not |
| `tint.tier` | int | `{0, 3}` | calculated | The full-frame layer's uniform; never 1 or 2 |

**Output Range:** `lit_anchor` is monotone in `reach_for` (a higher reach never unlights an anchor); at reach 0 the lit set is empty and the plate carries no red-tolerance pixel. `lit_light` lights at most `|{window}| + |POOL| = 2` layers in an interior and at most `|SODIUM| + 1 ≤ LIGHT_MAX` in a street; `lit_light_after` lights exactly 1. A sidecar whose `lit_phases` omits the current phase simply leaves that pole dark (Edge Case 23). `tint.tier` is 3 only when the protagonist's own reach is 3: a `subject` anchor at 3 in a room whose protagonist is at 2 lights the key light? No: `key_light` carries no `subject` (O07 shape), so the key light and the tint move together on the protagonist alone. **Example:** `v_office` (`private`), `slot day`, `reach 1`: `window_day` on, `lamp` off, `window_night` off; `stamp_door_01` lit, `prop_telephone` unlit; `tint 0`. Insert `v_rybar_call` (`private`, inherited `slot night`, `reach 1`): first table `window_night` on, `lamp` on, `threshold_telephone` off; second table all three slot layers off, `threshold_telephone` on; `stamp_door_01` lit in both. Street `j_vrba` at `phase 3`, `night`: `sodium_01 {lit_phases [0,1,2]}` dark, `sodium_02 {lit_phases [0,1,2,3]}` lit, `amber_windows` lit. *Rejected:* a non-cumulative reach (only tier = reach lit: paranoid would lose its margin stamps, and §2.2's axis reads "margin → props", not "margin, then props instead"); a tint at tiers 1–2 (a red grade is a meter in light; GDD 6 VA §2).

### 3. The plan as a function

The plan is defined as:

`OverlayPlan = plan(sidecar.overlay, snapshot, ctx, settings, caps)` with `ctx = {scene_id, scene_kind, slot, protagonist_id}`, `settings = {flicker, red_as_hatching}`, `caps = {can_count_flashes}`, and fields:

```
phase        = snapshot.phase                (0 if snapshot = null)
reach        = reach_for(default)            (§1)
ink_reach    = PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]          (1 if snapshot = null)
slot, scene_kind                              = ctx
lighting[l]  = on if lit_light(l, slot, space, phase) else off, for every l in sidecar.lighting   (§2)
lighting_after_threshold = { l: on if lit_light_after(l) else off } if scene_kind = insert else null
stamps       = sorted by id { a: kind = stamp,     lit_anchor(a) } each as {anchor, atlas, variant: red_style(), rect}
props        = sorted by id { a: kind = prop,      lit_anchor(a) } each as {anchor, hotspot, variant, rect}
key_light    = the one a: kind = key_light with lit_anchor(a), as {anchor, variant, rect}, else null
tint         = { tier: 3·[reach = 3], hatch: red_as_hatching }
grain        = sidecar.grain
flicker      = flicker_allowance(flicker, can_count_flashes)       (§5)
red_est_ppm  = red_estimate_ppm(lit anchors, variant, ink_reach)   (§4)
red_budget_ppm = PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM[phase]
ghosts_at_entry = sorted { h : availability(h) = filed }
findings     = codes raised during planning (SNAPSHOT_MISSING, SUBJECT_UNKNOWN, RED_BUDGET, STAMP_TIER_ABOVE_INK)
```

serialised with keys sorted, lists in id order, every number an integer. The plan is a pure function of its five inputs: two runs over equal inputs give byte-equal output on any host, and the `NullOverlayHost` log for a scene is the plan plus the ordered `apply`/`apply_after_threshold`/`apply_ghost` calls.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sidecar.overlay` | record | validated by O01–O15 | data file (GDD 3's `scene.json`) | Every red and every light the room can show |
| `snapshot` | `BoundaryView` or null | GDD 7 Rule 9's shape | `reveal` | null before the act's first boundary and after a load until `view_now()` is pushed (Core Rule 11; GDD 7 amendment) |
| `ctx` | record | ids and enums | `enter_scene` | Scene facts Scene Flow already holds |
| `settings.flicker` | enum | `off`, `limited` | `SettingsReader` at plan time | GDD 4 |
| `settings.red_as_hatching` | bool | — | `SettingsReader` at plan time | GDD 4 |
| `caps.can_count_flashes` | bool | — | `OverlayHost` | The host drives toggles through §5's gate on frame time |
| `availability(h)` | enum | `unfiled`, `filed`, `void`, `hidden` | GDD 3 host (set by Scene Flow before `scene_ready`) | For `ghosts_at_entry` |
| `OverlayPlan` | record | as above | calculated | The one output; immutable per scene |

**Output Range:** exactly one plan per `enter_scene`; no field is unbounded (`stamps + props + [key_light] ≤ STAMP_MAX`, `|lighting| ≤ LIGHT_MAX`, `|ghosts_at_entry| ≤ 8`, `red_est_ppm ≤ 10⁶`). The all-null-snapshot plan is the minimum: no anchor lit, `tint 0`, `ink_reach 1`, `red_est_ppm = HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[1] · 10⁶ / FRAME_PX = 1929`, `flicker` per settings, lighting per slot. The maximum is a state-space or insert plate at reach 3 with every anchor lit and `tint 3`. **Example:** `plan(v_office.overlay, {phase 0, per_character: {vera: {red_reach 1}}}, {v_office, authored, day, vera}, {limited, false}, {false})` → `{phase 0, reach 1, ink_reach 1, slot day, lighting {lamp off, window_day on, window_night off}, lighting_after_threshold null, stamps [{stamp_door_01, stamp_square_official, fill, [1612,118,96,96]}], props [], key_light null, tint {0, false}, grain {grain_bone_01, 10}, flicker {false, 0}, red_est_ppm 4147, red_budget_ppm 20000, ghosts_at_entry [], findings []}`. *Rejected:* a plan that holds a reference to the sidecar or the snapshot (a later mutation would change a plan already applied; the plan copies values); recomputing on `changed(red_as_hatching)` (two plans in one room; Core Rule 2).

### 4. Red estimate and the budget

The headless proxy for the §4.3 share is defined as:

`red_est_ppm = ⌈ ( Σ_{a : lit_anchor(a)} red_px(a, variant) + HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[ink_reach] ) · 10⁶ / FRAME_PX ⌉` with `red_px(a, fill) = a.red_px` and `red_px(a, hatch) = a.red_px_hatch = 0` (O08)

`within_budget(p) = [ red_est_ppm(p) ≤ PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM[p] ]`, where `red_est_ppm(p)` at build (O10) counts every anchor with `tier ≤ PHASE_RED_CAP[p]` lit under `fill` and uses `HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[PHASE_RED_CAP[p]]`

`proxy_agrees(capture_ppm) = [ capture_ppm − red_est_ppm ≤ RED_PROXY_TOLERANCE_PP · 10⁴ ]` (Gate B, CI: the capture may be below the proxy by any amount, since the proxy over-counts overlaps, but above it by at most one percentage point, since only the tint or a sheet could add red the proxy did not see)

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `a.red_px` | int | 0 – layer area | data file (written by the T7 gate script at export; O09 re-verifies) | Red-tolerance pixels (hue 348°–18°, S ≥ 0.55, V ≥ 0.35) in the anchor's `fill` layer or atlas cell |
| `a.red_px_hatch` | int | 0 | data file (O08 requires 0) | The hatch variant carries no red |
| `HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX` | int[4] | `[0, 4000, 8000, 41000]` (D; Paper UI confirms) | constant (this GDD, registry; co-owned with Paper UI) | The §7.1 date stamp at `dry/crisp/bleeds/floods`: a 640×64 box at 1080, strokes ≈ 10% / 20% / solid |
| `ink_reach` | int | 0–3 | snapshot (`PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]`) | Which reserve applies |
| `FRAME_PX` | int | 2,073,600 | constant (1920 × 1080; a fact of the composite, not a knob) | The capture's size |
| `PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM` | int[4] | `[20000, 50000, 150000, 10000]` | constant (art bible §4.3 by pointer, registry) | 2 / 5 / 15 / 1 % |
| `RED_PROXY_TOLERANCE_PP` | int | 1 (B) | constant (CI config) | Percentage points the capture may exceed the proxy by |
| `red_est_ppm` | int | 0 – 10⁶ | calculated | The plan's share |
| `within_budget`, `proxy_agrees` | bool | — | calculated | The build assert (O10) and the CI assert (Gate B) |

**Output Range:** `red_est_ppm` is bounded 0–10⁶ and is 0 exactly under `red_as_hatching` at `ink_reach 0`, or at reach 0 with `ink_reach 0` (collapsing: the proxy reads 0 against a 1% budget; the soot ≥ `SOOT_MIN_PPM` 400,000 half of Gate B has no proxy and is capture-only). Minimum with a snapshot and `fill`: `HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[1] = 4000` → 1,929 ppm at confident with nobody looked at. Maximum: every anchor lit under `fill` plus the floods reserve, which O10 bounds at 150,000. The proxy never under-counts a lit anchor (overlaps sum; occlusion by figures is ignored) and never sees the tint or a sheet; `proxy_agrees` is therefore one-sided. **Example (1080):** *Confident, `v_office`, reach 1, ink_reach 1:* `4600 + 4000 = 8600` → `⌈8600 · 10⁶ / 2073600⌉ = 4147` ppm, 0.41% ≤ 2% ✓. *Paranoid, `j_press`, reach 2, ink_reach 2:* `4600 + 11000 + 14000 + 8000 = 37600` → 18,133 ppm, 1.81% ≤ 5% ✓. *Panicking state space (act two), reach 3, ink_reach 3:* `4600 + 25000 + 180000 + 41000 = 250600` → 120,853 ppm, 12.1% ≤ 15% ✓. *Collapsing, any plate:* reach 0, `ink_reach 0` → 0 ppm ≤ 1% ✓. *Gate B on `j_press`:* capture 16,900 ppm (overlap of folder and stamp) → `proxy_agrees` (below the proxy); a capture of 29,000 ppm → fails (`+1.09 pp`), naming the tint or an open sheet as the suspect. *Rejected:* `red_px` as rect area × a fill factor (the gate script already counts exactly; an estimate of an exact number is a worse number); a budget tuned per plate (§4.3 is the state's colour budget, not a plate's).

### 5. Flicker allowance and the toggle gate

The allowance and the gate are defined as:

`max_hz = 0 if flicker = off ∨ ¬can_count_flashes else min(FLICKER_HZ, FLASH_MAX_PER_S)` · `flicker_allowance = { allowed: [max_hz > 0], max_hz }`

`toggle_ok(now_ms, last_ms) = [ max_hz > 0 ] · [ now_ms − last_ms ≥ ⌈1000 / (2 · max_hz)⌉ ]`

A flash (WCAG 2.3.1) is a pair of opposing luminance transitions; a square wave driven through `toggle_ok` produces at most `max_hz` pairs in any sliding one-second window, because two toggles are one pair and consecutive toggles are at least `1000 / (2 · max_hz)` ms apart, so GDD 4 Formulas §3's `flash_rate_ok(flashes, 1 s)` holds by construction with `flashes ≤ max_hz ≤ FLASH_MAX_PER_S`. A toggle refused by the gate is dropped, never deferred. `red_flashes = 0` always: no element carrying stamp red is ever toggled (Core Rule 10), so the red-flash threshold GDD 4 left to this system is met without a second gate.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `flicker` | enum | `off`, `limited` | `SettingsReader` at plan time | GDD 4; `limited` is the default, `off` under OS reduced motion at first run |
| `can_count_flashes` | bool | — | `OverlayHost.can_count_flashes()` | The engine twin drives every toggle through `toggle_ok` on frame time; `false` until the spike proves it; the null twin returns what a test sets |
| `FLICKER_HZ` | int | 1–3; 2 (D) | constant (this GDD, registry) | The portrait flicker's rate |
| `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` | int | 3 (may only be lowered) | constant (GDD 4, registry) | WCAG 2.3.1 ceiling |
| `max_hz` | int | 0–3 | calculated | The allowance |
| `now_ms`, `last_ms` | int | ≥ 0 | injected (the host's tick; a test counter) | Time of this frame and of the last accepted toggle |
| `toggle_ok` | bool | — | calculated | Whether the portrait may swap lit/unlit this frame |

**Output Range:** `max_hz ≤ 3` for every input and 0 for every input while `can_count_flashes` is false (GDD 4 Edge Case 21: `limited` behaves as `off` until the cap exists). The minimum interval is 167 ms at `max_hz 3`, 250 ms at 2, 500 ms at 1; at 60 fps those are 10, 15 and 30 frames. The gate is stateless beyond `last_ms`, so a replay with the same injected times gives the same toggles. **Example:** `limited`, `can_count_flashes true`, `FLICKER_HZ 2` → `{true, 2}`; toggles at 0, 250, 500 ms accepted, a toggle requested at 620 ms (120 ms after 500) refused and dropped, the next accepted at 750 ms; in any 1 s window at most 4 toggles = 2 pairs ≤ 3. `limited` with `can_count_flashes false` → `{false, 0}`: the portrait is steadily lit, the sheet says so. *Rejected:* counting luminance transitions from a frame capture (a count after the fact is not a cap, and the capture API is the spike that may not land); a per-player Hz slider (GDD 4's rejection: no value above the ceiling may exist).

### 6. Red style

The style every red element renders in is defined as:

`red_style(red_as_hatching) = hatch if red_as_hatching else fill`; `layer_of(a) = a.layer_hatch if red_style = hatch else a.layer` (atlas: `a.atlas + "_h"` for stamps); `tint.hatch = red_as_hatching`

Consumers: this system's stamps, props and key light (Core Rule 5); Paper UI's surveillance stamp, date stamp and timer fill **(PROV)**, which draw from the same atlas and the same style (Core Rule 12). The style is read at plan time and applies at the next scene entry.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `red_as_hatching` | bool | — | `SettingsReader` | GDD 4's §4.5 toggle |
| `red_style` | enum | `fill`, `hatch` | calculated | The variant every red element renders in |
| `a.layer`, `a.layer_hatch` | layer `{png, x, y}` | equal bounds (O04) | data file | The two paintings of one anchor; the hatch is soot hatching inside the same border, red-free (O08) |
| `tint.hatch` | bool | — | calculated | The full-frame shader drops any hue term and keeps the value shift |

**Output Range:** two values; under `hatch` the plate carries zero red-tolerance pixels at every reach (O08), so `red_est_ppm` reduces to the HUD reserve and the §4.3 budgets are met trivially; under `fill` the §4.5 shape backups (border, hatching, geometry) still carry every meaning, so the toggle removes hue and nothing else. **Example:** `red_as_hatching true`, `j_press` at reach 2: `stamp_door_01` from `stamp_square_official_h`, `prop_telephone` from `j_press_prop_telephone_h.png` (a soot-hatched receiver inside the same outline), `tint {0, true}`; `red_est_ppm = ⌈8000 · 10⁶ / 2073600⌉ = 3859` (the date stamp's reserve; Paper UI's own hatch rendering may make it 0, and the proxy stays conservative). *Rejected:* a hatched full-frame vignette under `hatch` (a vignette is a grade; GDD 6 VA §2); hatching rendered in a thinner red (GDD 4: "players who prefer no red at all").

### 7. Ghost placement and count

The carbon ghost is defined as:

`ghost(h) = { hotspot: h, source: layer_filed(h), rect: layer_filed(h).rect + (GHOST_OFFSET_PX, GHOST_OFFSET_PX), colour: CARBON_WORLD, z: directly beneath layer(h) }` for `availability(h) = filed`; `|ghosts| ≤ min(GHOST_MAX, n_hotspots)`

Drawn as a flat silhouette (the crop's alpha filled with one colour; mechanism a spike), by cut, never moved, never removed in-scene.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `layer_filed(h)` | layer `{png, x, y}` | ≤ 512², bounds equal to `layer(h)` (GDD 3 E08) | GDD 3 sidecar | The keyline-free crop; the ghost copies the object, not the mark |
| `GHOST_OFFSET_PX` | int | 3 (D); art bible §6.4 says 2–3 at 1080, and only 3 gives a whole pixel at ⅔ | constant (this GDD, registry) | Right and down |
| `CARBON_WORLD` | colour | `#434C7C` | constant (art bible §4.1 by pointer) | World carbon; the UI text tint `#2E3559` is never used on a plate |
| `n_hotspots` | int | 3–8 (GDD 3 E02) | sidecar | |
| `GHOST_MAX` | int | 16 | constant (art bible T3 by pointer) | Draw ceiling; 8 of it is reserve |
| `ghost(h).rect` | rect | inside the plate ± 3 px | calculated | May overhang a plate edge by the offset; clipped by the viewport |

**Output Range:** at most 8 ghosts per scene in practice, each one draw; the offset is 3 px at 1080 and exactly 2 at 720 under `canvas_items` ⅔ (breaking-changes.md line 39 for the stretch default; T6). A ghost's rect never intersects `figure_band` except where the hotspot already may (thresholds in the wall band, GDD 3 E06), since it is the hotspot's own crop displaced by 3 px. **Example:** `ledger` `layer_filed` at `(1210, 599, 96, 40)` → ghost at `(1213, 602, 96, 40)` beneath the `ledger` layer; on focus the `ledger` lifts 12 px (`LIFT_PX`, GDD 3) and the ghost stays, opening a 12 px gap above a 3 px overhang below. *Rejected:* a modulated copy of `layer(h)` (a blue keyline says "a fact is here" in another colour; GDD 3 VA 7c); 2 px (1.33 px at 720, a soft edge under T6's integer rule).

### 8. The validator predicates

Each check in Core Rule 3's table is defined as a boolean over the sidecar `S` with `O = S.overlay`, `H = S.hotspots`, `P = S.plate`, `F = S.figure_band`; a false predicate is the finding named, at the severity given there:

```
O01 = O.schema_version = OVERLAY_SCHEMA_VERSION ∧ O.space ∈ SPACES
O02 = ids(O) match ^[a-z0-9_]+$ ∧ unique(ids(O)) ∧ ids(O) ∩ ids(H) = ∅ ∧ ∀a: a.tier = TIER[a.kind] ∧ |{a : a.kind = key_light}| ≤ 1
O03 = ∀ rect r in O: integer(r) ∧ r ⊆ P ∧ ∀ layer: exists(png) ∧ pixel_size(png) = (w, h) ∧ integer(x, y) ∧ ∀a: a.kind = stamp ⇒ a.atlas ∈ ATLAS ∧ a.layer = null
O04 = ∀a: a.kind ∈ {prop, key_light} ⇒ bounds(a.layer) = bounds(a.layer_hatch) ∧ a.kind = stamp ⇒ (a.atlas + "_h") ∈ ATLAS
O05 = ∀a: a.kind = stamp ∨ (a.kind = prop ∧ a.hotspot = null) ⇒ a.rect ∩ (⋃_h bounds(h.layer) ∪ h.hit) = ∅ ∧ a.rect ∩ S.hero.rect = ∅ ∧ a.rect ∩ F = ∅ ∧ ∀h: h.kind = person ⇒ a.rect ∩ h.hit = ∅
O06 = ∀a: a.kind = prop ∧ a.hotspot ≠ null ⇒ a.hotspot ∈ ids(H) ∧ H[a.hotspot].kind ∈ {object, phone, paper} ∧ bounds(a.layer) = bounds(H[a.hotspot].layer)
O07 = ∀a: a.kind = key_light ⇒ (O.space = state ∨ S.kind = insert) ∧ a.rect ∩ F = ∅ ∧ ∀h: h.kind = person ⇒ a.rect ∩ h.hit = ∅ ∧ a.subject = null
O08 = ∀l ∈ O.lighting: l.red_px = 0 ∧ ∀a: a.red_px_hatch = 0
O09 = ∀a: a.red_px present ∧ a.red_px = recount(layer_of(a, fill))         [CI only; the recount is the gate script's]
O10 = ∀p ∈ 0..3: red_est_ppm(p) ≤ PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM[p]                      [static plate: E; scrolling: E per window at start_x, min_x, max_x; plate-wide W]
O11 = ∀l: l.id ∈ LIGHT_IDS ∧ (l.id = threshold_<t> ⇒ t ∈ ids(H) ∧ H[t].threshold) ∧ (l.id ∈ SODIUM ∪ {amber_windows} ⇒ O.space = street) ∧ (window_day ∈ ids ⇔ window_night ∈ ids) [W if one] ∧ (O.space ≠ street ⇒ POOL ∩ ids ≠ ∅) [W: NO_POOL]
O12 = S.kind = insert ⇒ |{l : l.id = threshold_*}| = 1
O13 = GRAIN_PLATE_MIN_PCT ≤ O.grain.opacity_pct ≤ GRAIN_PLATE_MAX_PCT ∧ O.grain.tile ∈ GRAIN_TILES
O14 = |O.red_anchors| ≤ STAMP_MAX ∧ |O.lighting| ≤ LIGHT_MAX ∧ ∀ layer: w ≤ LIGHT_LAYER_MAX_PX ∧ h ≤ LIGHT_LAYER_MAX_PX
O15 = report(bytes_overlay(O), owner = overlay)                                   [an observation into GDD 3's W13 sheet; never a finding of its own]
```

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `S`, `O`, `H`, `P`, `F` | records | GDD 3 Rule 1's sidecar | data file | The scene and its overlay block |
| `SPACES`, `TIER`, `LIGHT_IDS`, `SODIUM`, `POOL`, `ATLAS`, `GRAIN_TILES` | closed sets | as Core Rule 3 and §2 | constants (this GDD; the atlas and grain manifests are asset data) | The vocabularies |
| `OVERLAY_SCHEMA_VERSION` | int | 1 | constant (this GDD) | Bumped on any field add/remove/retype |
| `recount(png)` | int | ≥ 0 | calculated (the T7 gate script, `tools/asset-pipeline/`) | Red-tolerance pixel count |
| `red_est_ppm(p)` | int | 0 – 10⁶ | calculated (§4) | |
| `bytes_overlay(O)` | int | ≥ 0 | calculated (§9) | |
| `O01..O15` | bool | — | calculated | True is clean |

**Output Range:** fifteen booleans; a build is clean when all E-severity predicates are true and the release branch additionally requires the W ones. The predicates are independent of the snapshot (a sidecar is checked once, not per phase, except O10's loop over `p`), so the validator is a pure function over the file and runs headless in GDD 3's `validate_scenes.gd` and at load in debug builds. **Example:** `v_office`: O01–O09 true; O10 at `p = 0`: 4,147 ≤ 20,000, `p = 1`: 18,133 ≤ 50,000 (stamp + telephone + folder + 8,000), `p = 2`: no key light (private) → 25,000 + 41,000 = 66,000 → 31,829 ≤ 150,000, `p = 3`: 0 ≤ 10,000, true; O11: `lamp`, `window_day`, `window_night` declared, true; O12 vacuous (authored); O13 `10 ∈ [8, 12]`; O14 `3 ≤ 12`, `3 ≤ 6`, lamp 600×400 ≤ 1024; O15 reports 2.1 MB. ### 9. Draw and byte estimates

The estimates this system adds to GDD 3's are defined as:

`draws_overlay = 2 + n_light_on + n_red_lit + n_ghosts` (grain and tint are the 2; `n_light_on = |{l : lit_light(l)}|` or, for an insert, `max` over the two tables; `n_red_lit = |stamps| + |props| + [key_light ≠ null]`; `n_ghosts = |ghosts_at_entry| + ghosts added in-scene`)

| `LIGHT_MAX`, `STAMP_MAX`, `GHOST_MAX` | int | 6, 12, 16 | constants (this GDD; T3 by pointer for the last two) | Declared ceilings |
| `LIGHT_LAYER_MAX_PX` | int | 1024 | constant (this GDD) | Side bound of any overlay layer (O14) |
| `DRAW_TARGET` | int | 150 | constant (art bible T3 by pointer) | Steady-state target; 500 absolute |
| `mipmapped(t)` | bool | plates and layers yes; grain tile and atlas no (art bible §8.4) | constant | |
| `bytes(atlas_share)` | int | ≤ 16 MB (2048² RGBA8, no mips) | asset data | The stamp atlas, shared with Paper UI's sheet stamps; counted once |
| `draws_overlay`, `bytes_overlay` | int | 0–36; 0–`bytes_overlay_max` | calculated | Observations into GDD 3's debug overlay and W13 sheet |

**Output Range:** `draws_overlay ≤ 36` at any plan; with GDD 3's other terms the total stays under 150 at build, so W14 remains a runtime observation. `bytes_overlay_max = 6 × 5.59 + 12 × 1.40 + 1.0 + 16.0 ≈ 67.4 MB`, which is why `LIGHT_MAX` and `STAMP_MAX` are ceilings a plate may not reach together: W13 (`SCENE_BUDGET_WARN` 224) decides, and a plate that declares six 1024² lights with twelve 512² props warns at the canonical scene (197.8 + 67.4 − 2.1 ≈ 263 > 256, an E). The realistic MVP block is far smaller. **Example:** `v_office` at reach 1: `draws_overlay = 2 + 1 (window_day) + 1 (stamp) + 0 = 4`; `bytes_overlay = lamp 600×400 (1.28 MB) + window_day 300×500 (0.80) + window_night (0.80) + grain 512² (1.05) + atlas share 1024² (4.19) = 8.1 MB`; `bytes_est = 16.8 + 8.1 + 181 = 205.9 < 224` ✓ (GDD 3's 197.8 becomes 205.9 and still passes). Insert `v_rybar_call` adds `threshold_telephone` 400×700 (1.49 MB) and loads both tables: 9.6 MB. *Rejected:* an Overlay-local byte ceiling (the ceiling is per scene, GDD 3 §7's rejection); loading both variants of every red anchor (doubles the anchor bytes for a setting that applies only at the next entry anyway).

## Edge Cases

*Each case states what happens, who logs it and what the player sees. "Logged" means through the injected `DebugSink` (a recording twin in tests, an assert in debug builds, a logger in release); "build fails" means `validate_scenes.gd` refuses the sidecar on an O-rule (Core Rule 3). This system never repairs data, never invents a layer, and when it must degrade it degrades toward **less red and fewer layers**, never more: a missing or refused anchor is hidden, a missing light stays dark, and nothing is substituted. The player, in every case below, sees a still room; only the ghost ever changes while they are in it.*

1. **If no snapshot has been received when a scene is entered (the act's first scene before any boundary; the first scene after a load, GDD 7 Rule 11, until the `view_now()` amendment is pushed through the sink):** `plan()` runs with `snapshot = null` → `phase 0`, `ink_reach 1`, `view = {}`, every `reach_for = 0`; the slot lighting is applied normally, no anchor is lit, `tint 0`, `red_est_ppm` is the HUD reserve alone; logged `SNAPSHOT_MISSING(scene_id)` once per scene; debug asserts only when a `reveal` has been received earlier in the run (a null after a non-null is a wiring bug, not a start). The player sees the room lit for its slot with no red on it — correct at the act's opening, where nobody has been looked at yet; wrong on resume for a room that showed red before the save, which is why GDD 7 gains `view_now()` and Scene Flow pushes it at `open_chapter(resumed)`. *Rationale:* a room must always have a plan (Core Rule 2); the one honest value for an unknown reach is 0, since Principle A forbids red on a person the state has not been shown looking at.

2. **If an anchor's `subject` names an id that is not in the snapshot's `per_character` (not a person record, or a person record without `attention`, GDD 7 Edge Case 14):** `reach_for = 0`, the anchor is unlit in this plan; logged `SUBJECT_UNKNOWN(scene_id, anchor, subject)` once per scene. At authoring the `tools/` cross-check X02 fails the build if the id is not a `kind: person` record carrying `attention` in the dossier schema, so the runtime case is a schema edit after export or a person whose record the compiler warned `PERSON_WITHOUT_ATTENTION` about. The player sees that one anchor unlit and nothing else different. *Rationale:* a guessed subject (falling back to the protagonist) would put red on a door for a reason the data does not state; unlit is the only reading that is never a lie.

3. **If a red anchor's rect overlaps a hotspot's `layer` bounds or `hit` region, the `hero.rect`, a `kind: person` hotspot, or the `figure_band` (a stamp or unbound prop: O05; a key light over the band or a person: O07):** the build fails naming the anchor and the object it overlaps; nothing ships. Should a hand-edited sidecar reach a release build, the structural rules pass, the anchor is **hidden** (never drawn at any reach), logged `ANCHOR_HIDDEN(scene_id, anchor, O05 | O07)`, and the plan's `findings` carries the code; the player sees a plate with one less red element. *Rationale:* GDD 3's "a stamp never sits on a hotspot's rect" and §4.2's "never on skin" are hard rules; a release that drew the anchor anyway would be measurably wrong in Gate B, and hiding is the only degradation that cannot add red.

4. **If a plate has no `prop` anchor and the room is entered at reach 2 (a private room with no telephone or folder the painter chose to redden; paranoid, the protagonist looked at):** the plan lights the tier-1 stamps only; nothing is logged, because a room with no prop to redden is a legal room (§6.3: private spaces "only red and carbon reach do" change, and a room may have no object the state would mark). The validator warns `NO_PROP_ANCHOR` only when `space: state` (a state room at paranoid with no red prop is an authoring smell: the state's own objects are where its attention lands first). The player sees the margin stamps and a room that otherwise looks confident, and the sheet's `half-struck` stamp says more than the room does — which is Pillar 1 ("you learn you are watched by reading paper"). *Rationale:* reach is a ceiling on what may be lit, not a quota of what must be; the budgets are maxima too.

5. **If a `space: state` plate has no `key_light` anchor and is entered at reach 3 (act two, panicking, the protagonist watched):** the plan lights stamps and props, `tint.tier = 3` still applies (the hard light is the phase's, carried by the full-frame layer), no key light is drawn; the validator warns `NO_KEY_LIGHT(scene_id)` at build for every `space: state` sidecar without one, so the omission is a known choice, not an accident; nothing is logged at runtime. The player sees a state room under hard light with red on its props and none in its light. *Rationale:* §2.2 says key light "in state spaces" at panicking; a warning at authoring is the right force, since a corridor plate may honestly have no key light to tint, while a silent omission at runtime would hide a painter's mistake.

6. **If a `key_light` layer covers more than half the frame (rect area > `FRAME_PX / 2`), or any overlay layer exceeds `LIGHT_LAYER_MAX_PX` on a side (O14):** the build fails naming the layer and its size; a 1024² cap already bounds a single layer at 50.6% of the frame, so the half-frame case is reachable only by a scrolling plate's wide layer, and O10 will in any case fail such a layer at `p = 2` (a half-frame red light exceeds 15% by itself). In release the layer is treated as missing (case 21). *Rationale:* T9 allows no full-frame layer but plate, grain and tint; the key light is light on a wall, not a grade.

7. **If a `layer_hatch` (or a stamp's `_h` atlas cell) contains a red-tolerance pixel (Gate A over overlay layers, O08), or a lighting layer does:** the build fails naming the file and the count; `red_px_hatch` must be 0 and a lighting layer's `red_px` must be 0. There is no runtime path: the field is in the sidecar and O08 is structural enough to run at load, where a release build hides the anchor (case 3) or leaves the light dark (case 21) and logs `RED_IN_REDFREE_LAYER(file, count)`. The player under `red_as_hatching` sees exactly no red, which is what they asked for. *Rationale:* `red_as_hatching` promises "no red at all" (GDD 4); a hatch variant with a red pixel breaks the promise at the one place the player cannot check.

8. **If `red_px` is missing on a red anchor, or stale after a re-export (the PNG changed, the count did not):** O09 fails the build in CI (the gate script's recount disagrees), naming the anchor, the stored and the recounted value; the compiler never fills the field itself (the count is the gate's, not the compiler's). At load, a missing `red_px` is a structural failure of the block: the anchor is hidden with `RED_PX_MISSING(anchor)` logged; a stale value cannot be detected at load (no pixel scan at runtime) and the plan's estimate is simply wrong by the difference, which Gate B's `proxy_agrees` catches in CI. The player sees nothing different in the stale case; one fewer red element in the missing case. *Rationale:* the headless proxy is only as honest as the number it is handed; the honesty lives in CI, where the recount is cheap, not at runtime, where a pixel scan would be a load-time cost for a build error.

9. **If `enter_scene` arrives with `scene_kind: insert` and the sidecar declares no `threshold_*` layer (O12 missed because the sidecar was hand-edited after the build):** the plan's `lighting_after_threshold` is the slot layers off and nothing on — the room goes dark at the second cut; logged `INSERT_NO_THRESHOLD(scene_id)` and debug asserts. The player sees the figure arrive on the line, then the room's light go out with no replacement: wrong, but it is "the floor goes" and not a lit room pretending nothing happened. *Rationale:* substituting the slot light for the missing threshold light would show the §2.1 reactive-event state as an ordinary room; darkness is the lesser lie, and the assert makes it a short-lived one.

10. **If an insert sidecar declares two `threshold_*` layers (a door and a telephone, O12):** the build fails naming both. At load in release, the plan takes the one whose hotspot the entry dialogue's `person` hotspot is bound to if Scene Flow's `set_focus(threshold)` names it (GDD 8 Rule 11 step 3), else the first in id order; the other stays off; logged `INSERT_TWO_THRESHOLDS(scene_id, chosen, other)`. The player sees one light from one threshold. *Rationale:* GDD 6 VA §1 — one person through one threshold; two lights would be two arrivals, which GDD 8 VA §6 forbids.

11. **If `ghost_requested(h)` arrives for a hotspot whose availability is `void` or `hidden`, or `unfiled`:** no ghost; `ghost()` returns `null`, `apply_ghost` is not called; logged `GHOST_REFUSED(scene_id, h, availability)`; debug asserts, because GDD 3 emits the signal only on the `filed` transition and any other source is a caller bug. The player sees nothing change. *Rationale:* a ghost says "the state has a copy"; `void` says "the state removed the paper" and `hidden` says it was never there; drawing a copy of either would be a false fact on the plate.

12. **If more ghosts are requested than the scene has hotspots (`|ghosts| > n_hotspots`), or more than `GHOST_MAX`:** the request is refused, logged `GHOST_OVERFLOW(scene_id, h, count, n_hotspots)` and debug asserts; the first bound is the real one (a ghost is one per hotspot, and `set_hotspot_state(filed)` on an already-filed hotspot is idempotent at GDD 3 and emits nothing), so the case is reachable only by a duplicate id or a replayed signal. The player sees the ghosts already drawn and no more. *Rationale:* T3's 16 is a draw budget; the hotspot count is the semantic bound, and a second ghost on one object would read as a second copy the dossier does not hold.

13. **If `ghost_requested(h)` arrives while the scene is `suspended` under a block (the player filed from an open document sheet; GDD 3 Rule 5 permits availability changes under a block):** the ghost is drawn on the next frame beneath the sheet, by cut, exactly as when unblocked; nothing is logged; the ghost stays when the sheet closes. The player, closing the sheet, finds the object already double-struck — the room was read while they were reading. *Rationale:* GDD 3 Rule 5 names the block as a legal moment for availability to change; the ghost follows availability, and deferring it to `pop_input_block` would make the room change on an unblock, which is not the player's act.

14. **If a setting this system reads changes mid-scene (`red_as_hatching` toggled on the back page during a room; `flicker` likewise):** the store emits `changed` (GDD 4 Rule 10); this system holds no subscription, the plan is not recomputed, the room is unchanged until the next `enter_scene`; nothing is logged. Paper UI's settings sheet shows the GDD 4 live-preview note for `red_as_hatching` as "applies to rooms on entering the next one" (a string Paper UI owns; parallel to GDD 4 Edge Case 19's `timer_mode` "at the next beat"). The player sees the sheet's own stamps change at once (Paper UI applies `red_style` live to the sheet it is drawing) and the room behind it change when they next walk into one. *Rationale:* Core Rule 2 — a plan is immutable so a tier cannot be seen to move; a settings change is not a tier change, but re-planning a visible room would be the same frame a reviewer flags.

15. **If `flicker` is `limited` and `can_count_flashes()` is false (the engine twin before the frame-timing spike; the MVP and, since act one never reaches panicking, every shipped act-one build):** `flicker_allowance = {allowed: false, max_hz: 0}`; the portrait is steadily lit; nothing is logged (the state is expected); the settings sheet labels `limited` with GDD 4's help text unchanged, because the store never knows (GDD 4 Edge Case 21: the store is unchanged). The player sees no flicker and loses nothing: the flicker is mood, never information. *Rationale:* `limited` promises a ceiling; without the gate driving toggles on real frame time the ceiling is unproven, and an unproven ceiling is `off`.

16. **If `toggle_ok` denies a scheduled toggle (Paper UI's portrait driver asks to swap 120 ms after the last accepted swap at `max_hz 2`):** the swap is dropped, not queued; the portrait keeps its current state until the next request that passes (≥ 250 ms after the last accepted); nothing is logged in release; the null host's test log records `TOGGLE_DENIED(now, last)` so a test can count denials. The player sees a flicker that is at most 2 Hz and occasionally skips a beat — a bulb, not a strobe. *Rationale:* a queued toggle would fire the instant the gate opens and bunch with the next, which is exactly the burst WCAG 2.3.1 counts; dropping is the only policy under which the gate's arithmetic holds.

17. **If `capture_red_share_ppm()` (Gate B) is called on `NullOverlayHost`:** it returns `null`; the Gate B job treats `null` as "capture unavailable", **skips the comparison and fails the job** with `GATE_B_NO_CAPTURE` unless the job is marked `allow_no_capture` (the headless-only CI lane, where the O10 proxy is the whole check); the unit suite asserts `null` from the null twin. No player sees this. *Rationale:* a null twin that returned 0 would pass every budget; a gate that cannot measure must say so, never pass by default (`.claude/docs/context-management.md`: helpers emit observations, never verdicts — and a silent zero is a verdict).

18. **If the proxy and Gate B's capture disagree by more than `RED_PROXY_TOLERANCE_PP` (1 pp) with the capture above the proxy:** the CI job fails `RED_PROXY_DISAGREES(scene_id, phase, est_ppm, capture_ppm)`; the suspects, in order, are the tint carrying hue (Core Rule 5's Gate B sub-check also fails if any of it lands in the figure band), a sheet or the HUD open in the capture beyond the reserve, or a stale `red_px` (case 8). Capture below the proxy by any amount passes (overlaps and occlusion only lower the truth). No player sees this. *Rationale:* the proxy is one-sided by construction; the only way the truth exceeds it is a red source the proxy was never told about, and that is always a finding.

19. **If a plan's `red_est_ppm` exceeds `red_budget_ppm` (a budget breach at panicking: a key light plus every prop plus the floods reserve on a plate O10 should have refused; reachable only by a sidecar edited after the build):** in debug, `RED_BUDGET(scene_id, phase, est, budget)` is raised through the sink and asserts; the plan is applied as computed so the artist sees the breach. In release, the core hides lit anchors in order of descending `red_px` **until the estimate is within budget**, logs `RED_BUDGET_DEGRADED(scene_id, phase, hidden[])`, and applies the reduced plan; it never adds, swaps or shrinks a layer and never touches the tint. The player sees a room with less red than its painter intended; never more. *Rationale:* the budget is Principle A's measurable form and a release must honour it; the one degradation that cannot create a new defect is removal, and removing the largest first is the shortest path back under the line.

20. **If the snapshot carries a `stamp_tier(x) > ink_reach` for any `x` (the invariant GDD 7 Formulas §4 proves and this system asserts):** the plan is computed from `red_reach` as received (the plate uses `red_reach`, not `stamp_tier`; the two are equal by GDD 7 §4, so the plate is unaffected), `STAMP_TIER_ABOVE_INK(beat, x, stamp_tier, ink_reach)` is raised through the sink, debug asserts, release logs once per beat; Paper UI's sheet stamp, which does read `stamp_tier`, is Paper UI's to clamp **(PROV)** — this system recommends `min(stamp_tier, ink_reach)` there. The player sees a correct plate and, until Paper UI clamps, a sheet stamp one tier too full. *Rationale:* `min(a, c) ≤ c` cannot fail unless the Calendar's code and its formula have parted; the assert exists to name that day, not to repair it.

21. **If the host fails to load a lighting layer or an anchor layer at `load(plan)` (file missing at runtime, import failure, out of memory):** `load` returns `{ok: false, errors: [{layer, reason}]}`; the plan is applied with the failed layer treated as absent — a missing light stays dark, a missing anchor is unlit, a missing `layer_hatch` under `red_as_hatching` leaves the anchor unlit **rather than falling back to the `fill` variant**; logged `LAYER_LOAD_FAILED(scene_id, layer, reason)`; the scene still reaches `scene_ready` (this system never blocks GDD 3's lifecycle). The player sees a darker or less red room. *Rationale:* the fallback that would "look right" (fill for hatch) breaks the one promise the setting makes; the fallback that is merely dimmer breaks nothing the art bible names.

22. **If the plan is requested while the scene is `applied` or `relit` (a second `scene_preloaded` for the same id; a caller trying to re-plan on a settings change):** refused, `PLAN_LOCKED(scene_id)` logged, debug asserts; the existing plan stands; the host receives no call. The player sees nothing. *Rationale:* Core Rule 2's immutability is what makes "never mid-scene" checkable by one assert instead of by every consumer promising to behave.

23. **If a `sodium_<n>` layer's `lit_phases` omits the current phase (`[0, 1, 2]` at phase 3, the intended Collapsing darkening; or `[1, 2, 3]` at phase 0, a pole dark in a confident week):** the pole is dark; nothing is logged in either case, because both are authoring, not errors; the validator warns `SODIUM_NEVER_LIT` only when `lit_phases = []` and `SODIUM_DARK_AT_CONFIDENT` when `0 ∉ lit_phases` (a street dark before the state begins to withdraw is usually a typo). The player sees one fewer cone of light on the street. *Rationale:* "sodium poles dark one by one" (§2.2) is the painter's sequence across the act's streets; the data can say it and the warning only asks whether it was meant.

24. **If the entered "scene" is the act-close Calendar-transition screen, a chapter card, or any paper screen with no plate (GDD 8 Rules 2, 12; the `<act>_close` pseudo-chapter):** no `enter_scene` is emitted for it (Scene Flow calls `preload_scene` for no plate, and `enter_scene` precedes `preload_scene`), so no plan exists, nothing is drawn by this system, and `reveal`s received there update the stored snapshot as usual; nothing is logged. The screen's own red — the struck date, the pinned notices' stamps — is Paper UI's in `red_style`. The player sees paper. *Rationale:* this system draws on plates; a screen with no plate has no reach to show, and the act-close beats have no protagonist for a reach to belong to (GDD 8 VA §2: no stamp on those sheets, "no person is on that page for the state to be reading").

25. **If `enter_scene` arrives for a scene whose sidecar has no `overlay` block at all (a scene authored before this GDD; a sidecar from an older schema):** at build O01 fails. At load in release, the plan is the **bare plan**: no anchors, no lighting layers, grain at `GRAIN_PLATE_MIN_PCT`, `tint 0`, `red_est_ppm` = the HUD reserve; logged `OVERLAY_BLOCK_MISSING(scene_id)` once; the plate shows as painted, flat, with no slot light. The player sees a room with no lamp pool and no red — legible, dull, and obviously unfinished to a reviewer. *Rationale:* a plate with no block is a plate; refusing to show it would make this system able to block GDD 3's lifecycle, which Core Rule 1 forbids, and inventing a lamp would be the one substitution the degradation rule bans.

26. **If the two cuts of an insert would land on the same frame (`THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` set to 0, or a frame longer than the delay after a hitch):** the host enforces a minimum of one rendered frame between `apply` and `apply_after_threshold` regardless of the knob: the second cut is deferred to the frame after the first has been presented; logged `THRESHOLD_CUT_SAME_FRAME(scene_id)` once when the deferral was needed; the knob's safe range starts at 200 ms so the case is a hitch, not a setting. The player sees the figure, then the light — always in that order, never together. *Rationale:* GDD 8 VA §3's reviewer test is stated per frame ("if both land together … it is a defect"); a rule stated per frame must be enforced per frame, not per millisecond.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Written because this system's output is every red pixel on a plate and the light it sits in; drafted with the art director from art bible §2.1–2.2, §4.1–4.5, §6.2, §6.4, §7.4–7.5 and §8.7. Everything below is a constraint on what the plan may select and how the host may draw it, not a new visual: red reaches a plate only through authored anchors (Core Rule 3), light only through authored red-free crops (Rule 7), and nothing moves (Rule 14). Six art rulings this needed are stated where they bite: the flat ghost, the sourceless plate, the portrait-only flicker, **the key light in place of any tint** (the art director's extension of the main session's ruling: no full-frame layer at any phase, hue or no hue; `tint` leaves the plan and the key light's red is painted into its layer, never mixed at runtime, so it is counted at export), §2.2's other axes as plate authoring, and sodium by `lit_phases`. The art-bible amendments they imply (B1–B9) are proposals in Open Questions, not applied.*

**1. Four reaches, four looks, one per space, and every red is a bordered shape before it is a colour.** `reach 0`: no red-tolerance pixel on the plate; an unlit stamp anchor is a bare paper edge, an unlit prop anchor is the hotspot's own `layer` (the black telephone), an unlit key light is the room's slot light alone. This is every unlooked-at protagonist at every phase and everyone at Collapsing (§2.2 "withdraws"; the unanswered stamp on the sheet is Paper UI's **(PROV)**, GDD 7 VA §2). `reach 1`, **the margin stamp on paper**: one atlas impression, square or round, bordered, hatched at one angle in its shadow step (§4.5), on a state surface only: the edge of a state document on the desk, an official notice, the door frame of a state building, the seal on an envelope; never on a person, on people's paper (§9.3), on a hotspot `rect` (O05) or as decoration (§4.2). In a private room it is the state's paper that came in (the summons on the table); in a state space it is the room's own paper; in the street it is the notice on the wall. `reach 2`, **the prop variant**: §2.2's red telephone, folder, pen as a `layer` swap at equal bounds (O04), the object repainted in §6.2's three steps as *red field, red hatched shadow step, soot border*, so the hatching is the shadow and the border is the edge and the shape still reads with the hue covered; a prop is a state object (the line the state rings on, the folder the state opened), never a personal effect. `reach 3`, **the key light**: state spaces and inserts only (O07), a bordered geometric light region (the door's rectangle on the floor, the bulb's cone, the window's rectangle) in at most two red values with a hatched edge, red painted into the layer and counted at export (O09), never mixed at runtime; kept off `figure_band` and every person rect by authoring it as light the figure stands in the path of: backlit, casting the hard shadow, carrying none of the colour (§4.2; GDD 8 VA §3). State spaces: the overhead key, frontal and hard, grey-green walls going cold-red toward the pool's edge. Private spaces: a key light only with the state on the threshold (an insert). Street: no key light at any reach; the street's Panicking "hard" is plate authoring (§4). Reach is cumulative (Formulas §2): at 2 the stamps stay under the props, at 3 both stay under the light. *Reviewer test:* at each reach, hue covered, every lit anchor is still countable as a bordered shape, and the lower tiers are still present.

**2. The carbon ghost is a flat carbon silhouette, not a tinted copy, and it sits under the object.** On `filed` the host draws the hotspot's `layer_filed` crop as one colour, world carbon `#434C7C`, the crop's alpha as the shape, no keyline, no internal values, no transparency, offset `GHOST_OFFSET_PX` 3 px right and 3 px down at 1080 (2 px at 720; §6.4's 2–3 px; whole pixels, T6), directly beneath that hotspot's layer in GDD 3's order, so it shows on the object's right and lower edges and the object's own edge stays hard (§6.2). It appears on one frame with no fade, never moves, never answers a tier, and lies still when its object lifts on focus (GDD 3 VA 7c: the gap is the focus read). A tinted duplicate is refused: carbon over an ochre or bone midtone makes a muddy violet that is none of §4.1's seven colours, and a duplicate of `layer` carries the keyline in blue and says "a fact is here" after the fact has gone; a carbon copy is a one-ink impression with no tonal range, and so is this. Colourblind backup (§4.5 tritan row): on the plate, two shape changes, the keyline gone and the object doubled; the "cc" mark is the sheet's, Paper UI's **(PROV)**. *Reviewer test:* every filed hotspot, hue covered, reads as a doubled shape without a keyline; every unfiled one as a single keylined shape; T7's carbon count on the composite equals the ghost silhouettes plus the declared carbon-paper mask and nothing else.

**3. One grain, on top of everything in plate space, never moving.** The §6.2 bone grain is one tileable texture (`grain.tile`, 512², no feature under 3 px, mipmaps off) at `opacity_pct` 8–12 (O13), fixed to the frame, drawn above the plate, the lighting crops, the anchors, the ghosts, the hotspot layers, the figures and the stamps, and below every sheet (which carries its own ≤ 6% UI grain, §7.2; UI never takes plate light, §4.4): the room, its light, the state's red and the carbon copy are printed on one sheet. The grain is static: no per-frame reseed, no scroll, no shimmer; a moving grain is a pulse (§7.4) and a flicker no one toggled (Rule 10). On a scrolling plate the grain stays with the frame, not the plate: the frame is the paper. *Reviewer test:* two frames of an idle room ten minutes apart, outside a running timer, are pixel-identical.

**4. The plate is sourceless; the slot is a pane and a pool.** Every interior plate is painted with no light source in it, walls, floor and furniture in §4.1's 20–60% midtone band at §6.2's three steps, the window an empty aperture, no pool, no cast shadow, and the slot is two red-free crops the plan switches (Rule 7): the **pane** (`window_day` bone `#D8CDB2` at plate value, flat and overcast, never sun or sky; `window_night` soot `#1B1917`) and the **pool** (`lamp` in private and print-shop spaces: amber `#DDB065`, low side angle, over the working surface, its falloff the only gradient on the plate, bone reaching full value only inside it, §4.1; `overhead` in state spaces: cool, frontal, even, grey-green walls at their flattest). `morning` pane bone, pool on; `day` pane bone, pool off, the plate at its most even; `night` pane soot, pool on, the pool the mood carrier (§2.1 Investigation; GDD 7 VA §5). Nothing darkens a room at night: no full-frame layer but plate and grain (T9), no grade (GDD 6 VA §2); night is read from the soot pane and the pool's contrast against an unchanged room. Street plates: the sourceless street is overcast wet concrete `#585A57`, nothing lit by day (§4.3); at `night`, `sodium_<n>` cones from poles onto the ground in sodium `#D4862A` and `amber_windows` rectangles on walls in amber (§4.5: cone from a pole is the state, rectangle on a wall is private, no exceptions). A pole is lit when `phase ∈ lit_phases`, the list a prefix of `[0,1,2,3]`; Collapsing's "dark one by one" (§2.2) is the sequence of the act's street plates, each pole authored, none extinguished while the player stands there; a Collapsing-expected street plate is painted with soot ≥ 40% of frame so a dark pole uncovers darkness, not concrete (T7). §2.2's other two axes, paper order and light evenness (density, misregistration, leaning stacks, shredder strips, lamps lowered, hard light), are **plate authoring for the phase the scene is expected in** (`expected_phase` in the sidecar), one plate per scene; Overlay varies the red tiers and the sodium and nothing else (Rule 14). *Reviewer test:* hide the pane and the pool: the plate must not tell you the hour; show them: no pixel outside their crops has changed. *Open with GDD 3:* a hotspot whose `layer` lies inside the pool is painted sourceless and reads unlit at night; the remedy (a lit variant at equal bounds, or pools authored to keep hotspots in their outer step) is GDD 3's and the technical artist's (Open Questions).

**5. The threshold light is two cuts, and the figure is in the room before its light is.** Frame one of an insert (`apply`): the room as the player left it, the inherited slot's pane and pool, the ghosts, the anchors at the inherited reach, and the figure on the threshold line (Character Presentation **(PROV)**; GDD 8 VA §3). `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later (`apply_after_threshold`, one frame, never a crossfade, never shortened by `motion: reduced`, because it is a beat and not a tween): pane and pool off, `threshold_<id>` on; the state's light replaces the room's (§2.1 Reactive event). Three recipes, one per threshold kind: the **torch through the door**, a hard-edged cone of full-value bone from the door frame across the floor; **sodium through the window**, a sodium cone on floor and wall from the window's rectangle; the **bare bulb over the telephone**, a tight full-value circle on table and cord, the rest of the room at its sourceless value. Never amber: amber is private light and the state has just entered. The highest contrast in the game; the hard shadow is the figure's, painted into the layer from the figure's authored standing point. Red crosses the threshold line at the inherited reach exactly as in any room (Rule 9): the stamp on the door frame at 1, the red telephone at 2, and at 3 the key-light anchor lit over the threshold layer as the bordered, hatched red field inside the light's own region, the state's light turned the state's colour, the figure still backlit and red-free. *Reviewer test (GDD 8's, restated):* frame one shows the figure in the old light; no frame shows the new light without the figure; no frame shows both lights.

**6. `red_as_hatching` is the atlas's other column, not a filter.** Under the toggle every lit anchor renders its hatch twin at identical bounds (O04): a stamp's hatch cell, the same border and geometry, hatched in soot at one angle, no fill; a prop's `layer_hatch`, the object at its unlit values with its soot border and hatched shadow step kept; the key light's `layer_hatch`, the same region as value-only hard light with a hatched border. Every twin carries `red_px = 0` (O08), so with the toggle on the composited frame holds no red-tolerance pixel at any phase: that is the test, not a budget. Shape carries the whole meaning (§4.5): a hatched stamp is still a stamp because it is bordered and geometric, and it is never confusable with the people's paper, which is torn and misregistered and never bordered (§9.3). No hatched vignette, no hatched frame, no tint of any kind (GDD 6 VA §2). The sheet's red (surveillance stamp, date stamp, timer fill) takes the same style from the same atlas, drawn by Paper UI **(PROV)** (Rule 12), and the four surveillance states must still sort by hatch density alone: outline at 40% soot, hatched, lower half cross-hatched, full cross-hatch.

**7. What must never happen, and how a reviewer checks.** No full-frame layer but plate and grain (T9): no tint, grade, vignette, darkening or warming of the frame at any phase, slot or reach; `tint` is not in the plan. No red on a figure, a person hotspot, inside `figure_band`, on skin, clothing or people's paper (§4.2), at any reach including 3. No red that is not a bordered geometric shape with hatching (§4.5): no wash, no red falloff, no soft edge, no fill without its border. No procedural colour on the plate but the ghost's flat carbon. No change in an idle room: no flicker on any lighting layer, prop, stamp or ghost; no pole going out; no lamp lowering; no grain motion; the only flicker in the game is Paper UI's interrogation portrait under this system's allowance (Rule 10), a cut between the portrait's lit value and one shading step down, never to soot, never above `FLASH_MAX_PER_S`, and off while the cap is unmeasured. No tier change seen: a plate enters already at its reach by GDD 3's wipe. No meter, bar, numeral or banner. No plate per week; no plate that carries its own red (Gate A). *Reviewer tests:* (a) hue covered, every red element still counts as a shape; (b) `red_as_hatching` on, Gate B red = 0 at every phase; (c) Gate B per phase ≤ 2/5/15/1%, red inside every `figure_band` and person rect = 0, Collapsing-expected plates soot ≥ 40%; (d) two idle frames ten minutes apart pixel-identical; (e) pane and pool stripped, the plate is hourless; (f) an insert's three frames, room, room with figure, figure with the new light, in that order and never a fourth; (g) a filed room, hue covered, reads as doubled shapes without keylines.

**8. Audio.** This system plays nothing and names no cue. The sounds that sit in these frames are owned elsewhere and keyed on the same tiers: the reactive event's arrival (GDD 6), the surveillance stamp and the date strike (GDD 7 VA §7), the wipe and the slot's ambience (GDD 8; Audio 16 **(PROV)**). The visual mood they should match: a room gone quiet rather than loud; hard light, one sheet of grain, a copy where a paper was.

## Game Feel

Optional for a Core system; three feel properties follow from it and are stated so Paper UI, Character Presentation and the art pipeline inherit them as targets.

- **Red is read from the edge in.** A room's attention is legible in the first second: margin, props, light, or nothing. No element pulses, breathes or creeps; a tier lands with the room, by a cut, and stays.
- **A read room looks read.** The carbon ghost appears the instant a hotspot turns `filed`, by a cut, two pixels off true at 720 and three at 1080, and never moves again; the player's own hand is the only thing that changes a room while they are in it.
- **The state arrives in two cuts.** The figure on the threshold line first, then the light replaced `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later; never a crossfade, never a dimming, never a sting from this system.

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen and draws nothing in sheet or HUD space. The date stamp, the surveillance stamp and the interrogation timer fill are Paper UI's, drawn from the Calendar's tiers and Dialogue's fraction in this system's `red_style` and stamp atlas; the settings that reach this system (`flicker`, `red_as_hatching`) are Paper UI's settings sheet (GDD 4). Debug builds only: an inspector that prints the current `OverlayPlan`, the red estimate against the phase budget, the ghost list and the flicker allowance, and a `capture_for_gate_b()` trigger that writes the composited capture and its measured red share to the evidence folder; both reachable through the debug menu and absent from release (the same rule as GDDs 2, 6, 7 and 8's debug surfaces).

> **📌 UX Flag — Overlay & Surveillance Rendering**: no player-facing UI of its own. `design/accessibility-requirements.md` must state the three facts this system guarantees: no attention or phase signal is carried by colour alone (every red is a bordered shape with hatching, and `red_as_hatching` removes the colour entirely); no element flickers above `FLASH_MAX_PER_S`, and `flicker: limited` behaves as `off` until the cap is measured; and nothing on a plate changes state while the player is idle.
