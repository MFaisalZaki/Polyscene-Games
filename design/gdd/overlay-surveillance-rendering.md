## Overview

*Framing: infrastructure first (Category `Core`, Layer Foundation), with its player-facing effect named because this is the one Core system whose whole output is pixels. No ADR cites it yet; ADR-5 (the renderer) will be built from it.*

Overlay & Surveillance Rendering is the system that puts the state's attention on the screen. It never computes a tier and never reads a file: it takes the Calendar's per-boundary snapshot (the regime phase, the red reach per character, the ink reach, the stamp tier) and the scene's authored overlay data, and from them builds a plan of what to draw above the painted plate, the four layers of the art bible's overlay stack: grain on everything; the carbon ghost that makes a filed object look read; the stamp instances placed where the phase's reach allows; the red that climbs from the paper margin to the props to the state's key light and withdraws when the state collapses. It also owns the lighting switches a plate has (the lamp, the window, the threshold light when the state arrives), the Panicking flicker under the accessibility cap, and the rendering of the interrogation timer's margin fill, the one continuous motion in the game. The player never operates it; they read it: a room that is a little redder than the last one, a telephone that has turned red since this morning, a drawer that has gone carbon blue because they read what was in it. Without it Principle A ("attention has a colour") would have no pixels, Pillar 5 would have no pressure the player could see, and every other system's tiers would be numbers nobody rendered. Its logic is headless (a plan, plain data, byte-equal across hosts); its drawing lives behind a host seam with a null twin, like the Scene & Interaction Framework's, so the simulator and the unit tests run without a frame. It reads three settings (`flicker`, `motion`, `red_as_hatching`) and changes how the room is shown, never what is in it.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees a meter, a counter or a gradient of attention; they read it off the frame the way they read a page. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "attention has a colour" chosen, art bible §1.2 Principle A, as GDD 7 left it to this system; "a read room looks read" kept as the anchor moment's last beat, from §6.4; "the room knows what the file knows" rejected, because this system never reads the file and the room never reacts within a scene).*

**Attention has a colour.** This system serves two feelings that share one room. The first is being looked at without being told. Stamp red belongs to the state alone. It sits on the margin when the state is merely keeping records, reaches the telephone when it starts counting, and stains the light itself when it panics. Sodium is the state's light and the desk lamp is yours, and the player learns to tell them apart without being taught. The second feeling is leaving a mark. When you file something, the room keeps a carbon of it: the thing you filed carries a blue double strike, so a room you have read looks read. Red is the state reading you. Blue is what you have handed it.

The anchor moment: Vera's office, Friday 6 October, the day slot. Bone window, the lamp lit, one red stamp on the cover sheet of report 4471 in the margin of the frame, and nothing else red in the room. She files the ledger line, and the ledger's ghost appears beside it by a cut, blue and two pixels off true. In week three it is the same office at night. The window is soot, the lamp pool is lower, and the telephone and one folder on the desk are red. Nobody has said the state is paranoid. Then Rybar comes. The lamp does not dim, it is replaced: a torch through the door, a hard shadow from the threshold, a stamp on the door frame. By week five the overhead light in a portrait is red and flickers. In week six the red goes, the sodium poles go dark one by one, and the player finds this is the worst room of all. Nothing in any of these rooms counted down. The player learned to read the room the way Vera reads a file: from the edge in.

Pillar alignment. Pillar 5, "pressure comes from attention, not reflexes": the tension is how far the red has reached, and the room never makes the player hurry. The only red that moves is the interrogation timer filling the page margin, in five ticks and with no numerals, and it exists only where the pillar allows a clock. Pillar 2, "the state is a character": its moods are four kinds of light, tidy, counting, noise and ash, and it always arrives the same way: through a threshold, replacing your light with its own, never as a flash, a sting or a vignette. Pillar 4, "consequences cross lives": attention is tracked per character, so red in Jan's room can be the reach of something Vera filed. Pillar 3, "no one is clean": red is never drawn on a person, their clothing or their paper. Colour here says who is watching, never who is guilty.

**The one rule this system must obey: the plate shows the state's attention as it stood when the player walked in, in one of four steps and never on a person, and nothing on it changes state while the player reads except by the player's own act.** Red reach comes only from the tiers handed over at the boundary. The system never computes attention, never infers it from the file, and never draws a gradient the player could watch creep. A tier change lands with the next room, by a cut; a slot change is a cut; the state's arrival is a cut. Within a scene, the carbon ghost is the only thing that appears, and it appears because the player filed something. Two things move and neither is a change of state: the Panicking flicker was in the room when the player entered, at 3 Hz or less, or not at all if they asked for none; and the timer is Dialogue's clock in the margin, under interrogation only. And red never carries meaning by colour alone: every stamp and fill is a bordered shape with hatching, so a player who turns red into hatching loses the colour and keeps the lesson.

*Cost accepted:* the room cannot answer the state's interest while the player is in it. A filing that will draw the state's eye shows only its own ghost until the door, and the red arrives one room late. Attention is felt in four coarse steps, so a room looks exactly as calm one filing before it turns as it did a week earlier. Every red the player sees is authored: each room needs its margin, prop and key-light layers and its threshold light drawn by hand, and a room built without a red prop can show the Paranoid phase only in its margin. The Collapsing phase withdraws the red, and some players will read the empty room as safety; soot, loose paper and dark sodium have to carry the dread the colour no longer does. Players who turn flicker off lose the Panicking noise in the light, so the state's own misregistered paper has to carry it alone.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary), the technical artist (render constraints) and the engine programmer (engine integration); rulings where they differed are stated in place. Engine facts are quoted only from the pinned reference (`modules/rendering.md`: 4.7 `DrawableTexture2D` exists, `CanvasItem` lines draw without a feather, `LinearToSRGB` no longer clamps; nothing on `CanvasLayer` order, `CanvasModulate`, blend modes, screen texture, uniforms or read-back is sourced, and the stack is designed so that only sibling draw order must be proven). Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a GDD not yet written (Paper UI 13, Dialogue 11, Character Presentation 10, Audio 16). Four rulings by the main session on the specialists' options: Paper UI draws the interrogation timer fill from Overlay's style; tier-3 red is carried by the authored key light with a red-free figure band, never a frame tint; an anchor may carry an authored `subject`; the only flicker in the game is the interrogation portrait's. The amendments to GDDs 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 this needed are applied there and named where they bite; the art-bible amendments are proposals in Open Questions.*

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary); the technical artist's T4 spike list and the art director's §4.2/§4.5 rules are treated as locked and cited by section. Where this draft departs from the brief's working proposals the departure is stated in place with the alternative it displaced.*

**0. Vocabulary.** A *tier* is a small integer from a closed set (GDD 7 Rule 0); this system receives tiers and produces pixels, and no float crosses its boundary in either direction. The *snapshot* is GDD 7's `BoundaryView`, received only through Scene Flow's `PresentationSink.reveal` (GDD 8 Rule 13). The *reach* of a scene is one tier, `red_reach(protagonist)` from the snapshot, that selects which authored red is lit (Rule 4). An *anchor* is an authored red-carrying layer in the scene sidecar's `overlay` block: a *stamp* (tier 1, an atlas instance on a document edge, a door frame, a notice), a *prop* (tier 2, the tinted variant of an object: the red telephone, folder, pen) or the *key light* (tier 3, the state's hard light tinted red). A *lighting layer* is an authored, cropped, red-free layer switched on or off by the plan: `lamp`, `window_day`, `window_night`, `overhead`, `threshold_<hotspot>`, `sodium_<n>`, `amber_windows`. The *plan* (`OverlayPlan`) is the plain-data result of one computation at scene entry: every layer's state, every stamp's placement and variant, the tint tier, the grain, the flicker allowance and the red estimate. A *ghost* is the carbon double-strike on a filed hotspot (art bible §6.4). The *host* is the thin seam through which the plan becomes pixels; its *null twin* records. A *cut* is a change that completes on one frame with no intermediate value.

**1. Overlay is a renderer of tiers: a headless core computes a plan, an engine host applies it, and the two never share a type the simulator cannot load.** `OverlayCore extends RefCounted`, constructed with nothing but a `DebugSink`, exposes pure functions: `plan(input) → OverlayPlan`, `ghost(plan, hotspot_id) → GhostOp`, `flicker_allowance(flicker, enforceable) → {allowed, max_hz}`, `red_style(red_as_hatching) → {variant: fill | hatch}`, `red_estimate_ppm(...)`, and the validator `OverlayDataValidator.check(sidecar) → findings[]` (Rule 3). Its `input` is plain data assembled by the engine `PresentationSink` fan-out (GDD 8 Rule 13): `{sidecar.overlay, snapshot | null, protagonist_id, scene_kind ∈ {authored, insert}, slot, settings: {flicker, red_as_hatching}, host_caps: {can_count_flashes}}`. `OverlayHost extends RefCounted` (abstract; `@abstract` sourced for 4.5, breaking-changes.md line 69, syntax verified in the spike) has two implementations: `EngineOverlayHost` holds the layer nodes under the scene root GDD 3 provides and draws the T4 stack; `NullOverlayHost` records every call in order into a dictionary tests and the story simulator read, and returns the capability flags a test sets. Calls: `load(plan) → {ok, errors[]}` (layers resident, nothing visible; called at `scene_preloaded`), `apply(plan)` (at `scene_ready`, a cut), `apply_after_threshold(plan)` (inserts only, Rule 9), `apply_ghost(op)` (Rule 8), `clear(scene_id)`, `can_count_flashes() → bool`, `capture_red_share_ppm() → int | null` (debug and CI only, Gate B, Rule 6; **NOT SOURCEABLE**, the frame-capture spike), `report_resident_bytes` (into GDD 3's W13 sheet). The CI path check extends to `src/overlay/`: `src/overlay/core/*` imports nothing from the scene tree or `src/overlay/engine/*`; `tools/sim/` and `tests/unit/overlay/` bind `NullOverlayHost`. The `OverlayHost` contract suite runs against both twins. *Rationale:* the one system that draws is still mostly decisions (which tier → which layer; is the budget met; may this flicker), and every decision must be replayable by the simulator with no renderer, exactly as GDD 3's `SceneHost` and GDD 8's `PresentationSink` are. *Rejected:* a scene-tree `Node` as the system's root with the logic in `_ready` (one import and the simulator cannot load it; GDD 3 Rule 8's argument); the core calling the Calendar for the snapshot (GDD 7 Rule 9 forbids every consumer that pull; the snapshot is pushed through the sink).

**2. The plan is plain data, computed once per scene entry, immutable for the life of the scene, and byte-equal across hosts.** Shape (keys sorted at serialisation; no float anywhere, fractions are integer parts-per-million, opacities integer percent):

```yaml
OverlayPlan:
  schema_version: 1
  scene_id: a1_vera/v_office
  scene_kind: authored                  # authored | insert
  phase: 0                              # snapshot.phase, 0–3
  reach: 1                              # red_reach(protagonist) — the plan's tier (Rule 4)
  ink_reach: 1                          # PHASE_RED_CAP[phase]; read for the assert only
  slot: day                             # morning | day | night
  lighting:                             # every declared lighting layer, by id (Rule 7)
    lamp: off
    window_day: on
    window_night: off
    threshold_telephone: off
  lighting_after_threshold: null        # inserts only (Rule 9): the second cut's table
  stamps:                               # tier-1 anchors lit at this reach; ≤ STAMP_MAX 12
    - { anchor: stamp_door_01, atlas: stamp_square_official, variant: fill, rect: [1612, 118, 96, 96] }
  props: []                             # tier-2 anchors lit; each {anchor, hotspot | null, variant, rect}
  key_light: null                       # tier-3 anchor lit, or null; {anchor, variant, rect}
  tint: { tier: 0, hatch: false }       # the full-frame phase layer's uniform (Rule 5)
  grain: { tile: grain_bone_01, opacity_pct: 10 }
  flicker: { allowed: false, max_hz: 0 }   # the allowance Paper UI's portrait obeys (Rule 10)
  red_est_ppm: 4147                     # Formulas F3; the headless proxy
  red_budget_ppm: 20000                 # PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM[phase]
  ghosts_at_entry: [ledger]             # hotspots already filed when the room is entered
  findings: []                          # DebugSink codes raised while planning (empty on a clean plan)
```

The plan is computed at `scene_preloaded` (so `load` can bring the selected layers in before `scene_ready`) from the latest snapshot received at `reveal` and the `enter_scene` context (Rule 11), and is **never recomputed while the scene is `active` or `suspended`**: not on a settings change (a `red_as_hatching` or `flicker` change applies at the next scene entry and the sheet says so, mirroring GDD 4 Edge Case 19's "at the next beat"), not on a `changed` signal, not on a clock. The one in-scene change is the ghost (Rule 8), which is a separate `GhostOp`, not a plan mutation. *Rationale:* GDD 7 Rule 9 makes every tier a per-boundary value; a plan that could change mid-scene would be a place for a tier to move inside a room, and GDD 8 VA §6 forbids any room change while the player is idle. Byte-equality is what lets the simulator diff a `NullOverlayHost` log against the engine's, the same guarantee Scene Flow gives with `BoundaryRecord`. *Rejected:* live-applying `red_as_hatching` mid-scene (the plan would have two versions in one room, and the budget assert would need re-running against a frame the player is looking at); a `Resource` subclass for the plan (engine serialisation drift; GDD 1's ADR-4 principle); floats for the estimate (float formatting is not byte-stable across platforms, and tiers are never floats).

**3. Red reach on a plate is authored, never computed; the sidecar's `overlay` block declares every red pixel the room can show, and the validator makes the art bible's rules asserts.** GDD 3's `scene.json` gains one block (GDD 3 Rule 1 amendment; written by the art export, normalised by the compiler, never hand-edited):

```yaml
overlay:
  schema_version: 1
  space: private                        # state | private | street | print_shop (art bible §6.3 areas)
  grain: { tile: grain_bone_01, opacity_pct: 10 }        # 8–12 on plates (T4); UI grain is Paper UI's
  red_anchors:
    - { id: stamp_door_01, kind: stamp, tier: 1, atlas: stamp_square_official, rect: [1612, 118, 96, 96], red_px: 4600, red_px_hatch: 0 }
    - { id: prop_telephone, kind: prop, tier: 2, hotspot: telephone, layer: { png: v_office_prop_telephone.png, x: 1210, y: 599 }, layer_hatch: { png: v_office_prop_telephone_h.png, x: 1210, y: 599 }, red_px: 11000, red_px_hatch: 0 }
    - { id: key_light, kind: key_light, tier: 3, layer: {...}, layer_hatch: {...}, red_px: 180000, red_px_hatch: 0 }   # state space or insert only
  lighting:
    - { id: lamp, layer: { png: v_office_lamp.png, x: 880, y: 300 } }
    - { id: window_day, layer: {...} }
    - { id: window_night, layer: {...} }
    - { id: threshold_telephone, hotspot: telephone, layer: {...} }     # inserts: exactly one
    - { id: sodium_01, layer: {...}, lit_phases: [0, 1, 2] }           # street only; dark from the phase named
```

`red_px` is the count of pixels inside T7's red tolerance (hue 348°–18°, S ≥ 0.55, V ≥ 0.35) in that layer, written by the T7 gate script in `tools/asset-pipeline/` at export; the validator requires the field and CI re-verifies it. **4. Whose reach a room uses: the chapter protagonist's, per anchor overridable by an authored `subject`, resolved from the snapshot alone.** `reach_for(anchor) = snapshot.per_character[anchor.subject ?? protagonist_id].red_reach` (Formulas F1), and an anchor is lit when `anchor.tier ≤ reach_for(anchor)` (F2: reach is cumulative; at tier 2 the margin stamps stay and the props join them, per §2.2's margin → props → key light). The default is the chapter protagonist: the room is theirs and §4.2 says red on a thing means the state is reading *it* now, which in a protagonist's room means reading them. An anchor may carry `subject: <person_id>` (a dossier person record id; cross-checked in `tools/` only, X02, against the content vocabulary, since the sidecar validator cannot see the dossier schema) so that Marta's door in Jan's chapter can wear the stamp Vera's filing earned her (Pillar 4), the same rule GDD 7 VA §2 gives sheets: a person's own file carries that person's tier. A `kind: person` hotspot never carries an anchor (§4.2: red never on people; O05–O07). A `threshold` hotspot's anchors use the room's default: Rybar's telephone reddens by Vera's reach because the call is the state's attention on *her*. If `per_character[subject]` is absent from the snapshot, the anchor's reach is 0 (GDD 7 Edge Case 14's shape) and `SUBJECT_UNKNOWN(anchor, subject)` is logged once per scene. The `protagonist_id` arrives in the `enter_scene` context (Rule 11), never from a string on the sidecar. *Rationale:* the snapshot already carries every watched person; keying on it keeps Overlay dossier-blind while letting a consequence cross chapters on the plate as it already does on paper. *Rejected:* a per-room blend or max over everyone present (`max(red_reach)` makes a calm protagonist's room red because a watched friend is in it, GDD 7 §3's rejected "red for no reason of his own"); the brief's "person hotspot's threshold uses the subject's" as a rule by kind (a telephone is not a person; the subject binding is per anchor and explicit, or there is none).

**5. Four tiers, four looks, and the tint carries no red where a person stands.** At `reach 0` nothing authored is lit and `tint.tier = 0`: the plate shows no red-tolerance pixel (Collapsing's "withdraws", and every unlooked-at protagonist at any phase). At `reach 1` the tier-1 stamps are lit from the one stamp atlas. At `reach 2` the tier-2 prop layers join them. At `reach 3` the `key_light` layer joins them and `tint.tier = 3`: the full-frame phase layer (T4's fourth layer, T3's one draw) takes the uniform `3`. the stamp-red of tier 3 is carried by the authored `key_light` layer, which O07 keeps off the `figure_band` and off people; the full-frame tint is the "hard light" of §4.3 ("tinting state key light, hard light") — a value and temperature shift of the plate toward the state's hard overhead — and must leave **no red-tolerance pixel inside any `figure_band` or `kind: person` rect** after compositing (a Gate B sub-check, Rule 6). Whether the tint shader (`CanvasModulate` versus a full-frame tint shader, blend mode: **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike) carries any red hue outside the band is the art director's call under that check; the plan carries `tint.tier` either way. Under `red_as_hatching` (GDD 4), `red_style` returns `hatch` and every lit anchor renders its hatch variant: a stamp's hatch atlas cell, a prop's `layer_hatch`, the key light's `layer_hatch` (a hatched edge on a value-only light), each guaranteed red-free by O08; the tint's `hatch: true` tells the shader to drop any hue term and keep the value shift. Tier 1 and 2 are the act-one sights (the MVP shows `reach ≤ 1`, the Vertical Slice `reach 2` in Jan's chapter at paranoid, GDD 7 Open Questions); tier 3 is first seen in act two. *Rationale:* §4.2 ("never on skin or clothing") and GDD 8 VA §3 ("no red is on the figure") are absolute, and a full-frame red over a y-sorted figure cannot honour them; authored red on authored rects can. *Rejected:* lifting figures above the tint (breaks GDD 3 Rule 10's y-sort under walking avatars); a hatched full-frame vignette under `red_as_hatching` (a vignette is a grade, GDD 6 VA §2 forbids one; and §4.5 wants shape, not a frame).

**6. The red budget is an assert, not a tuning: the plan's estimate is the headless proxy, Gate B's composited capture is the truth.** `PHASE_RED_BUDGET_PPM = [20000, 50000, 150000, 10000]` (art bible §4.3's 2/5/15/1%, registered as a pointer, not retunable in any GDD). The core computes `red_est_ppm` (Formulas F3) from the lit anchors' `red_px` plus `HUD_RED_RESERVE_PX[ink_reach]` (Paper UI's date stamp, the one red HUD element always on screen; art bible A8 puts the HUD inside the measurement) over `FRAME_PX` 2,073,600, and raises `RED_BUDGET(scene_id, phase, est, budget)` through the `DebugSink` when `red_est_ppm > red_budget_ppm`; O10 runs the same function for every phase at build so no shipped plate can trip it at runtime. Gate B (art bible T7): a CI job loads each plate at each phase through `NullSettings` + `EngineOverlayHost`, calls `capture_red_share_ppm()` (frame capture **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike), and asserts the measured share ≤ budget, Collapsing soot ≥ `SOOT_MIN_PPM` 400,000, and (Rule 5) red inside every `figure_band` and person rect = 0. The proxy over-counts (overlaps are summed, occlusion ignored) and never under-counts, so a plan that passes the proxy may fail Gate B only through the tint or Paper UI's sheets, which the capture sees and the proxy cannot. *Rationale:* the budgets are the art bible's way of making Principle A measurable; a tuning knob on them would let a plate negotiate with the state's colour. *Rejected:* measuring per plate on export (GDD 1's open question, answered by T7: the plate carries no red, so only the composite can be measured); clamping the estimate by hiding anchors at runtime (a room would differ between players for a build error).

**7. Slot lighting is two switches, red-free, cut at entry, and never a function of the phase.** From `slot` and `space` the plan sets every lighting layer (Formulas table below); the layers are authored crops (GDD 7 VA §5: "three slots on two switches, lighting layers rather than plate variants"; the technical artist confirms the blend in the spike):

| `space` | `morning` | `day` | `night` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `state`, `private`, `print_shop` | `window_day` on, `lamp`/`overhead` on | `window_day` on, pool off | `window_night` on, `lamp`/`overhead` on |
| `street` | nothing lit (overcast, §4.3) | nothing lit | `sodium_<n>` on where `phase ∈ lit_phases`, `amber_windows` on |

Switch one is the window's value (bone by day, soot by night; never blue, never sun); switch two is the pool (lamp in private, overhead in state). A slot change between consecutive scenes is seen only as the next plate entering already lit (GDD 7 VA §5: a cut, never a crossfade); Overlay plays nothing at `transition`. The only phase term in this table is `sodium_<n>.lit_phases`: Collapsing's "sodium poles dark one by one" (§2.2) is authored per pole per plate, so the sequence across the act's street plates is the artist's, and no count, beat or clock chooses a pole (GDD 7 Rule 13: no tier is a function of the beat). *Rationale:* §2.1's light recipes have one source per state and the Calendar already said the slot never changes a tier; two booleans per plate are all the lighting state the game has. *Rejected:* three plate paintings per room (T1: three plates is 33 MB per scene); lowering lamps by phase at runtime (§2.2's "lamps lowered" is plate authoring for the phase a scene is expected in, a conflict recorded in Open Questions for the game designer and art director; a runtime dimming would be a meter in light).

**8. The carbon ghost is the one in-scene change, made by the player's own act, drawn by cut, never moved.** On GDD 3's `set_hotspot_state(id, filed)` the host emits `ghost_requested(id)`; the core returns `GhostOp {hotspot: id, source: layer_filed, offset: [GHOST_OFFSET_PX, GHOST_OFFSET_PX], colour: CARBON_WORLD}` and the host draws, on the next frame and with no fade, a flat carbon-blue silhouette (`#434C7C`, the crop's alpha filled with one colour; mechanism — a mask export or a one-line shader — **NOT SOURCEABLE**, spike) of the hotspot's `layer_filed` crop, offset `GHOST_OFFSET_PX` = 3 px right and down at 1080 (2 px at 720 under the ⅔ stretch, whole pixels; art bible §6.4's 2–3 px), **directly beneath that hotspot's layer in GDD 3's draw order** so the ghost peeks out on two edges and the object's hard edge stays intact. Ghosts are drawn only for `filed`, never for `void` (the state removed the paper; nothing was copied) or `hidden`; a hotspot already `filed` at entry is in `ghosts_at_entry` and drawn with `apply(plan)`; a ghost stays put when its hotspot lifts on focus (GDD 3 VA 7c: the gap is the focus read) and costs no extra draw for the lift (answers GDD 3's open question). Count ≤ `GHOST_MAX` 16 (T3) and, by GDD 3 E02, ≤ 8 in practice; the plan asserts `|ghosts| ≤ n_hotspots`. The ghost never animates, never fades, never reacts to a tier, and is not removed in-scene (availability moves `filed → unfiled` only through Scene Flow at a boundary). *Rationale:* "a read room looks read" (§6.4); the carbon copy is the state's duplicate and a duplicate does not move; drawing it as a flat silhouette makes T7's carbon count predictable and keeps the keyline out of the copy (a blue keyline would re-say "a fact is here", GDD 3 VA 7c). *Rejected:* a modulated duplicate of the `layer` crop (restores the keyline in blue, and modulating a midtone object by carbon gives a muddy third colour, not a copy); a 2 px offset at 1080 (1.33 px at 720, a blurred edge under T6's integer rule).

**9. The reactive-event threshold light is two switches on two cuts, and the figure comes first.** For `scene_kind: insert` the plan carries two lighting tables. `lighting` is the room as the player left it: the inherited slot's recipe, `threshold_<id>` off. `lighting_after_threshold` is the §2.1 reactive-event recipe: every slot layer off ("the room's light source is replaced"), `threshold_<id>` on (torch through the door, sodium through the window, the bare bulb over the telephone), and the red anchors lit at the inherited reach exactly as in any scene, so red crosses the threshold line at the phase's reach (a stamp on the door at tier 1, the red telephone at tier 2, the key light at tier 3 — O07 admits the key light on inserts). The host applies `lighting` with `apply(plan)` at `scene_ready` (the figure is already on the line, Character Presentation **(PROV)**; GDD 8 VA §3 frame one) and `lighting_after_threshold` with `apply_after_threshold(plan)` exactly `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later (500 ms; a knob), **never on the same frame and never as a crossfade**. The delay is a story beat, not a tween, so `motion: reduced` does not shorten it; the two cuts within one second are at most one opposing luminance pair, under `FLASH_MAX_PER_S` by construction. *Rationale:* GDD 6 VA §7 and GDD 8 VA §3: the floor goes after the state is in the room, and no light announces a person not yet on the line. *Rejected:* letting Scene Flow time the second cut (it owns no clock inside a scene, GDD 8 Rule 13; the host's tick is the one clock here, injected for the null twin); a single cut with the threshold light on frame one (GDD 8's reviewer test fails it by name).

**10. Flicker is an allowance this system issues, enforced by construction, and nothing in a room ever flickers.** `flicker_allowance(flicker, can_count_flashes)` (Formulas F4): `off` → `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}`; `limited` → `{allowed: true, max_hz: min(FLICKER_HZ, FLASH_MAX_PER_S)}` when the host's toggle gate is live, else `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}` (GDD 4 Edge Case 21: `limited` behaves as `off` until the cap exists). The gate is the core's pure function `toggle_ok(now_ms, last_toggle_ms) = now_ms − last_toggle_ms ≥ 1000 / (2 × max_hz)` with injected time, so a driven square wave can never exceed `max_hz` flashes per sliding second (WCAG 2.3.1; `flash_rate_ok` in the registry is satisfied by construction rather than by measurement), and `can_count_flashes()` is the host's statement that it drives toggles through the gate on real frame time (the engine twin returns `false` until the spike proves frame timing; `NullOverlayHost` returns what the test sets). The **only** flicker in the game is §2.2's Panicking overhead flicker **on the portrait pinned to the interrogation transcript sheet** (interrogation is "the only state with a pulse", §2.1); it is Paper UI's **(PROV)** to draw from this allowance and this gate, and it never carries red (the portrait has none, §4.2), so the red-flash threshold GDD 4 left to Overlay is met with `red_flashes = 0`. No lighting layer, stamp, prop, tint or ghost ever toggles, periodically or once, while a scene is `active` (GDD 3 Game Feel "nothing moves unprompted"; GDD 8 VA §6). Overlay reads `motion` for nothing: every change it makes is a cut at 0 ms under `full` and `reduced` alike, so it holds no subscription (GDD 4's reader row amended for this GDD). Act one never reaches Panicking (`first_beat(2) = ∅`), so the allowance is exercised by tests and the Vertical Slice's portrait only. *Rationale:* a cap that is measured can be missed for a frame; a cap that is a minimum interval between toggles cannot, and the one periodic element in the game is then the whole sum WCAG asks about. *Rejected:* an overhead lighting layer flickering in Panicking state rooms (it would be the one thing moving in an idle room, Open Questions); counting luminance transitions from a frame capture (the spike that may never land, and a count after the fact is not a cap).

**11. Inputs arrive by push through the presentation seam; Overlay calls nothing that computes.** The engine `PresentationSink` fan-out (GDD 8 Rule 13) delivers to Overlay: `reveal(records, snapshot, paper[], counts)` → `on_snapshot(snapshot)` (stored; the plate is hidden behind the full-frame sheet at that moment, GDD 8 VA §1, so nothing is applied); and a new call, **`enter_scene(scene_id, scene_kind, slot, protagonist_id)`** (GDD 8 amendment, amendment applied), emitted by Scene Flow immediately before `host.preload_scene` for an authored scene and before `activate_scene(wipe = false)` for an insert, carrying the facts Scene Flow already holds (`date_for(id).slot`, the chapter's protagonist, the row's `kind`). GDD 3's host delivers `scene_preloaded(id)` (plan, `load`), `scene_ready(id)` (`apply`), `ghost_requested(id)` (Rule 8) and `scene_exited(id)` (`clear`). Settings values are read through a `SettingsReader` at plan time only (`flicker`, `red_as_hatching`) and never subscribed to. If no snapshot has arrived (the act's first scene; the first scene after a load, GDD 7 Rule 11), the plan treats the snapshot as `{phase 0, ink_reach 1, per_character: {}}`: every reach 0, slot lighting applied, `SNAPSHOT_MISSING(scene_id)` logged, and Open Questions asks the Calendar for the view the room should have had. *Rationale:* GDD 7 Rule 9 forbids consumers calling `phase()` or `attention_view()`; GDD 8 Rule 13 forbids signals from Scene Flow; what remains is a recorded call list, which is what the simulator diffs. *Rejected:* reading the slot off `transition(data)` (not called for `kind: none`, for inserts or at chapter open; Open Questions); reading the protagonist off the sidecar (a content string in a presentation system, and wrong the moment a room is reused across chapters).

**12. Sheet red is Paper UI's to draw and this system's to style; the timer's one float never enters here.** `red_style(red_as_hatching) → {variant: fill | hatch}` and the stamp atlas (with its hatch cells, Rule 5) are this system's; every red on a sheet — the §7.5 surveillance stamp from `stamp_tier`, the §7.1 date stamp from `ink_reach` (both already Paper UI's by GDD 7 Rule 9), and the §7.5 **interrogation timer margin fill** — is drawn by Paper UI **(PROV)** from that style and that atlas. The timer fill is Dialogue's state **(PROV)**: Dialogue owns `fraction ∈ [0, 1]` under `timer_mode` and hands it to its own transcript sheet (a Paper UI Control), which fills the right margin top-to-bottom in `fill` or `hatch`, bordered, with hatching ticks at every `1 / TIMER_TICKS` (5; §7.5's 20%), no numerals, the screen-reader countdown string beside it. It is the one continuous motion in the game and it stays under `motion: reduced` (GDD 4: information, not decoration). Overlay never receives the float, never draws in sheet space, and never knows a timer is running. *Rationale:* the fill lives on a sheet, and sheets are Control trees above the T4 stack (§4.4: UI never takes tint; §7.2: UI grain is its own ≤ 6%); GDD 7 Rule 9 already put the surveillance stamp on Paper UI from a tier, and the timer is the same kind of object with one more input. Giving Overlay a second drawing path into UI space would put one system on both sides of the tint for one bar. *Rejected:* the brief's proposal 7 (Overlay renders the fill from Dialogue's `fraction`): it makes Overlay the one system that handles a float, and it needs the sheet's layout under `ui_scale`, which is Paper UI's; GDD 7 Rule 10's "Dialogue's and Overlay's" is amended to "Dialogue's and Paper UI's; Overlay supplies the red style" (amendment applied).

**13. Budgets are pointers, reported as observations.** Draws (art bible T3, `DRAW_TARGET` 150): grain 1, tint 1, ghosts ≤ `GHOST_MAX` 16, stamps ≤ `STAMP_MAX` 12, lighting layers ≤ `LIGHT_MAX` 6, props and key light ≤ 1 + the prop count (each lit anchor one draw); the core's `draws_est_overlay` feeds GDD 3 Formulas §8's `STAMP_MAX + 2 + n_vis` terms and the lighting term is new (amendment applied: GDD 3 §8 gains `+ LIGHT_MAX`). Memory (T1): every overlay layer is cropped (T9; O14) and summed into GDD 3's resident-byte sheet under owner `overlay` (O15), with the budget lines `SCENE_BUDGET_WARN` 224 / `SCENE_BUDGET_MAX` 256 deciding; the art bible's worksheet line "overlays and layers ≈ 10 MB" is revised to carry three 1024² lighting layers (Open Questions). The debug overlay shows `measured − draws_est` (GDD 3 W14) and Overlay's bytes; neither is a verdict. *Rejected:* an Overlay-local ceiling (the ceiling is per scene, not per owner; GDD 3 Formulas §7's rejection).

**14. What this system never does.** Never reads a dossier field, `A_eff`, `red_band`, a threshold constant (`T_*`, `ATTN_*`) or the beat; never calls `phase()`, `attention_view()` or `snapshot()`; never recomputes a plan while a scene is `active` or `suspended`; never animates, tweens, fades or crossfades anything (every change is a cut; the one periodic element is Paper UI's portrait under this system's allowance); never draws red on a figure, a person hotspot or inside a `figure_band`; never draws anything in a room the player is idle in that was not in the plan at entry, except the ghost the player's own filing made; never draws a meter, bar, dial, numeral or banner (the timer fill is Paper UI's, from Dialogue); never touches a plate's pixels or a crop's (Gate A); never draws in sheet or HUD space; never changes a stamp, tint or light on a phase change (the announcement is paper, GDD 6 VA §2); never plays a sound; never holds UI state or persists anything (a plan is rebuilt at every entry from pushed inputs); never reads a wall clock (the host's tick is injected; the core has none); never varies a plate's density or lamp height by phase; never substitutes a layer at runtime for one the validator rejected (release degrades by *hiding* the anchor and logging, so a missing stamp is less red, never more). Its errors go through the injected `DebugSink`, so every assert is observable headless.

### States and Transitions

**The system, per scene (one state machine, keyed by `scene_id`):** `idle` (no plan for this id) → `planned` (at `scene_preloaded`: `plan()` computed from the stored snapshot and the `enter_scene` context; `host.load(plan)`; layers resident, nothing visible; the plan is immutable from here) → `applied` (at `scene_ready`: `host.apply(plan)` on one frame — slot lighting, lit anchors at the reach, tint tier, grain, ghosts for `ghosts_at_entry`; for an insert this is the first-cut table) → [inserts only] `relit` (at `scene_ready + THRESHOLD_CUT_MS`: `apply_after_threshold(plan)`, one frame, the slot layers off and `threshold_<id>` on) → `applied`/`relit` holds for the life of the scene, accepting exactly one kind of change, `ghost_requested(id)` → `apply_ghost(op)` (next frame, by cut, additive, never reversed in-scene) → `cleared` (at `scene_exited`: `host.clear(scene_id)`; layers released; the id returns to `idle`). A `reveal` arriving while a scene is `applied` updates the **stored snapshot only**; nothing on the plate changes (the plate is behind the full-frame sheet and will be left by the wipe, GDD 8 VA §1). A `plan()` request for an `applied` scene is refused with `PLAN_LOCKED(scene_id)` (debug assert; release ignores and logs).

**The snapshot, as this system holds it:** `none` (act start; after a load) → `held(beat n)` at each `reveal` → `held(beat n+1)` …; never mutated, never saved (GDD 7 Rule 11), never requested. `none` plans as all-zero reach with `SNAPSHOT_MISSING` (Rule 11; Open Questions).

**The ghost, per hotspot:** `absent` → `drawn` on `ghost_requested` or on entry with `filed` availability → stays `drawn` through focus (lift above it), reveal, sheets, the insert's two cuts → `absent` only at `clear` or when the next entry's availability says `unfiled`/`void`/`hidden` (re-derived by Scene Flow at a boundary, GDD 3 Rule 5).

**The tint, as one uniform:** `0` for every scene at `reach ≤ 2` → `3` only for a scene entered at `reach 3`; never any value between, never changes within a scene, never carries red into a figure band (Rule 5). With `red_as_hatching`: the same tiers with `hatch: true`.

**The flicker allowance, per plan:** `{allowed: false, max_hz: 0}` under `flicker: off`, or under `limited` while `can_count_flashes()` is false → `{allowed: true, max_hz: FLICKER_HZ}` under `limited` once the gate is live. Consumed by Paper UI's portrait; the allowance changes only with the plan, so a settings change mid-interrogation applies at the next scene entry (parallel to GDD 4 Edge Case 19).

**The timer fill (for the record; not this system's state):** Dialogue's `fraction` 0 → 1 under `timer_mode`, drawn continuously by Paper UI in `red_style`; Overlay has no state for it.

**The red budget, per plan:** `within` (`red_est_ppm ≤ red_budget_ppm`; the plan's normal state) or `over` (`RED_BUDGET` raised through the `DebugSink`; the plan is still applied in release, since hiding anchors at runtime would make rooms differ between players, and O10 means a shipped build never reaches this state).

## Game Feel

Optional for a Core system; three feel properties follow from it and are stated so Paper UI, Character Presentation and the art pipeline inherit them as targets.

- **Red is read from the edge in.** A room's attention is legible in the first second: margin, props, light, or nothing. No element pulses, breathes or creeps; a tier lands with the room, by a cut, and stays.
- **A read room looks read.** The carbon ghost appears the instant a hotspot turns `filed`, by a cut, two pixels off true at 720 and three at 1080, and never moves again; the player's own hand is the only thing that changes a room while they are in it.
- **The state arrives in two cuts.** The figure on the threshold line first, then the light replaced `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later; never a crossfade, never a dimming, never a sting from this system.

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen and draws nothing in sheet or HUD space. The date stamp, the surveillance stamp and the interrogation timer fill are Paper UI's, drawn from the Calendar's tiers and Dialogue's fraction in this system's `red_style` and stamp atlas; the settings that reach this system (`flicker`, `red_as_hatching`) are Paper UI's settings sheet (GDD 4). Debug builds only: an inspector that prints the current `OverlayPlan`, the red estimate against the phase budget, the ghost list and the flicker allowance, and a `capture_for_gate_b()` trigger that writes the composited capture and its measured red share to the evidence folder; both reachable through the debug menu and absent from release (the same rule as GDDs 2, 6, 7 and 8's debug surfaces).

> **📌 UX Flag — Overlay & Surveillance Rendering**: no player-facing UI of its own. `design/accessibility-requirements.md` must state the three facts this system guarantees: no attention or phase signal is carried by colour alone (every red is a bordered shape with hatching, and `red_as_hatching` removes the colour entirely); no element flickers above `FLASH_MAX_PER_S`, and `flicker: limited` behaves as `off` until the cap is measured; and nothing on a plate changes state while the player is idle.
