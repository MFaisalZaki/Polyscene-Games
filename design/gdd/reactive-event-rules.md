## Summary

Reactive Event Rules & Story Simulator is the system that makes the state and the other people act: writer-authored rule data (who wants what, what a move needs on paper, what the world does on its own, what may be assumed, what the story may never do) is compiled once and read by two consumers. At design and build time it becomes a polyscene topic, and the story engine enumerates every admissible telling, proves which endings are reachable and which are forbidden by which law, and diagnoses any telling the writers ask about. At runtime a small deterministic evaluator fires the same rules at scene boundaries only, with a fixed order, caps and a bounded cascade, writing the dossier as the `rules` writer; a parity suite proves the two agree. It exists because Pillar 2 is only true if the state's moves are read off paper, Pillar 4 only if a filing in one chapter can become a raid in another without a hand-authored branch, and Pillar 3 only if "every heroic line costs" is a law the engine checks.

## Overview

Reactive Event Rules & Story Simulator is the system that makes the state and the other people in Paper Walls act. Every scene is hand-written, but whether a raid, a query slip, a memo from the next desk, a courier at the gate or a review card fires, and at whom, is decided at each scene boundary by rules that read the dossier, never by a script that knows what the player did. The rules are the writers' material: who wants what (preferences), what a move needs on paper before it may happen (licences), what the world does on its own (world moves within calendar windows, drift), what may be assumed at the opening (the unknowns), and what the story may never do (the laws). The same rules are the source of two things. At design and build time they become a polyscene topic, and the story engine enumerates every admissible telling, proves which ending classes are reachable and which are forbidden by which law, and diagnoses any telling the writers ask about; that is the story simulator, and it is the acceptance criterion for the dossier budgets, the fact uses and the ending table. At runtime the same rules are evaluated by a small deterministic evaluator inside the game, at scene boundaries only, with a capped cascade and a fixed tie-break, writing the dossier through GDD 1's effect records as the `rules` writer. The system exists because Pillar 2 is only true if the state's moves are read off paper, Pillar 4 is only true if a filing in one chapter can become a raid in another without a hand-authored branch, and Pillar 3 is only enforceable if "every heroic line costs something" is a law the engine checks rather than a note the writers remember. The index describes the system as "a polyscene topic: dossier flags as fluents, rules as preferences, licences and laws; `classes()` enumerates the branches, `diagnose()` says why a branch is forbidden", evaluating at scene boundaries only, emitting events and never calling Feature code; this GDD keeps that scope. It does not own the dossier (GDD 1), the content a rule fires (GDD 2's beats and scenes), the scene-boundary order (Scene Flow), the regime phase and attention functions (Calendar), or the chapter graph (Chapter & Story Structure). Which evaluator is the source of truth and how parity between them is proved is ADR-2's decision; how the enumerated branches ship as data is ADR-3's.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees a rule; they see the state move. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "the state keeps its books" chosen, with one line from "it had been looking for weeks"; "the answer comes to someone else" left as GDD 1's payoff, not this system's identity).*

**The state keeps its books.** The emotion this system serves is being audited, not being hunted. The state is not angry and it is not clever. It is thorough. Every page that leaves a desk is expected somewhere else, and every gap is noticed.

The anchor moment: Vera burns the report. The scene ends quietly and nothing happens in the archive. A beat later, in the next scene, the telephone on her desk rings. It is Rybar. He is polite and he does not raise his voice. He only asks whether the report reached her, because his copy shows it was sent. He accuses no one. He is balancing a ledger, and the player feels the column that does not add up.

Pillar 2 as feel: the state is a character with habits the player comes to recognise. It always balances the ledger. It never strikes where it has not looked. Its attention is visible before its action, so its action reads as a verdict on what the player failed to see, not as an ambush. As it moves from confident to paranoid it does not get crueller; it sends more paper, thicker and closer together, not louder. The dread is that the player can see where its next query is going to land.

*Cost accepted:* the state can only answer what can be framed as a discrepancy. Every reactive event needs a "missing or contradicting page" reading, which narrows what writers can author.

**Pillar alignment.** Pillar 2, "the state is a character": every move is a habit read off paper, never a script. Pillar 3, "no one is clean": the laws make a costless heroic line and a protectionless cowardly line impossible in the world, not merely discouraged. Pillar 4, "consequences cross lives": a filing in one chapter licenses a move in another without a hand-authored branch.

**The one rule this system must obey: the state only moves on paper the player could have read, and when that paper is there, it always moves.** The first half keeps the state from being a random hazard: nothing fires without a cause in the dossier that a protagonist wrote, read, filed, withheld or was seen near. The second half keeps it from being a scripted villain: if the licence is there, the move comes, every time, whatever the plot would prefer; remove the licence and the move cannot happen. Two clarifications carried into Detailed Design: calendar clocks are the world, not the state (Marta's collapse and the review convening run on time, but the state's response to them, the ambulance, who gets summoned, must still be licensed by the dossier); and the state speaks only in the clerical passive (*a query was raised*, *the line was disconnected*), never naming a cause to a character, so the brief's "nobody is told what they did to the others" holds by construction.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary), the technical director, the AI programmer and the narrative director. Rulings where they differed are stated in place. Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a system whose GDD is not yet written; one amendment to GDD 2 is applied (Rule 4). Engine facts are quoted only from the pinned reference; the rest is marked **NOT SOURCEABLE**.*

**0. Vocabulary.** A *rule* is one authored row: an NPC `move`, a `world_move`, a `drift`, an `unknown`, a `law`, an `incompatible` pair, or an `agent` preference row. A *boundary* is one `begin_boundary … end_boundary` sequence (GDD 1 Rule 4a); the *beat* is GDD 1's boundary counter within the act. A *step* is one polyscene action occurrence in the solver, which is one event, not one beat. A *candidate* is a rule whose `needs` hold and whose licence is satisfied at the current cascade step. The *policy* is the deterministic choice among candidates. A *telling* is one ordered sequence of player decisions, fired rules and world moves from act open to act close: the simulator's unit and the runtime's trace. The *world space* is every admissible telling; the *game space* is the subset the shipped policy can produce.

**1. One source of truth: writer-authored rule data; everything else is generated (ADR-2, option A).** Rules live in `assets/data/rules/<act>.rules.yaml` in GDD 2's style (the same effect and condition grammar; never ASP). The build tool `tools/sim/topic_gen.py` reads the compiled content (the player's effectful decision options and dialogue answers become the PDDL's player actions: preconditions from `when`, effects from the batch tagged `filing` or `dialogue`, principal the protagonist; effectless options are omitted because they do not branch the state), the dossier schema (fluents, ranges, defaults) and the rules file, and emits: the act's PDDL domain and instance; `act.lp` (preferences, licences, windows, drift, incompatibles, the unknowns block with `#const assume_min`, the laws' `broken/2` rules, the axes), which must state the assumption mechanism itself because the pinned polyscene encodings lack it; `policy.lp` (Rule 5); the Topic subclass; and `assets/data/rules/<act>.rules.json`, the flat compiled form the GDScript evaluator loads. Neither consumer is authoritative over the other; the parity suite (Rule 12) is the contract between them. The prototype's hand-written `act1.lp`, transcribed into the YAML, is a golden fixture (`tests/fixtures/act1-golden/`): the generator's output must be *semantically* equal (the same `classes()` table over the flow axes, the same 136 end states at the prototype's horizon, `diagnose(E0) = {no_one_unchanged}`), checked in CI, never byte-equal; when the brief changes (the V1 amendment of GDD 5 is the first such change) the fixture is re-cut formally, never edited until it passes. *Rationale:* writers never write ASP; one source cannot drift from itself; the player's actions come from content, which nobody else owns. *Rejected:* a hand-authored `.lp` exported to JSON (it makes the solver file the writers' editing surface, which the narrative director will not maintain, and hides the player's actions inside a file nobody else owns).

**2. The rule format.** Seven record kinds; fields marked `K` are keys the generator maps to `.lp` rows. Every condition uses GDD 2's grammar evaluated by the one `Condition.evaluate`; constants appear by name only.

- `move` (an NPC's action, the brief's §4 rows): `id` (`K`, `[a-z0-9_]+`, unique, cited at fire time as `rules:<chapter>/<scene>:<id>`), `agent` (`K`, a registered entity, never a protagonist), `class` (`K`, the beat key and the `world_classes.json` action class), `kind` (`K`, `C+`, `C-` or `N`), `turning_point` (`K`, meaningful for `C+`; selects `cost_is_a_person`), `priority` (0–100, default `PRIORITY_DEFAULT`; two moves of one `state: true` agent may not share a priority, a loader error, because the state's choice must be readable off the file), `delay` (beats; an override of the agent's habit on its `agent` row, legal only with `delay_reason`, a written sentence; there is no `pick` on a `move`, Rule 5.3), `needs` (the grammar plus the three wrapper leaves of Rule 4; for a `state: true` agent every field read here or in a licence must be rendered in some protagonist's dossier view, a generator check against the schema's view tags **(PROV: Paper UI renders them)**, because the state only moves on paper the player could have read), `licences` (named **any-of**; empty = always licensed; a licence may read an unknown through `was_assumed` unless the agent is `state: true`, since the state never moves on an assumption the player cannot read; `needs` may not read `was_assumed`, because a need on an assumption is a hidden licence), `effects` (GDD 1 effect records; `source` is generated, never authored), `cost` and `protects` (terms over the actor's `weighs` row), `beat` (`insert` | `paper`; the protagonists' own moves are `chapter` scenes and are never rules), `crosses_to` (the receiving protagonist, for `crossing_read`).
- `agent` (one per NPC): `id`, `state` (bool, default false: the agent acts for the state, as Rybar, the captain and the regime pseudo-agent do; a state agent's licensed moves must fire in the game space, Rule 5.3 and EC 8, its licences may not read `was_assumed`, its moves may not share a priority), `delay` (beats, default 0, safe range 0–2: the agent's habit; its moves become candidates at the boundary `delay` beats after their needs first held; Berta carries `delay: 1` because she sits on things, not because the schedule needs her late), `weighs: [{field, want: true|false|up|down, w: 1–3}]`, the preference rows the generator emits as `pref/5`; a *cost term* is a preference of the actor's that the move lowers, a *protect term* one it raises or keeps; weights are the actor's own weighing (V5 costs Berta at 3 in Berta's row and Vera lists it at 2; the law reads the actor's row); `attention` is never a term (it is weather, not a price).
- `world_move`: `id`, `class` (`K`, kind `event`), `window: [lo, hi]` (`K`, beats, inclusive, within `[1, HORIZON_ACT]`), `pick` (`fixed` = the earliest licensed beat; `seeded` = a seeded beat in the window, legal only on a move under a non-state clock, the post, the weather, breath, never on a regime announcement or any move a `state: true` agent is the author of, in every act; the loader rejects the rest), `licences` (named any-of), `effects`, `beat` (`insert` | `paper`).
- `drift`: `id`, `field` (a scalar, or a `calendar.*` clock **(PROV: Calendar)**), `op: add`, `value`, `period` (a constant name, e.g. `DRIFT_PERIOD_ATTENTION`), `while` (a condition; omitted = unconditional, legal only on a `calendar.*` clock: a drift on an attention or regime scalar must carry `while`, or the loader rejects it, because the state does not drift toward the player for no reason on paper).
- `unknown`: `id`, `field` (the chapter's `assumes` entry, GDD 1 Rule 8), `licenses: [move ids]` (documentation and the licence-coverage check), `settles_as` (a tag, GDD 1's settlement rule).
- `law`: `id`, `pattern` from a closed set (`class_cost` for costless_courage, unprotected_cowardice and cost_is_a_person; `licensed_silence`; `standing_unanswered` for gap_is_noticed, gate_decides and breath_is_a_clock; `cross_read_within` for crossing_read; `touched_at_close` for no_one_unchanged; `event_at_close` for review_sits; `licensed_only` for attention_precedes_action and no_panic_before_crowd; `licensed_answers` for the state agents: a licensed state move fires at that boundary or a later one while still licensed), `params`, `pillar`, `tier` (Rule 10).
- `incompatible`: state pairs, emitted verbatim and asserted at runtime in debug after every `apply`.
- `axes` and the ending classes are GDD 1's (Formulas §5); this file only cites them.

*Rationale per sub-rule:* licences as named any-of match polyscene's `licence/3` and keep `diagnose()` able to name *which* licence a telling rests on (the unknowns' whole point); cost and protect terms over the actor's `weighs` row let polyscene's gain machinery decide per step whether the actor lost or gained, with a static authoring check that a `C+` move names a cost the actor weighs, a `C-` move a protect term, and a turning point a non-`attention` cost whose carrier is a person (a field on a record whose GDD 1 `kind` is `person`, never `unit` or `regime`); laws from a closed pattern set keep ASP out of the writers' file. *Rejected:* a Rules-only expression language (breaks the single-evaluator invariant and the read-budget count); folding licences into `needs` (loses the diagnosis); numeric cost scores on the move (two numbers for one fact; the solver would not use them); free-form ASP bodies in YAML (option B with a wrapper).

**3. When: scene boundaries only.** The evaluator runs exactly once per boundary, inside Scene Flow's rules step, after the decision batch and before phase, attention and reveal **(PROV: Scene Flow owns the order)**; a calendar beat is a boundary with an empty decision batch **(PROV: Calendar)**. Never mid-scene, never from Feature code; the evaluator has no signal inputs and emits no signals: Scene Flow calls `run_boundary` and emits `rule_fired` from the returned list. *Rejected:* evaluating on every dossier change (the state would react inside a scene it has not yet seen, and the simulator's grain would no longer match the runtime's).

**4. Reads.** The evaluator reads only through GDD 2's `Condition.evaluate` with an injected `RulesConditionReader` that composes GDD 5's reader shape (`read`, `has_fact`, `phase`) with the boundary-local change list: `has_fact` always returns false, and the loader rejects `has_fact` in rule data (held facts are the player's, GDD 5). The wrapper adds three leaves, legal only in rule data and a V18 error in content: `{changed: F}` (a change record for F exists in this boundary so far), `{contradicted: F}` (such a record has `contradicts ≠ null`), and `{beat_in: [lo, hi]}` (the compiled form of a window, never hand-written in `needs`, because it is history by GDD 2's own rule). `was_assumed` is already a core leaf; its reader is GDD 1's `last_source`. **Amendment applied to GDD 2 Rule 6:** `Condition.evaluate` consults a reader fallback `leaf(name, args) → bool` for any leaf name outside the core grammar, so a wrapper can add leaves without re-implementing `all`/`any`/`not`. The boundary-local change list is the decision batch's records plus the evaluator's own `apply` returns; no earlier boundary is read. *Rejected:* a separate change-log reader (near-redundant; `was_assumed` reads provenance from the dossier).

**5. Evaluation: candidates, policy, fire, cascade.** For cascade step `s` from 0:
1. *Drift first.* At step 0 every `drift` whose `while` holds and whose period gate ticks (Formulas §4) is applied as one batch before any candidate is considered; drift is exempt from the caps below.
2. *Candidates.* Every `move` and `world_move` whose `needs` evaluate true against the current boundary view (pre-boundary state plus batches applied so far), whose licence any-of holds, whose `delay` has elapsed, that has not fired this boundary (one fire per rule per boundary) and, for a world move, whose window contains the beat and which has not fired this act.
3. *Policy.* Group candidates by `agent` (world moves under the pseudo-agent `world`, exempt from the per-boundary cap); in each group order by `(priority desc, id asc)` and keep `RULES_PER_AGENT_PER_BOUNDARY` (1); across groups order the survivors by the same key and keep `RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX − fired_so_far` agent moves (the cap counts across all steps, so an agent cannot react to a reaction within one boundary). The ordering is total because ids are unique; a losing candidate is dropped, not queued, and is simply re-evaluated at the next step or boundary, so the evaluator holds no queue. There is no seeded tie-break among agent moves: `pick` is a `world_move` field (Rule 2), ties are broken by `id`, and equal priority between two moves of one `state: true` agent is a loader error. **Act-one data may not use `pick: seeded` anywhere** (the generator rejects it; Open Questions). For an agent tagged `state: true` the policy may delay a licensed move but never bury it: a state move whose needs and licence hold at some boundary fires at that boundary or a later one while still licensed, which the generator proves with the space-level law `licensed_answers`; a state move that is a candidate in some game-space telling and fires in none is a build failure, not a lint warning (EC 8). A state move's needs may not rest only on the boundary-local leaves `changed` and `contradicted` unless the generator shows it wins the policy at every boundary where they hold, because a boundary-local licence that loses the policy is gone at the next boundary, and the state would have looked and not moved.
4. *Fire.* Build one rules batch from the selected rules' effects in selection order, each effect stamped `source: rules:<chapter>/<scene>:<id>`; `apply(batch)`; append the change records to the boundary-local list; append `{rule_id, kind, class, agent, effects, source, step, licence}` to the result, where `licence` is the name of the satisfied licence (the first in declaration order when several hold; `null` for an unlicensed rule), so a trace carries what each firing rested on.
5. *Cascade.* If the step produced at least one change record and `s + 1 < CASCADE_MAX`, repeat from 2 with `s + 1`; otherwise stop. The result carries `cascade_steps`, `truncated` (candidates remained when the cap was reached: a runtime log, a build failure in the simulator, because a telling that needs four reactions to one decision is a pacing bug, brief §8) and the fired list.

*Rationale:* the runtime is not a solver; the solver enumerates every policy and the runtime realises one, so the runtime's policy must be a function of state that the simulator can reproduce, and priority-then-id is the smallest total order the writers can read off the file. *Rejected:* a utility score summing the actor's `weighs` over the move's effects (a second preference model; when it disagrees with polyscene's gain semantics the parity test fails for reasons no writer can see); capping world moves and drift (a clock that skips a tick because Kastan was busy is a bug); a fixpoint with no cap (unprovable, and the solver's grain assumes a bound).

**6. The policy is an encoding, not a post-filter.** The generator emits `policy.lp`, switched on by a constant, that constrains the solver to the runtime's choice (`:- occurs(A, t), cand(B, t), agentOf(A, G), agentOf(B, G), prio(B) > prio(A)`, with the tie-break and the boundary grouping). Two spaces follow: the world space (policy off: what can happen) and the game space (policy on, player choices free: what the shipped game does), game ⊆ world. "E0 unreachable" is proved once in the world space and carries over; "E1–E9 reachable" is proved in the game space, because that is what players can reach. *Rationale:* a post-filter sees only a sample of tellings; `classes()` projects over the whole space only if the policy is a constraint inside it.

**7. Beats, steps and the horizon.** The beat is GDD 1's boundary counter, read and never written by Rules. In the solver one step is one action (polyscene `core.lp`), so a beat is a counter fluent that advances on every player action and world move and not on an NPC reaction, and windows and drift periods are stated over the beat fluent, never over the step index. `BEATS_PER_ACT` (12, registry) is the act's beat length; `HORIZON_ACT = BEATS_PER_ACT + HORIZON_SLACK` (14) is the beat horizon, the two slack beats being the act-close boundaries at which world moves may still fire (the review sits at the closing bell, window 10–14) but no decision is offered **(PROV: Chapter & Story Structure)**; `HORIZON_EVENTS` is the solver's step horizon, an event budget (decisions plus reactions plus world moves), set from measurement in the first sprint (the prototype's 14 steps were 14 beats because it had one action per beat; act one may need 20–30) and bounded by `GROUND_BUDGET_S`; if it fails, the simulator solves per chapter with the instance seeded from the previous chapter's end state, which the dossier as the only channel makes natural. The generator rejects a window with `hi > HORIZON_ACT`.

**8. World moves fire at the earliest licensed beat in their window; drift is anchored.** A world move whose licence holds at a boundary with beat in `[lo, hi]` fires at the first such beat, once per act; if the window closes unlicensed it never fires (the review that is never convened is a telling; `review_sits` says whether it is admissible). Under `pick: seeded` the firing beat is drawn once when first licensed and held, discarded if the licence lapses. A drift fires at a boundary iff `while` holds and `(beat − anchor) mod period = 0 ∧ beat > anchor`, where `anchor` is the beat `while` last became true; when `while` turns false the anchor clears and the count restarts, it does not resume. The generator pins the same anchoring in `driftWhile`. Breath is a Calendar clock declared here as a drift on `calendar.breath_marta` **(PROV)**, and `marta_collapses` is a world move licensed by `{at_most: [calendar.breath_marta, BREATH_ZERO]}`. *Rejected:* always-seeded windows (every playthrough differs for no authored reason and the pacing targets become statistical); a separate drift scheduler in the Calendar (two writers of `add` on one scalar with no shared order; GDD 1 names `rules` the writer for drift).

**9. A fired rule becomes a scene.** `run_boundary` returns `RulesResult {fired[], cascade_steps, truncated, calendar_effects[]}`. Scene Flow **(PROV)** takes each firing in order and, by `class` and the rule's `beat` field, resolves GDD 2's `beat(class)`: `insert` becomes an insert scene queued before the next authored scene (a person in the room with the protagonist, entering through a threshold: Rybar's call at the telephone, the raid at the door, a knock at the gate, Berta at the next desk with her memo, the captain reading the log, the hospital's clerk asking for papers); `paper` becomes a line or a document in the reveal step with no scene (the review card by the morning post, Kastan's slip Jan never sees, more paper on every desk, a drift tick); `calendar.*` effects are routed to the Calendar by Scene Flow, never applied by the dossier. Beat resolution reads only `class` and `beat` from a firing; `licence`, `source`, `rule_id` and `agent` are trace fields, never bound by content or presentation (a content lint fails any binding of them, an amendment requested of GDD 2's validator), so the hand on an arriving document identifies the clerk or the office that sent it, never the protagonist whose assumption licensed the move. Rules never calls Scene Flow, Feature code or the beat store; it returns a value. *Rejected:* a signal per fire (a signal is a call into whatever is connected; a returned list is replayable headlessly and keeps "Scene Flow calls Rules, never back").

**10. Laws in three tiers.** (a) *Space-level*, simulator only: all twelve brief laws plus `review_sits`, `gate_decides` and `licensed_answers` (every `state: true` agent answers a licensed move; Rule 5.3), as polyscene `broken/2` over every telling. (b) *Trace-level*, CI: every runtime trace replayed through `diagnose()`; a broken law here is a policy bug, since the space already forbids it. (c) *Runtime debug asserts*, cheap and history-free: `attention_precedes_action` (a Rybar move fires only if its target is flagged or at `attention ≥ ATTN_LOOK`, enforced by its `needs` and asserted after selection), `no_panic_before_crowd` (a phase announcement fires only when licensed, by construction), the `incompatible` pairs after every `apply`, `one_file_per_household` (an incompatible), and a firing whose effects all no-op (an authoring smell). History laws (`gap_is_noticed`, `crossing_read`, `licensed_silence`, `no_one_unchanged`, `breath_is_a_clock`) are never asserted at runtime: they need beats of trace, and a violation at runtime would have no remedy but a crash. *Rejected:* a runtime law engine over the change log (a second solver; every violation it could find the CI replay finds first).

**11. The simulator (`tools/sim/`).** Four commands over one evaluator: `topic_gen` (Rule 1); `space` (`ground()`, `classes()`, `coverage_table()`, `forks()`, `diagnose()` over the world and game spaces, producing `world_classes.json` `{class, kind}` for GDD 2's V22–V24, `branches.json` `{class, witness telling, axes, assumptions, crosses}` with a header `{rules_hash, content_hash, tuning_hash}` over the compiled rules, the compiled content and the tuning it was built from (the release export refuses on any mismatch) for ADR-3 and Chapter & Story Structure **(PROV)**, `rules_export.json` with `R_c^rules` and `W_c^rules` per chapter computed from the **reachable firings in the game space** (never a static over-approximation of every rule at every boundary, which exceeds `WRITE_MAX`) for GDD 2's V20, and `fact_use.json`, the join of the Fact registry's gates against the schema for GDD 5); `replay` (headless Godot, `--import` first and retried once because exit 139 has been observed on 4.7.x, then a script that drives `NullSceneHost → Scene Flow → the evaluator` from scripted intents `decide`, `hold`, `pair`, `answer`, with NPC and world choices from the runtime policy or forced from a witness through an injected `ChoiceSource`, writing a trace); `parity` (Rule 12). Writers' outputs: one witness telling per ending class as prose with the paper line under each beat; a forbidden telling's diagnosis in plain words naming one law and the beats; the "rests on" assumption list per telling; the coverage table per axis value with a witness id per cell and empty cells named; a per-chapter "what fired" list (beat, rule, agent, class, beat type); the emergent-chain report as a named chain. Never: probabilities, scores, summed weights, raw state counts, grounding times, rankings, fractions or frequency words ("usually", "often", "rarely", "most common"); the parity rates `adm` and `real` go to the CI log and the `never_fired` counts to the lint log, never to the writers' output; the engine says admissible or forbidden and why, never likely. The simulator imports the model, the content store and the evaluator, never the reverse (GDD 1 Rule 11); polyscene is pinned at commit 0a0e8cb in the tools venv (an ADR decision, because an upgrade that adds the assumption mechanism would duplicate the generated block); nothing from this repo goes upstream (coordination rule 7). The trace is JSONL, one line per boundary so a crash leaves a partial file: header `{seed, rules_hash, content_hash, tuning_hash}`, per boundary `{beat, day, scene_id, chapter_id, decision_batch, steps: [{candidates (sorted), firings, rng_draws, changes}]}`, footer `{axes, ending}`; `candidates` lets parity tell "the policy chose X" from "X was the only option"; `explain(rule_id, ctx) → {holds, failing_leaves[]}` serves diagnosis parity and the debug inspector.

**12. Parity and acceptance.** Two directions. *Completeness (solver → runtime):* every polyscene witness telling, its NPC and world choices forced through the `ChoiceSource`, is realised by the runtime through Scene Flow on `NullSceneHost` and ends with the same key-axis values and ending class; a forced choice that is not a candidate fails as `CHOICE_NOT_ADMISSIBLE {rule, failing_leaves}`, which is the parity signal. *Soundness (runtime → solver):* every runtime trace, pinned as `occurs(a, t)` facts, returns `diagnose() = ∅` (an empty list means admissible; `None` means the physics itself forbids the trace, an encoder bug, and also fails). Because the policy is deterministic and act-one data has no RNG, the runtime tree branches only on player inputs, so CI enumerates the player-input tree exhaustively (a DAG merged by `(boundary, dossier digest)`), replays every leaf and diagnoses each: at MVP tens of traces, exhaustive verification rather than sampling; if the tree later exceeds `PLAYER_DAG_MAX` leaves, CI falls back to `SIM_RUNS_CI` seeded runs and records the fallback as debt, and a release export refuses while that debt stands (a sampled proof may gate a branch, never a shipped build). The generator also rejects a `move` whose `needs` reads exactly one field that exactly one decision option writes and nothing else reads (a private flag: a hand-authored branch wearing a rule's clothes, the anti-pillar) and warns when `needs` spans three or more decision-written fields. Acceptance: E1–E9 each reachable with a witness in the game space; E0 unreachable in the world space and `diagnose(E0)` names exactly `{no_one_unchanged}`; every axis value reachable; `act_fallback` selected in no witness (GDD 1 Formulas §5); `ground()` within `GROUND_BUDGET_S` at `HORIZON_EVENTS`; admissibility and realisability rates equal `PARITY_MIN`; the golden act-one fixture regenerates in meaning; every effectful content option maps to exactly one generated action and the reverse (`Topic.check()` under warnings-as-errors is a gate).

### States and Transitions

**Per rule instance, within one boundary (reset at `end_boundary`):** `dormant` (needs false or unlicensed) → `candidate` (needs and licence hold, delay elapsed, not spent) → `selected` (survived the policy) → `fired` (effects applied, in the result) → `spent` (cannot fire again this boundary). `candidate` → `deferred` when it lost the policy; a deferred rule may be a candidate again at the next step or boundary.

**World moves, per act:** `pending` (beat < lo) → `open` (beat in the window) → `armed` (licensed; `pick: seeded` draws its beat here) → `fired` (terminal for the act) or `lapsed` (beat > hi, terminal).

**Drift, per act:** `idle` (while false; anchor null) → `counting` (while true; anchor set) → ticks `fired` at each period multiple → `idle` when while turns false (anchor cleared).

**The evaluator:** `idle` → `evaluating` (inside Scene Flow's rules step) → `idle`; its state is `fired_this_boundary`, `drift_anchor[]`, `world_move_state[]`, `delay_anchor[]` and the RNG, all serialised by Save/Load **(PROV)**; it holds no dossier state and no queue.

## Formulas

*Proposed by the systems designer; the RNG from the AI programmer. Conventions as GDD 1: `[p]` is the Iverson bracket; values are integers unless stated; constants are names in `assets/data/rules/tuning.json` (D) or GDD 1's (by pointer).*

### 1. Candidate selection key

The selection formula is defined as:

`key(r) = (−priority(r), id(r))`; within a group `sel_agent = sort_key(candidates(agent))[0 : RULES_PER_AGENT_PER_BOUNDARY]`; across groups `sel = sort_key(∪ sel_agent)[0 : RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX − fired_so_far]` for agent moves, with world moves appended unbounded; ties on `priority` are broken by `id`, never by the RNG (equal priority within a `state: true` agent is a loader error).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `priority(r)` | int | 0–100 | data file (rule) | Authored ordering; default `PRIORITY_DEFAULT` |
| `id(r)` | string | `[a-z0-9_]+` | data file | Lexicographic tie-break; unique, so the order is total |
| `fired_so_far` | int | 0–`RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX` | calculated | Agent fires already applied this boundary, all steps |
| `RULES_PER_AGENT_PER_BOUNDARY` | int | 1–2 | constant (1) | Fires per agent per boundary |
| `RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX` | int | 1–8 | constant (4) | Agent fires per boundary across steps; world moves and drift exempt |
| `sel` | list | 0–`RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX` + world moves | calculated | Rules fired this step, in order |

**Output Range:** a list of length 0 to the remaining cap plus world moves, total and deterministic. **Example:** Vera's s3 boundary after `burn_report`: candidates `r1_query_ledger` (rybar, 70, `state: true`, `beat: insert`) and `b2_cover_vera` (berta, 55, `beat: paper`); `b1_report_colleague` (berta, 60) is held by Berta's `delay: 1` and is not yet a candidate → `[r1, b2]`; cap 4 → both fire, one insert and one paper line; at step 1 nothing new → stop. *Rejected:* a utility score from `weighs` (a second preference model).

### 2. Cascade bound

The cascade bound is defined as:

`fires_max = RULES_PER_BOUNDARY_MAX` (agent moves, across steps); `steps ≤ CASCADE_MAX`; `truncated = [candidates(step = CASCADE_MAX) ≠ ∅]`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `CASCADE_MAX` | int | 1–5 | constant (3) | Re-evaluation steps per boundary |
| `steps` | int | 1–`CASCADE_MAX` | calculated | Steps run |
| `truncated` | bool | — | calculated | Runtime: logged; simulator: build failure |

**Output Range:** bounded; one step minimum (no change), `CASCADE_MAX` maximum; cycles are impossible by construction because each rule fires at most once per boundary. **Example:** decision → K2 (step 0) → R1 reads `under_observation` (step 1) → nothing (step 2 ends early): `steps = 2`. *Rejected:* an unbounded fixpoint.

### 3. World-move firing beat

The world-move beat is defined as:

`b_fire(m) = min{b ∈ [lo, hi] : licensed(m, b)}` for `pick: fixed`; for `pick: seeded`, `b_fire = b_arm + rng.next_int(hi − b_arm + 1)` drawn once at `b_arm` (the first licensed beat) and discarded if the licence lapses before it; `b_fire = ∅` if never licensed in the window.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lo`, `hi` | int | 1–`HORIZON_ACT`, `lo ≤ hi` | data file | Window in beats |
| `licensed(m, b)` | bool | — | calculated | Any licence true at beat `b` |
| `b_arm` | int | `[lo, hi]` | calculated | First licensed beat |
| `b_fire` | int or ∅ | `[lo, hi]` | calculated | Firing beat |

**Output Range:** in the window or empty. **Example:** `inquiry_at_post [5, 10]`, `border.log_gap` set at beat 3 → `b_fire = 5`; the gap covered at 4 and `log_covered` licensing → still 5. *Rejected:* always-seeded windows.

### 4. Drift tick

The drift tick is defined as:

`tick(d, b) = [while(d, b)] · [b > a_d] · [(b − a_d) mod period_d = 0]`; the effect `add value_d` applies when `tick = 1`; `a_d` is the beat at which `while` last became true, cleared when it turns false.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `period_d` | int | 2–8 (0 off) | constant name (`DRIFT_PERIOD_ATTENTION` 3, `DRIFT_PERIOD_STABILITY` 3, GDD 1) | Beats per point |
| `value_d` | int | −2–+2 | data file | Signed step |
| `a_d` | int or null | 0–`HORIZON_ACT` | calculated (evaluator state) | Anchor beat |
| `tick` | int | 0–1 | calculated | Fire this beat |

**Output Range:** 0 or 1; the scalar is clamped by GDD 1's `scalar_apply`; over an act a drift contributes at most `⌊(HORIZON_ACT − a_d) / period⌋ · value`. **Example:** `vera.discrepancy` true at beat 3 → `vera.attention +1` at 6, 9, 12; with V3's own +1 the count reaches `ATTN_LOOK` at beat 9, which is why R1's flag licence, not the number, is what the writers rely on (`gap_is_noticed` needs an answer by beat 7). *Rejected:* stateless `beat mod period` (a drift that began at beat 4 would tick at 6, not 7, and the generator could not pin the same reading).

### 5. Parity predicate

The parity predicate is defined as:

`parity(T) = [diagnose(T) = ∅] ∧ ⋀_i [K_i^rt(T) = K_i^sol(T)] ∧ [ending^rt(T) = ending^sol(T)]`; `adm = |{T ∈ RUNS : diagnose(T) = ∅}| / |RUNS|` (soundness) and `real = |{W ∈ WITNESSES : axes and ending match}| / |WITNESSES|` (completeness); both must equal `PARITY_MIN`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `K_i^rt`, `K_i^sol` | enum | per key axis | calculated | Axis `i` at close, runtime versus solver |
| `RUNS` | set | the player-input DAG's leaves, or `SIM_RUNS_CI` seeded runs on fallback | calculated | Runtime traces |
| `WITNESSES` | set | one per ending class | calculated (`branches.json`) | Forced replays |
| `PLAYER_DAG_MAX` | int | 100–5,000 | constant (1,000) | Leaves above which CI falls back to sampling (recorded as debt) |
| `SIM_RUNS_CI` | int | 20–200 | constant (50) | Seeded runs on fallback |
| `PARITY_MIN` | float | 0.9–1.0 | constant (1.0) | Required rate |

**Output Range:** 0.0–1.0 each; the build fails below `PARITY_MIN`, naming the trace and the law (`adm`) or the axis (`real`), so a failure says "policy bug" or "realisation bug". **Example:** 23 DAG leaves and 9 witnesses, one leaf whose end state the solver calls `broken(crossing_read)` → `adm` 22/23 → fail naming the trace and the law. *Rejected:* a sampled rate accepted below 1.0 (an inadmissible telling would ship).

### 6. Coverage

The coverage formulas are defined as:

`class_cov = |{c ∈ C_req : witness_game(c)}| / |C_req|`; `axis_cov = Σ_a |reach(a)| / Σ_a |dom(a)|`; `e0_ok = [¬witness_world(E0)] ∧ [diagnose(E0) = {no_one_unchanged}]`; `fallback_ok = [∀ W : ending(W) ≠ act_fallback]`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C_req` | set | E1–E9 | data file (GDD 1's ending table) | Required classes |
| `reach(a)`, `dom(a)` | sets | per axis | calculated / data file | Reachable and declared values |
| `class_cov`, `axis_cov` | float | 0.0–1.0 | calculated | Must both be 1.0 |
| `e0_ok`, `fallback_ok` | bool | — | calculated | Must be true |

**Output Range:** 0–1 and booleans; acceptance needs 1.0, 1.0, true, true. **Example (prototype, world space):** 9/9, every axis value, E0 by exactly one law → pass; the game-space run is the one that must also pass. *Rejected:* a class threshold below 1.0.

### 7. Grounding-time budget

The grounding check is defined as:

`ground_ok = [median_{GROUND_SAMPLES}(t_ground(HORIZON_EVENTS)) ≤ GROUND_BUDGET_S]`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `t_ground` | float s | > 0 | measured | polyscene `ground()` wall time |
| `HORIZON_ACT` | int | 12–16 | constant (14 = `BEATS_PER_ACT` + `HORIZON_SLACK` 2) | Beat horizon |
| `HORIZON_EVENTS` | int | 14–40 | constant (set from measurement; prototype 14) | Solver step horizon |
| `GROUND_BUDGET_S` | float s | 2–15 | constant (5) | CI budget |
| `GROUND_SAMPLES` | int | 1–5 | constant (3) | Median sample count |

**Output Range:** boolean. **Example:** the prototype grounds in 1.3 s at 14 steps; act one at 24 steps is the first measurement; if it fails, solve per chapter (Rule 7). *Rejected:* a budget per `classes()` call only (grounding is the fixed cost every call pays).

### 8. Seeded RNG

The RNG is defined as:

`s ← s ⊕ (s ≪ 13); s ← s ⊕ (s ≫ 17); s ← s ⊕ (s ≪ 5)` over 32 bits; `next_int(n) = s mod n`; seed 0 is remapped to `0x9E3779B9`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `seed` | int | 1–2³²−1 | data file (Save/Load) | The saved seed |
| `s` | int | 32-bit | calculated (evaluator state) | Current state |
| `draws` | int | ≥ 0 | calculated | Draws consumed; a replay asserts the same count |
| `n` | int | 2–8 | calculated | Window length (the RNG never breaks a tie among agent moves) |

**Output Range:** `[0, n)`; modulo bias is irrelevant at `n ≤ 8`. **Example:** seed 1, the first 1,000 draws equal a checked-in golden list produced by the Python twin. *Rejected:* the engine's `RandomNumberGenerator` (seed persistence and cross-platform stability **NOT SOURCEABLE**).

## Edge Cases

*From the systems designer over the rule and formula space; two resolutions it proposed beyond the rulings are accepted and marked.*

1. **If two selected rules of different agents write the same field in one step**: both effects enter the one rules batch in selection order (Formulas §1) and GDD 1's precedence for list order within writer `rules` resolves them: a later `set` overrides an earlier one, `add` accumulates, `max` keeps the larger; each record keeps its own `source` and change record. *One batch per step is the contract with GDD 1; per-rule `apply` would expose cascade order to the dossier.*
2. **If every effect of a fired rule is a no-op** (before equals after on every record): a debug assert names the rule and boundary; no change record is written; the firing stays in `fired[]` (it was selected, and Scene Flow still resolves its beat); if the step produced no other record the cascade stops. *An all-no-op firing is Rule 10's authoring smell, not a runtime error.*
3. **If a world move is first licensed at the boundary whose beat equals `hi`**: it fires there (windows are inclusive); at `hi + 1` it is `lapsed`.
4. **If a world move's window lies entirely in the slack beats**: legal; it can fire only at the act-close boundaries, so its licence must be satisfiable by state reached at beat `BEATS_PER_ACT`; `beat: insert` on such a move is a lint warning (no next authored scene to insert before, **PROV: Scene Flow**); the coverage table names it "no witness" if it is never reachable.
5. **If a `delay` move's needs lapse before the delay elapses**: the delay anchor clears; the move is not a candidate; when the needs hold again the anchor restarts from that beat (restart, not resume, as drift). *Delay elapsed is necessary, never sufficient: needs are re-evaluated at the firing boundary.*
6. **If a `move` carries `window` or a `world_move` carries `delay`**: the loader rejects it. *An NPC move is timed by needs and delay, a world move by window and licence; a window on an NPC move is `beat_in` by another name, history in `needs`.*
7. **If the cascade reaches `CASCADE_MAX` with candidates remaining**: at runtime `truncated = true`, a log lists the remaining candidate ids, and they are dropped, not queued (re-evaluated next boundary); in the simulator it is a build failure naming the boundary and the fired chain, so for shipped data runtime truncation is unreachable and the runtime log guards only data that bypassed CI. *Four reactions to one decision is a pacing bug.*
8. **If a rule is a candidate at some boundary in every telling and never survives the policy**: starvation. For a non-state agent it is not a law: the lint log lists `never_fired` rules with the count of boundaries at which they were candidates, as a warning, and raising the priority is the writer's call. For a `state: true` agent it is a build failure naming the move and a boundary at which it was a candidate, because `licensed_answers` (Rule 5.3) says the state always moves when the paper is there. *A non-state rule dead in the game space may still shape the world space; a state rule may not.*
9. **If act-one data contains `pick: seeded`**: the generator rejects it naming the rule id; in every act the loader rejects `pick` on a `move`, and `pick: seeded` on a regime announcement or on a world move authored for a `state: true` agent. *The field stays in the format for non-state clocks; act-one parity rests on no RNG so the player-input DAG is exhaustive.*
10. **If rule data contains `has_fact` or a hand-written `beat_in`**: the loader rejects both. *Held facts are the player's (GDD 5); `beat_in` is emitted only from `window`.*
11. **If a licence reads `was_assumed F` and no chapter assumes F**: `was_assumed` returns false (GDD 1's provenance carries no assumption) and the licence fails; the generator warns, not errors, because a later chapter in the act may assume it; the `unknown` record's `licenses` list is the coverage check that turns an unused unknown into an error.
12. **If an unknown is contradicted mid-act** (a change record with `contradicts ≠ null` on the assumed field): `{contradicted: F}` is true for that boundary, `was_assumed F` is false from then on, and moves licensed only by the assumption lose their licence at the next step; the `settles_as` tag is not written, because settlement (GDD 1 Rule 8) applies at act close to assumptions that survived.
13. **If `diagnose()` returns `None` for a runtime trace**: the physics forbids the pinned facts; no model matches. This is an encoder bug (the generator or the action mapping), reported as `TRACE_NOT_GROUNDABLE` with the first step that has no model, counted separately from the admissibility rate. *Folding it into a broken-law count would send a writer hunting for a law that does not exist.*
14. **If the golden fixture disagrees with the generator after a brief amendment** (GDD 5's V1 amendment is the first): the fixture is re-cut from the amended prototype, its `classes()` table, end-state count and `diagnose(E0)` recorded and committed as its own change; the old fixture is never edited until it passes. *A fixture patched to pass is no fixture.*
15. **If `Topic.check()` emits a warning**: the build fails with the warning text as the message. *The check is the proof that every effectful option maps to exactly one generated action and back.*
16. **If a content option has effects the generator cannot map to a player action** (an effect outside the schema, or batches under mixed writers): the option-mapping lint fails the build naming the option and effect; the generator never emits a partial action. *A half-mapped decision would make the game space lie.*
17. **If the player-input DAG exceeds `PLAYER_DAG_MAX` leaves**: CI stops enumerating at the cap, runs `SIM_RUNS_CI` seeded runs, computes the admissibility rate over the sample and records the fallback as debt with the leaf count; a release export refuses while the debt stands. *The exhaustive check is restored by raising the constant or merging more aggressively, never by lowering `PARITY_MIN`.*
18. **If `ground()` exceeds `GROUND_BUDGET_S` at `HORIZON_EVENTS`**: the budget check fails and the remedy is per-chapter solving: each chapter's instance seeded from every digest-distinct end state of the previous chapter's game space, acceptance checked over the union *(accepted beyond the rulings)*. *`HORIZON_EVENTS` is measured, not tuned down to fit.*
19. **If a trace file has no footer** (a crash mid-run): the run fails as `TRACE_INCOMPLETE` naming the last boundary line; the partial JSONL is kept as the artefact; the trace is excluded from the rate (neither admissible nor inadmissible). *The partial file is the diagnostic.*
20. **If Save/Load presents evaluator state whose chapter id differs from the loading context's**: `deserialize` refuses with `STATE_CHAPTER_MISMATCH`; nothing is restored; Save/Load owns the fallback **(PROV)**. *A drift or delay anchor from another act is a beat on a different counter.*
21. **If `run_boundary` is called twice for one boundary** (beat unchanged and `fired_this_boundary` non-empty, drift ticks counting as fired *(accepted beyond the rulings)*): refused with `BOUNDARY_ALREADY_RUN`, nothing applied. *Without the guard drift would tick twice at one beat and the trace would carry two lines for one boundary.*
22. **If `regime_paranoid`'s licence holds before `PARANOID_EARLIEST`**: nothing fires; the window gate precedes the licence and the move is `pending`; at `PARANOID_EARLIEST` it opens and, if still licensed, fires there. *Facts license, windows time.*
23. **If an `incompatible` pair holds after an `apply`**: a debug assert names the pair, batch and `source`; release logs the same at error level and continues; nothing is rolled back, because the dossier is the truth. *A runtime rollback would desynchronise trace from state, and the CI replay already finds the pair as a space-level violation.*
24. **If Scene Flow's context lacks a chapter or scene id**: `run_boundary` refuses with `CTX_INCOMPLETE` and applies nothing, drift included. *The `source` stamp cannot be formed, and a firing with no citable source cannot be diagnosed or replayed.*

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Required for a Gameplay system; written with the art director from art bible §2.1–2.2, §3.3, §6.3, §7.1, §7.4–7.5 and the brief's tone section. This system draws nothing. The player sees its output only as what the art bible already fixes for the state's arrival: a person through a threshold, or paper on a surface. The requirements are constraints on the rendering of a `RulesResult`, not new visuals.*

**1. What a fired rule looks like, by `beat`.** *`insert`, a person enters through a threshold.* The art bible fixes the reactive-event state (§2.1): the room's light source is replaced by light from the threshold (torch through the door, sodium through the window, one bare bulb over the telephone) with hard shadow cast from the threshold and red crossing the threshold line at the phase's reach (§2.2); thresholds are drawn thick and hard (§3.3) precisely so this entry reads. This system guarantees that **every `insert` enters through a declared threshold hotspot of GDD 3** (telephone, door, window, the gate, the post), one of the plate's 3–8 hotspots, never an added object: Rybar's call is the telephone; the captain reading the log is the door of the post; a knock is a person at the door. The person is lit by the threshold's light, in portrait crop as on the transcript sheet (§7.1); the hand on any arriving document names the clerk or office that sent it, never the protagonist whose assumption licensed the move (Core Rule 9); nothing about an insert is a cutscene, a camera move or a vignette, and no insert is announced by a light change before the figure is on the threshold line. *`paper`, a document arrives.* "More paper, not more police." Every `paper` event is a **document object of GDD 2 with a form and a hand**: a query slip in the intake tray, a card by the morning post, a list retyped in the stamp cabinet, a line in the reveal step. It lands by §7.4's paper motion (rigid slide, 180 ms, ease-out, no rotation beyond 2°); its form follows §2.2's paper-order axis (gridded and sparse at Confident, carbon copies at Paranoid, misregistered state typing only from Panicking, not in act one); its hand is the only authorship signal. **Nothing fires as floating text, a toast, a banner or a notification** (§7.1's reviewer test). A `paper` beat the protagonist never sees (Kastan's slip to Jan's file) renders nowhere in that chapter and appears as overprinted ink in the dossier of the chapter that owns it (§2.1, Pillar 4).

**2. The regime announcement is paper density.** `regime_paranoid` is a `paper` world move whose announcement is the state-space prop count doubling per phase (§6.3), carbon copies multiplying, lamps lowering; nothing else. The move must not fire a red flash, a sting, a vignette, a colour grade or any overlay change of its own: red is the Calendar's attention signal, the date stamp's ink reach and the surveillance stamp's fill (§2.1, §7.5), moved by the phase function and rendered by Overlay. A phase change seen on the plate before it is seen as more paper is a defect.

**3. The cascade.** When two things fire at one boundary they present as **one insert scene, then paper in the reveal step**; the second firing becomes the slip, card or line the player finds after the person has left. Never two inserts at one boundary: Scene Flow's presentation rule orders the firings and turns the second insert into paper; no rule data is written to arrange it (Berta's `delay: 1` is her habit, Core Rule 2, not a scheduling device). A truncated cascade shows nothing extra: the paper that did not fire does not arrive.

**4. Drift ticks** are invisible on the plate. Attention and stability drift are read only through the dossier: the Files view's threshold line at `ATTN_LOOK`. No prop, light or sound marks a tick.

**5. Simulator and debug outputs.** The writers' outputs are plain text in the terminal, unstyled: no colour, icons or box drawing. The runtime rule inspector is debug-only, a developer overlay, never a sheet, never shipped, exempt from §7.1 because it is not in the game.

**6. Audio naming** (for the audio director; the state arrives with its object, so each cue is named by its threshold or paper, never by its rule, following §8.2): `sfx_threshold_telephone_ring_01` (Rybar's call), `sfx_threshold_door_knock_01` (door, gate), `sfx_threshold_boots_captain_01` (the captain at the post), `sfx_paper_post_drop_01` (the morning post), `sfx_paper_card_slide_01` (the card, the slip, the list), and the `sfx_state_moves_*` family for `paper` world moves and the regime announcement, dry and clerical (a stamp, a drawer, a typewriter carriage, a tray), never a sting, a swell or a tone. The audio director owns how they play.

**7. The one art rule this system must obey.** The state never arrives as a sound or a light before it arrives as paper or a person. The threshold light change belongs to the figure already on the threshold line; the cue belongs to the object already in the frame. If a rule fires and nothing is in the room yet, the player sees nothing yet.

> 📌 **Asset Spec** — Visual/Audio requirements are defined. After the art bible is approved, run `/asset-spec system:reactive-event-rules` to produce per-asset visual descriptions, dimensions, and generation prompts from this section.

## Game Feel

Not a feel system in itself, but two feel properties follow from it.

- **The state moves only at scene boundaries.** Nothing fires mid-scene; a room never changes while the player is reading it (GDD 3's "the room waits"). The evaluation and its cascade complete within the transition, under the technical director's budget, so the paper wipe never waits on a rule.
- **A move is felt as paper, a beat late.** A fired class reaches the player as an inserted scene or a paper line at the next boundary, in the clerical passive, never as a pop-up and never explained. The dread is the lag, not a jump scare.

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen and must not have one in release builds. It owns no HUD element, no event log the player can read, and no indicator of attention or phase (those are the Calendar's signal rendered by Overlay). Debug builds only: an inspector that lists the boundary's candidates with `explain(rule_id, ctx)` (which leaves held and which failed), the firings and the cascade steps, reachable only through the debug menu and absent from release (the same rule as GDD 2's debug surfaces). The writers' tools are command-line under `tools/sim/`: the coverage table, the witness tellings, the plain-words diagnosis, the "rests on" list, the per-chapter "what fired" list and the emergent-chain report; none of them is in-game.

> **📌 UX Flag — Reactive Event Rules & Story Simulator**: no player-facing UI. The one player-visible consequence of this system, the inserted scene and the paper line, is Scene Flow's and Paper UI's; the `/ux-design` work belongs to those systems.
