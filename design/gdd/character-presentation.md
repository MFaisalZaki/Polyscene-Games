## Overview

*Framing: infrastructure first (Category `Core`, Layer Foundation), with its player-facing effect named because people are what the player looks at. A stub at MVP: this GDD fixes the interface and ships the standing-layer behaviour; the walking outcome stays a conditional paragraph until the art-pipeline proof decides `presentation.mode`. No ADR cites it yet; ADR-5 (the renderer) and ADR-6 (assets) will read it.*

Character Presentation puts people in rooms and faces on sheets. It decides which named characters are present in a scene and in which pose, from content conditions resolved at scene entry and never mid-scene; it draws each as a standing figure layer on the plate, y-sorted with the hotspots by its baseline, at the second-highest contrast in the frame, with its printed keyline and the thicker keyline when the player's focus is on it; it hands Paper UI the ID-photo portrait for the transcript sheet in one of four states (neutral, guarded, evasive, exposed) that the writer chose for that line and the engine never infers; it places the state's person on the threshold line when a reactive event cuts in; and it supplies the one mark that turns a citizen into an informer, the carbon "cc" overprint on the portrait, only once the player holds the fact. The player never operates it; they read it: a figure where the file said someone would be, a face that will not meet the lens, a door with a captain in it. Without it Pillar 3 would have no faces to be unclean in and Pillar 2 no body to arrive with; with more than it (a mood meter on a face, a figure that breathes while you read) Pillar 5 and the art bible's stiff, printed people would be gone. Its logic is headless (a cast plan per scene entry, plain data, byte-equal across hosts) and its drawing lives behind a host seam with a null twin, like the Scene & Interaction Framework's and Overlay's. Under the walking outcome it would also own the player's avatar, its walk, facing and hand-over; that paragraph is written and dormant.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees Character Presentation; they see people standing in rooms and faces pinned to paper, and learn that neither one gives a verdict. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "nobody looks guilty" chosen, from art bible §5.2–5.3 and Pillar 3; "the face is the writer's, the mark is yours" kept as the one rule; "the file has a face, the room has a body" kept as how the plate and the sheet divide a person, §5.5).*

**Nobody looks guilty.** This system serves two feelings that pull against each other. The first is uncertainty about people. Everyone in the city stands the same way: stiff, weight on both feet, hands low, faces too small to read at plate scale. The informer on the stairs is drawn exactly like the woman in the bread queue, and the room will never tell you which is which. The second is the moment that changes, and it comes only from paper. When you hold the fact, the face on the sheet gets a carbon double of its own outline, two pixels off true, and a small "cc" in its corner. It is the same blue mark your own filings leave, because knowing someone informs and having it on file are one thing. The figure in the room stays unmarked. The room shows people; only the file can mark one.

The anchor moment: Vera's office, Friday 6 October. Berta is at the next desk as a standing layer, seated, still, at second-highest contrast, with a 2 px soot keyline that thickens to 3 px when Vera's attention rests on her. In conversation Berta's ID photo is stapled top-left on the transcript sheet. When Vera asks after the missing copy, the photo changes because the writer asked it to: chin down five degrees, mouth tightened. That is Guarded, the look of anyone asked a question at work, and it proves nothing. The same week Vera reads report 4471 to the end and holds the fact that the hand is Kastan's, the caretaker at Vrba Street. The next time Kastan's face is on a sheet, on the landing asking after the parcel, it carries the blue double strike and the "cc". Kastan is still on his stairs, drawn as he always was. Then Rybar calls. The receiver is off its cradle on the first cut, and the light over the telephone is replaced on the second; his ID photo is pinned to the transcript sheet, lit from the bulb, and no man is drawn in the room, because no man is in it. When the state does come in person, at a door, a gate or a post, its figure is on the threshold line before the light changes. It does not walk in, it does not gesture, and the camera does not move. The state arrives the way people do in this game: already standing there when you look up.

Pillar alignment. Pillar 3, "no one is clean": expression is the only acting in this game, and it is limited to gaze, chin and mouth in four checkable states, Neutral, Guarded, Evasive, Exposed (§5.4). Evasive is how a frightened innocent looks too. Shame is carried by the eyes looking away, not by performance, and no face tells the player whom to condemn. Pillar 1, "every fact is a weapon": the informer's mark is a fact turned into a drawing, and it exists only because the player holds that fact; no fact ever lives on a figure at plate scale (§5.5). Pillar 2, "the state is a character": the state has a body here too, and it enters through a threshold, standing where the light changes, never in a cutscene. Pillar 5, "pressure comes from attention, not reflexes": nobody moves while the player reads. Figures do not breathe, shift or wander. A person is present or absent from the moment the room opens, decided by the file as it stood at the door.

**The one rule this system must obey: a face shows only what the writer asked of it, and a person is marked only by what the player holds.** Every portrait state comes from a one-word request on a dialogue item. If a line has no request, the face is Neutral. The engine never infers an expression from attention, suspicion, phase or anything else in the dossier, so a frightened face is always a choice someone made and never a reading of the meter. The informer overprint is a layer, not a fifth state. It appears on the portrait once the fact is held, and it never appears on the figure, the costume or the plate. Who stands in a room is settled when the player walks in, and a held fact never makes someone appear, so no one becomes an option just because the player learned something. No figure is tinted red; colour says who is watching, never who is guilty.

*Cost accepted:* this is a stub at MVP and honest about it. Vera's chapter ships the standing outcome: each named character is a per-scene plate layer with one pose per scene state, removed when the file says they are absent; the player's own figure is never shown; and the portraits carry all the expression. Whether protagonists later walk to the desk (walk cycles, facing, idle, hand-over, page-turn) waits for the MVP art-pipeline proof; it is written as a conditional so the switch costs nothing, and plate-scale detail is identical under both outcomes. Until then a person cannot cross a room, and the player may feel the rooms are tableaux. Restrained faces mean some players will misread a Guarded face as guilt; the game lets them, because the city did too. And the player who spots the informer early, from instinct, gets no confirmation from the drawing. Until the paper says so, Kastan looks like everyone else, and some players will find that frustrating. That frustration is the cost of the pillar, and it is accepted.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary), the art director (the six art rulings), the technical artist (the figure pipeline) and the gameplay programmer; rulings where they differed are stated in place. Engine facts are quoted only from the pinned reference (4.7 `CanvasItem` lines draw without a feather, so keylines are painted, never coded; `AnimationPlayer` is sourced for the walking outcome, the sprite node is a spike). Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a GDD not yet written (Dialogue 11, Paper UI 13, Audio 16). Two rulings by the main session where the specialists differed: the telephone insert's receiver is painted into the insert sidecar's telephone `layer` (the systems designer's mechanism, because the transcript's input block would drop a focus lift) **and the caller's ID-photo portrait stays pinned on the transcript sheet** (the art director's ruling: §7.1 is unconditional, and the four states must live somewhere); a portrait word holds until the next word within one dialogue run and every run opens Neutral (the art director's ruling: an unannotated line snapping back to Neutral is a flinch nobody wrote). The amendments to GDDs 2, 3, 8 and 9 this needed are applied there and named where they bite.*

*Drafted with the systems designer (primary); art bible §5 and §8.7 and GDD 3 Rules 1, 5, 10 are treated as locked and cited by section. Where this draft departs from the brief's working proposals the departure is stated in place with the alternative it displaced. The stub ships the standing outcome; the walking outcome is one conditional rule (Rule 12), live only under `presentation.mode = walking` (GDD 3 Rule 10's flag), exactly as GDD 3 carries its own.*

**0. Vocabulary.** A *figure* is one named character drawn on a plate: under the standing outcome a cropped layer (≤ 512², art bible §8.7 T8), under walking a sprite. A *pose* is one authored figure crop for one body position (`stands`, `sits`, `reads`, `hands_over`, `on_threshold`; Rule 2); a *scene state* is the pose a scene shows a person in, chosen once at entry from the person's `poses[]` by GDD 2's condition grammar (Rule 3). *Presence* is whether a named character is in the room at all, resolved once at entry (Rule 3). A *person hotspot* is GDD 3's `kind: person` record: the hit region, `baseline_y`, `lift_anchor` and `name_key`, and nothing drawn (Rule 2). A *portrait* is the ID-photo asset of one named character in one of exactly four *portrait states*, `neutral | guarded | evasive | exposed` (art bible §5.4), pinned to the transcript sheet by Paper UI **(PROV)**. A *portrait request* is the one word a dialogue item may carry (GDD 2 Rule 5c); the *vocabulary* is the closed set V31 checks it against (Rule 7). The *overprint* is the informer tell, a carbon duplicate of the portrait's outline offset 2–3 px plus a "cc" mark, applied in-engine once the player holds the informer fact (§5.2; Rule 8). The *keyline* is the 2 px soot line painted into every figure crop, 3 px in its focus variant (§5.5, GDD 3 VA §2). The `figure_band` is GDD 3's sidecar polygon (walkable floor plus one figure-height of wall, §6.6). The *plan* (`CastPlan`) is the plain-data result of one computation at scene entry: every present figure with its pose, crop and draw order, the insert's threshold figure, the portrait set to make resident, the byte and draw estimates. The *host* is the thin seam through which the plan becomes nodes; its *null twin* records. A *cut* is a change that completes on one frame with no intermediate value.

**1. The stub ships the standing outcome whole, and the walking outcome as one dormant rule.** The MVP is Vera's chapter and the art-pipeline proof runs inside it, so the MVP cannot wait for the proof's verdict: this system ships, under `presentation.mode = standing` (GDD 3 Rule 10; "standing until then", GDD 3 Open Questions), (a) named characters as per-scene standing layers, one pose per scene state (§5.5); (b) presence by authored condition, resolved at entry (Rule 3); (c) portraits in four states on the transcript sheet, chosen per dialogue item by the writer's word (Rule 7); (d) the informer overprint flag (Rule 8); (e) the 3 px focus keyline on a focused person (Rule 5); (f) the insert's figure on the threshold line at the first cut (Rule 9). Rule 12 (walking) is written in full so that a `walking` verdict costs no GDD rewrite, and **nothing in Rules 2–11 reads the flag except Rule 5's draw-order mechanism and Rule 10's byte line**; every data file in Rule 2 is valid under both outcomes (plate-scale detail is identical in both, §5.5). *Rationale:* GDD 3 made the flag select a live paragraph, not a live system; this GDD must be the same shape or the MVP would ship a presentation layer nobody specified. *Rejected:* a stub that defers portraits to Vertical Slice (the transcript sheet is MVP, Paper UI row 13, and a transcript with no face is a different design); shipping a walking avatar provisionally "to test" (the proof is the test, and an avatar in the MVP build before the creative director's call pre-empts the call).

**2. Two data files, two owners: the artist's `figures` block in the sidecar, the writer's `persons[]` in the content scene; the vocabularies are this system's data.** GDD 3's `scene.json` gains one block (GDD 3 Rule 1 amendment, the same shape as GDD 9's `overlay` block; written by the art export, normalised by the compiler, never hand-edited):

```yaml
figures:
  schema_version: 1
  entries:
    - hotspot: berta_desk            # a kind: person hotspot (authored scene) or the threshold hotspot (insert)
      cast_id: berta                 # GDD 2 cast table id; cross-checked in tools/ (X03)
      poses:
        sits:    { layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_sits.png,   x: 1180, y: 412 }, focus_layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_sits_f.png,   x: 1180, y: 412 } }
        stands:  { layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_stands.png, x: 1174, y: 388 }, focus_layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_stands_f.png, x: 1174, y: 388 } }
```

The content scene row (GDD 2 Rule 5, `scene` type; amendment) gains `persons[]`:

```yaml
persons:
  - cast_id: berta
    hotspot: berta_desk
    present_when: { is_false: berta.hospitalised }      # optional; absent = always present; has_fact forbidden (Rule 3)
    poses:                                              # first true wins; the last entry has no when (the default pose)
      - { pose: stands, when: { is_true: berta.implicated } }
      - { pose: sits }
```

Two vocabularies live in `assets/data/characters/`: `portrait_vocab.json` (`neutral, guarded, evasive, exposed`; Rule 7) and `pose_vocab.json` (`stands, sits, reads, hands_over, on_threshold`; art bible §3.2's "walking, sitting, handing over, turning a page" plus standing idle, with `reads` as the bible's page-turn held still and `on_threshold` for Rule 9; `walks` is the avatar's and never a pose). A third file, `cast_presentation.json`, maps each cast id to its four portrait asset ids and its `informer_fact` (Rule 8). The person hotspot record itself carries **no `layer`, `focus_layer` or `layer_filed`** (GDD 3 Rule 1 and E08 amendment: those three are absent for `kind: person` and required for every other kind): the figure is this system's layer, the hotspot is GDD 3's hit region, `baseline_y` and ring position. Lints, run by GDD 2's validator in "registry absent" mode until this GDD lands, then as errors: **CP-E1** every `kind: person` hotspot in an authored scene has exactly one `persons[]` entry and every entry names a `kind: person` hotspot (an insert's entry names its `threshold: true` hotspot, Rule 9); **CP-E2** `pose` in `pose_vocab.json`; **CP-E3** the last `poses[]` entry carries no `when`; **CP-E4** `persons[]` never names the chapter's protagonist (the player's own figure is never shown, §5.5); **CP-E5** `|persons[]| ≤ FIG_MAX` (6, T3); **CP-E6** `cast_id` exists in the cast table. In `tools/` only (GDD 3 Rule 2's precedent): **X03** every content `(hotspot, pose)` has a crop in the sidecar's `figures` block with the same `cast_id` (error), and every sidecar crop is named by some content pose (warning: an unused crop is resident bytes for nothing). The sidecar validator (`CastDataValidator`, Rule 4): **CF-E1** `layer` and `focus_layer` exist, ≤ 512², even dimensions, integer offsets, equal bounds (GDD 3 E08's argument: a swap must not shift); **CF-E2** every crop's bounds lie inside `figure_band`; **CF-E3** `red_px = 0` on every figure crop (T7 Gate A runs over figure crops as it does over hotspot crops, GDD 3 VA §6); **CF-W4** crop height outside 15–20% of plate height, 162–216 px at 1080 (§5.5, T8). *Rationale:* who is in the room and why is a writer's fact; what they look like standing there is a painter's; GDD 9 split `overlay` the same way and the cross-check in `tools/` is where the two may legally meet. *Rejected:* conditions in the sidecar (it is never hand-edited and the art export cannot write a dossier condition); crops in content (a writer's file naming PNG offsets); one free-text `pose` string (the asset-spec, the lint and T8's naming need a closed set; adding `writes`/`signs` is an art-bible §3.2 amendment for the art director, Open Questions).

**3. Presence and pose are resolved once, at `enter_scene`, by Scene Flow's reader, and this system never evaluates a condition.** Scene Flow evaluates each `persons[]` entry's `present_when` and `poses[].when` through the chapter's `ConditionReader` at the moment it emits `enter_scene` (GDD 8 Rule 13; after the boundary's apply, so the dossier it reads is the one the room is entered with, GDD 8 Rule 5) and carries the result in the sink call: **`enter_scene(scene_id, scene_kind, slot, protagonist_id, cast[])`** with `cast = [{cast_id, hotspot, present: bool, pose}]` (GDD 8 Rule 13 amendment). Scene Flow's availability derivation (GDD 8 Rule 6) gains the one line that produces `hidden`: `hidden iff kind(h) == person and cast[h].present == false` (amendment: the rule's "never at MVP; reserved" is spent here), and `set_hotspot_state(h, hidden)` is called before activation as every entry derivation is (GDD 3 Interactions). An absent person is therefore no layer, no hit region, out of the ring, uncounted (GDD 3 Rule 5), and this system draws nothing for them. **`has_fact` is forbidden in `present_when` and in any pose `when`** (CP-E7, GDD 2 V18's forbidden-leaf list): a person who appears because two cards clicked is a late-appearing affordance, GDD 5 Rule 4's "existence, not selection" (GDD 5 Edge Case 23 forbids the same for exits). `at_least`/`at_most` in `present_when` is legal (the state posts a watcher at the next desk when `vera.attention ≥ ATTN_WATCHED`: a consequence on paper and in the room); in a pose `when` it **warns** (CP-W8: "a posture keyed on a scalar is a meter in a body", Pillar 5 and the anti-pillar on morality meters; the writer may keep it). Reads in `present_when` and pose `when` are content reads counted in `R_c` by `Condition.fields_in` and declared in the chapter's `reads[]` like any `when` (GDD 2 V18). Nothing is re-resolved in-scene: not on a `changed` signal, not on a settings change, not on a held fact, not on a clock; the one in-scene change a figure undergoes is its focus variant (Rule 5). After a load, `enter` re-runs the same evaluation (GDD 8 Rule 14's "the same path as an unloaded game"); this system persists nothing. *Rationale:* GDD 3 Rule 5 lets only Scene Flow set availability, and GDD 1's headless rule and GDD 9 Rule 14's precedent keep a presentation core dossier-blind; one evaluation, by the system that already derives availability at entry, is the only arrangement that satisfies both and gives the simulator one record (`enter_scene`'s `cast[]`) to diff a `CastPlan` from. *Rejected:* this system evaluating `present_when` itself through an injected reader, GDD 5 Rule 8's pattern (two systems evaluating one condition at two moments, and the `hidden` call would still have to be Scene Flow's); `set_present(cast_id, bool)` as a host call (the brief's seam: a call that can be made mid-scene is a door to GDD 8 VA §6's forbidden idle change, so presence is a plan field, not a call); presence as a dossier field (`berta.in_room` is presentation-only state, which GDD 1 forbids).

**4. A headless core plans, an engine host places, and the plan is plain data byte-equal across hosts.** `CharacterCore extends RefCounted`, constructed with a `DebugSink` (`mode ∈ {assert, log}`, GDD 9 Rule 1's shape), exposes pure functions: `plan(input) → CastPlan`, `resolve_portrait(cast_id, word, context, scene_kind, threshold_kind, has_fact: Callable) → PortraitSpec` (Rules 7–8), `draw_order(figures) → [cast_id]` (Rule 5), and the validator `CastDataValidator.check(sidecar) → findings[]` (Rule 2). Its `input` is plain data assembled by the engine `PresentationSink` fan-out (GDD 8 Rule 13): `{sidecar.figures, sidecar.hotspots (baseline_y, kind, threshold), sidecar.figure_band, cast[] (Rule 3), scene_kind, protagonist_id, speakers[] (the distinct speakers of the scene's non-telephone dialogues, from the content store), cast_presentation, mode}`. The plan:

```yaml
CastPlan:
  schema_version: 1
  scene_id: a1_vera/v_office
  scene_kind: authored                 # authored | insert
  mode: standing                       # standing | walking (Rule 12)
  figures:                             # present persons only, already in draw order (Rule 5)
    - { cast_id: berta, hotspot: berta_desk, pose: sits, baseline_y: 640,
        layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_sits.png, x: 1180, y: 412 },
        focus_layer: { png: v_office_fig_berta_sits_f.png, x: 1180, y: 412 } }
  absent: [kastan]                     # resolved absent; their hotspots are hidden by Scene Flow, not by this plan
  threshold_figure: null               # inserts only (Rule 9): {cast_id, hotspot, pose: on_threshold, layer} or null (telephone)
  threshold_kind: null                 # inserts only: door | window | phone — selects the portrait lighting layer (Rule 7)
  portraits:                           # the resident set: ≤ PORTRAITS_RESIDENT_MAX 24
    - { cast_id: berta, states: { neutral: por_berta_neutral, guarded: por_berta_guarded, evasive: por_berta_evasive, exposed: por_berta_exposed }, informer_fact: null }
    - { cast_id: kastan, states: { ... }, informer_fact: riba_is_kastan }
  bytes_est: 2621440                   # figure crops + portraits, Formulas (Section D); integer bytes
  draws_est: 1                         # one per present figure (+1 threshold figure); ≤ FIG_MAX
  findings: []                         # DebugSink codes raised while planning
```

Keys sorted at serialisation, no floats, computed at `scene_preloaded` (so `load` can bring crops and portraits in before `scene_ready`) and **never recomputed while the scene is `preloading`, `ready`, `active` or `suspended`** (a second `scene_preloaded` for the same id is refused `PLAN_LOCKED`, GDD 9 Edge Case 22's shape). `CharacterHost extends RefCounted` (abstract; `@abstract` sourced for 4.5, breaking-changes.md line 69, syntax verified in GDD 3's spike) has two implementations: `EngineCharacterHost` holds the figure nodes parented under the y-sort root GDD 3 provides (GDD 3 Interactions) and `NullCharacterHost` records every call in order and returns what a test sets. Calls: `load(plan) → {ok, errors[]}` (crops and portraits resident, nothing visible; at `scene_preloaded`); `apply(plan)` (at `scene_ready`, a cut: every figure and the threshold figure appear on frame one); `set_focus_keyline(hotspot_id, on: bool)` (Rule 5; the one in-scene call); `clear(scene_id)` (at `scene_exited`); `report_resident_bytes(owner = "character", scene_id, bytes)` into GDD 3's W13 sheet; `portrait_texture(asset_id)` for Paper UI **(PROV)**, which draws the sheet. The walking twin adds `walk_to`, `face`, `play` (Rule 12). `src/character/core/*` imports nothing from the scene tree or `src/character/engine/*`; `tools/sim/` and `tests/unit/character/` bind `NullCharacterHost`; the `CharacterHost` contract suite runs against both twins; the CI path check (GDD 1 Rule 11, GDD 2 Rule 14, GDD 3 Rule 14) extends to `src/character/`. *Rationale:* GDD 3, 8 and 9 each put every decision in a headless core and every pixel behind a null-twinned seam; the simulator must be able to say "Berta is seated at the next desk and Kastan is absent" from a recorded `enter_scene` with no renderer. *Rejected:* the brief's per-aspect calls `set_pose`, `set_present`, `place_on_threshold` (each is a mid-scene mutation path; as plan fields they cannot be called at the wrong time); a scene-tree `Node` as the system's root (GDD 3 Rule 8's argument: one import and the simulator cannot load it); a `Resource` for the plan (engine serialisation drift; GDD 1's ADR-4 principle).

**5. Draw order is `baseline_y`, computed once under standing; the keyline is painted, and focus is a crop swap on GDD 3's focus signal.** Figures and hotspot layers share GDD 3's y-sort root and sort by `baseline_y` ascending, ties by id (GDD 3 Rule 1: `baseline_y` is "the y-sort key Character Presentation reads"). Under standing nothing moves after `scene_ready`, so the order is a pure function computed at plan time (`draw_order`, unit-tested headless) and applied as sibling order or `z_index` at `apply`; the engine's `y_sort_enabled` is needed only under walking (Rule 12) and stays on GDD 3's spike list (NOT SOURCEABLE, GDD 3 Rule 15). Every figure crop carries its 2 px soot keyline painted in (§5.5; the painted keyline costs no draw, GDD 3 Formulas §8's rejection of "a draw per keyline") and ships with a `focus_layer` twin carrying the 3 px keyline at equal bounds (CF-E1). **Focus on a person is the swap to `focus_layer`, no lift, no translate** (GDD 3 VA §2: "people do not lift"), performed by this system when GDD 3 reports focus landing on a `kind: person` id and reversed when it leaves. GDD 3 Rule 8's host declares no focus signal today; **amendment to GDD 3 Rule 8:** the host gains `focus_changed(hotspot_id | none, prev_id | none, source)`, emitted by `FocusController` on every set and clear, and `NullSceneHost` emits it synchronously from `set_focus`. The keyline follows GDD 3 Rule 13 exactly as the lift does: when a sheet pushes a block the 3 px drops to 2 px ("one focus on screen, the sheet's"), and the restore on pop re-swaps. Under `reveal` (GDD 3 Rule 6) figures do nothing: a person is never undiscovered (always drawn at second-highest contrast with a keyline), so there is nothing to reveal, and a figure lifting would be a body behaving like paper. The 4.7 note that `CanvasItem` lines draw without an anti-aliasing feather (rendering.md line 11) is one more reason no keyline is drawn by code. *Rationale:* GDD 3 VA §1's principle ("nothing visual is derived at runtime: every mark is a painted crop or a 120 ms translate") applied to people; a painted 3 px line is crisp at the ⅔ stretch where a coded 2 px line is not (T6). *Rejected:* an outline shader or a second outline texture (NOT SOURCEABLE; a third asset per pose); the figure as the person hotspot's own `layer` drawn by GDD 3 (one static crop per hotspot cannot carry a pose per scene state or absence, and GDD 3 Interactions says "figures are never keylined by this system"); the engine y-sort under standing (a sort of static objects every frame to reproduce an order a list already states).

**6. Nothing in a room moves: a standing figure is a cut at `scene_ready` and nothing after; activation changes no pose.** GDD 3 Rule 10 (standing) leaves "any cut or pose change" to this system's listener on `hotspot_activated`; **this system's listener does nothing at MVP**. The transcript sheet opens over the room (Paper UI **(PROV)**, GDD 3 Rule 13), the figure stays in its scene pose beneath it, and the conversation is on the sheet, where the portrait carries all expression (§5.5 standing: "portraits carry all expression"). No idle animation, no breathing, no blink, no sway (§7.4: "no breathing idle"; GDD 9 VA §3's reviewer test, two idle frames ten minutes apart pixel-identical, includes figures). No gesture above the shoulder in any crop (§5.3); weight on both feet. The only two things a figure does after `scene_ready` are the Rule 5 focus swap, in answer to the player's focus, and disappearing with the scene at `scene_exited`. *Rationale:* a turn-to-face on click is a second crop per person per scene (doubles Rule 10's figure bytes) for a frame the sheet covers; and §5.5 fixed one pose per scene state so that a later switch to walking costs nothing. *Rejected:* a `turns` pose on activation (above); a per-item figure pose driven by the dialogue (a crop per spoken line; T8 says one per pose per scene state).

**7. Portraits: four states, chosen per spoken item by the writer's one word, `neutral` when no word is given, never inferred from anything the dossier holds.** The vocabulary V31 checks (GDD 2 Rule 15, `portrait_vocab.json`) is **exactly `neutral | guarded | evasive | exposed`**, the four checkable poses of art bible §5.4 (gaze, chin, mouth). A `line` or `ask` item may carry `portrait: <word>`; the sheet shows the item's speaker in that state while the item is current; **an item with no word keeps the state of the last word in the run, and every run opens `neutral`** (the art director's ruling; a swap is always authored). A `stage` item has no speaker and changes nothing on the portrait (the last spoken item's portrait stays). During an `ask` the asker's portrait stays in the asker's requested state while the player chooses among `says` brackets. **No portrait state is ever a function of a dossier field, a scalar, a tier, the phase, attention, the held set or anything but the word on the item**: there is no "attention makes them look guilty" meter, and GDD 2 EC 29's `tell: true` flag is kept in the compiled output for this system but **drives nothing at MVP** (a wrong-register line is the writer's to make `guarded` or `evasive` by the word; reserved). **Telephone:** a dialogue whose `context` is `telephone` still pins the caller's portrait on the transcript sheet, lit by the telephone's bulb layer (art bible §7.1 is unconditional; GDD 2's `no portrait` token is struck); no figure is drawn in the room; a `portrait:` word on a telephone item warns (CP-W9). **Lighting layer** (§5.4: "state lighting ... are layers applied by technical-artist, never repainted"): `PortraitSpec.light ∈ {none, overhead, torch, sodium}` from `(scene_kind, context, threshold_kind)` and nothing else: `context: interrogation` → `overhead`; `scene_kind: insert` with `threshold_kind: door` → `torch`, `window` → `sodium` (GDD 8 VA §3: "lit by the threshold's light"); otherwise `none`. The §2.1 crop tightening under a running timer is Dialogue's fraction drawn by Paper UI **(PROV)** (GDD 9 Rule 12's precedent for the margin fill), and the Panicking flicker is Paper UI's under Overlay's `flicker_allowance` (GDD 9 Rule 10); this system supplies the base portrait and the layer id only, and consumes none of Overlay's five registered formulas (Open Questions: registry amendment). Per-scene residency: the plan's `portraits[]` is every distinct speaker of the scene's non-telephone dialogues, four states each, ≤ `PORTRAITS_RESIDENT_MAX` 24 (T1's "≤ 24 portraits ≈ 128 MB", §8.8), so **CP-E10: at most `PORTRAIT_SPEAKERS_MAX` = 24 / 4 = 6 distinct portrait speakers per scene**; the lint counts, the plan asserts. *Rationale:* Pillar 3 is "carried by the eyes looking away, not by acting" (§5.3), and a face the engine could darken by a number would be a morality meter in a face (anti-pillar); the writer chooses, and "chosen per item with a loud default" is the rule the narrative director's review item 8 ("wrong-register tell lines read as deliberate") can check line by line in the source. *Rejected:* GDD 2 Rule 5c's example words `reads`, `waits` as portrait requests (an ID photo cropped at the collar cannot read or wait; the only gaze-down state is `exposed` and the only off-lens one is `evasive`, so either alias would smuggle a meaning; the words move to the pose vocabulary, Rule 2, and GDD 2's example is amended, Open Questions); a sticky state inherited across items (a gated item that carries the word and is hidden changes every line after it, making the face a function of the gate's dossier read, inference by the back door); a fifth "reading" portrait (§5.4: the overprint "is a layer, not a fifth state", and 12 × 5 breaks the 48 budget).

**8. The informer tell: this system owns the mapping and the flag, Paper UI draws the mark, the plate is never marked.** `cast_presentation.json` carries, per cast id, `informer_fact: <fact id> | null` (act one: `kastan: riba_is_kastan`, GDD 5 Rule 12's L4; the brief's `knows_ilona` is L5's `ilona_in_circle`, a fact about Ilona, and is not the exposure of an informer, Open Questions for the narrative director). `resolve_portrait(..., has_fact)` returns `overprint: has_fact(informer_fact)` when the fact id is non-null, else `false`; `has_fact` is the injected reader callable GDD 5 exposes (`FactSystem.has`), a read of the player's held set and **not a dossier read** (GDD 2 Rule 6: `has_fact` counts zero; GDD 5 Rule 8), so the core stays dossier-blind. Evaluated **per spoken item as it is shown**: a `line` whose `yields[]` holds the informer fact of its own speaker shows the overprint from the next item on, which is the fiction working as written ("the tell is applied ... once the player holds the fact", §5.2). Paper UI **(PROV)** draws the mark from the flag: the carbon duplicate of the portrait's outline offset 2–3 px down-right in sheet carbon `#2E3559` plus the "cc" glyph in the lower-right corner, the same mark family as a filed span (GDD 5 VA §4, art bible §4.5), applied to every surface a portrait appears on through this one function so the file and the sheet never disagree. **The plate-scale figure is never marked** (§5.2 "the walking city cannot be read, only the file can"; §5.6). CP-E11: `informer_fact` names a fact in the Fact registry (GDD 2 V28's check reused); the overprint is presentation and is **not** a "dossier use" for V29 (GDD 2 Rule 15: a use is a path to an effect). *Rationale:* "informer" and "filed" are one mark (§5.2) and Paper UI already draws the filed mark on spans, so the overprint is one more call to the same renderer with one boolean; which fact exposes whom is content, and content this system owns because the cast table has no presentation fields (GDD 2 Rule 5 `cast`). *Rejected:* a fifth portrait asset per informer (above); Paper UI reading `cast_presentation.json` itself (two readers of one mapping; the simulator could not assert the flag); the overprint as a dossier flag (`kastan.exposed` is the player's knowledge, GDD 5 Rule 1's "held facts are not dossier state").

**9. The insert's figure is on the threshold line at the first cut, before the light; a telephone threshold has no figure.** For `scene_kind: insert` the content row's `persons[]` has **at most one** entry (GDD 8 VA §6: "no second figure on a threshold"), with `pose: on_threshold`, `hotspot` = the insert's `threshold: true` hotspot named by Overlay's O12 `threshold_<id>` layer, and no `present_when` (the state arrived; its presence is the rule's, CP-E12). `apply(plan)` at `scene_ready` places the figure on frame one, in the room's inherited light, which is GDD 8 VA §3's frame one and GDD 9 VA §5's "the figure is in the room before its light is"; Overlay's `apply_after_threshold` follows `THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later and this system does nothing at it (the threshold light layer's hard shadow is the figure's, painted into Overlay's layer from the figure's authored standing point, GDD 9 VA §5; CF-E5: the `on_threshold` crop's bounds equal the standing point that layer was painted for, recorded in the sidecar as `figures.entries[].standing_point`, so the two exports agree). The threshold figure is not a hotspot: no hit region, no ring position, no focus swap; Scene Flow's entry focus lands on the threshold hotspot (GDD 8 Rule 11 step 3) and the door's focus crop is its ajar notch (GDD 3 VA §3). **When the threshold is `kind: phone`, `persons[]` must be empty (CP-E13) and `threshold_figure` is null**: the caller is a voice in the room and a photograph on the sheet (GDD 2: `context: telephone`), the player's own figure is never shown (§5.5), so nobody can stand on a telephone's line; what the first cut shows is **the receiver off its cradle, painted into the insert sidecar's telephone hotspot `layer`** (the insert is "a second sidecar in any case", GDD 8 Rule 11; "the room as the player left it with one difference"), with the bare bulb following at the second cut (GDD 9 VA §5) and the ring at `rule_fired` (GDD 8 VA §7). The receiver cannot be GDD 3's focus lift (the transcript's block drops the lift, GDD 3 Rule 13), so it is authored into the layer. Act one's one insert (`query_ledger`, Rybar by telephone, GDD 8 Rule 10's worked example) therefore ships **no threshold figure**; the door and window cases are specified here and first exercised by the Vertical Slice. *Rationale:* GDD 6 VA §1's "nothing before the figure is on the line" is about the state arriving before its light; for a telephone the state arrives as a ringing instrument, and the receiver off the cradle is the one object on that line that can be "already there". *Rejected:* Vera's own figure at the telephone (§5.5); Rybar's figure on the telephone threshold (he is at the other end of the line; a figure there is a hallucination); an empty frame one for telephones (GDD 8's reviewer test "frame one has the figure and the old lamp" needs a referent, Open Questions: amend to "the figure, or the receiver off its cradle").

**11. ### States and Transitions

**Presence per scene (per `persons[]` entry).** `declared` (content) → `present | absent` at `enter_scene` (Scene Flow's reader; absent ⇒ its hotspot `hidden` before activation) → `loaded` at `scene_preloaded` (`load(plan)`: crops resident, nothing visible) → `shown` at `scene_ready` (`apply(plan)`: a cut) → `cleared` at `scene_exited`. No transition exists between `shown` and `cleared`; `absent` has no `loaded` or `shown` (nothing is loaded for an absent person).

**Pose per scene state.** Chosen once at `enter_scene` from `poses[]`, first true, last ungated; fixed for the scene. The only in-scene swap is between the pose's `layer` and `focus_layer` (same pose, same bounds).

**Portrait state per dialogue item (on the transcript sheet; Paper UI draws).** Dialogue open → the first spoken item's `{speaker, word | neutral}`; each `line`/`ask` → that item's speaker in that item's word or `neutral`; `stage` → unchanged; `end` → cleared. Lighting layer fixed for the dialogue from `(scene_kind, context, threshold_kind)`. Overprint re-evaluated at each spoken item from `has_fact(informer_fact)`; may turn on mid-dialogue, never off (GDD 5: knowledge is not un-known). Telephone context: the caller's portrait on the sheet, no figure in the room. Every transition is a cut on the sheet.

**Focus keyline (per person hotspot).** `2px` → `3px` on `focus_changed(id)` where `id` is a `kind: person` hotspot; `3px` → `2px` on `focus_changed` away, on a `push_input_block` (a sheet; GDD 3 Rule 13), on `hidden` (cannot occur in-scene for a person at MVP), on `scene_exited`; `2px` → `3px` again on `pop_input_block` when the restored focus is that person. `reveal` changes nothing.

**Insert figure (door or window threshold).** `loaded` at `scene_preloaded` → `on_line` at `scene_ready` (frame one, inherited light) → unchanged through Overlay's `apply_after_threshold` (`THRESHOLD_CUT_MS` later) and the whole transcript → `cleared` at `scene_exited`. Telephone threshold: no figure state; the receiver off its cradle is the sidecar's authored `layer`.

**Walking (CONDITIONAL, `presentation.mode = walking`).** Avatar: `idle(facing)` → `walking(path)` on `activate` (GDD 3 Rule 10) → `arrived` (emits for GDD 3's `hotspot_activated`) or `aborted` (`cancel`, new `activate`) → `idle(facing from final segment)`; `arrived` at a `paper` hotspot → `page_turn` (6 frames) → `idle`; `arrived` at a `person` hotspot whose action carries a `document` → `hand_over` (6 frames) → `idle`. Named NPC: `stands(idle 4 @ ≤ 12 fps) | sits(2)` per pose; never `walking`. Under `motion: reduced`: `idle` → `arrived` by cut.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Optional for a Core system; written because this system is every face and every body the player sees. Drafted with the art director from art bible §2.1 (Conversation/interrogation, Reactive event), §3.2, §3.4, §4.2, §4.5, §5.1–5.6, §6.6, §7.1, §7.4 and §8.7 T1–T3, T6, T8, and from GDD 3 Visual/Audio §1–§3 and §7, GDD 8 Visual/Audio §3 and §6, GDD 9 Visual/Audio §4–§5 and §7, GDD 6 Visual/Audio §1 and §7 and GDD 5 Visual/Audio §4, which this section inherits rather than restates. This system draws figures and supplies portraits; it plays nothing. Everything below is a constraint on what a figure crop may contain, when it may change, and what a portrait is; not one of it is a new visual. Six art rulings this needed are stated where they bite: the telephone's figure is the receiver, the four states are the only expression and the writer chooses them, the focus keyline is a painted twin, the pool never holds a face, the informer mark is Paper UI's on the portrait alone, and nothing on a figure moves while the player is in the room. Art-bible amendments these imply (C1–C10) are proposals in Open Questions, not applied.*

**1. A figure at plate scale is a printed silhouette at second-highest contrast, and no fact lives on it.** A standing figure is 15–20% of plate height (162–216 px at 1080, §5.5), six heads, narrow shoulders, stooped at the neck, hands always visible (§3.2); at that size the player reads the silhouette trait, the costume dominant, the one ink accent, head direction and hands, and nothing else: eyes are two marks, mouths are absent, buttons are one line (§5.5). Type reads by the §5.2 straight-edge count on body and costume only (officials ≥ 2, conscript 1, everyone else 0; carried props never count), and informers are drawn as citizens with no variation at all. The costume dominant is a tint of bone or concrete differing from the plate's dominant neutral by ≥ 20% value, the accent ≤ 15% of costume area in the character's own ink (§5.1). Every figure carries a permanent 2 px soot keyline at 1080 (≈ 1.33 px at 720; §5.5, T6) and sits at the second-highest contrast in the frame (§3.4, §6.6): hero-adjacent, never background, never hero; inside `figure_band` (GDD 3 Rule 1), where the plate holds no keyline and no value outside 20–60% (§6.6), so a figure is never over darkness and never over a hero. Figures are painted **sourceless**, as plates are (GDD 9 VA §4), so one drawing serves any room; they take light only as §3 and §4 below allow. A figure is a separate layer, a ≤ 512×512 crop with even dimensions at an integer position in 1080 space (T2, T6; GDD 3 VA §6), y-sorted with hotspots by its hotspot's `baseline_y` (GDD 3 Rule 1), at most six per scene (T3). Nothing is ever drawn on a plate figure: no carbon ghost, no stamp, no informer mark, no red pixel at any reach (§4.2, §5.2; GDD 9 VA §7: `figure_band` and every person rect red-free by validation), no runtime shadow (the only shadow a figure ever has is the one painted into a threshold-light layer, §4). Painted background figures (queues) are plate, not figures: still, non-interactive, inside the midtone band (§5.2). *Reviewer test:* at a 64 px black fill every figure identifies its type by one trait; hue covered, the figure is the second-strongest shape in the frame after the hero; a magnifier over any figure finds no mouth, no mark, no red.

**2. The portrait is an ID photograph in one of four states, and the four states are the whole of expression.** ID framing (§3.2, §5.4): frontal, cropped at the collar, head 60–70% of frame height, eyes on the upper-third line, bone background, stapled corner, drawn scanner-flat, exported 896×1120 with 20% bleed beyond the crop (T2, T9). Four states per named character, protagonists included, each the §5.4 row and nothing softer: **Neutral** gaze to lens, chin level, mouth closed; **Guarded** to lens, chin down 5°, mouth tightened; **Evasive** off-lens to the side, chin level, mouth closed; **Exposed** gaze down, chin down, mouth parted; brows move one notch, nothing else moves (§5.3). The content vocabulary is these four words, `neutral | guarded | evasive | exposed`, the one-word request on a dialogue item that V31 checks (GDD 2 Rule 5c); the default is Neutral; a request holds until the next request within one dialogue run and every run opens Neutral; a request on an answer applies to the interlocutor's portrait on the frame its `record` lands. **The writer chooses; the engine never infers**: no state is ever derived from `attention`, phase, `register`, `tell`, `under_observation` or any dossier field, and `reads`/`waits` are not portrait states (nor plate poses) and are rejected by V31. A state change is a cut on the item's first frame, never a crossfade and never a tween. State lighting and crop are **layers, never repaints** (§5.4): the Conversation keeps the lamp (§2.1), so a conversation portrait is the flat ID print; the **interrogation overhead** (frontal, hard shadow under the brow, high contrast) and the **threshold lights** (torch from the door's side; sodium from the window's side; the bare bulb overhead at the telephone, which shares the overhead recipe because a query on the line is a reading) are applied over the print, the §2.1 crop tightening over that, and the informer overprint (§5) over everything, because light is in the photograph and ink is on it; the Panicking flicker is Paper UI's under Overlay's allowance (GDD 9 VA §7), cuts the lit value one step and never touches the overprint. The transcript sheet pins one portrait, the `speaker`'s, top-left (§7.1); which face an interrogation pins is Dialogue & Interrogation's (PROV). Budget: 48 core, 72 stretch (§5.4); ≤ 24 resident per scene, ≈ 128 MB with mipmaps (T1). *Reviewer test:* cover the face below the eyes: the four states still sort by gaze and chin alone; cover the lighting layer: the print beneath is identical in every state's lit and unlit forms.

**3. Under the standing outcome a named character is a pose chosen at the wipe, and the only thing that changes on it is the keyline.** Each named NPC has one reusable **standing** pose (frontal S, weight on both feet, hands visible, authored sourceless) and one **seated** pose per piece of furniture that occludes it (the crop ends at the desk or counter edge, so it is bound to that plate); act one authors at most three poses per named NPC; the protagonists have none, because the player's own figure is never shown (§5.5). Every pose is one ≤ 512² crop (T8) with a **`_focus` twin at identical bounds** carrying the 3 px keyline in place of the 2 px, and a **`_lit` twin at identical bounds** only where the pose's bounds intersect a pool crop (§4 below). A scene's `persons[]` names, per cast id, the person hotspot, the pose per scene state and `present_when`; presence and pose resolve **at scene entry only** and never change until the exit (GDD 3 Rule 5's idle rule; GDD 8 VA §6: no room changes while the player is idle): an absent person has no layer and a `hidden` hotspot, and a figure arrives or leaves only under GDD 3's wipe or on an insert's cut, never while the player stands there. **Focus** on a person is the one in-scene change (GDD 3 VA §2: "people do not lift"): on GDD 3's focus signal this system swaps the crop for its `_focus` twin on the first frame and swaps back on blur; no translate, no tween, no lift; `revealed` changes nothing on a figure. Nothing on a figure moves at any other moment: no idle cycle, no breathing (§7.4), no turn of the head when spoken to, no pose change when a conversation opens or closes (the sheet covers the figure; when it lifts, the figure is as it was). **The pool never contains a face** (ruling): the lamp pool is authored over the working surface (§2.1) and standing points in its outer step; a face is lit only by the state's light (the interrogation overhead, the threshold light). Where a seated pose's hands and forearms enter a pool crop the `_lit` twin is required by the export check (bounds intersect → twin or error) and selected once at entry by the plan's pool state (GDD 9 Rule 7); it differs from the base by value on the lit parts alone, never hue, never the keyline. Resident figure crops ≤ 12 per scene (six figures, base and focus at the entry's pool state), ≈ 17 MB with mipmaps, in place of the walking worksheet's sprite sheet (T1). *Reviewer test:* two frames of an occupied idle room ten minutes apart, outside a running timer, are pixel-identical; hover a person and only its keyline changes; strip the pool crop at night and no figure's face changes value.

**4. The insert's figure is the state at the threshold, in the room before its light is, and at a telephone it is the receiver.** On an insert's first cut (GDD 8 VA §3; GDD 9 VA §5) the room is as the player left it, under its own lamp, with one difference: the state's figure already on the threshold line of the declared `threshold` hotspot. At a **door, gate, window or post** it is a person: a standing pose placed on the threshold line inside the frame, facing the room (the one time an official faces the player at plate scale: centred in the frame, upright, two straight edges, §3.2), weight on both feet, hands visible, nothing above the shoulder, at its sourceless value. On the **second cut** the threshold light replaces the room's (never on the same frame, never a crossfade, GDD 9 VA §5): the figure is backlit, its hard shadow painted into Overlay's `threshold_<id>` layer from the figure's authored standing point, and it reads as a silhouette against the light; it needs no lit twin and takes no colour; red crosses the threshold line at the inherited reach and never the figure (§4.2). At the **telephone** no person is drawn: Rybar is a voice, and the state arrives as its object. The threshold's figure is GDD 3's focus pose for `kind: phone`, the receiver risen off its cradle with the cord showing (the cord is the threshold line, §2.1), which GDD 8 Rule 11 step 3 already sets at `scene_ready`; the receiver must be off the cradle before the bulb comes on (`THRESHOLD_CUT_MS ≥ LIFT_MS`), and under `motion: reduced` the lift is instantaneous. `place_on_threshold(cast_id, hotspot_id)` draws nothing for a phone threshold and asserts that nothing else is drawn. In both cases the **portrait** is on the transcript sheet that opens without a click (GDD 8 Rule 11), pinned top-left, lit by the threshold's light layer of §2 (torch, sodium or overhead bulb); GDD 2's `no portrait` is struck: a transcript always carries its speaker's photograph (§7.1). The figure never moves after the cut; the insert leaves by paper (GDD 8 VA §3) and the figure leaves with the room. Nothing about the insert is a cutscene, a camera move, a vignette or a letterbox. *Reviewer test (GDD 8's and GDD 9's, restated for figures):* frame one shows the threshold's figure, person or receiver, in the old light; no frame shows the new light without it; no frame shows both lights; at the telephone no human figure is anywhere on the plate.

**5. The informer is told on the portrait, by the file's own mark, and nowhere else.** Once `has_fact(informer_fact(cast_id))` is true (the fact id per cast id is this system's content; the predicate is GDD 5's), Paper UI draws on that character's portrait, in every one of its four states and wherever the portrait appears, a **carbon duplicate of the portrait's outline** offset down-right by the sheet's ghost vector (GDD 5's `GHOST_OFFSET_PX`, 3 px at 1080, 2 px at 720) plus the typewriter **`cc`** glyph at label size in the lower-right corner inside the stapled frame, in UI carbon `#2E3559` (GDD 5 VA §4: one mark family with the held span's double-strike and `cc`; world carbon `#434C7C` is the plate's and never on a sheet). It is the topmost portrait layer, above state lighting and crop tightening; it does not flicker; it is never removed (a held fact is never unheld). It is a layer, not a fifth state (§5.4), and it is the only in-engine mark a portrait ever takes; it is driven by what the player holds, never by what the state knows. **The plate figure is never marked** (§5.2, §5.6): an informer in the room is a citizen in the room, with the same crop, keyline and contrast as before the fact was held; the walking city cannot be read, only the file can. *Reviewer test:* screenshot a held informer's portrait beside a filed hotspot's ghost and a held span's `cc`; a viewer names all three as one thing; screenshot the same informer on the plate before and after the hold; the frames are identical.

**6. What must never happen, and how a reviewer checks.** No idle animation on any figure: no breathing, sway, blink, weight shift or head turn (§7.4); the only motion a figure ever has is the walking outcome's authored cycles (§7). No gesture above the shoulder except the walking outcome's hand-over (§5.3); no pointing, waving, shouting, no open mouth at any scale; no drawn tears. No inferred expression: no portrait state computed from a dossier scalar, phase, register, tell or attention; no "guilty-looking" meter; a portrait that changes without a writer's request is a defect. No red on a figure: not in costume, not as light, not as tint, at any reach (§4.2; GDD 9 VA §7), and no sodium or amber tint mixed onto a figure at runtime either (lit twins are painted). No player figure under the standing outcome (§5.5), on any plate, in any insert, in any reflection or shadow. No camera move: under standing the camera is static per plate and the insert is a cut (GDD 3 Rule 11; GDD 8 VA §6); under walking the one camera motion is GDD 3 Rule 11's follow during a walk, owned there, never here. Further: no figure appears, leaves, or changes pose between `scene_ready` and the exit; no figure marked on the plate (ghost, stamp, `cc`, keyline change but focus); no fifth portrait state, no repainted portrait for light, no crossfade or tween between states; no mouth, lip-sync or talking head at any scale; no runtime shadow, outline shader or drawn keyline (keylines are painted twins); no figure outside `figure_band`; no figure as a hero shape (its keyline is 2 px, not the hero's hard edge and lifted corner); no plate-scale Rybar at the telephone. *Reviewer tests:* (a) two idle frames ten minutes apart, pixel-identical with figures present; (b) hue covered and red filter on, zero red-tolerance pixels inside any figure's bounds at every reach; (c) run a dialogue with every request removed: every portrait is Neutral throughout; (d) step an insert's first three frames: room, room with the threshold's figure, figure with the new light, never a fourth and never a person at a phone; (e) the informer screenshots of §5.

**7. Walking outcome (CONDITIONAL; live only when `presentation.mode = walking`, GDD 3 Rule 10).** The protagonist is an avatar on one 2048² sheet of 256² cells (T8): walk 8 cells × three authored facings (N, S, E; W mirrored, which §5.1's bilateral traits make lossless), hand-over 6, page-turn 6, sit 2, and four **idle cells that are held frames, not a loop**: one per facing, selected by the dominant axis of the final segment (GDD 3 Rule 10), shown while the avatar stands and never sequenced, so §7.4's "no breathing idle" holds under walking too. Playback ≤ 12 fps; the foot-slide ratio within 0.8–1.25 at `WALK_SPEED_PX_S` is the proof's criterion (GDD 3 Formulas §9), and `HOP_S` is the proof's gate, not this section's. Hand-over and page-turn play once on arrival at the hotspot that is their act (a person who takes paper; a document opened on a surface), 0.5 s at 12 fps, before the sheet opens, and are skipped at `motion: reduced` (GDD 4; §7.4 durations 0); hand-over is the one gesture that reaches shoulder height (§5.3), and the dissident's ink-stained fingers appear in it and nowhere else at plate scale (§5.1). Named NPCs get idle (one facing, held) and sit only; nobody but the player walks (§5.5). Everything in §1 holds on every cell: silhouette trait, accent, 2 px keyline (the `_focus` twin becomes a focus row of the idle cells), second-highest contrast, no mark, no red, no shadow, sourceless paint, inside `figure_band` (which must then contain `walkable`, GDD 3 E10). The avatar's position is never state and never lit specially: a walk through the pool is a walk through the pool, the lit twin rule of §3 applies to cells only where the proof shows the pool crossing reads wrong, which is a proof observation, not a rule here. Portraits are unchanged in every respect (§2, §5). In an insert the avatar stands still at the insert sidecar's declared standing point, facing the threshold, from frame one; it never walks during an insert, and the insert's figure rules of §4 are unchanged. The camera follows the avatar during a walk and returns to focus (GDD 3 Rule 11); that follow is the only camera motion in the game and is not this system's.

**8. Audio.** This system plays nothing and names no cue. A person's activation carries no sound (GDD 3 VA §8: "nothing for `person`", the conversation is Dialogue's); the insert's arrival is GDD 6's threshold family at `rule_fired(class, "insert")` with the figure already in the frame (GDD 6 VA §7; GDD 8 VA §7); the receiver off its cradle is GDD 3's activation cue for `kind: phone`; the portrait's state cut is silent. The visual mood the audio director's choices should match: stiff bodies that do not shift their weight, a face that changes only when someone decides it should, a man who is not in the room.

## Game Feel

Optional for a Core system; three feel properties follow from it and are stated so Dialogue, Paper UI and the art pipeline inherit them as targets.

- **People hold still.** A figure never breathes, sways, blinks or turns while the player is in the room; the only change on it is the thicker keyline when focus rests on it, by a cut. A room with people in it is as still as a room without.
- **A face changes only when someone decided it should.** A portrait cuts between its four states on the item that asked for it and otherwise holds; no state ever creeps, tweens or follows a number.
- **The state is already there when you look up.** At a door, gate or post its figure is on the threshold line on the first cut, before its light; at a telephone the receiver is off its cradle and the caller's photograph is on the sheet.

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen and draws nothing on a sheet. The transcript sheet, the pinned portrait, its state-light layer, the crop tightening, the "cc" overprint and the screen-reader strings (`acc.portrait.*`, resolved by Paper UI from the `PortraitSpec`) are Paper UI's; the room's person hotspots, their accessible names and focus are the Scene & Interaction Framework's. Debug builds only: an inspector that prints the current `CastPlan`, the last `PortraitSpec` per speaker and the resident figure and portrait bytes, reachable through the debug menu and absent from release (the same rule as GDDs 2, 6, 7, 8 and 9's debug surfaces).

> **📌 UX Flag — Character Presentation**: no player-facing UI of its own. `design/accessibility-requirements.md` must state the two facts this system guarantees: the four portrait states are distinguishable by pose (gaze, chin, mouth) without colour or motion and are announced as `acc.portrait.<state>` when `sr_verbosity` is `full`; and the informer mark on a portrait is announced as the same filed mark the held span carries, never as a verdict.
