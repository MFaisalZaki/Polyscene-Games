## Overview

*Framing: infrastructure first (Category `Core`, Layer Foundation), with its player-facing effect named because people are what the player looks at. A stub at MVP: this GDD fixes the interface and ships the standing-layer behaviour; the walking outcome stays a conditional paragraph until the art-pipeline proof decides `presentation.mode`. No ADR cites it yet; ADR-5 (the renderer) and ADR-6 (assets) will read it.*

Character Presentation puts people in rooms and faces on sheets. It decides which named characters are present in a scene and in which pose, from content conditions resolved at scene entry and never mid-scene; it draws each as a standing figure layer on the plate, y-sorted with the hotspots by its baseline, at the second-highest contrast in the frame, with its printed keyline and the thicker keyline when the player's focus is on it; it hands Paper UI the ID-photo portrait for the transcript sheet in one of four states (neutral, guarded, evasive, exposed) that the writer chose for that line and the engine never infers; it places the state's person on the threshold line when a reactive event cuts in; and it supplies the one mark that turns a citizen into an informer, the carbon "cc" overprint on the portrait, only once the player holds the fact. The player never operates it; they read it: a figure where the file said someone would be, a face that will not meet the lens, a door with a captain in it. Without it Pillar 3 would have no faces to be unclean in and Pillar 2 no body to arrive with; with more than it (a mood meter on a face, a figure that breathes while you read) Pillar 5 and the art bible's stiff, printed people would be gone. Its logic is headless (a cast plan per scene entry, plain data, byte-equal across hosts) and its drawing lives behind a host seam with a null twin, like the Scene & Interaction Framework's and Overlay's. Under the walking outcome it would also own the player's avatar, its walk, facing and hand-over; that paragraph is written and dormant.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect. The player never sees Character Presentation; they see people standing in rooms and faces pinned to paper, and learn that neither one gives a verdict. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "nobody looks guilty" chosen, from art bible §5.2–5.3 and Pillar 3; "the face is the writer's, the mark is yours" kept as the one rule; "the file has a face, the room has a body" kept as how the plate and the sheet divide a person, §5.5).*

**Nobody looks guilty.** This system serves two feelings that pull against each other. The first is uncertainty about people. Everyone in the city stands the same way: stiff, weight on both feet, hands low, faces too small to read at plate scale. The informer on the stairs is drawn exactly like the woman in the bread queue, and the room will never tell you which is which. The second is the moment that changes, and it comes only from paper. When you hold the fact, the face on the sheet gets a carbon double of its own outline, two pixels off true, and a small "cc" in its corner. It is the same blue mark your own filings leave, because knowing someone informs and having it on file are one thing. The figure in the room stays unmarked. The room shows people; only the file can mark one.

The anchor moment: Vera's office, Friday 6 October. Berta is at the next desk as a standing layer, seated, still, at second-highest contrast, with a 2 px soot keyline that thickens to 3 px when Vera's attention rests on her. In conversation Berta's ID photo is stapled top-left on the transcript sheet. When Vera asks after the missing copy, the photo changes because the writer asked it to: chin down five degrees, mouth tightened. That is Guarded, the look of anyone asked a question at work, and it proves nothing. The same week Vera reads report 4471 to the end and holds the fact that the hand is Kastan's, the caretaker at Vrba Street. The next time Kastan's face is on a sheet, on the landing asking after the parcel, it carries the blue double strike and the "cc". Kastan is still on his stairs, drawn as he always was. Then Rybar calls. The receiver is off its cradle on the first cut, and the light over the telephone is replaced on the second; his ID photo is pinned to the transcript sheet, lit from the bulb, and no man is drawn in the room, because no man is in it. When the state does come in person, at a door, a gate or a post, its figure is on the threshold line before the light changes. It does not walk in, it does not gesture, and the camera does not move. The state arrives the way people do in this game: already standing there when you look up.

Pillar alignment. Pillar 3, "no one is clean": expression is the only acting in this game, and it is limited to gaze, chin and mouth in four checkable states, Neutral, Guarded, Evasive, Exposed (§5.4). Evasive is how a frightened innocent looks too. Shame is carried by the eyes looking away, not by performance, and no face tells the player whom to condemn. Pillar 1, "every fact is a weapon": the informer's mark is a fact turned into a drawing, and it exists only because the player holds that fact; no fact ever lives on a figure at plate scale (§5.5). Pillar 2, "the state is a character": the state has a body here too, and it enters through a threshold, standing where the light changes, never in a cutscene. Pillar 5, "pressure comes from attention, not reflexes": nobody moves while the player reads. Figures do not breathe, shift or wander. A person is present or absent from the moment the room opens, decided by the file as it stood at the door.

**The one rule this system must obey: a face shows only what the writer asked of it, and a person is marked only by what the player holds.** Every portrait state comes from a one-word request on a dialogue item. If a line has no request, the face is Neutral. The engine never infers an expression from attention, suspicion, phase or anything else in the dossier, so a frightened face is always a choice someone made and never a reading of the meter. The informer overprint is a layer, not a fifth state. It appears on the portrait once the fact is held, and it never appears on the figure, the costume or the plate. Who stands in a room is settled when the player walks in, and a held fact never makes someone appear, so no one becomes an option just because the player learned something. No figure is tinted red; colour says who is watching, never who is guilty.

*Cost accepted:* this is a stub at MVP and honest about it. Vera's chapter ships the standing outcome: each named character is a per-scene plate layer with one pose per scene state, removed when the file says they are absent; the player's own figure is never shown; and the portraits carry all the expression. Whether protagonists later walk to the desk (walk cycles, facing, idle, hand-over, page-turn) waits for the MVP art-pipeline proof; it is written as a conditional so the switch costs nothing, and plate-scale detail is identical under both outcomes. Until then a person cannot cross a room, and the player may feel the rooms are tableaux. Restrained faces mean some players will misread a Guarded face as guilt; the game lets them, because the city did too. And the player who spots the informer early, from instinct, gets no confirmation from the drawing. Until the paper says so, Kastan looks like everyone else, and some players will find that frustrating. That frustration is the cost of the pillar, and it is accepted.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Optional for a Core system; written because this system is every face and every body the player sees. Drafted with the art director from art bible §2.1 (Conversation/interrogation, Reactive event), §3.2, §3.4, §4.2, §4.5, §5.1–5.6, §6.6, §7.1, §7.4 and §8.7 T1–T3, T6, T8, and from GDD 3 Visual/Audio §1–§3 and §7, GDD 8 Visual/Audio §3 and §6, GDD 9 Visual/Audio §4–§5 and §7, GDD 6 Visual/Audio §1 and §7 and GDD 5 Visual/Audio §4, which this section inherits rather than restates. This system draws figures and supplies portraits; it plays nothing. Everything below is a constraint on what a figure crop may contain, when it may change, and what a portrait is; not one of it is a new visual. Six art rulings this needed are stated where they bite: the telephone's figure is the receiver, the four states are the only expression and the writer chooses them, the focus keyline is a painted twin, the pool never holds a face, the informer mark is Paper UI's on the portrait alone, and nothing on a figure moves while the player is in the room. Art-bible amendments these imply (C1–C10) are proposals in Open Questions, not applied.*

**1. A figure at plate scale is a printed silhouette at second-highest contrast, and no fact lives on it.** A standing figure is 15–20% of plate height (162–216 px at 1080, §5.5), six heads, narrow shoulders, stooped at the neck, hands always visible (§3.2); at that size the player reads the silhouette trait, the costume dominant, the one ink accent, head direction and hands, and nothing else: eyes are two marks, mouths are absent, buttons are one line (§5.5). Type reads by the §5.2 straight-edge count on body and costume only (officials ≥ 2, conscript 1, everyone else 0; carried props never count), and informers are drawn as citizens with no variation at all. The costume dominant is a tint of bone or concrete differing from the plate's dominant neutral by ≥ 20% value, the accent ≤ 15% of costume area in the character's own ink (§5.1). Every figure carries a permanent 2 px soot keyline at 1080 (≈ 1.33 px at 720; §5.5, T6) and sits at the second-highest contrast in the frame (§3.4, §6.6): hero-adjacent, never background, never hero; inside `figure_band` (GDD 3 Rule 1), where the plate holds no keyline and no value outside 20–60% (§6.6), so a figure is never over darkness and never over a hero. Figures are painted **sourceless**, as plates are (GDD 9 VA §4), so one drawing serves any room; they take light only as §3 and §4 below allow. A figure is a separate layer, a ≤ 512×512 crop with even dimensions at an integer position in 1080 space (T2, T6; GDD 3 VA §6), y-sorted with hotspots by its hotspot's `baseline_y` (GDD 3 Rule 1), at most six per scene (T3). Nothing is ever drawn on a plate figure: no carbon ghost, no stamp, no informer mark, no red pixel at any reach (§4.2, §5.2; GDD 9 VA §7: `figure_band` and every person rect red-free by validation), no runtime shadow (the only shadow a figure ever has is the one painted into a threshold-light layer, §4). Painted background figures (queues) are plate, not figures: still, non-interactive, inside the midtone band (§5.2). *Reviewer test:* at a 64 px black fill every figure identifies its type by one trait; hue covered, the figure is the second-strongest shape in the frame after the hero; a magnifier over any figure finds no mouth, no mark, no red.

**2. The portrait is an ID photograph in one of four states, and the four states are the whole of expression.** ID framing (§3.2, §5.4): frontal, cropped at the collar, head 60–70% of frame height, eyes on the upper-third line, bone background, stapled corner, drawn scanner-flat, exported 896×1120 with 20% bleed beyond the crop (T2, T9). Four states per named character, protagonists included, each the §5.4 row and nothing softer: **Neutral** gaze to lens, chin level, mouth closed; **Guarded** to lens, chin down 5°, mouth tightened; **Evasive** off-lens to the side, chin level, mouth closed; **Exposed** gaze down, chin down, mouth parted; brows move one notch, nothing else moves (§5.3). The content vocabulary is these four words, `neutral | guarded | evasive | exposed`, the one-word request on a dialogue item that V31 checks (GDD 2 Rule 5c); the default is Neutral; a request holds until the next request within one dialogue run and every run opens Neutral; a request on an answer applies to the interlocutor's portrait on the frame its `record` lands. **The writer chooses; the engine never infers**: no state is ever derived from `attention`, phase, `register`, `tell`, `under_observation` or any dossier field, and `reads`/`waits` are not portrait states (nor plate poses) and are rejected by V31. A state change is a cut on the item's first frame, never a crossfade and never a tween. State lighting and crop are **layers, never repaints** (§5.4): the Conversation keeps the lamp (§2.1), so a conversation portrait is the flat ID print; the **interrogation overhead** (frontal, hard shadow under the brow, high contrast) and the **threshold lights** (torch from the door's side; sodium from the window's side; the bare bulb overhead at the telephone, which shares the overhead recipe because a query on the line is a reading) are applied over the print, the §2.1 crop tightening over that, and the informer overprint (§5) over everything, because light is in the photograph and ink is on it; the Panicking flicker is Paper UI's under Overlay's allowance (GDD 9 VA §7), cuts the lit value one step and never touches the overprint. The transcript sheet pins one portrait, the `speaker`'s, top-left (§7.1); which face an interrogation pins is Dialogue & Interrogation's (PROV). Budget: 48 core, 72 stretch (§5.4); ≤ 24 resident per scene, ≈ 128 MB with mipmaps (T1). *Reviewer test:* cover the face below the eyes: the four states still sort by gaze and chin alone; cover the lighting layer: the print beneath is identical in every state's lit and unlit forms.

**3. Under the standing outcome a named character is a pose chosen at the wipe, and the only thing that changes on it is the keyline.** Each named NPC has one reusable **standing** pose (frontal S, weight on both feet, hands visible, authored sourceless) and one **seated** pose per piece of furniture that occludes it (the crop ends at the desk or counter edge, so it is bound to that plate); act one authors at most three poses per named NPC; the protagonists have none, because the player's own figure is never shown (§5.5). Every pose is one ≤ 512² crop (T8) with a **`_focus` twin at identical bounds** carrying the 3 px keyline in place of the 2 px, and a **`_lit` twin at identical bounds** only where the pose's bounds intersect a pool crop (§4 below). A scene's `persons[]` names, per cast id, the person hotspot, the pose per scene state and `present_when`; presence and pose resolve **at scene entry only** and never change until the exit (GDD 3 Rule 5's idle rule; GDD 8 VA §6: no room changes while the player is idle): an absent person has no layer and a `hidden` hotspot, and a figure arrives or leaves only under GDD 3's wipe or on an insert's cut, never while the player stands there. **Focus** on a person is the one in-scene change (GDD 3 VA §2: "people do not lift"): on GDD 3's focus signal this system swaps the crop for its `_focus` twin on the first frame and swaps back on blur; no translate, no tween, no lift; `revealed` changes nothing on a figure. Nothing on a figure moves at any other moment: no idle cycle, no breathing (§7.4), no turn of the head when spoken to, no pose change when a conversation opens or closes (the sheet covers the figure; when it lifts, the figure is as it was). **The pool never contains a face** (ruling): the lamp pool is authored over the working surface (§2.1) and standing points in its outer step; a face is lit only by the state's light (the interrogation overhead, the threshold light). Where a seated pose's hands and forearms enter a pool crop the `_lit` twin is required by the export check (bounds intersect → twin or error) and selected once at entry by the plan's pool state (GDD 9 Rule 7); it differs from the base by value on the lit parts alone, never hue, never the keyline. Resident figure crops ≤ 12 per scene (six figures, base and focus at the entry's pool state), ≈ 17 MB with mipmaps, in place of the walking worksheet's sprite sheet (T1). *Reviewer test:* two frames of an occupied idle room ten minutes apart, outside a running timer, are pixel-identical; hover a person and only its keyline changes; strip the pool crop at night and no figure's face changes value.

**4. The insert's figure is the state at the threshold, in the room before its light is, and at a telephone it is the receiver.** On an insert's first cut (GDD 8 VA §3; GDD 9 VA §5) the room is as the player left it, under its own lamp, with one difference: the state's figure already on the threshold line of the declared `threshold` hotspot. At a **door, gate, window or post** it is a person: a standing pose placed on the threshold line inside the frame, facing the room (the one time an official faces the player at plate scale: centred in the frame, upright, two straight edges, §3.2), weight on both feet, hands visible, nothing above the shoulder, at its sourceless value. On the **second cut** the threshold light replaces the room's (never on the same frame, never a crossfade, GDD 9 VA §5): the figure is backlit, its hard shadow painted into Overlay's `threshold_<id>` layer from the figure's authored standing point, and it reads as a silhouette against the light; it needs no lit twin and takes no colour; red crosses the threshold line at the inherited reach and never the figure (§4.2). At the **telephone** no person is drawn: Rybar is a voice, and the state arrives as its object. The threshold's figure is GDD 3's focus pose for `kind: phone`, the receiver risen off its cradle with the cord showing (the cord is the threshold line, §2.1), which GDD 8 Rule 11 step 3 already sets at `scene_ready`; the receiver must be off the cradle before the bulb comes on (`THRESHOLD_CUT_MS ≥ LIFT_MS`), and under `motion: reduced` the lift is instantaneous. `place_on_threshold(cast_id, hotspot_id)` draws nothing for a phone threshold and asserts that nothing else is drawn. In both cases the **portrait** is on the transcript sheet that opens without a click (GDD 8 Rule 11), pinned top-left, lit by the threshold's light layer of §2 (torch, sodium or overhead bulb); GDD 2's `no portrait` is struck: a transcript always carries its speaker's photograph (§7.1). The figure never moves after the cut; the insert leaves by paper (GDD 8 VA §3) and the figure leaves with the room. Nothing about the insert is a cutscene, a camera move, a vignette or a letterbox. *Reviewer test (GDD 8's and GDD 9's, restated for figures):* frame one shows the threshold's figure, person or receiver, in the old light; no frame shows the new light without it; no frame shows both lights; at the telephone no human figure is anywhere on the plate.

**5. The informer is told on the portrait, by the file's own mark, and nowhere else.** Once `has_fact(informer_fact(cast_id))` is true (the fact id per cast id is this system's content; the predicate is GDD 5's), Paper UI draws on that character's portrait, in every one of its four states and wherever the portrait appears, a **carbon duplicate of the portrait's outline** offset down-right by the sheet's ghost vector (GDD 5's `GHOST_OFFSET_PX`, 3 px at 1080, 2 px at 720) plus the typewriter **`cc`** glyph at label size in the lower-right corner inside the stapled frame, in UI carbon `#2E3559` (GDD 5 VA §4: one mark family with the held span's double-strike and `cc`; world carbon `#434C7C` is the plate's and never on a sheet). It is the topmost portrait layer, above state lighting and crop tightening; it does not flicker; it is never removed (a held fact is never unheld). It is a layer, not a fifth state (§5.4), and it is the only in-engine mark a portrait ever takes; it is driven by what the player holds, never by what the state knows. **The plate figure is never marked** (§5.2, §5.6): an informer in the room is a citizen in the room, with the same crop, keyline and contrast as before the fact was held; the walking city cannot be read, only the file can. *Reviewer test:* screenshot a held informer's portrait beside a filed hotspot's ghost and a held span's `cc`; a viewer names all three as one thing; screenshot the same informer on the plate before and after the hold; the frames are identical.

**6. What must never happen, and how a reviewer checks.** No idle animation on any figure: no breathing, sway, blink, weight shift or head turn (§7.4); the only motion a figure ever has is the walking outcome's authored cycles (§7). No gesture above the shoulder except the walking outcome's hand-over (§5.3); no pointing, waving, shouting, no open mouth at any scale; no drawn tears. No inferred expression: no portrait state computed from a dossier scalar, phase, register, tell or attention; no "guilty-looking" meter; a portrait that changes without a writer's request is a defect. No red on a figure: not in costume, not as light, not as tint, at any reach (§4.2; GDD 9 VA §7), and no sodium or amber tint mixed onto a figure at runtime either (lit twins are painted). No player figure under the standing outcome (§5.5), on any plate, in any insert, in any reflection or shadow. No camera move: under standing the camera is static per plate and the insert is a cut (GDD 3 Rule 11; GDD 8 VA §6); under walking the one camera motion is GDD 3 Rule 11's follow during a walk, owned there, never here. Further: no figure appears, leaves, or changes pose between `scene_ready` and the exit; no figure marked on the plate (ghost, stamp, `cc`, keyline change but focus); no fifth portrait state, no repainted portrait for light, no crossfade or tween between states; no mouth, lip-sync or talking head at any scale; no runtime shadow, outline shader or drawn keyline (keylines are painted twins); no figure outside `figure_band`; no figure as a hero shape (its keyline is 2 px, not the hero's hard edge and lifted corner); no plate-scale Rybar at the telephone. *Reviewer tests:* (a) two idle frames ten minutes apart, pixel-identical with figures present; (b) hue covered and red filter on, zero red-tolerance pixels inside any figure's bounds at every reach; (c) run a dialogue with every request removed: every portrait is Neutral throughout; (d) step an insert's first three frames: room, room with the threshold's figure, figure with the new light, never a fourth and never a person at a phone; (e) the informer screenshots of §5.

**7. Walking outcome (CONDITIONAL; live only when `presentation.mode = walking`, GDD 3 Rule 10).** The protagonist is an avatar on one 2048² sheet of 256² cells (T8): walk 8 cells × three authored facings (N, S, E; W mirrored, which §5.1's bilateral traits make lossless), hand-over 6, page-turn 6, sit 2, and four **idle cells that are held frames, not a loop**: one per facing, selected by the dominant axis of the final segment (GDD 3 Rule 10), shown while the avatar stands and never sequenced, so §7.4's "no breathing idle" holds under walking too. Playback ≤ 12 fps; the foot-slide ratio within 0.8–1.25 at `WALK_SPEED_PX_S` is the proof's criterion (GDD 3 Formulas §9), and `HOP_S` is the proof's gate, not this section's. Hand-over and page-turn play once on arrival at the hotspot that is their act (a person who takes paper; a document opened on a surface), 0.5 s at 12 fps, before the sheet opens, and are skipped at `motion: reduced` (GDD 4; §7.4 durations 0); hand-over is the one gesture that reaches shoulder height (§5.3), and the dissident's ink-stained fingers appear in it and nowhere else at plate scale (§5.1). Named NPCs get idle (one facing, held) and sit only; nobody but the player walks (§5.5). Everything in §1 holds on every cell: silhouette trait, accent, 2 px keyline (the `_focus` twin becomes a focus row of the idle cells), second-highest contrast, no mark, no red, no shadow, sourceless paint, inside `figure_band` (which must then contain `walkable`, GDD 3 E10). The avatar's position is never state and never lit specially: a walk through the pool is a walk through the pool, the lit twin rule of §3 applies to cells only where the proof shows the pool crossing reads wrong, which is a proof observation, not a rule here. Portraits are unchanged in every respect (§2, §5). In an insert the avatar stands still at the insert sidecar's declared standing point, facing the threshold, from frame one; it never walks during an insert, and the insert's figure rules of §4 are unchanged. The camera follows the avatar during a walk and returns to focus (GDD 3 Rule 11); that follow is the only camera motion in the game and is not this system's.

**8. Audio.** This system plays nothing and names no cue. A person's activation carries no sound (GDD 3 VA §8: "nothing for `person`", the conversation is Dialogue's); the insert's arrival is GDD 6's threshold family at `rule_fired(class, "insert")` with the figure already in the frame (GDD 6 VA §7; GDD 8 VA §7); the receiver off its cradle is GDD 3's activation cue for `kind: phone`; the portrait's state cut is silent. The visual mood the audio director's choices should match: stiff bodies that do not shift their weight, a face that changes only when someone decides it should, a man who is not in the room.
