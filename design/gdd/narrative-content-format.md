## Overview

Narrative Content Format & Strings is the data format in which every piece of Paper Walls' story is written and the service that hands it to the systems that need it. A document on Vera's desk, a line Rybar says on the telephone, the beat that opens Jan's chapter, the sign Jan reads in the street, the order Tomas receives, the epilogue card at the end of an act: each is a content object in a declared format, authored by writers in plain text files, compiled by a build tool into plain data, validated against the dossier schema, and loaded by the game at startup. Every player-visible text is a string id resolved per locale, never a literal in code or in a scene. The format exists because the branches of Paper Walls are generated by the story engine, not hand-written: the writing attaches to action classes and ending classes, not to paths, and that only works if the content is data with a strict, checkable shape. Without this system the eight consumers that read story content (Investigation & Fact, Reactive Event Rules, Dialogue & Interrogation, Filing & Decision, Scene Flow, Chapter & Story Structure, Paper UI and Audio) would each invent a format of their own, the writers would author in six places, and the flag budgets GDD 1 enforces at build time would have nothing to count.

The player meets this system directly, in that every word they read passes through it, and never meets it as a system: the authoring layer, the compiler, the string table and the validator are invisible, and what remains on screen is the paper. The index describes it as "documents, dialogue, scene/chapter graph, decision and effect data, string-ID scheme, lookup and export; all text is a string", and this GDD keeps that scope. It owns formats and lookup. It does not own the meaning of a decision (Filing & Decision), the gating of a line (Dialogue & Interrogation), when a sign is shown (Dossier projections, Paper UI) or what a fact unlocks (Investigation & Fact); each of those systems consumes a format defined here and applies its own rules. Implementation questions raised during design (which Godot translation mechanism carries the string table, whether the compiler runs in the editor or in CI) are recorded as ADR inputs, not decided here.
