## Summary

Narrative Content Format & Strings is the shape every piece of Paper Walls' story is written in and the service that hands it out: documents, dialogue, scene and chapter graphs, decisions, the prose beats keyed to the story engine's action classes, the epilogues keyed to its ending classes, and the sign, order and file forms through which the dossier reaches each protagonist. Writers author in plain text, a build tool compiles it to id-only data plus per-locale string tables, and a validator refuses anything that names a field the dossier schema does not declare, exceeds a chapter's flag budget, gates a line on history instead of state, or leaves a class without a beat. It exists because the branches are generated, not hand-written, so the writing has to attach to state and classes through a format strict enough to check and loose enough to write a chapter in without touching JSON.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Foundation` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `Dossier State Model` (build-time schema only); read by Investigation & Fact, Reactive Event Rules, Dialogue & Interrogation, Filing & Decision, Chapter & Story Structure, Scene Flow, Paper UI, Audio

## Overview

Narrative Content Format & Strings is the data format in which every piece of Paper Walls' story is written and the service that hands it to the systems that need it. A document on Vera's desk, a line Rybar says on the telephone, the beat that opens Jan's chapter, the sign Jan reads in the street, the order Tomas receives, the epilogue card at the end of an act: each is a content object in a declared format, authored by writers in plain text files, compiled by a build tool into plain data, validated against the dossier schema, and loaded by the game at startup. Every player-visible text is a string id resolved per locale, never a literal in code or in a scene. The format exists because the branches of Paper Walls are generated by the story engine, not hand-written: the writing attaches to action classes and ending classes, not to paths, and that only works if the content is data with a strict, checkable shape. Without this system the eight consumers that read story content (Investigation & Fact, Reactive Event Rules, Dialogue & Interrogation, Filing & Decision, Scene Flow, Chapter & Story Structure, Paper UI and Audio) would each invent a format of their own, the writers would author in six places, and the flag budgets GDD 1 enforces at build time would have nothing to count.

The player meets this system directly, in that every word they read passes through it, and never meets it as a system: the authoring layer, the compiler, the string table and the validator are invisible, and what remains on screen is the paper. The index describes it as "documents, dialogue, scene/chapter graph, decision and effect data, string-ID scheme, lookup and export; all text is a string", and this GDD keeps that scope. It owns formats and lookup. It does not own the meaning of a decision (Filing & Decision), the gating of a line (Dialogue & Interrogation), when a sign is shown (Dossier projections, Paper UI) or what a fact unlocks (Investigation & Fact); each of those systems consumes a format defined here and applies its own rules. Implementation questions raised during design (which Godot translation mechanism carries the string table, whether the compiler runs in the editor or in CI) are recorded as ADR inputs, not decided here.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: direct. The player reads every line this system holds; the format itself is invisible. Shaped with the creative director (three candidate framings; "the paper is the event" chosen, with the two-voice rule from "reading over the state's shoulder" folded into Detailed Design as a register tag; "paper arriving" rejected because Pillar 2 already owns the pressure).*

**Nothing happens in Paper Walls that is not written down.** The emotion this system serves is dread by reading: the slow, close attention of someone who knows every word on the page was put there on purpose.

The anchor moment is Vera at the desk at seven fifty. Six folders sit in the intake tray. Five are blue-tabbed. The sixth is grey-green, with a red tab, string knotted twice, serial IV/4471/R.31, and a slip in a handling officer's hand: *for reconciliation*. Nothing has happened yet, and the player already knows the act has begun. The words did it. There was no cutscene and no music sting.

Every line the player meets, whether it is a leaf, a log page, a spoken sentence or the state's closing paper line, is an object the player handles and reads. None of it is narration laid over the game. The language is dry and procedural. It is in the present tense. It names things exactly: the serial, the custodian, the knot. Like Papers, Please, the document is the game. Like Pentiment, the way a line is set down (hand, carbon, stamp, strike-through) carries meaning as much as its words do.

The format holds two voices and never lets them mix. The human voice is the beat prose and the dialogue: interior, specific, a hand on a dry press. The state's voice is the paper line, the form wording, the order line: clerical, agentless, stamped. The player learns to hear the gap between them, and that gap is where the game lives. *Cost accepted:* every string carries material metadata (hand, form, carbon or original, register), which makes authoring heavier and gives Paper UI more surface to render.

**Pillar alignment.** Pillar 1, "every fact is a weapon": a document with a name in it beats a document with none, so the format binds documents to facts and facts to dossier uses, and the validator refuses flavour-only paper. Pillar 2, "the state is a character": the state's voice is a first-class register in the data, never a style note. Pillar 4, "consequences cross lives": the writing attaches to state, never to path.

**The one rule the format must obey: the object in the room, never the branch.** A string may refer only to what is present in its own scene: objects, people and the current dossier projections. It may never refer to another beat, scene, choice or path. Content may be gated only by state (dossier reads, held facts, chapter sheet, phase); it may never be gated by history (`visited`, `chose`, `after_beat`). Three consequences follow and are enforced in Detailed Design: string ids are keyed to an owner (action class, document, form, speaker), never to a route; the condition language has no scene-id, beat-id or option-id predicate; and the validator rejects a line whose text or metadata names another beat's id or class. If a line needs the past, the past is written into the dossier and read back from it, which is Pillar 4's mechanism and the only one allowed.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

*Drafted with the game designer (primary), the narrative director, the localization lead and the writer. Where the four disagreed, the ruling is stated in the rule with the alternative it displaced. Items marked **(PROV)** rest on a dependent system whose GDD is not yet written; each becomes a reciprocal line in that GDD.*

**0. Vocabulary.** A *content object* is one authored thing with an id: a chapter, scene, document, dialogue, decision, beat, epilogue, form table or cast entry. A *string* is one piece of player-visible text with a string id, resolved per locale. A *source file* is what a writer edits. A *compiled file* is what the game loads. A *class* is a polyscene action, event or ending class name (the story engine's vocabulary; this GDD never invents one). A *segment* is one string of kind `prose` (a paragraph). The *lock* is the id registry (Rule 3).

**1. Two layers, one direction.** Content is authored in source files, compiled by a build tool into plain data, and loaded by the game from the compiled data only.

- *Authoring sources* live under `assets/content-src/`, listed in `assets/content-src/manifest.yaml`. Prose-shaped types (beats, epilogues, chapter openings, documents, dialogue) are Markdown files with a YAML front-matter block for metadata and a line-oriented body whose syntax is fixed per type (Rule 5). Graph- and table-shaped types (chapters, scenes, decisions, the three form tables, the cast and hand registries, `ui` strings) are YAML. **Player-visible text never appears as a YAML value except in the form tables and `ui` strings, where every string is short and quoted.** The approved `design/narrative/dialogue/act-one-beats.md` is already in the beat syntax and migrates by being moved, not rewritten.
- *The compiler* `tools/content/compile.py` (Python, so it runs beside the polyscene topic under `tools/`) parses sources, allocates ids through the lock, emits compiled structure JSON under `assets/data/content/` (one file per chapter plus `content.common.json` for forms, cast, beats and epilogues), emits one translation CSV per string domain under `assets/data/content/strings/` in the engine's CSV format (plural and context columns), snapshots the polyscene world's class list to `world_classes.json` (`{class, kind: action|event|ending}`), and writes `manifest.json` with `content_schema_version`, a content hash and `shipped_locales`. Output is deterministic: sorted keys, LF line endings, no timestamps. Compiled files are committed; CI recompiles and fails on any diff.
- *Structure JSON holds string ids only, never text.* Text travels in the CSVs. Pure-logic systems therefore handle ids and never strings, which is what lets them stay headless.
- *The runtime* loads `manifest.json` and the structure JSON at startup through `ContentStore` (Rule 9), and text through `StringLookup` (Rule 10). It never parses Markdown or YAML.
- *Rationale:* writers keep writing prose in the shape they already use; diffs of compiled data are reviewable; the player's machine needs no parser and no Python. *Rejected:* compiling at game start or in an editor plugin (not reproducible across machines, and the validator could not run in CI); authoring directly in JSON or YAML (the writer's ruling: the state's wording is full of colons, brackets and hashes that break unquoted YAML, and nobody writes a 650-character paragraph in a JSON string).

**2. The compiler lints; the validator validates.** Two checkers run, in this order, and both are build gates.

- *Compiler lint* (Python, inside `compile.py`): everything that can be decided from the sources alone. Syntax, id grammar, duplicate ids, string caps, ASCII rule, dangling references between content objects, missing beats for a class, missing `paper` lines, missing `fallback` answers, serial mismatches against a document's front matter. It is fast (target under two seconds per changed file in watch mode, Tuning Knobs) and reports every error at once as `file:line` plus a one-line fix.
- *Validator* `ContentValidator` (GDScript, headless, under the code root, run by `godot --headless -s`): everything that needs the dossier schema, the condition evaluator, the budget counter or another system's registry. It is the one implementation of those three things; the runtime uses the same classes. It runs over the compiled output, the lock, the CSVs, `assets/data/dossier/schema.json`, the Fact index and `world_classes.json`. Exit non-zero on any error; the release branch also runs it with warnings as errors.
- *Rationale:* GDD 1 §9 and §11 place the budget counter and the validator in the headless model code that the simulator imports; a second Python copy of the condition evaluator would drift from the one the game runs. The lint exists because the writer needs feedback faster than an engine start-up. *Rejected:* one Python validator (two evaluators); one GDScript checker only (too slow for a watch loop).

**3. The id lock.** `assets/content-src/idlock.yaml` is committed and records every content id and string id ever compiled: `{id, owner, kind, rev, state, first_shipped, replaced_by}`.

- A new source slot gets its id at first compile. A sequence number takes the next unused ordinal for its owner and is never reused; file order and id order may differ.
- A segment is matched to its lock entry by normalised-text alignment, so an inserted paragraph does not shift its neighbours. Alignment is *ambiguous* when a segment's normalised text is within `ALIGN_MAX_EDIT` edits of two or more lock entries of the same owner, or of none; then the lint fails and asks for an explicit anchor comment (`<!-- id: beat.read_file.3 -->`), which is the only time an id appears inside prose. Before freeze the writer never sees an id.
- **Content ids obey the same permanence rule as string ids** (chapter, scene, action, dialogue, answer and decision ids), because GDD 1's effect `source` tags are persisted in saves and change logs. A post-ship rename of a content id is a Save/Load `migrate` job **(PROV: Save/Load)**.
- States: `draft` (in source only) → `assigned` (in the lock; text editable; rename forbidden) → `frozen` (text locked for the localisation hand-off) → `shipped` (`first_shipped` set; no purge) → `retired` (tombstone; permanent; may carry `replaced_by`). Forward only. `idlock purge <id>` deletes only an id with no `first_shipped`.
- A retired id referenced by live content is an error; a retired id found in a save resolves through `replaced_by` with a warning, never a blank.
- *Rationale:* ids must survive typo fixes (so no text hashes), paragraph insertion (so no line numbers) and class renames (so the class name is copied into the owner slug once, at birth, and the lock maps class to owner thereafter). *Rejected:* content-hash ids; positional ids; hand-assigned ids.

**4. String ids.**

- *Grammar:* `id := seg ("." seg){2,4}`, `seg := [a-z0-9][a-z0-9_]{0,23}`, whole id at most 64 characters, ASCII lowercase only, no diacritics ever. Only the last segment may be all digits (the ordinal).
- *Domains* (first segment, closed list; adding one amends this GDD): `beat`, `epi`, `doc`, `dlg`, `dec`, `sign`, `order`, `file`, `fact`, `cast`, `place`, `chap`, `scene`, `form`, `ui`, `cap`, `acc`. `cap` is Audio captions and `acc` accessibility text **(PROV: Audio, Paper UI)**.
- *Semantics:* the second segment is the owner key (class slug, document slug, chapter id, scene id, form id, speaker). Ids carry no mutable in-world data: **no serials, no dates, no field values in ids.** `doc.reg_212.l14` is legal; `doc.iv_4471_r31.l14` is not, because a serial is in-world data that can be corrected. Examples: `beat.read_file.1`, `epi.e1.body.2`, `sign.car_in_street`, `dlg.a1_vera_s2.query_ledger.rybar.1`, `file.vera.discrepancy.entry`, `chap.a1_vera.open.1`.
- *Kinds and caps.* The domain and position fix the string's kind, and the kind fixes the length rule (counted in NFC code points, markup excluded, each placeholder at its declared worst-case length):

| Kind | Used for | Source cap (`en`) |
|---|---|---|
| `line` | dialogue lines, `says` and `record` texts, paper lines, sign, order and file-form lines, stage lines | `LINE_CAP` 120, error |
| `label` | answer labels, UI buttons, tabs, stamps | `LABEL_CAP` 48, warning |
| `prose` | beat paragraphs, epilogue and opening paragraphs, document leaf prose | `PROSE_WARN` 800 warning, `PROSE_CAP` 1,200 error |
| `invariant` | cast names, place names, serials, form codes | identical in every locale, error on difference |

- *Ruling on the 120-character rule:* the beats file's convention "every line is a localisation string; no line exceeds 120 characters" is read as applying to **`line` kinds**: everything that is spoken, stamped or written on a form. A prose paragraph is one string and is capped by `PROSE_CAP`. The narrative director proposed sentence segments of 120 characters each with compiler anchors; the writer, the game designer and the localization lead each argued that sentence-level units break word order and context in translation and put ids in the writer's prose. The paragraph wins; the narrative director's reading survives as `PROSE_WARN`, which flags a paragraph long enough to deserve a second look.
- *Placeholders* are named only (`{courier_name}`, `{n}`, `{gap}`, `{title}`), declared per string with a type from a **closed list** (`cast` name, `place` name, `title` from the cast table, `plan_week`/`date` from the Calendar, a `count` from a document's own front matter, `gap`) and a worst-case length; **no placeholder is ever filled from a dossier field** (V44), so no scalar can reach the player through a string; the set must be identical in every locale. Plurals use the engine CSV plural columns: `{n, plural, one{…} few{…} many{…} other{…}}`, `other` always required, every category of the locale's plural rules supplied. No gender `select` in MVP: a string id belongs to one speaker, and the translator inflects from the speaker and addressee metadata.
- *Changed meaning test:* would an existing translation now be factually wrong? Yes: new id, old id retired. No (typo or same-meaning rewording): same id, `rev` bumped, other locales marked `stale`.
- *Rejected:* one string per hard-wrapped source line (breaks sentence order in translation); source text as key (gettext style: a typo renames the key, and "Spolny." means different things in different rooms).

5a. *Beats carry no gates, variants, conditions or effects.* A beat that read differently by state could not stand alone in any order, and effects are the engine's. The `paper` line's blank is the protected token `{gap}`, never the literal "[blank]". `kind: opening` beats are keyed per protagonist and listed on the chapter.

5b. *Documents.* Source body syntax is one line per entry: `slug | binding | text`, with `^slug` marking marginalia and an empty binding meaning "always prints". The writer writes each entry once; the compiler derives the struck form (`false`) and the gap (`unknown`) from the one string, so there is nothing to keep in sync. A scalar may be bound only as a threshold against a **constant name** (`vera.attention >= ATTN_LOOK`), never a number, matching GDD 1's rule that scalars never project. A line *yields* facts where they are first read (`@ fact parcel_was_medicine`), so there is one place to look for a fact's birth.

5c. *Dialogue is a forward-only sequence, not a node graph.* Procedure is linear; a polite room has no back button. `next` may point only forward, there are no loops, and the lint proves every path reaches `end`. Every `ask` has at least one answer with no `when` (the writer's `fallback`; declining is always an answer and writes "Declined to state." with `carry: true`, so the item returns on the form). Gated answers are **hidden**, never greyed, because a greyed option leaks the dossier. Each answer shows two texts: `says`, the player's words, and `record`, the other side's dry version on the form; the gap between them is where the pressure lives. Every spoken item carries `register` (`public`/`private`) and `tell` (the wrong address is deliberate). Portrait requests are one word from Character Presentation's closed vocabulary, `neutral | guarded | evasive | exposed` (GDD 10; default Neutral, sticky within a dialogue run, never inferred from the dossier; `reads`/`waits` are stage directions V31 rejects as portrait requests; amendment applied). *Rejected:* Ink or Yarn (a node-graph model and an external runtime, neither covered by the pinned engine reference); a general `goto` graph (nothing in the tone needs it, and it cannot be proven loop-free by a lint).

5d. *The reserved `pace` field.* Pillar 5 permits timed choices only under interrogation or search, and the systems index places timers at Vertical Slice. Dialogue `context` is a closed enum (`desk`, `cellar`, `gate`, `telephone`, `landing`, `interrogation`, `search`); the format reserves `pace {seconds, default_answer}` on an answer set, **legal only where `context` is `interrogation` or `search` and never in act one**, and **does not define its semantics**. `default_answer` must name an authored, visible answer that carries at least one effect: a timeout is a choice with a cost (Pillar 3), never a free exit. Paper UI renders no numeric countdown for it (no meters; reciprocal line for the Paper UI GDD). MVP content never sets it, and the lint errors on it while `content_schema_version` is the MVP version. Dialogue & Interrogation defines *how* it works **(PROV)**; the narrative director's recommendation that it never be used is recorded in Open Questions as a recommendation, not the default.

**6. Conditions are a closed data grammar, with one evaluator.**

```
cond := {is_true: F} | {is_false: F} | {is_unknown: F}
      | {at_least: [F, K]} | {at_most: [F, K]}          # scalars only; K is a constant NAME, never a literal
      | {has_fact: fact_id} | {was_assumed: F} | {phase_at_least: 0..3}
      | {all: [cond…]} | {any: [cond…]} | {not: cond}
F    := <record>.<field> | sheet.<field>
```

- Depth at most 4 (error); at most 8 leaves per condition (warning). `K` in `at_least`/`at_most` must be a constant name from the dossier tuning file; a literal number in content is a V18 error (test fixtures only). *A number in content is a projection of a scalar, which GDD 1 forbids.*
- `not` is boolean negation, so `not is_true(x)` is true on `false` and on `unknown` (GDD 1 §6.7, closed world).
- **There is no `visited`, `chose`, `after_beat`, scene-id, beat-id or option-id leaf**, and none will be added: content is gated by state, never by history (Player Fantasy's one rule). If a line needs the past, the past is written into the dossier or the chapter sheet and read back.
- `Condition.evaluate(cond, reader)` is one shared headless implementation owned here; `reader` is injected with `read(target, field)`, `has_fact(id)`, `phase()` and a fallback `leaf(name, args) → bool` consulted for any leaf name outside this grammar (so a system may add leaves, as Reactive Event Rules does with `changed`, `contradicted` and `beat_in`, without re-implementing `all`/`any`/`not`). Consumers never re-implement it. `Condition.fields_in(cond)` feeds the budget counts.
- *Budget accounting* (GDD 1 Formulas §6: `R_c` is the set of distinct dossier, sheet and calendar fields read): dossier and sheet fields count as distinct reads; `has_fact` counts zero (held facts are not dossier state); `phase_at_least` counts as one virtual read `regime.phase` **(PROV: confirm with GDD 1's owner; the alternative is four regime fields)**.
- The Rules system may need `changed:` or `contradicted:` leaves for the current boundary's change records; it adds them in its own wrapper, never by editing this grammar, and **they are illegal in any content `when`** (V18 error) **(PROV: Rules)**.
- **The chapter sheet is state, not history.** A sheet field names the current state of an object or person in the chapter (`burned`, `ledger_open`, `has_medicine`), never an event, a visit or a choice (`visited_s2`, `asked_rybar`, `chose_burn`), because `sheet.saw_s2` is `visited` under another name. The lint checks sheet field names against `assets/content-src/lint/sheet_wordlist.yaml` (`visited`, `seen`, `saw`, `asked`, `chose`, `met`, `done`, `after`, `_once`), warning normally and error on the release branch (V43); the human-review checklist carries the judgement call.
- *Rejected:* an embedded expression language (code in data; the validator could not count reads exactly); the orchestrator's `{sheet: burned}` shorthand (GDD 1 reads the sheet through the same `read(target, field)`, so it is `{is_true: sheet.burned}`).

**7. Effect batches and `source`.** Authored effects live in four places: a scene action's `effects`, a decision option's `effects`, a dialogue answer's `effects`, and a chapter's `assumes`. Authors write `@ set vera.discrepancy true` and **never write `source`**; the compiler derives it from position and container: decision or action → writer `filing`; dialogue → `dialogue`; `assumes` → `assume`. The derived form is GDD 1's `writer:chapter_id/scene_id:action_id`, with `action_id = <dialogue_id>_<ask_id>_<answer_id>` for dialogue; the compiled JSON stores the full string and the validator re-parses it with GDD 1's parser. Rules batches are not authored here; Rules data cites `rules:chapter/scene:rule_id` under its own format **(PROV)**. *Rationale:* GDD 1 makes `source` mandatory and parsed, and a hand-written tag drifts. *Rejected:* authors write `source` by hand. **(PROV: confirm with GDD 1 that `action_id` is `[a-z0-9_]+` with no cap below 64.)**

**8. Form tables.** Their row ids are the `form id` values that `schema.json`'s `projections` attribute references, so GDD 1 and this GDD meet at the id.

- *Sign table:* rows `{id, fields[] (any-of), at_least?, lag (default SIGN_LAG_DEFAULT), text, overprint {text}}`. Two rows may share one `text` id (`sign.paper_not_come` for `border.courier_detained` and `border.route_burned`). Deliberate silence is an explicit row `{field, no_sign: true}`; act one declares `kastan.rewarded`, `vera.reported_jan`, `jan.source_traced`. The overprint text is the sign's second line when GDD 1's overprint condition holds (another protagonist's hand).
- *Order forms:* rows `{id, field, value, text, form}`; text may carry named placeholders (`{courier_name}` from the cast table); `border.log_gap` is `{field, no_order: true}`.
- *File forms:* live on document lines (Rule 5b), not in a table: the `entry` text is the `true` form, the compiler derives struck and gap. Scalar threshold lines `{field, at_least: <constant>, text}` and marginalia templates `{tag_prefix, text, hand, side, values {tag_value: cast id}}` are the only table-shaped file forms.
- *Blank safety:* a blank means `unknown` in the Files view, so a missing or failed string must **never** render as blank (Rule 10).

**9. Lookup API.** `ContentStore` is a headless `RefCounted`, injected, never an autoload: `load(manifest_path) → errors[]`; `chapter(id)` with its declarations; `scene(chapter_id, scene_id)`; `document(id)`, `dialogue(id)`, `decision(id)`, `cast(id)`; `beat(class) → beat | null`; `epilogue(ending_class)` falling back to `act_fallback` with a logged warning; `sign_table()`, `order_forms()`, `file_forms()`; `fact_index()` (every `yields` by document line and answer); `string_ids()`; `content_version()`. Returned objects are read-only views; a test asserts that mutating one does not alter the store.

**10. String lookup.** `StringLookup` is an interface with two implementations and one contract: `text(id, params) → String`, `text_n(id, n, params)`, `has(id)`, `locale()`. Fallback order in every build: current locale → `en` → the literal `⟦id⟧`. **Never blank** (Rule 8). The *engine implementation* lives in the presentation layer, is the only class that calls `tr()`, and loads the compiled CSVs through the engine's translation system (the sourced path: `tr()`, CSV with plural and context columns, live translation preview in the editor). The *headless implementation* reads the same CSVs' `en` column directly and serves the story simulator, the validator's messages and the unit tests. Locale choice comes from Settings Store **(PROV: Settings Store's index row lists no `locale`; raised in Open Questions)**. **NOT SOURCEABLE** from the pinned reference, and gated to an ADR spike before the engine implementation is designed: the `TranslationServer` 4.7 API shape and missing-key behaviour; whether `tr()` works without a scene tree; whether the CSV importer tolerates our extra metadata columns and the exact plural and context column names; PO import mapping; per-script font fallback; `JSON.parse_string` size and precision limits. *Rejected:* a custom JSON string table bypassing `tr()` for everything (loses the editor's live preview, the one translation workflow the pinned reference documents); PO consumed directly by the engine (makes the simulator depend on unsourced behaviour). PO remains the *translator exchange* format: the compiler exports PO per locale per domain from the CSVs and ingests translated PO back, so agencies see a standard format and the game sees CSV.

**11. Localisation.** The source locale is `en`; its CSV column is derived-only (the compiler regenerates it and merges the other locales by key). The compiler also emits a dev-only `pseudo` locale (`PSEUDO_EXPANSION` longer, accented Latin, bracketed, protected spans untouched) to catch overflow and unlocalised literals. **Completeness is judged per content object**: if any string of a beat, document or dialogue is missing in a locale, the whole object falls back to `en`, because a single sheet of paper must never be half in one language. Targets are not capped at `LINE_CAP`; they are capped by `TARGET_LINE_HARD` and the string's `max_chars`, and warned above a banded expansion factor (Formulas §3). Invariants: cast and place names are never translated and stay ASCII; serials and form codes are data, never in translatable text; `Spolny`/`Sp.`, `pan`, `pani` are invariant glossary terms, `{title}` resolves from the cast table to a protected id; dates are stored ISO with two renderers, a `form` style (fixed numeric, in-world, no month names) and a `ui` style (locale-formatted, save slots only); "Plan Week {n}" is a template; no thousands separators in-world. The no-diacritics rule applies to Zelmavian names, not to target-language prose. MVP ships `en` only, with the `pseudo` locale and a `de`+`ru` sample of `SAMPLE_STRINGS` strings as non-shipping proof (Acceptance Criteria). Stamps are stored already cased; nothing is uppercased at runtime. Every string in the state register (`paper`, `record`, order, file-form and sign lines, epilogue `head`) carries a mandatory compiler-emitted translator note, "agentless and clerical; never name an agent or a cause; do not convert to active voice"; translator notes describe the room, never the route (V41); and "state voice still agentless" is a linguistic-QA item for a `reviewed` locale.

**12. Freeze.** Two stages. *Id freeze* (at `validated`, Rule 13): ids may be added, never renamed, retired only by tombstone, which protects saves, tests and evidence screenshots even for an `en`-only MVP. *Text freeze*, `TEXT_FREEZE_LEAD_WEEKS` before translation hand-off: CI compares every string against a frozen `id → hash` manifest. Post-freeze change classes: (a) typo or punctuation, same id, `rev` bump, no retranslation; (b) same-meaning rewording, same id, `rev` bump, other locales `stale`, the old translation ships as fallback; (c) meaning change, new id, old retired. Classes (b) and (c) need the narrative director's and the localization lead's approval; a gate or effect change on a frozen line is a behaviour change and goes through normal review, not the string process.

**13. Hot reload is editor and debug only.** `ContentStore.reload()` runs only between scene boundaries, emits `content_reloaded`, refuses if the open chapter's `reads`, `writes` or `assumes` changed (a chapter restart is required), and is absent from release builds. Patches ship as a new `content_version`, never a live swap. The writer's author-time tools (`content preview doc <id> --set field=value [--matrix]`, dialogue preview under a given state, the budget footer `reads 14/20 writes 19/24 xread 3/6 xwrite 5/8 (+2 vs last)` naming the edit that moved each count, the longest-string readout per kind, watch mode) are part of `tools/content/` and are specified in Acceptance Criteria. The preview tools may list hidden answers and gates for authors; **no debug overlay, preview output, gate listing or budget footer is reachable in a release build**, the same rule as hot reload.

**14. Headless rule.** `ContentStore`, `Condition`, `ContentValidator`, the headless `StringLookup` and the lock reader import nothing from the scene tree, from `tools/` or from `tests/`. Only the engine `StringLookup` may call `tr()`. The story simulator and the unit tests load content through the same `ContentStore`. A CI path check enforces the direction, as GDD 1 §11 does for the dossier.

### States and Transitions

**Content set** (per act):

| State | Entered when | Allowed | Leaves to |
|---|---|---|---|
| `draft` | Sources exist | Free renames and deletions; `idlock purge` | `validated` when lint and validator pass with 0 errors in CI |
| `validated` | CI green; compiled output and lock committed | Text and structure edits (revalidate); ids now permanent (id freeze) | `string_frozen` |
| `string_frozen` | Act content-complete; narrative director signs off; `freeze` command writes the hash manifest | Text changes only through `revise` (classes a/b/c, Rule 12); new ids allowed; removals tombstone | `shipped` |
| `shipped` | Release tag sets `first_shipped` on every lock entry | New ids; `revise`; tombstone | `patched` (same rules, new `content_version`) |

Backward from `string_frozen` is a structural rework, allowed only through a recorded decision, and it leaves tombstones.

**Locale** (per locale): `absent` → `partial` (object-level fallback to `en`) → `complete` → `stale` (a source string was revised; the old translation ships as fallback) → `reviewed` (linguistic QA and the layout test passed, screenshots retained). Only `reviewed` locales are listed in `manifest.shipped_locales`; `en` must be `reviewed` to ship.

**String id**: `draft` → `assigned` → `frozen` → `shipped` → `retired` (Rule 3). Forward only; `retired` may carry `replaced_by`.

**A dialogue at runtime** (the format's view; Dialogue & Interrogation owns the rest): `items` are visited in order; a `line` with a false `when` is skipped; an `ask` shows its ungated answers plus those whose `when` holds; an answer applies its `effects` (batched for the scene boundary), appends its `record` to the transcript, and moves to `next`; `carry` re-queues the `ask` after the next item; `end` closes.

## Formulas

*Proposed by the systems designer. All constants named here live in `assets/content-src/tuning.yaml` and compile to `assets/data/content/tuning.json`, so the Python lint and the GDScript validator read one copy. Every length is counted in NFC code points, markup excluded, placeholders at declared worst-case (Core Rule 4). Two proposed constants were challenged and one added (`TARGET_LABEL_HARD`); the challenges are recorded in place.*

### 1. String id validity

The id validity formula is defined as:

`id_ok(id) = match(id, ID_RE) ∧ cp(id) ≤ ID_MAX_LEN ∧ dom(id) ∈ DOMAINS ∧ ∀ i < last: ¬all_digits(seg_i)` with `ID_RE = ^[a-z][a-z0-9_]{0,SEG_MAX_LEN−1}(\.[a-z0-9][a-z0-9_]{0,SEG_MAX_LEN−1}){2,4}$`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `id` | string | ASCII | data file (lock, sources) | Candidate string or content id |
| `SEG_MAX_LEN` | int | 16–32 | constant (24) | Longest segment |
| `ID_MAX_LEN` | int | 48–96 | constant (64) | Longest whole id |
| `DOMAINS` | set | 17 names | constant (Core Rule 4) | Closed first-segment list |
| `seg_i` | string | — | calculated | i-th dot-separated segment; only the last may be all digits (the ordinal) |
| `id_ok` | bool | — | calculated | V02 passes |

**Output Range:** boolean. Five full segments would be 124 characters, so `ID_MAX_LEN` binds before the segment rule does; the lint reports length first.
**Example:** `dlg.a1_vera_s2.query_ledger.rybar.1` is 35 code points, five segments, domain `dlg`, last segment digits only: pass. `beat.read_file.1` (16) passes; `Beat.read_file.1` fails the charset; `beat.3.1` fails the ordinal rule (segment 2 all digits). *Rejected:* `ID_MAX_LEN` 128 (PO and CSV keys would cope, but the budget footer, the lint's `file:line` output and the translator sheet wrap, and nothing needs it: the longest act-one id is 41).

### 2. Effective length and source cap per kind

The effective length formula is defined as:

`len_eff(s) = cp(strip_markup(s)) − Σ_p cp("{" + p + "}") + Σ_p worst(p)` (for a plural message, the maximum over its categories), and the source cap check is `cap_ok(s) = len_eff(s) ≤ min(CAP[kind(s)], [hand(s) ∉ typewriter]·ANNOTATION_CAP)`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `s` | string | — | data file | Source (`en`) text |
| `p` | placeholder | 0–4 per string | data file (string metadata) | Named placeholder with declared `worst(p)` |
| `worst(p)` | int | 1–48 | data file | Worst-case rendered length; cast names use the longest `cast.name` (act one: 14), `{n}` 2, `{gap}` the line's `gap_width` |
| `LINE_CAP` | int | 100–140 | constant (120) | `line` kind, error |
| `LABEL_CAP` | int | 32–64 | constant (48) | `label` kind, warning |
| `PROSE_WARN` | int | 500–1,000 | constant (800) | `prose` kind, warning |
| `PROSE_CAP` | int | 1,000–1,600 | constant (1,200) | `prose` kind, error |
| `ANNOTATION_CAP` | int | 60–100 | constant (80) | Any line in a non-typewriter hand (Core Rule 16), error |
| `len_eff` | int | 0–unbounded | calculated | Length as the widest rendering would be |
| `cap_ok` | bool | — | calculated | V09 passes |

**Output Range:** `len_eff ≥ 0`, unbounded above; zero is itself an error (an empty string would render as a blank, Core Rule 8). `invariant` strings have no cap; V10 checks them for equality across locales.
**Example:** `order.let_pass.courier` = "Pass {courier_name}. Papers in order." is 37 code points; minus 14 for the token, plus 14 worst-case gives `len_eff = 37 ≤ 120`. A `record` text "Declined to state." is 18. A marginal note in Berta's hand of 84 code points fails on `ANNOTATION_CAP` although it passes `LINE_CAP`. *Rejected:* counting UTF-8 bytes (Cyrillic doubles, so `ru` would trip caps `en` never could); grapheme clusters (no sourced engine API; code points are also what the font-matrix check V40 counts, so one unit serves both).

### 3. Target fit check

The target hard-cap check is defined as:

`fit_hard(s, L) = len_eff(s_L) ≤ min(hard(kind(s)), max_chars(s))` with `hard = {line: TARGET_LINE_HARD, label: TARGET_LABEL_HARD, prose: ⌈PROSE_CAP × 1.3⌉}`; the band budget is `budget(L, n) = max(⌈b(L, n) × n⌉, [n > 20]·⌈b₁(L) × 20⌉, [n > 60]·⌈b₂(L) × 60⌉)` where `b(L, n) = b₁ if n ≤ 20, b₂ if n ≤ 60, b₃ otherwise`; and `expansion_ok(L, len_en, len_L) = len_L ≤ budget(L, len_en)`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `L` | locale | shipped ∪ partial | data file (manifest) | Target locale; an unlisted locale uses `de`'s bands (the widest) |
| `len_en`, `len_L` | int | 0–`PROSE_CAP`, 0–unbounded | calculated (§2) | Effective lengths of source and target |
| `max_chars(s)` | int/absent | 8–1,560 | data file (string metadata, derived from the container fit caps `DOC_FIT`, `FILE_ENTRY_FIT`, `SAYS_FIT`, `SIGN_FIT`, `STAMP_FIT` × `max_lines`; fixtures carry `max_chars` explicitly until Paper UI declares containers) | Container cap; absent → `hard` alone |
| `TARGET_LINE_HARD` | int | 140–200 | constant (160) | Hard cap for `line` targets, error |
| `TARGET_LABEL_HARD` | int | 48–80 | constant (64, added) | Hard cap for `label` targets, error |
| `b₁, b₂, b₃` | float | 0.5–2.5 | data file (`expansion_band_<L>`) | de 1.9/1.5/1.30, ru 1.6/1.3/1.20, fr 1.6/1.3/1.20, ja 1.0/0.8/0.70 (post-MVP, needs line-height 1.15) |
| `fit_hard` | bool | — | calculated | V38 error |
| `expansion_ok` | bool | — | calculated | V38 warning |

**Output Range:** `budget` runs from 0 (empty source) to 156 for `de` at a 120-code-point line, which is below `TARGET_LINE_HARD`: a full-cap `en` line translated within band can never trip the hard cap, so the hard cap only fires on `max_chars` or an over-band translation. That ordering is deliberate: the band is advisory, the container is law. The raw band table is non-monotone at its boundaries (`de`: 20 allows 38, 21 allows 32), which is why the formula takes the envelope: `budget(de, 21) = max(32, 38) = 38`, `budget(de, 61) = max(80, 38, 90) = 90`. *Challenge adopted:* a 160 hard cap on a `label` is meaningless (a 48-code-point tab translated to 160 fits the rule and breaks the tab), hence `TARGET_LABEL_HARD`. `max_lines ≤ 3` is Paper UI's headless layout test, not this formula: code points cannot see wrapping.
**Example:** `sign.car_in_street` = "A car in the street that was not there yesterday." (48) → `de` "Ein Wagen auf der Straße, der gestern nicht da war." (51; ß is one code point); `budget(de, 48) = max(72, 38) = 72`; `51 ≤ 72`, pass; `51 ≤ min(160, max_chars = 96)` (`SIGN_FIT` 48 × 2 lines), pass. *Rejected:* the raw bands without the envelope (a writer who lengthens a 20-point source by one character shrinks the translator's room by six); a single 1.3 factor for all lengths (short strings expand far more).

### 4. Pseudo-locale expansion

The pseudo length formula is defined as:

`len_pseudo(s) = max(⌈(1 + PSEUDO_EXPANSION) × len_en⌉, budget(de, len_en))`; the pseudo text is `"[" + accent(en) + pad("~", len_pseudo − len_en) + "]"` with placeholders, protected spans and `{gap}` untouched; `accent` is the committed map `tools/content/pseudo_map.json`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PSEUDO_EXPANSION` | float | 0.2–0.6 | constant (0.35) | Floor expansion fraction |
| `budget(de, ·)` | int | §3 | calculated | Band budget of the widest candidate locale |
| `len_pseudo` | int | `len_en`–unbounded | calculated | Padded length, brackets excluded (they are the overflow marker, +2) |

**Output Range:** `len_pseudo ≥ len_en`; the brackets push every string two code points past its budget on purpose, so the pseudo locale is exempt from V38 (it would fail by construction) and is judged only by the headless layout test and by eye. *Challenge adopted:* a flat 0.35 never catches label overflow, where `de` runs to 1.9; taking the maximum of the floor and the `de` band makes pseudo the worst shipped-candidate locale rather than an average one.
**Example:** `ui.dossier.tab.files` = "Files" (5): flat → 7, band → `⌈1.9 × 5⌉ = 10`; pseudo `[Fïlés~~~~~]`, 12 wide with brackets. *Rejected:* a flat 1.3 pseudo (matches the UI design rule, misses exactly the strings that overflow first).

### 5. Object-level locale completeness and coverage

The object completeness predicate is defined as:

`object_complete(obj, L) = ∀ s ∈ strings(obj): defined(L, s) ∧ placeholders(L, s) = placeholders(en, s) ∧ fit_hard(s, L)`; lookup resolves `text(s, L) = L[s] if object_complete(owner(s), L) else en[s] if defined(en, s) else ⟦s⟧`; coverage is `locale_coverage(L) = |{obj : object_complete(obj, L)}| / |objects|`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `obj` | content object | — | data file | Beat, epilogue, document, dialogue, decision, chapter opening; for `ui` the object is the screen (second segment); each form table is one object |
| `strings(obj)` | set | 1–~60 | calculated | Every string id the object references, including `says`, `record`, `paper`, overprint and the derived struck form |
| `owner(s)` | object | — | data file (lock `owner`) | The object a string belongs to |
| `locale_coverage` | float | 0.0–1.0 | calculated | Fraction of objects wholly translated (INFO) |

**Output Range:** 0.0 (`absent`) through `partial` to 1.0 (`complete`); `shipped` additionally requires locale state `reviewed`. A `stale` string is still `defined`, so a stale object stays complete and ships its old translation (Core Rule 12b).
**Example:** the MVP `de` sample of 200 strings spans 31 objects; 27 are whole → `locale_coverage(de) = 27/31 = 0.871` (`partial`); string coverage 200/2,900 = 0.069 is reported beside it as INFO so nobody mistakes the object figure for progress. *Rejected:* string-weighted coverage as the headline figure (one missing `record` leaves a whole dialogue in English, so the object count is the honest number); string-level fallback (half a sheet in one language, Core Rule 11).

### 6. Content coverage

The content coverage formulas are defined as:

`beat_coverage = |{c ∈ W_action ∪ W_event : beat(c) ≠ null ∧ paper(beat(c)) ≠ null}| / |W_action ∪ W_event|`, `epilogue_coverage = |{e ∈ W_ending ∪ {act_fallback} : epilogue(e) ≠ null}| / (|W_ending| + 1)`, `orphan_beats = {b : class(b) ∉ W}`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `W_action`, `W_event`, `W_ending` | set | act one: ~41, ~9, 9 | data file (`world_classes.json`) | Snapshot of the polyscene world |
| `beat(c)`, `epilogue(e)` | object/null | — | calculated (`ContentStore`) | Lookup by class |
| `beat_coverage`, `epilogue_coverage` | float | 0.0–1.0 | calculated | Must equal 1.0 (V22, V23) |
| `orphan_beats` | set | — | calculated | Must be empty (V22) |

**Output Range:** both ratios are in [0, 1] and anything under 1.0 is an error, naming the classes.
**Example:** act one's world has 50 action and event classes; the beats file carries 49 with `paper` lines → 0.98, error "beat missing: `burn_report`". Endings: nine plus `act_fallback` = 10/10. *Rejected:* a threshold below 1.0 ("90% is fine for a prototype"): a missing beat is a scene the narrator shows as nothing, and the simulator cannot tell silence from a bug.

### 7. Budget contribution per chapter

The content contribution to GDD 1 §6's sets is defined as:

`R_c = ⋃_{k ∈ conds(c)} fields_in(k) ∪ {f : line ∈ docs(c), line.bind.field = f} ∪ {f : line.bind.at_least = (f, K)} ∪ R_c^rules` and `W_c = {(t, f) : e ∈ effects(actions(c) ∪ decisions(c) ∪ answers(c))} ∪ W_c^rules`, where `fields_in` maps `is_true | is_false | is_unknown | at_least | at_most | was_assumed` on `F` to `{F}`, `has_fact` to `∅`, `phase_at_least` to `{regime.phase}`, and `all | any | not` to the union of their children.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `conds(c)` | set | — | calculated | Every `when` in `c`: action, exit, dialogue item, answer, decision option |
| `docs(c)` | set | — | data file | Documents listed on any scene of `c` |
| `R_c^rules`, `W_c^rules` | set | — | data file (Rules export) **(PROV)** | Fields the Rules system reads/writes at `c`'s boundaries; empty when the export is absent |
| `sheet.*` | fields | — | data file (chapter `sheet`) | Count as distinct reads/writes, GDD 1 §6 |
| `R_c`, `W_c` | set | 0–schema size | calculated | Fed to `budget_ok(c)`, GDD 1 §6; the limits are GDD 1's and are not restated here |

**Output Range:** sets, sized 0 to the schema; the thresholds and `X` are GDD 1 §6; this GDD computes only membership. `assumes` is excluded from `W_c` because GDD 1 §6 lists filing, dialogue and rules, and assumptions have their own budget (V21). A document binding is a read: the player seeing the field is the chapter reading it, which is why a bound field must be in `reads` (V18) **(PROV: confirm with GDD 1's owner; the alternative, projection reads free, makes the budget toothless for Vera, whose whole chapter is reading files)**. Sign and order rows are global tables, not chapter-scoped, and contribute nothing here.
**Example:** Vera's act-one chapter: conditions touch 9 dossier and 2 sheet fields; bindings on `reg_212` and folder 4471 add 7, of which 3 overlap → 15; the Rules export adds 2 → `|R_c| = 17 ≤ 20`; footer `reads 17/20`. *Rejected:* counting references rather than distinct fields (GDD 1 says distinct); counting `has_fact` (a held fact is the player's, not the state's).

### 8. Condition depth and leaf count

The condition shape formulas are defined as:

`depth(leaf) = 1; depth(not c) = 1 + depth(c); depth(all|any [cᵢ]) = 1 + max depth(cᵢ)` and `leaves(leaf) = 1; leaves(not c) = leaves(c); leaves(all|any [cᵢ]) = Σ leaves(cᵢ)`; the check is `depth ≤ COND_DEPTH_MAX` (error) and `leaves ≤ COND_LEAVES_WARN` (warning).

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `COND_DEPTH_MAX` | int | 3–6 | constant (4) | Nesting limit |
| `COND_LEAVES_WARN` | int | 4–12 | constant (8) | Leaf warning |
| `depth`, `leaves` | int | 1–unbounded | calculated | Per condition |

**Output Range:** minimum 1 and 1 (a bare leaf); an empty `all`/`any` is an error, not depth 1, because `all([])` is vacuously true and a gate that gates nothing is a mistake.
**Example:** `{all: [{is_true: vera.discrepancy}, {not: {any: [{is_unknown: marta.report_4471}, {has_fact: parcel_was_medicine}]}}]}` → depth 4 (all → not → any → leaf), leaves 3: passes at the limit. *Rejected:* no limit (V06b's satisfiability check is exponential in leaves; 8 leaves bound it at 256 assignments).

### 9. The `rev`/stale predicate

The staleness predicate is defined as:

`stale(L, s) = defined(L, s) ∧ rev_L(s) < rev_stale(s)`, where the lock stores `rev` (bumped on class a or b) and `rev_stale` (set to `rev` on class b only); the freeze check is `frozen_ok(s) = ¬frozen(s) ∨ hash(en[s]) = manifest[s] ∨ revise_logged(s, rev(s))`.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rev`, `rev_stale` | int | 1–unbounded | data file (lock) | Source revision; last meaning-preserving rewording |
| `rev_L(s)` | int | 1–`rev` | data file (locale CSV `rev` column) | Revision the translation was made against |
| `manifest[s]` | hash | — | data file (freeze manifest) | Frozen `en` hash |
| `stale`, `frozen_ok` | bool | — | calculated | V38 warning; freeze gate error |

**Output Range:** boolean; a class (c) change never evaluates here because it is a new id at `rev 1`.
**Example:** `dlg.a1_vera_s2.query_ledger.rybar.1` is at `rev 3, rev_stale 2`; `de` was translated at rev 2 → not stale (rev 3 was a comma); `ru` at rev 1 → stale, ships rev-1 text with a warning. *Rejected:* stale on every `rev` bump (a typo fix would re-queue four locales for nothing, and Core Rule 12a promises it will not).

### 10. Lint watch-mode budget

The watch budget is defined as:

`t_lint(Δ) = t_parse(Δ) + t_lock(Δ) + t_xref(index) ≤ LINT_WATCH_MS`

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Δ` | set of files | 1–all | calculated | Files changed since the last pass |
| `t_xref(index)` | ms | 0–unbounded | calculated | Cross-reference pass over the cached id index, never the sources |
| `LINT_WATCH_MS` | int | 1,000–5,000 | constant (2,000) | Measured on the CI reference machine |

**Output Range:** `t_lint ≥ 0`, unbounded; exceeding the budget prints a warning "lint slow: 2,340 ms" and never fails a build (a slow lint is a tooling bug, not a content error). The full compile has no budget, only a reported time.
**Example:** one beat file changed: parse 40 ms, lock alignment 120 ms, cross-reference over the cached index 300 ms → 460 ms. *Rejected:* a per-file budget (the cross-reference pass is global and cannot be attributed to a file).

## Edge Cases

*Cases 1–20 from the systems designer over the rule and formula space; cases 21–32 from the narrative director over the writing. All resolutions are exact.*

1. **If polyscene renames an action class after ship** (`read_file` → `open_file`): the beat keeps its owner slug `read_file` and its `beat.read_file.*` ids forever; the compiler's `class → beat_slug` map in `world_classes.json` gains `{open_file: read_file}`, V22 resolves through the map, and saves are untouched because they hold ids, not class names. *Core Rule 3: the class name was copied once, at birth.*
2. **If a beat names a class that is not in `world_classes.json`**: V22 error "orphan beat `burn_reprot`, nearest `burn_report`", suggesting only a class within Levenshtein distance 3 of the slug (otherwise "no near match"); the compile still emits the beat so a writer ahead of the engineer is not blocked in watch mode, only in CI. *A typo is the common case, and silent orphans are how beats vanish.*
3. **If two sign rows share one text and one field flips true while the other is unknown**: the sign shows (`fields[]` is any-of), lagged by the row whose field changed, and the overprint condition is evaluated on that row alone; the unknown field contributes nothing (closed world, GDD 1 §6.7). *One cause is enough.*
4. **If a document line is bound to, or an answer's `when` reads, a field outside the chapter's `reads`**: V18 error naming the line or answer and the field; the fix is to add it to `reads` (then V20 re-counts) or unbind. Never auto-add to `reads`. *The declaration is the writer's promise and the budget's input; a tool that widens it hides the god object GDD 1 killed.*
5. **If a `record` text exceeds `LINE_CAP`**: V09 error; `record` is kind `line` because it lands on a form. *The state's wording is short by nature; a long record is a tone failure before it is a length failure.*
6. **If a save holds a retired string or content id**: `ContentStore` resolves `replaced_by` transitively and logs a warning with both ids; a tombstone with no `replaced_by` resolves to `⟦id⟧` with an error in the log; the Files view never blanks, because blank means unknown. Save/Load's `migrate` job rewrites the save on next write **(PROV: Save/Load)**.
7. **If a locale is missing one string of a document**: `object_complete` is false and the whole document renders in `en`, warning "doc.reg_212 falls back: 1 of 23 missing"; the locale stays `partial` and cannot enter `shipped_locales`. *One sheet of paper, one language.*
8. **If a target string lacks a placeholder the source declares, or adds one**: V11 error (shipped) or warning (partial) and the object falls back to `en`. The compiler never substitutes into a mismatched string. *A missing `{courier_name}` is a sentence about nobody; an added one would raise a key error at substitution.*
9. **If a translator renders `{gap}` as a word**: V11 error, since `{gap}` is a protected span absent from the target set. *The blank is what `unknown` looks like; a translated word would assert a fact the state does not hold.*
10. **If an `at_least` binds to a constant name the dossier tuning file does not define**: V12 error "unknown constant `ATTN_LOOKS`; defined: `ATTN_LOOK`, `ATTN_WATCHED`"; the validator joins against `assets/data/dossier/tuning.json` by name only and never inlines a number. *A number in content is a projection of a scalar, which GDD 1 forbids.*
11. **If `pace` appears in MVP content**: lint error at `content_schema_version` 1 "reserved field `pace` has no semantics at schema 1", before the validator runs. *The key stays reserved while Pillar 5's timer question stays with Dialogue & Interrogation.*
12. **If lint passes and the validator fails on the same file, or the reverse**: the validator's verdict stands, since it alone sees the schema; the discrepancy is filed as a tooling bug against `compile.py` with the file attached, and the lint rule is not loosened to match. *The lint exists to be fast, not to be the authority.*
13. **If two branches allocate the same ordinal in `idlock.yaml`**: the merge conflicts textually and must not be resolved by hand; `content idlock remerge` re-runs allocation for the incoming branch's `draft`/`assigned` ids, which have never shipped, and re-anchors their segments by text. An id with `first_shipped` on both sides pointing at different text is an error and a human decision.
14. **If CI's fresh compile differs from the committed JSON or CSV**: the build fails with the diff; the usual causes are an uncommitted recompile, a different Python version, or a non-deterministic emitter, and the third is a tooling bug. *Committed output is the thing reviewed; drift between review and ship is what the rule prevents.*
15. **If `ContentStore.reload()` is called while a dialogue is open**: it refuses with `ERR_BUSY`, logs the open dialogue id, and retries at the next scene boundary. *A dialogue is forward-only state whose item indices could shift under it. Release builds have no reload at all.*
16. **If `ending_select` returns a class with no epilogue**: `epilogue()` returns `act_fallback` and logs a warning; V23 should have caught it at build, so at runtime this is evidence of an unvalidated build and the simulator's run report flags the act. *The fallback epilogue carries no `crosses`, so nothing is invented.*
17. **If a `.tscn` references a `ui.*` id absent from the CSV**: V07 error at build (scene files are scanned for `tr("ui.` literals); at runtime the engine `StringLookup` returns `⟦ui.dossier.tab.flies⟧` visibly, never blank. *A raw id on screen is a bug the tester can read.*
18. **If a cast `name` carries a diacritic**: V10 error "name outside `[A-Za-z'-]`"; the fix is the naming bible's ASCII spelling, never a transliteration table. *Zelmavian has no diacritics; a target-locale prose line may.*
19. **If a `yields` fact has no reader anywhere** (no `has_fact` gate that leads to an effect, no dossier use in the Fact registry; a gate on prose alone does not count): V29 warning until the Fact GDD registers its uses, then error; the fact is kept, not deleted. *A fact born in one chapter may be read in act two; Pillar 1 says every fact lands somewhere, and the validator is where that promise is checked.*
20. **If the Rules export is absent when V20 runs**: V20 counts content references only, prints "V20: Rules export absent; counts are lower bounds", and the release branch (warnings as errors) fails. *A release cannot be cut without knowing what the rules read; while the Rules GDD is unwritten, `manifest.yaml` carries `rules_export_required: false` with a date (Open Questions).*
21. **If a beat's prose names a person its `present` list omits** (a servant, "the captain"): a cast name, or a title that resolves through the cast table's `titles` to one cast id, is a V30 error; add the id to `present` or cut the mention. A role noun that resolves to no cast entry compiles: the person is furniture, not a witness, and may not speak or be handed paper. *The narrator uses `present` to decide who saw the action.*
22. **If an epilogue's body asserts a state-specific detail** (Kastan's cigarettes in E1 when `kastan.rewarded` never held): the sentence is cut or rewritten to hold across every end state that folds into that class; if the detail is load-bearing it moves to the action beat keyed to the class that made it true. No machine check; this is the "assumed facts hold" line of the human review. *Epilogues and beats carry no gates (Core Rule 5a), and `kastan.rewarded` is `no_sign`, so the epilogue is the one place the fact could leak into the wrong branch.*
23. **If a chapter is resumed from a save mid-act**: the `kind: opening` beat and the chapter's `opening[]` play on chapter entry only and are never replayed on resume; the resumed scene starts with no recap. *A recap is history, and Core Rule 6 has no history leaf by design.*
24. **If a `paper` line has no `hand` because the wording is typed**: V24 still errors. Typed is a hand: the registry carries machine hands (`typewriter_intake`, `typewriter_security`), owned by narrative, drawn by art. *Which machine typed a leaf is evidence, and Paper UI cannot render an unattributed line.*
25. **If two fields of one sign row are written by two protagonists in one boundary**: the row prints its `text` once and at most one `overprint` line; which hand overprints is GDD 1's overprint condition, not restated here. The writer therefore writes overprint text that is true under either hand and names nobody. *A sign is one line and its second line.*
26. **If a document's custodian changes at runtime** (the household file goes to security when `vera.reported_jan`): `custodian` is front matter and never changes. The move is authored as a `slip` line on the file bound to the field ("Transferred: Security, PW {n}") plus a second document (a `card`) under the security custodian citing the same serial, also bound. *Files membership is GDD 1's projection from one enum; a transfer slip is how the state moves paper.*
27. **If a `record` or order form must name a courier whose name the state has not established**: `{courier_name}` always resolves from the cast table and never renders empty; the knowing is a dossier field, so the writer authors two lines, one gated on the knowing field with the placeholder, one ungated reading "courier, name not established". Never a conditional inside a string. *Core Rule 10 forbids blanks; a placeholder is a name, not a fact.*
28. **If a `carry: true` answer is taken and the next item is `end`**: the carry is dropped; the dialogue closes and "Declined to state." stays on the form. The lint warns when a carry can never fire. *The end of a call is the end; the record is what the form keeps.*
29. **If a line sets `tell: true` where its register matches the room's**: a warning (V33's inverse), and the flag is kept in the compiled output. *Usually a stale edit from a scene whose register changed; Character Presentation may still want the flag.*
30. **If `act_fallback` is reached with `crosses` empty**: `closing[]` is still required and the page ends on it, but it names no person's next chapter and no hand-off; it closes the act on the stamp. *A crossing sentence would promise a route the ending table does not make.*
31. **If a protagonist's opening beat and a world action beat share a name**: V01/V23 error. Opening keys live in their own owner slug (`beat.open_vera.n`) and may never equal a `world_classes.json` class, because `beat(class)` returns one object.
32. **If a `stage` line names a character who is not in the room**: V30 checks it against the dialogue's speakers and the chapter's protagonist; any other cast name errors. An off-stage presence is written as sound or paper ("a second receiver lifts"), never a name. *A stage direction naming a listener tells the player a fact, and facts are born only on yielding lines.*

**Human review the validator cannot run.** Before an act leaves `validated` for `string_frozen`, the narrative director and the writer read the act end to end under at least two divergent saves and check ten items: (1) every epilogue's assumed details hold in every end state that folds into its class; (2) no player-visible string of any kind (beat, epilogue, `closing`, sign, overprint, order, file form, `record`) tells a protagonist what they or another protagonist did to anyone outside the room; (3) no number reaches the player anywhere; (4) no line refers to another beat, scene or choice; (5) no chapter-sheet field is history under another name; (6) the cost of each decision appears as a person in a room, not a line on a form; (7) each `record` is drier than its `says`; (8) `Spolny` and wrong-register `tell` lines read as deliberate; (9) no two chapters' prose contradict a dated leaf, and typed and handwritten hands match whose desk the paper was on; (10) the pseudo locale shows no clipped line on the sign, order or file views, and the state voice is still agentless in every `reviewed` locale. The reviewer records the two save ids and the findings as the documented-review evidence (Acceptance Criteria).

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Required for a Narrative-category system; specified with the art director from art bible §1–4, §7 and §8. Constraints and vocabulary only; asset specs follow in `/asset-spec`.*

**Art-bible principles carried in the data.**
- *One-line rule (§1.1): every content object is a paper object.* `document.kind` is a closed list art draws (below); a new kind amends the art bible, not only this GDD.
- *Hands select letterforms (§1.2, §4.2).* The hand registry is closed; narrative decides which hand a line uses, art decides what it looks like. Each hand resolves to one of exactly four letterforms (typewriter, cursive, hand-set block, stencil) and one ink (soot, carbon, grey-green, khaki). A new hand is a variant, never a fifth face. `paper`, `record`, marginalia and **sign overprint** lines all carry `hand` (default `office`): an overprint is by definition another protagonist's ink and cannot render without one.
- *Red is budgeted (§4.3, §4.4).* Stamps are the only red at the Confident phase. Every stamp registry row carries `red_class`: `red` (state attention), `carbon` (the copy ghost), `soot`, or `hatch` (outline only; Collapsing's "stamps unanswered"). No `ui.*` or `cap.*` string is ever red; red is never an error colour.
- *Stamps are glyphs, never text (§4.5, §8.1).* A stamp is a greyscale mask the engine tints; the registry `label` is the screen-reader text and the glyph-missing fallback. A `stamp` line's text is the registry label, never free prose.
- *Carbon blue means filed (§4.2).* An answer's `record` and any copied sheet render as carbon with double-strike and "cc"; documents carry `copy: true` when they are duplicates.
- *Annotation limits (§7.2).* Any line in a non-typewriter hand is annotation: at most two rendered lines and `ANNOTATION_CAP` 80 characters, set at least 1.2× body. This is tighter than `LINE_CAP` and is a per-hand cap.
- *No triumphant colour on any ending (§2.1, Pillar 3).* Epilogue stamps are `red`, `soot` or `hatch` only.

**MVP vocabulary for act one** (registries this GDD ships; one visual note each).
- *Hands:* `office` typewriter, soot ribbon (default); `handler_1` typewriter, carbon (handler reports arrive as copies); `rybar` typewriter, soot (the telephone's wording on the form); `typewriter_intake`, `typewriter_security` (machine hands for typed paper lines); `berta` clerk pencil, small cursive, soot tint (initials and counts only); `vera` cursive, grey-green; `jan` hand-set block, soot; `tomas` stencil capitals, khaki; `press` hand-set block, misregistered (samizdat only); `ondra` and other named people: a variant of their side's letterform (state typewriter, people block), never a new face.
- *Forms:* `k1` intake cover sheet (gridded, tab); `district_register` ledger leaf (numbered margin, ruled); `brenava_log` gate log (ruled columns, khaki stencil header); `source_report` fond S (carbon throughout); `transfer_order`; `memo_internal` half sheet; `notice_public` state notice as a torn fragment (sign board); `leaflet_samizdat`; `calendar_leaf`; `dossier_cover` (menu and epilogue last page). Each row carries `paper_kind` and `side` (`state`/`people`).
- *Stamps (red class):* `received` red; `archived`, `destroyed`, `released` red (epilogue closers; narrative director to confirm the E1–E9 mapping); `noted` red (handler); `passed`, `detained` red (gate); `copy` carbon; `date` red (reach by phase). UI-owned, not content: `surveillance`, `void`.
- *Paper kinds:* `file` (bone, grid, tab), `folder` (cover, string tie), `memo` (half sheet), `slip` (narrow strip, lifted corner), `card` (ruled index card, heavier bone), `letter` (ochre-tinted bone, fold crease; the only private paper), `log_page` (ruled columns), `register_leaf` (numbered margin), `leaflet` (soot, misregistered, torn edge).

**Font and rendering requirements the format must make possible.**
- *State's voice:* one monospaced typewriter face, ribbon texture, regular weight, Latin Extended-A plus Cyrillic, no italics. It is the body for every `line`, `prose`, `label`, `invariant` and every transcript.
- *Human voice:* not a body face (art bible §7.7 rejected hand letterforms for dialogue body). The human voice is carried by `hand` on paper lines and marginalia in the three annotation letterforms, Latin Extended-A only; an uncovered glyph falls back to typewriter in the same ink.
- *`{gap}`:* a ruled blank of `gap_width` character cells (integer, monospace), drawn as a baseline rule in the sheet's rule colour, never underscores or a literal "[blank]"; minimum `GAP_MIN` 4 cells, default the entry's worst-case length. In the dissident view the same token renders as a torn absence, decided by the renderer, not the data.
- *Struck line:* a single horizontal strike in the striking hand's ink through legible text; never redaction black, never red. Redaction bars are content (art bible §3.5) and act one has none.
- *Marginalia:* `side` left or right, in the annotating hand, in the margin column outside the 55–72 column text block, never over body; cross-chapter overprints default right.
- *Fit caps per container* (a string cap is not a fit cap; `max_chars`/`max_lines` are derived from these): documents `DOC_FIT` 72 characters per rendered line; dossier field lines `FILE_ENTRY_FIT` 40; answer `says` `SAYS_FIT` 60; the sign board `SIGN_FIT` 48 per line × 2 lines; stamps `STAMP_FIT` 12 per line × 2 lines. The Files entry at 40 per line is the sharpest difference from `LINE_CAP`.
- *Misregistration* is a phase effect on plates, never a styling flag in strings. Markup is closed to double-strike, underline and literal CAPS; no italic token exists.

**Audio.** This system owns no sound. `cap.*` caption strings carry a `speaker` tag (cast id or `sound`) and a `source` tag (`paper`, `stamp`, `typewriter`, `phone`, `door`, `street`); captions render as a typed bracketed line on the transcript sheet, soot, never red. Sounds the paper implies, for the Audio system to own and the audio director to specify: stamp impression (one hit matching art bible §7.4's timing), carbon sheet slide, page turn, calendar tear, typewriter on page arrival and never per character, since text never moves. The mood they match is dry, close and unmusical: paper is the loudest thing in the room.

## Game Feel

Not applicable as a feel target: this system has no input, no motion and no timing of its own. Two feel properties it must not break are stated for the systems that own them: Paper UI's reveal and page turn must never wait on a string lookup (every `text()` call is a dictionary read after load; Acceptance Criteria set the budget), and Dialogue's pace must come from `stage` lines and `carry`, never from a timer this format defines (Rule 5d).

## UI Requirements

This system has no screen of its own. It supplies Paper UI and the HUD with what a screen needs from a string, and it owns three author-time tools that are command-line, not in-game.

- **Per string, for every screen:** `max_chars` and `max_lines` (from the kind and the container Paper UI declares), the placeholder set with worst-case lengths, the `hand`, `form` or `stamp` key, the register, and `{gap}` with `gap_width` so a ruled blank is sized like the missing entry.
- **Never-blank guarantee:** a failed lookup renders `⟦id⟧`, so a blank on screen always means `unknown` on paper and never a missing string.
- **Object-level locale fallback:** a sheet, a beat or an exchange is in one language or the other, never mixed.
- **Author-time tools (CLI under `tools/content/`):** `content preview doc <id> --set field=value [--matrix]` prints a document as plain text with entry, struck and gap forms; `content preview dlg <id> --set …` prints which answers show under a state; `content validate` ends with the budget footer and the longest-string readout per kind; `content watch` re-lints the changed file within `LINT_WATCH_MS`.
- **Accessibility inputs this format carries** (implemented by Paper UI, recorded project-wide by `/ux-design`): the `acc.*` namespace for alt text on stamps and hands; `register` and `speaker` on every spoken string for screen-reader prefixes; nothing in the format assumes spaces, hard-wrapped lines or byte counts.
