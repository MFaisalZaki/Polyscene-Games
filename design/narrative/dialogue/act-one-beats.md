> **Conventions**: present tense throughout. Every beat stands alone in any order the engine admits;
> no beat names an earlier beat. Names follow the narrative director (Sokol, Marek); flat numbers
> follow the brief (Marta 3, the Halms 5). Serial IV/4471/R.31 is the widow's file; IV/7781/R.40 is

> **Narrator key**: `### <class>` opens a beat; the `> paper:` line closes it and is the state's wording.
> `> paper:` is the state's wording, or the nearest paper the state will read — the beam tally (read at the raid),
> the building book (copied to the handler's form).
> **Epilogue stamp**: given on the first line of each card so the page can end on the crossing.

## Openings

### Vera, at the desk

Seven fifty. The banking hall holds its cold until ten. Vera pulls the finger-cot on, signs
the key book, and the grille slides back on its brass track. Berta is already at the next desk
with the tea urn and the week's calendar sheet, not looking up. The intake tray holds six
folders. Five are blue-tabbed and want only their leaf count. The sixth is grey-green with a
red tab, the string tie knotted twice, the way the corridor knots it. The serial on the gummed
tab reads IV/4471/R.31. Nine years old, and active again. A slip on the cover, in a handling
officer's hand: for reconciliation. She draws it towards her and does not untie it yet.

### Jan, in the cellar

The hatch under the chestnut takes two hands and a knee. Below, the cellar smells of coal, lamp
oil and the ink that never comes off. Jan lights the lamp and the press comes out of the dark a
piece at a time: the platen, the chase, the type cases two sorts short of an alphabet, the
roller wrapped in a rag. The last sheet of the Leaf is pinned to the beam, two columns, the
masthead cut from lino, the right margin smudged where the roller ran dry. There is no paper on
the shelf. He counts the ream he does not have, twice, and puts his hand on the press, which is
dry.

### Tomas, at the gate

Brenava at 13:40 is sand, wire and the river doing nothing. The bridge has one gate and the gate
has one stamp cabinet: twelve stamps, one ink. Tomas has the gate; Ondra has the log. The dogs
are fed. The train from Vienna is due on the bridge at 14:10 and the company will walk it, car
by car, staff compartment last. Over the kiosk the Plan Calendar says Week 40 and a figure for
sugar beet. Ondra sits on the stool with the log open on his knee, writes the date at the top
of a clean page in his tidy, left-leaning hand, and holds the pen out.

## Beats

### read_file

The string tie comes undone in two pulls. Form K-1, then forty leaves numbered in the corner by
a hand that changed twice in nine years. Vera reads as the office reads: cover, index, last leaf
first. Leaf 40 is a source report, two lines and a foot. A parcel carried up the stair on a
Tuesday by a man in a railway coat. Contents stated as medicine, foreign. At the foot, a source
and a receipt number. The stair is Vrba Street, number 9; the flat is 3, a widow's. Vera knows the
stair. She knows the cough through the wall. She closes the folder on her thumb to keep the
place, and the tab stripe is red.

> paper: IV/4471/R.31 leaf 40. Source report, fond S: parcel up stair to Vrba 9/3, Tues., rail staff. Stated: medicine, foreign.

### file_report

Vera uncaps the ink and opens the District Register to the next free line. The pen is the
office's; the hand is hers. Serial, year opened, subject, address, status. The line takes
eleven seconds and makes no sound but the nib. She blots it. Then the cross-reference, fond S,
a codename and a receipt number, so that the one who told is paid. Then the household card, tab
turned from pending to complete. She ties the folder with the corridor's double knot and sets
it in the out tray, where Berta's eyes will pass over it and not stop. The ledger balances.
Nothing in the room has changed except the line.

> paper: Register leaf 212 line 14. IV/4471/R.31 SOKOL Marta, Vrba 9/3. ACTIVE, observation. Ref. fond S/RIBA, rcpt 118.

### burn_report

The stove in the banking hall is lit from October. Vera carries the folder across the room at
the pace of someone going for tea. Forty leaves, numbered in the corner, go in by fours. The
carbons curl first, violet, then white. The K-1 board cover she keeps, because a cover is not a
report. Nobody looks up; the urn is loud. Back at the desk the intake log shows a serial with no
leaf count beside it, and the initials against that line are not hers. Berta signed the folder
in this morning. Vera uncaps her pen and does not write anything.

> paper: Intake log, Mon.: IV/4471/R.31 received, B.L. Leaves: [blank]. Out: [blank].

### recover_report

The violet carbon of leaf 40 is still in the district, because the district keeps everything.
Vera signs for the carbon box, finds the source report by serial, and types the leaf back onto
office stock through two fresh carbons, with the margin slightly wrong. A folder with a missing
leaf is a gap; a folder with a retyped leaf is a folder. She numbers it in the corner in the
hand the index already shows, and writes the leaf count into the intake log where the line was
blank. Berta's initials stay beside it. The ledger will balance tonight. Vera washes the ink
from her thumb.

> paper: Intake log: IV/4471/R.31, leaves 40, reconstituted from district carbon. V.H.

### doctor_ledger

The intake log has a line with a serial and nothing after it, and the initials beside the line
are Berta's. Vera does not strike anything; the Register is not opened. She takes the pen from
the dish by the finger-cots, Berta's pen, and fills the line in a hand close enough to Berta's:
leaves, forty; out, to handling officer; the initials again, B.L. Nine years at the next desk
is long enough to learn a loop. The log now says a folder came in whole and went out whole, and
the name on both ends of the line is not hers. She blots it and sets the pen back in the dish.
Across the desk Berta is counting a blue tab and does not look up.

> paper: Intake log, Mon.: IV/4471/R.31 received B.L. Leaves 40. Out: to H.O. B.L.

### warn

Ten past eleven. The stair light clicks off before she reaches the second gallery and she lets
it. Vera knocks at flat 3 the way one knocks after dark, with a knuckle. The chain, the cough,
the door. She does not go in. "Pani Sokol. The Tuesday parcel. Not for a while." Marta looks at
her the way the building looks at a clerk from the Records Office. "Not on Tuesdays," Vera
says, and that is all she brought. Above them, at the third-floor rail, a cigarette is lit and
not smoked. The stair light clicks back on without either of them touching it.

> paper: Building book, Vrba 9: 23:10, HALM V. (5) at door of 3, approx. 1 min. No entry. K.

### leak_file

The pink carbon goes to the handling officer, the white to Central, the violet stays. Vera has,
for one evening, all three on her desk, and the count in the carbon box is Berta's to keep. She
folds the violet of leaf 40 into the back of a Plan Calendar sheet, slogan outward, and puts it
in her coat. On Vrba Street the cellar hatch is under the chestnut, which the third floor
cannot see. She does not go down. She leaves the folded sheet under the bin lid where paper for
the press is left, and goes up to flat 5, and runs the tap.

> paper: Carbon box, district: IV/4471/R.31 leaf 40, violet. Count 0 of 1. Checked B.L.

### take_blame

The memo form has a line for clerk responsible and Vera writes her own name on it. Subject:
discrepancy, intake log, serial as above. Cause: handling error, clerk class II, Records
Section. The next line asks for the initials against the entry and she writes V.H. where the
log says otherwise, and signs so that the signature is the louder of the two. Berta, at the
next desk, keeps her head down over a blue-tab count and does not stop counting. Vera puts the
memo in the registrar's tray face up. On paper there is now one name near the gap, and it is
hers.

> paper: Memo, Records IV, W40: discrepancy at intake, IV/4471/R.31. Clerk responsible: HALM V., class II. Signed.

### report_jan

The household card for flat 5 has a line for contacts of the household, and the review will
not sit until it is filled. Vera fills it. Not her daughter's name; a name the file can use
instead. Vrba Street 9, the cellar, the printing. She writes the hatch, the lamp, the hour she
has seen light under the chestnut. The pen does not shake. She reads the line as the corridor
will read it, and it reads as a clerk doing her work. She turns the tab from pending to
complete and ties the string. The card is one line heavier. The cellar is a serial now.

> paper: Household card IV/7781/R.40, HALM, Vrba 9/5. Contacts: RUSEK J., Vrba 9 cellar, unregistered press. COMPLETE.

### receive_courier

Two knocks on the hatch, a pause, one knock. Marek comes down the ladder backwards with the bag
against his chest, still in the railway coat. On the bench he sets out what the staff
compartment carried for the press: two reams of thin Western stock and a tin of ink. The
smaller parcel, brown paper and a Vienna chemist's string, stays in his coat. "Flat 3," Marek
says. "Before she books the call." Jan weighs the reams the way the press weighs them: four
hundred sheets, eight readers a sheet. The cellar has paper again. Marek is already on the
ladder, and goes up by the stair. He never sits.

> paper: Lis cellar tally (pencil, beam): stock in, 2 rm. Ink, 1.

### print_leaflets

The platen comes down and the house takes the sound through its floor. Jan sets the type by
lamp: two columns, the masthead cut from lino, the week's sheet. Ink on the roller, roller on
the chase, sheet in, sheet out, the right margin smudged where the roller always runs dry. He
stops reading what he prints after the first fifty; the hands know it. Stove cold, lamp low,
the tram bell at the loop at half past four. By first light the stack under the beam is four
hundred, uneven, inked wrong, people's paper. He wipes his hands on the rag and the hands stay
black.

> paper: Lis cellar tally: run 400. Stock out, 1 rm. Sheet W40.

### distribute

The sheets go out the way they always go: folded into Delnik under doormats, under the seats of
tram 11 on the loop run, in the lockers at the baths, through the parcel counter. Jan takes Lom
himself, landing by landing, one sheet a floor, the stair lights clicking off behind him every
ninety seconds. He does not look at who reads. By the evening programme a sheet is being read
aloud through a wall somewhere, badly. By Thursday the Vienna transmitter reads it in a better
voice.

> paper: 4th Bn. order of the day: post sacks and staff compts. opened in full. Reason: hostile print in circulation.

### expose_informer

The older signatories want names printed; the wall-writers want none. Jan sets the name
himself, so that nobody else has set it. Third floor, front, caretaker since 1984, a telephone
in one year instead of eight, a source with a receipt. Beneath it, as proof, the line from a
source report, with the receipt number left on, in the state's own wording, serial and all.
Four hundred copies. One sheet is read by eight. One of the eight will be the corridor, and
the corridor will know which office the serial came from. Jan prints it anyway. The press does
not care whose name it is.

> paper: Ops. IV: Lis W40 col. 2 recovered. Quotes IV/4471/R.31 leaf 40 verbatim. Source office: Records IV. Trace by desk.

### ask_carbon

Ilona comes down the ladder quicker than the others. Jan waits until the circle has gone up.
"Your mother brings work home." It is not a question. "There is a report. A source report, with
a serial on it. The violet copy stays in the district. In a drawer, probably. In a kitchen."
Ilona says her mother does not bring work home. Jan says all right. Then he says the sheet
cannot print a name without the paper behind it, and that the paper is in flat 5. He does not
say please. He lets her go up the ladder first and does not watch her go.

> paper: Lis cellar tally: col. 2 held, pending source document (district carbon).

### cut_ilona

She comes down with the others and Jan is at the foot of the ladder before she reaches the
bottom rung. "Not you. Not any more." He says it to the wall above her head. "Go up, and go
home by the street, not the yard." She asks why, once. He has a reason and does not give it,
because a reason is a thing that can be repeated. The circle behind him pretends to count
sheets. The hatch comes down. On the gallery she stands a moment with the courtyard lamp on
her and the third-floor rail empty, then goes up to flat 5 by the stair, as told.

> paper: Lis cellar tally: circle, W40: 3. (Struck: 4.)

### recant

The room at the Ribbon has a table, two chairs and a typewriter whose sample page is on file.
Rybar does not raise his voice; he has not needed to. The declaration is already typed. Jan
reads it. It says that he, Jan Rusek, published without licence, that the publication was
unauthorised and its contents untrue, that he regrets. It does not ask for names, which is the
kindness. He signs. The pen is the state's. It goes through two carbons, white and pink, and
the pink stays on the table where he can see it. "Thank you," says Rybar, and means the
signature.

> paper: Declaration, RUSEK Jan: publication unlicensed, contents untrue. Regret expressed. Signed. Copies 2.

### give_courier

The question is asked once, politely, and then the room waits. The inventory from the cellar is
on the table: two reams of thin Western stock, a tin of ink, a chemist's string. "The paper is
foreign. Somebody carries it." Jan looks at the string for a long time. Then he gives the name
that is already on the railway's staff roster and the Tuesday train's duty sheet, where they
could have found it in an hour. "Marek. The sleeping car." Rybar writes it in a hand that does
not hurry. The string stays on the table. The train is due on the bridge at 14:10.

> paper: Statement RUSEK J., add.: supply via rail staff, Tues. intl. train, attendant MAREK. To list, Brenava.

### give_ilona

"The circle." Rybar says the word without weight, as if it were a chess club. Most of the names
are already in the room, in a folder with a red tab. He turns one card face up on the table: a
household card from Vrba Street 9 with the contacts line empty. "This one." Jan says the girl
sat in twice, read, said nothing, set no type. He says it as a kindness and hears it land as a
confirmation. Rybar writes attended, two sittings, and the flat number, and thanks him. The
card is one line heavier. It is not Jan's card.

> paper: Household card IV/7781/R.40, HALM, Vrba 9/5. Contacts: HALM Ilona, attended cellar, 2 sittings. Per RUSEK J.

### give_vera

The sheet is on the table, column two, the quoted line with the serial left on. "The district
keeps the violet copy," Rybar says. "Records Section. The clerks are class II. There are two at
that desk." He does not ask which. He lays two cards on the table, a name on each, and waits,
and does not look at his watch. Jan knows the stair at number 9 and which kitchen window faces
the courtyard. He touches the card with the name from flat 5. He does not say it aloud, which
changes nothing. Rybar thanks him, takes that card, and leaves the other.

> paper: Statement RUSEK J., add.: source of district carbon identified: HALM V., clerk II, Records IV.

### let_pass_courier

The staff compartment is last. Marek has his railway card in his hand and the parcel on the
shelf behind his head, brown paper, chemist's string. Tomas looks at the card, and at the
parcel, and at the parcel. The dogs are on the platform, fed. Ondra is on the step with the log
open. "Clear," Tomas says, and stamps the card, and the stamp is the right one. The compartment
is not in the log as opened, because it was not opened. The train pulls off the bridge at
14:19. Ondra's pen is still over the line. There is a line in the log with nothing after the
time.

> paper: Brenava log, Tues. 14:10 intl.: cars 1-9 insp. Staff compt.: [blank]. O.P.

### detain_courier

"Step down, please." Marek steps down. The parcel comes off the shelf and onto the inspection
table on the bridge, and the chemist's string comes off under Tomas's knife. One inhaler, blue
plastic, foreign, the label in another alphabet. Beneath it, flat, two reams of thin stock that
are not medicine. Ondra writes it down as Tomas calls it. The sergeant comes, then the
lieutenant; the train is held eleven minutes. Marek is walked to the post and stands there with
his hands behind him, polite, saying nothing. By evening the company is commended by name. The
inhaler goes into an envelope with a serial.

> paper: Brenava log, 14:10 intl.: staff compt. opened. Seized: inhaler (1), paper 2 rm. Detained: MAREK, rail staff.

### let_pass_family

The car on the road from Kalenov holds a man, a woman, two children and a sheet of paper that
is not a grey card. The Standing Order says the bridge, the strip and the train; it says
nothing about a road. The lieutenant is at the Ribbon. Tomas reads the paper, which is a letter
from a hospital in Vienna, and reads it again. The woman says nothing. The younger child is
asleep. He lifts the bar. He stamps nothing, because there is nothing to stamp. The car crosses
at walking pace. Ondra writes the time and looks at him for the length of the line.

> paper: Brenava log: 15:52, vehicle, road, 4 pers. Documents: none. Passed. T.B.

### detain_family

The car on the road from Kalenov holds a man, a woman, two children and a sheet of paper that
is not a grey card. Tomas reads it and hands it back. "I can't take this." The man says it is a
hospital letter. Tomas says he can see that. The Standing Order says the bridge, the strip and
the train; it says nothing about a road, and nothing is the answer. The bar stays down. The man
reverses twenty metres and stops, and then another twenty. The dogs watch the car. Ondra writes
the plate and the number of persons and the word returned, which is the word.

> paper: Brenava log: 15:52, vehicle, road, 4 pers. No grey card. Returned. Plate noted. T.B.

### confess_gap

The captain reads the log the way the Ribbon reads: last page first. "This line." Tomas can see
it upside down: the time, and nothing after. Ondra's hand. Ondra stands at the guardroom door
with his cap under his arm, nineteen, with a father at the depot and an uncle who is a mark on
a card. "Mine," Tomas says. "I had the compartment. I called it clear and he wrote what I
called." The captain nods, which is not agreement, and writes. The report form has room for one
name. Tomas watches the pen put his there, and not Ondra's.

> paper: 4th Bn. report, Brenava: staff compt. not inspected, 14:10 intl. Responsible: BREN T., pte. Log clerk cleared.

### name_route

The captain is polite. He lays the log on the table between them, open at the line with nothing
after the time, and does not point. "I'm not interested in the line," he says. "I'm interested
in what comes through it." Ondra is at the door with his cap under his arm. Tomas has seen the
parcel on the shelf, the chemist's string, the railway card. He says what the parcel was, whose
coat carried it, and which day of the week. The captain writes it down and underlines the day.
The log closes. Ondra is told to go back to the stool. Nobody says his name again.

> paper: 4th Bn. report, Brenava: supply route: Tues. intl. train, rail staff, parcels, Vienna. Log clerk cleared.

### cover_log

The log is bound and the pages are numbered, so a page cannot go. A line can be filled. Ondra
waits for the bridge to empty, then writes after the time in his tidy, left-leaning hand: staff
compt., insp., nil, and his initial. The ink is the same ink. He blows on it. He does not tell
Tomas, because telling is a second line and there is room for one. The log goes back in the
cabinet with the twelve stamps. If a captain reads it, it will read as a clean page in a clean
book, in the hand of the man who keeps it.

> paper: Brenava log, 14:10 intl.: staff compt., insp., nil. O.P.

### report_tomas

The company clerk's door is open in the evenings. Ondra goes in with the log under his arm,
sets it down open, and puts a finger by the line with nothing after the time. He says who had
the compartment. He says it quietly and once, and gives the minute the train pulled off, which
is the kind of detail a clerk likes. The clerk writes it on a form with a serial. Ondra signs
beneath as log keeper, which is a job. Back in the hut Tomas is cleaning the stamp. Ondra sits
on his bunk and takes his boots off and says nothing about the clerk.

> paper: 4th Bn. report, Brenava: staff compt. not inspected, 14:10 intl. Gate: BREN T. Per PETRIK O., log keeper.

### join_circle

The hatch under the chestnut is heavier than it looks. Ilona goes down the ladder in her
mother's old coat and finds three people, a press, a lamp, and a smell of ink that will be on
the coat tomorrow. Nobody asks her name. Somebody passes her a sheet, two columns, smudged on
the right. She reads it; it says what the radio does not. The man at the press does not look
at her until the hour is up, and then says: Tuesdays. Above, in the courtyard, the sodium lamp
comes on. At the third-floor rail a cigarette is lit. She climbs out into the light.

> paper: Lis cellar tally: circle, W40: 4. New: 1 (student, no. 9).

### take_carbon

The kitchen table in flat 5 has one drawer, and the drawer holds the finger-cots her mother
brings home by mistake and a folded sheet the colour the state uses for the copy that stays.
Ilona reads the serial, the word parcel, the name of the widow through the wall. She reads it
twice, because it is her mother's work and her mother's work does not come home. She folds it
along its own crease and puts it inside her anatomy notes. The drawer closes with the sound it
always makes. Her mother, in the next room, is listening for the time signal.

> paper: Carbon box, district: IV/4471/R.31 leaf 40, violet. Count 0 of 1.

### renounce

The form is called a Statement of Position and has a place for a signature and a place for
particulars. The particulars are the point. Ilona writes that she attended, that she did not
know the nature, that she renounces. Then the line asks where, and the pen stops, and she
writes the street, the number, and the word cellar. Her handwriting is her mother's, slightly.
The registrar reads it without expression and says the committee can sit again this week. On
the stair at home the light clicks off, and she does not press it, and goes up in the dark to
flat 5.

> paper: Statement of Position, HALM Ilona: attendance admitted, renounced. Particulars: Vrba 9, cellar, press. Resit.

### send_word

The booth at the Lom post office smells of other people's three minutes. Marta has the Tuesday
slot, 18:00. The line hums; her son's voice, then the soft double click two seconds in. She
says the weather here is fine. She says: do not send the parcel. Not this month, not the next.
She says it twice because the line is loud. He asks about her breath and she says it is her
own. The clerk taps the glass. The line stays open half a second after she sets the receiver
down. She walks home by Vrba Street slowly, for the stairs, and climbs them stopping twice.

> paper: Lom PO call book: SOKOL M., Vienna, 18:00, 3 min. Tape ref. IV/4471.

### plead_kastan

The lodge hatch is open and the cigarette on the ledge is the caretaker's. Marta holds the
rail. "Pan Kastan." Her breath comes in short sets and he waits with his hands on the ledge.
"My son's parcel. Tuesdays. If it did not come up the stair." He says the gate is his, the
stair is the cooperative's, and a parcel is not his business. She tells him what he can put in
the book instead: the Halm girl, flat 5, evenings, the courtyard, the hatch under the chestnut.
He writes nothing while she is there. He takes the cigarette back off the ledge. She goes up,
stopping twice.

> paper: Building book, Vrba 9: HALM I. (5), evenings, courtyard, cellar hatch. Per 3. K.

### go_to_hospital

The ambulance takes forty minutes because the gate is locked at 22:00 and the caretaker must
be woken. Marta is carried down the stair in her coat by two men who stop twice. The Lom
hospital admits her to a corridor bed and puts her on the oxygen it does have. In the morning a
nurse with a clipboard asks for the household card, the pharmacy book, and the name of whoever
supplies the medicine that is not on the pharmacy's list. Marta says her son writes. The nurse
writes that down. The bed has a number. The clipboard has a serial.

> paper: Lom Hospital adm. 03:40: SOKOL M., Vrba 9/3, asthma, acute. Foreign medication noted. Papers requested, SKS IV.

### report_colleague

Berta's memos are typed, because her hand is known. Subject: intake, District Register,
discrepancy. Serial as above. The memo says what the log says: a folder signed in, no leaf
count against it, no out time, the initials beside the line hers. It says which desk the folder
went to. It gives no name; in this office a desk is a name. She reads it once, pulls the
carbon, and puts the white in the registrar's tray beneath a blue-tab count, where it will be
found in the course of things. She does not look across. Four years from pension. She sharpens
the pencil.

> paper: Memo, Records IV, W40: intake discrepancy, IV/4471/R.31. Folder to desk 2. No leaf count. B.L.

### cover_vera

The intake log has a line with a serial and nothing after it, and the initials beside the line
are Berta's. She uncaps her pen. A gap against her name is hers whether she writes or not; so
she writes. Leaf count: forty. Out: to handling officer. Her initials again, firmer. The log
now says a folder went out that nobody will find, and the name on that is hers alone. She blots
it. Across the desk Vera is reading a blue-tab folder she has already counted. Berta does not
look at her. She sets the pen in the dish by the finger-cots and takes the next folder from the
tray.

> paper: Intake log, Mon.: IV/4471/R.31 received B.L. Leaves 40. Out: to H.O. B.L.

### inform

Thursday is the meeting. The handler has a corner table at the baths cafe and a receipt book
with a carbon. Kastan gives the week: the gate round, who pressed the stair light after ten,
which flat has the radio tuned west, who came up the stair with a parcel and on which day. He
gives it the way he keeps the building book: by the hour. The handler writes, turns the book,
and Kastan signs for a sum that would not buy a coat. The sum is not the point; the signature
is. He gets two packets of cigarettes with it, which is the custom, and goes home on tram 11.

> paper: Receipt book IV/S, no. 118: source RIBA, W40, paid, signed. Report filed to IV/4471/R.31 and IV/7781/R.40.

### report_neighbour

The building book sits on the lodge ledge and Kastan writes in it in pencil. Tonight's entry:
the hour, the landing, a figure at the door of flat 3 after the gate was barred, how long,
which flat the figure went up to. He writes it as he saw it from the third-floor rail: a coat,
a knuckle on the door, no light. He does not write what was said, because he did not hear.
Thursday he copies the line onto the handler's form and the handler asks nothing. The line goes
into a folder whose subject is the widow, and across to a card whose subject is not.

> paper: Source RIBA, W40: 23:10, HALM V. (5) at door of 3, approx. 1 min, no light. Cross-ref. IV/7781/R.40.

### tell_archivist

He waits on the second landing with the keys, where the stair is narrow. "Pani Halm." Vera has
her bag and her coat. "Your girl. Evenings.
The hatch under the chestnut." He says it to the keys. "I haven't put it in the book." He does
not say yet, and does not need to; he is a caretaker and she works in records, and both of them
know what a book is. He thanks her, for nothing, and goes up to the third floor, and she stands
on the landing until the light clicks off, and then presses it.

> paper: Building book, Vrba 9: HALM I. (5), evenings, courtyard. [Not entered.] K.

### E1 The complete file

Stamp: DESTROYED
