## 1. The State Records Service (SKS) — Rybar's service

**What it is**: a security police that governs by paper. Its power structure follows its name: whoever holds the complete record holds the Service, so Records and Operations are rivals, not partners. [K]

**Goal**: a complete, central, duplicated record of everyone who matters, before 12 November. [K] Operations wants the ledger complete because a complete ledger is insurance — evidence to bargain with if the Party opens a "dialogue", a weapon if it does not. [D]

**Methods**: informers, taps, the K-1 sheet, the "invitation" (a summons that is not an arrest), the search with inventory form. Interrogation is paperwork performed aloud. [K]

**Internal cracks**: the paper side (Vera, Berta — clerks class II, women, underpaid, indispensable) versus the corridor (officers, men, carry the pink carbons). The Ribbon's central registry distrusts district offices. [D] Since Week 41 the corridor has been ordering files "to Central" that never arrive: shredding has begun, and the paper side can see it in the ledger. [H]

**Sees the others**: informers are consumables; printers are a crime with a serial number; the garrison is a rival service with worse typists.

## 2. The informer network — Kastan's kind

**What it is**: the Service's eyes, bought one receipt at a time. [K]

**Filed as**: an **A-source** has signed a commitment and has a codename; a **B-source** is paid occasionally and signed for; a **C-source** does not know. Codenames are tree names by District IV convention, because the first registrar used a botanical dictionary; a source is "LIPA" or "JASEN", never a surname. The one exception is a caretaker whose surname already *is* a tree (see lore-naming.md). The informer file (fond S) is cross-referenced to the subjects it reports on, never the reverse. [D]

**Paid**: small sums signed for in the handler's **receipt book**; favours worth more — a caretaker's post, a telephone line in two years instead of eight, a daughter's place at the faculty. The receipt is the leash: the state can always prove you were paid; you can never prove it was little. [D]

**Burned**: when exposed or exhausted, the S-file goes to the Cold Shelf and the receipt-book page is cut out and sent to Central. The source keeps nothing. Informers call this "going into the drawer without the tab". [D]

**Internal cracks**: there is no network, only spokes; sources never meet, and fear each other more than the Service. Caretakers are the most common A-source in Lom: they hold keys, see the stair, and are poor. [K]

**Sees the others**: the Service is a landlord; printers are rent; the garrison is where their sons are.

## 3. The print network — Jan's circle

**What it is**: a dozen cells of three, loosely called **"the Leaf"** (*Lis*), after the sheet they print. Nobody knows more than one person upstream. [K]

**The sheet**: A4 cut and folded to A5, two columns, hand-set type on a platen press salvaged from a closed Lom print shop, with a linocut masthead. Misregistered, inked unevenly, smudged — people's paper. State typewriters and duplicators are registered with a sample page on file, which is why a hand press, which has no sample, is worth a prison term. [K]

**How leaflets move**: inside *Delnik*, folded; under tram 11 seats on the loop run; in the public baths' lockers; through the parcel counter at Lom post office; by the Tuesday train attendants westward, which is how the Vienna transmitter reads them aloud two days later. "One sheet is read by eight." [D]

**Internal cracks**: older signatories who want names printed as a moral act versus the young wall-writers who want no names at all; and the standing suspicion that one cell is a source. [D] One is. [H — identity: narrative-director's decision; the Service's file calls it "JASEN".]

**Sees the others**: the Service is an archive that will outlive it; informers are neighbours; the garrison is boys with their own parents' names in the Drawer.

## 4. The garrison — Tomas's unit

**What it is**: 4th Battalion, Border Guard, Brenava Post. Conscription at eighteen or nineteen for twenty-four months; border service for those with a clean family card and no relatives abroad. Officers are career men; sergeants are conscripts in their second year. [K]

**Orders**: the **Standing Order of 1951**: nobody crosses the Oren bridge without a stamped grey card, and anyone on the raked strip is challenged once and then fired upon. "Hold the line" means the bridge, the strip, and the train. [K] Since Week 40 it also means the road from Kalenov, where the Order says nothing. [D]

**What it fears**: being ordered to shoot; being the ones left on the bridge when the officers drive to the Ribbon; the dogs, which are the only thing at Brenava fed on schedule; the family card, on which an uncle in Vienna is already a mark. [K] The commandant has a grey card of his own in his desk. [H]

**Sees the others**: the Service is a second, better-paid uniform; informers include the company clerk; printers are the ones whose sheets arrive in the post sacks the garrison is supposed to open.
